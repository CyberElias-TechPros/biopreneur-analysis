Galatians 5:1 contains two commands, and most preaching only gets to the first one.

"Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage."

The first command tells you the liberty is real and already given. The second tells you it can be **lost again** — not legally, but practically.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **liberty is not a place you arrive at; it is a position you hold — and the command in Galatians 5:1 assumes that a freed person can walk back into the same cage and close the door from the inside.**

## The Two Commands

### Stand Fast

The Greek is *stēkete* — a military word, to hold a position under pressure. It is not "relax in your freedom."

A soldier who has taken ground and then drifted from it has lost it as surely as one who never took it.

**Freedom is held, not merely received.**

### Be Not Entangled Again

The second verb is *enechō* — to be held in, caught in a net. The picture is of someone who has been freed and then gets caught in the same mesh.

And note the word *again*. Paul is writing to people who had already been released.

**He is not warning strangers about a cage. He is warning escaped prisoners about going back.**

### The Yoke

A yoke is not a punishment; it is a tool. It distributes weight and directs movement.

The problem is that a yoke decides where you are going, and the man wearing it usually does not.

Whatever directs your life is your master, whatever you call it.

### What the Yoke Was in Galatia

### Not Licence, Legalism

The specific error in Galatia was not looseness. It was the addition of circumcision and law-keeping to the gospel, as though Christ's work needed completing (Galatians 5:2–4).

The false teachers were not asking the Galatians to sin. They were asking them to be *careful*.

**The most common yoke in church history is not immorality. It is a religious system that promises security for compliance.**

### The Logic Paul Uses

His argument is severe: "whosoever of you are justified by the law; ye are fallen from grace" (Galatians 5:4).

That is not a threat to be lost. It is a description of what happened inside them — they had begun trusting their own performance as the basis of standing.

You cannot simultaneously rely on Christ's work and your own record. One of them has to be the ground.

### Why It Felt Safe

And this is the part worth understanding: the yoke felt *safe*. Rules are measurable. A checklist tells you where you stand.

Grace feels unstable because it puts your standing entirely outside your control — which is exactly why it is rest.

**Legalistic religion is not an excess of devotion. It is a way of managing anxiety about whether you are acceptable.**

### The Other Yoke

### Licence, Which Is Also Slavery

And there is a second yoke on the other side, and Paul addresses it in the same paragraph: "use not liberty for an occasion to the flesh" (Galatians 5:13).

A Christian who treats freedom as permission to obey his appetites has not escaped slavery; he has changed owners.

License is not the opposite of legalism. It is the same disease — self at the centre, with different symptoms.

#### The Yoke of People

There is a third, quieter one, and James names it: respect of persons, the fear of man that makes you partial (James 2:1–4).

A believer who cannot say an unwelcome truth, or who reshapes his convictions depending on the room, is wearing something.

**The fear of people is a yoke that fits comfortably and steers constantly.**

### How You Re-Enslave Yourself

### By Adding Conditions

The first mechanism is the one Paul is fighting: adding a condition to the gospel — obedience, attendance, performance, experience — until you are effectively justified by that thing.

It usually happens gradually, and it usually starts with a good intention.

### By Withdrawing

The second is retreat, and Hebrews names it directly: "Cast not away therefore your confidence, which hath great recompence of reward" (Hebrews 10:35).

Withdrawing from fellowship, from prayer, from the assembly — the absence is not a neutral decision; it is a return to self-management.

### By Measuring

And the third is the most common among sincere believers: the constant audit. Am I acceptable? Am I doing enough? Did God's mood change?

That question, asked of yourself, is the sound of a yoke being refitted.

**Every time you measure your standing by your performance, you have moved the foundation back to yourself.**

### How to Stand

### By Knowing What Was Paid

Freedom is maintained by knowledge, not willpower. Jesus said it plainly: "If ye continue in my word... ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free" (John 8:31–32).

The truth being known is not a general principle. It is the specific fact of the completed work.

### By Refusing the Second Opinion

When the yoke is offered — and it comes as advice, as accountability, as spiritual maturity — the answer is a settled no.

Paul's response to the Galatians was not a debate about practices. It was an astonishment: "I marvel that ye are so soon removed" (Galatians 1:6).

**You do not have to argue with a yoke. You have to step out of it and stay out.**

### By Using the Freedom

And the second half of verse 13 tells you what freedom is for: "by love serve one another."

Freedom that is only enjoyed for its own sake becomes a new kind of self-absorption. Freedom that serves becomes the shape of a released person.

**The **Righteousness** you received was not given so you could be comfortable. It was given so you could be useful without being afraid.**

### The Boundary

One guardrail matters here: standing in liberty is not standing against all instruction, authority, or discipline. A soldier in a battle is under orders; that is not a yoke, that is an assignment.

The distinction is the basis of your acceptance. Discipline that flows from an already-settled standing is obedience. Discipline that is performed to secure standing is a yoke.

**The test is not what you do. It is what you are trying to accomplish by it.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Name your yoke.** Is it a rule you keep, a licence you enjoy, or an audience you cannot disappoint?

**2. Ask what you are trying to secure.** If the answer is "God's acceptance," that is a yoke.

**3. Stop refitting it.** Notice the audit in your head and answer it with Hebrews 10:14.

**4. Use your freedom to serve one person** this week.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Freedom is not carelessness. **The Finished Work** is a fact to rest in; the discipline it produces is a response, not a payment. Your **Legal Position** is settled, and the behaviour is now free to be honest.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Galatians 5:1–13** in one sitting.
- **Write down the last time you felt you had to earn God's approval.**
- **Read John 8:31–36** and mark the word *free*.
- **Refuse one unnecessary rule** this week, consciously.
- **Serve someone** with no record kept.
