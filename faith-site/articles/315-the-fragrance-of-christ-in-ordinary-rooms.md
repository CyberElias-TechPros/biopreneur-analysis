Second Corinthians 2:15 introduces an image for Christian influence that is unlike anything else in Paul's letters, and the difference matters.

"For we are unto God a sweet savour of Christ, in them that are saved, and in them that perish."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **a fragrance is the only kind of influence that cannot be refused at the door — it enters a room before you speak, it works on people who will never agree with you, and it is the same scent whether they receive it as life or as death.**

## The Image

### Euōdia

And the word is *euōdia* — a sweet smell, a good odour. It is the word the Greek Old Testament uses for the acceptable offering, the smell that rose from the altar.

Paul is borrowing sacrificial language and applying it to people.

**You are not merely carrying a message. You are the offering that carries it.**

### The Procession

And the verses before it supply the scene: God "always causeth us to triumph in Christ, and maketh manifest the savour of his knowledge by us in every place" (2 Corinthians 2:14).

A triumph, again — the Roman parade, with incense burning along the route. The smell was part of the victory.

**The knowledge of God is spread by presence, not only by proclamation.**

### In Every Place

And the phrase is emphatic: *en panti topō*. Every place.

Which means the doctrine is not confined to churches, and neither is its carrier.

**The room you are in is the room the scent is in.**

## The Two Reactions

### Life Unto Death

And verse 16 is startling: "To the one we are the savour of death unto death; and to the other the savour of life unto life."

The same scent, two verdicts. Which means the reaction is about the receiver, not about the fragrance.

**The gospel does not become a different thing when it is refused. It becomes a different thing to the person refusing it.**

### Why Fragrance Divides

And the division makes sense of something that confuses believers. A gentle, decent Christian can provoke hostility simply by being in a room.

The hostility is not usually about the person. It is about the scent.

**You are not responsible for how the smell is received. You are responsible for what you smell of.**

### The Terror and the Adequacy

And Paul's reaction is human: "And who is sufficient for these things?" (2 Corinthians 2:16).

And his answer is in the next chapter: "our sufficiency is of God" (2 Corinthians 3:5).

**The question is honest, and the answer is not a technique.**

### What Makes the Scent

### The Person, Not the Pitch

And the first point is that a fragrance is not an argument. You cannot debate somebody out of a smell, and you cannot fake one for long.

Which is why the Christian life is so much more than a set of talking points.

**The argument may open the door. The scent is what stays in the room.**

### Poured Out

And John 12 gives the picture. Mary broke a jar of ointment worth a year's wages and the house was filled with the odour (John 12:3).

The fragrance came from something broken and something expensive being spent.

**You cannot smell like Christ and keep the jar closed.**

### From the Altar

And the Old Testament incense was burned on the altar, day after day, with a recipe that could not be improvised (Exodus 30:34–38).

The scent of a life comes from a routine of offering, not a moment of inspiration.

Fragrance is a habit, and a habit is a routine of costly things done quietly.

### In Ordinary Rooms

### The Kitchen and the Car

And the test of the doctrine is domestic. Paul's "every place" includes the kitchen, the commute, the group chat, and the queue.

Nobody is impressed by a fragrance in a perfume shop.

The proof of a scent is how it behaves outside the bottle.

### Lingering

And a fragrance outlasts the visit. People remember how a room felt after you left, and they rarely remember what you said.

That is a stewardship, and it is also a warning.

The residue is the testimony.

### Without Announcement

And the last quality is silence. Nobody has ever had to point out a smell.

The moment you announce your influence, it stops being fragrance and becomes advertising.

A life that needs to be noticed has not yet become a scent.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a substitute for words. Paul does preach (2 Corinthians 2:17), and faith comes by hearing (Romans 10:17). Fragrance prepares a room; it does not replace the message.

Second, this is not a call to be inoffensive. The scent divides. If nobody has ever found your presence unsettling, that is worth examining.

**The **The Presence of God is what you are carrying; The Anointing is the oil in the jar; and Kingdom Stewardship is the ordinary room you were posted to.

## What This Means for You

1. Read 2 Corinthians 2:12–17 twice.

2. Ask: what do people smell when I leave?

3. Break one jar this week — spend something costly on Him.

4. Serve in an ordinary room with no announcement.

5. Keep faith and wisdom together. Scent is not strategy. **The Anointing** is what rests on a life; wisdom decides which rooms a carrier should walk into and how to leave them.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read John 12:1–8.**
- **Read Song of Solomon 1:12–14.**
- **Read Exodus 30:22–38.**
- **Do one costly, hidden thing for Christ.**
- **Leave one room better than you found it** and say nothing.
