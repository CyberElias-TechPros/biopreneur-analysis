There is a version of the Christian life that is structurally identical to a job interview you can never finish.

Every morning you present your evidence. Every evening you grade the presentation. And no amount of good work finally settles the question, because the question was never about the work.

"To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved" (Ephesians 1:6).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **a performance trap is not escaped by performing better; it is escaped by discovering that the acceptance you are chasing was already granted before you began, in a Person who is not you.**

## What the Trap Actually Is

### Two Halves, Both Necessary

The trap needs a belief and a habit. The belief is that acceptance depends on output. The habit is the constant self-audit that keeps checking.

Take away the belief and the audit loses its purpose. Take away the audit and the belief starves.

**A performance trap is a conviction about worth, run as a daily routine.**

### Why It Feels Like Devotion

The trap is almost never recognised, because it wears the clothing of zeal.

It looks like diligence, standards, accountability, "pressing in." A person in it is usually the person everyone commends.

**The trap's best disguise is that it produces genuinely good results while quietly consuming the person producing them.**

### The Tell

The diagnostic is not how hard you work. It is what happens inside you when the work is not seen.

If nobody notices and it costs you something real, you have found the edge of the trap.

**Anything you need to be admired for is something you have not yet received freely.**

## The Word Paul Uses

### Accepted, Not Tolerated

The phrase *made us accepted* is one verb in the Greek, *charitoō* — to bestow favour on, to grace. It is not a word of forgiveness sliding past the problem; it is a word of positive, delighted reception.

Not merely let in. Welcomed.

You were not admitted grudgingly. You were received as something He wanted.

### "In the Beloved"

And the location of the acceptance settles everything: *in the beloved* — in Christ, the One the Father has always loved (Matthew 3:17).

The acceptance is not generic goodwill toward humanity. It is the Father's disposition toward His Son, extended to everyone found in Him.

You are not accepted for what you resemble. You are accepted because of where you are.

### Done Before You Arrived

And Ephesians 1 has already answered the timing question: "According as he hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world" (Ephesians 1:4).

Whatever the date on your performance review, this decision predates it.

The verdict you are working so hard to reach was entered before the world had a coastline.

### What the Trap Never Tells You

### It Never Says "Enough"

There is no score that satisfies it, because the trap is not asking for a score. It is asking for a verdict.

So the goalposts move: each success raises the standard rather than releasing you.

Fear that something can always be demanded will always find something to demand.

### It Confuses Service With Security

Galatians confronts the same instinct with a good question: "Are ye so foolish? having begun in the Spirit, are ye now made perfect by the flesh?" (Galatians 3:3).

The trap's core error is not activity. It is using activity to finish something only grace could begin.

### It Cannot Rest

And the burden lands physically. A person in the trap has no sabbath, because stopping feels like falling behind.

Hebrews says plainly what remains for the people of God: "Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest" (Hebrews 4:11).

**Rest is itself an act of obedience, and it can only be entered by people who believe the work is finished.**

### The Way Out

### Notice the Question Underneath

Every performance spiral is answering an older question. What is your version — *am I enough, am I wanted, am I going to be left?*

Name it. A question that stays unnamed drives everything.

### Answer It With Ephesians, Not With Effort

The remedy is not more evidence. It is the verdict already given.

"And you hath he quickened, who were dead in trespasses and sins" (Ephesians 2:1) — the work happened while you were incapable of helping.

### Separate Service From Standing

Service is good, commanded, and joyful. But it is the fruit of acceptance, never the purchase price.

**A servant who is not trying to become a son is the freest man in the house.**

### Practise Being Unseen

Do something excellent this week and tell no one. Let the discipline of invisibility teach your heart where the audience actually is.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, freedom from the performance trap is not freedom from obedience. The same Ephesians letter that says "accepted" says "walk worthy of the vocation wherewith ye are called" (Ephesians 4:1). The difference is that *worthy* has become a description rather than a price.

Second, grace is not a shortcut past honesty. If you have been coasting, the answer is not to hide it under grace but to look at it — grace always makes honesty safe.

**The **Justification** that ends the trap is the same doctrine that makes honest self-examination bearable in the first place.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Find the question.** What are you actually hoping to hear from people?

**2. Read Ephesians 1:3–14 this week.** Count every phrase that describes a gift already given.

**3. Do one invisible good thing** and let it stay invisible.

**4. Stop the morning self-audit** and replace it with Ephesians 1:6.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Grace does not cancel diligence; it redefines its purpose. **Righteousness** is the place you stand; **The Finished Work** is why the stand holds even when the day went badly.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Ephesians 1:3–14 three times.**
- **Write down what you fear people will discover about you.**
- **Read Luke 15:11–32** and watch the father run.
- **Take a full day off** without producing anything.
- **Say Ephesians 1:6 aloud** before you open your laptop.
