Job 22 is a speech by Eliphaz, and that matters for how you read it. Eliphaz was one of the friends who spent most of the book being wrong about Job — comforting him with accusations and then being corrected by God at the end (Job 42:7).

So why does the church quote verse 28 so often?

Because not everything a mistaken friend says is mistaken. Eliphaz was wrong about Job's situation and right about the character of God, which is a distinction worth learning. And what he describes here is not a technique for getting whatever you want. It is a portrait of a life that has been put right with God, and the kind of speech that comes out of it.

"Thou shalt also decree a thing, and it shall be established unto thee: and the light shall shine upon thy ways" (Job 22:28). Here is the central truth of this teaching: **a decree in Scripture is a settled word spoken by someone whose life is settled in God — established not by the force of the speaker's will but by the standing he speaks from.**

## Read the Verses Before It

The single most important thing you can do with Job 22:28 is to read the sentence it sits inside, because the verse has been lifted out of a paragraph that explains it.

"Acquaint now thyself with him, and be at peace: thereby good shall come unto thee. Receive, I pray thee, the law from his mouth, and lay up his words in thine heart. If thou return to the Almighty, thou shalt be built up... Then shalt thou lay up gold as dust... Yea, the Almighty shall be thy defence... For then shalt thou have thy delight in the Almighty, and shalt lift up thy face unto God. Thou shalt make thy prayer unto him, and he shall hear thee... Thou shalt also decree a thing, and it shall be established unto thee" (Job 22:21–28).

Count the conditions. Acquaint yourself with Him. Receive His law. Lay up His words in your heart. Return to the Almighty. Delight in the Almighty. Make your prayer to Him.

Then comes the decree.

The decree is the last thing in a sequence, not the first thing in a technique. It is what a reconciled, Word-filled, God-delighting person does — and it is established because the person speaking it is standing where God put him.

**The Authority of the Believer** — the right to stand in His name — is the standing. **Confession of the Word** — saying the same thing God says — is the content. And **the Word of God** — His own living address — is what makes the words established rather than merely loud.

Consider a signature on a legal document. The pen is ordinary and the ink is ordinary. What gives the signature weight is the office of the person holding the pen and the process behind the document. Nobody is impressed by the pen.

### What "Established" Means

The Hebrew word behind "established" carries the sense of being set up, made firm, raised as a permanent structure. It is the same word used of a house being built or a kingdom being secured.

That is a stronger idea than "it will happen." A decree that is established is not a lucky wish that came true; it is a structure that now stands. Which is why the verse is not primarily about getting things. It is about a person whose words have weight because his life has a foundation.

There is a further detail in the verse worth noticing: "the light shall shine upon thy ways." The decree is followed by clarity. The person who speaks from that settled place finds the next step lit, not because he demanded a lamp, but because the road opens for someone travelling in the right direction.

### The Decreeing That Is Not Decreeing

A great deal of what is called decreeing today is not this at all, and it deserves to be named plainly.

There is a kind of speech that treats words as a mechanism — as though the universe were a vending machine and the right formula dispensed the product. That is not the faith of Scripture; it is closer to a superstition with Christian vocabulary. The Bible's own warnings about words run in the opposite direction from a technique: "Be not rash with thy mouth, and let not thine heart be hasty to utter any thing before God: for God is in heaven, and thou upon earth: therefore let thy words be few" (Ecclesiastes 5:2).

There is another kind of speech that is really just insisting — the volume rising as the evidence falls. Scripture never models this. When Jesus stood before Pilate, "he answered him to never a word" (Matthew 27:14). The authority was not diminished by the silence.

Set them side by side and the gap shows. The first man decrees because he is trying to make something true. The second speaks because something already is. One is pressure applied to reality; the other is agreement with it. Only one of them is faith.

### The Decree That Has Content

If decrees are not formulas, what are they made of? Look at the pattern through Scripture.

God says, "So shall my word be that goeth forth out of my mouth: it shall not return unto me void, but it shall accomplish that which I please" (Isaiah 55:11). Notice whose pleasure is being accomplished. Not the speaker's convenience — God's intention.

Jeremiah is given a commission with the same shape: "Behold, I have put my words in thy mouth" (Jeremiah 1:9). The prophet's authority is entirely derived. He does not invent the message; he is given it, and then he is told he will be set over nations and kingdoms to root out and to plant (Jeremiah 1:10). That is an astonishing transfer, and it is bounded completely by the words that were put in his mouth.

So the content of a scriptural decree is the will of God, spoken with understanding, by a person whose life is aligned with it. Which means most of the work of decreeing happens before you open your mouth — in the Word you have taken in, and in the life you have submitted.

## What This Means for You

**1. Put the Word in first.** Eliphaz said to lay up His words in your heart, and only then to decree. The order is not optional. A mouth with no Scripture in it has nothing to decree that heaven is committed to.

**2. Speak about the thing God has actually said.** The safest decree is a text. When you find yourself wanting to declare something the Bible has not promised, stop and ask whether you are speaking faith or pressure.

**3. Keep your speech few and settled.** A believer learning this discipline talks less, not more. The rash mouth is the one that contradicts itself by Friday.

**4. Let the decree be followed by the road.** "The light shall shine upon thy ways" — the point of a settled word is that you now walk in clarity. If your declaration has not changed how you live this week, it was a performance.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom test every declaration.** Faith speaks with confidence in God; wisdom asks whether the thing declared is actually His will, whether the timing is His, and whether the life behind the mouth is in order. Faith and wisdom work together: faith refuses to be silent about what God has said, wisdom refuses to put words in His mouth.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Job 22:21–30 as one paragraph** and list the conditions that precede the decree.
- **Write one decree** — a single sentence taken from a promise of Scripture that applies to your situation — and speak it daily, not as a formula but as agreement.
- **Check one thing you have been declaring** against the actual wording of the Bible.
- **Read Jeremiah 1:4–12** and note where the prophet's authority came from.
