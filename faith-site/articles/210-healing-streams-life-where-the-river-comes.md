Ezekiel's vision of the river is one of the strangest and most hopeful passages in the Old Testament, and almost everything about it is in the wrong direction from what we would expect.

The water does not gather in the temple to bless the worshippers. It comes *out* — under the threshold, past the altar, out of the house, down into the valley, and into the sea.

"And it shall come to pass, that every thing that liveth, which moveth, whithersoever the rivers shall come, shall live" (Ezekiel 47:9).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the river in Ezekiel does not flow toward the sanctuary; it flows from it, which means healing is not something you chase down but something that comes out of the place where God is honoured.**

## The Direction of the Flow

### Out of the House

"Afterward he brought me again unto the door of the house; and, behold, waters issued out from under the threshold of the house eastward" (Ezekiel 47:1).

The threshold is the doorway. The water begins where the feet of the priests stood, and it moves eastward, away from the building.

That is the whole architecture of the passage: **blessing flows outward from presence.** Not the reverse.

### Down to the Desert

The river does not stay in the pleasant places. It goes "toward the east country, and goeth down into the desert, and shall go into the sea" (Ezekiel 47:8).

The desert is *arabah* — the dry, sterile plain. And the sea in view is the Dead Sea, whose salinity kills everything.

The prophecy is that the river changes both. **Healing goes where the need is worst, and it goes because the river kept moving.**

### Life Everywhere It Comes

"Every thing that liveth, which moveth, whithersoever the rivers shall come, shall live" (Ezekiel 47:9).

Notice the phrase *whithersoever*. The life is not distributed equally along the bank; it follows the water.

Which means the question is not whether there is life in the region. It is whether the river has reached your part of it.

### The Trees That Never Wither

The result on the banks is one of the most beautiful descriptions of sustained life in Scripture: "And by the river upon the bank thereof, on this side and on that side, shall grow all trees for meat, whose leaf shall not fade, neither shall the fruit thereof be consumed: it shall bring forth new fruit according to his months, because their waters they issued out of the sanctuary" (Ezekiel 47:12).

Ezekiel explains his own image: the trees are fruitful *because* their waters come from the sanctuary. **The source explains the fruitfulness.**

And Psalm 1 uses the same picture for a person: "He shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that bringeth forth his fruit in his season; his leaf also shall not wither" (Psalm 1:3).

### The River in the New Testament

#### Jesus Standing and Shouting

At the Feast of Tabernacles, water was poured out ceremonially each day. On the last, great day, Jesus stood and cried: "If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink. He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water" (John 7:37–38).

John tells us what He meant: "But this spake he of the Spirit, which they that believe on him should receive" (John 7:39).

So the river of Ezekiel is not a future landscaping project. **It is the Spirit of God, and He is now inside the believer.**

#### Rivers From the Throne

Revelation takes the image to its final form: "And he shewed me a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb" (Revelation 22:1).

And the tree returns, with leaves for medicine: "In the midst of the street of it, and on either side of the river, was there the tree of life... and the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations" (Revelation 22:2).

**The final picture of eternity is not a garden gate but a river and a healing tree.** God's intention for the world has always been wholeness. So why do we treat health as an interruption of His plan rather than its destination?

#### The City That Is Glad

Psalm 46 gives the same image in miniature: "There is a river, the streams whereof shall make glad the city of God" (Psalm 46:4).

And it sits in a psalm about earthquakes and mountains falling into the sea. The river is what remains available when everything else is collapsing.

**Divine Life** is not disturbed by the collapse of circumstances because it is not drawn from them.

## What This Means for You

**1. Be the place where the river starts.** A life that honours God becomes a source, not a destination. The water goes out from you, not toward you.

**2. Do not despise the dry place.** The river went into the desert on purpose. Your own difficult season may be exactly where the flow is meant to arrive.

**3. Stay planted.** Psalm 1's tree is planted, not wandering. Consistency near the source matters more than intensity in the search.

**4. Drink before you pour.** The river in John 7 begins with "let him come unto me, and drink." You cannot give out water you have not received.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** The leaves are for healing, and the doctor's hands are not a rival to the river. God's supply often runs through ordinary channels.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Ezekiel 47:1–12 slowly**, and trace the direction of the water.
- **Read John 7:37–39** and note who the river is.
- **Read Revelation 22:1–5** and count the mentions of healing.
- **Ask one honest question:** what is drying up in me, and where is the water coming from?
- **Drink daily** — read one psalm of the presence each morning this week.
