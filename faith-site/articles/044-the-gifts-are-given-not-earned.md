Have you ever looked at another believer's gift and wondered why yours seems smaller — or whether you have one at all? Gift comparison is one of the quickest ways to miss the point of gifts entirely.

First Corinthians 12:7 says, "But the **manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man to profit withal**." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **spiritual gifts are given by the Spirit to every believer for the common good.**

## Every Man, Given, For Profit

Paul makes three claims in one sentence. *Every man* — no believer is giftless. *Is given* — gifts are received, not achieved. *To profit withal* — the purpose is the benefit of others, not the elevation of the recipient.

**The Power of the Holy Spirit** — the Spirit's supernatural enablement for service — is what manifests through these gifts. **The Anointing** — the Spirit's empowering presence resting within you — is the source. **Kingdom Stewardship** — managing God-given life, gifts, and resources as a trustworthy steward — is the posture that keeps gifts fruitful.

Think of a household where each member is given a tool suited to the family's needs. One receives a lamp, another a rope, another a key. The tools differ, but the purpose is identical: the household functions. No one boasts about the lamp, and no one apologises for the key. Each uses what was placed in his hand.

## Diversity on Purpose

Paul's chapter celebrates variety: word of wisdom, word of knowledge, faith, healings, miracles, prophecy, discerning of spirits, tongues, interpretation. The variety is not a ranking; it is a distribution. The Spirit divides "to every man severally as he will" (1 Corinthians 12:11).

Paul goes further by insisting that no member can dismiss another as unnecessary. In verses 21–26 he imagines the eye telling the hand, "I have no need of thee," and the head telling the feet the same. His conclusion is that the parts which seem more feeble are necessary, and those we think less honourable receive more abundant honour. God arranges the body deliberately, mixing visible and hidden gifts so that no member can boast and no member can withdraw. The believer who thinks his gift is too small has misunderstood the design; the body is built precisely from what looks unimpressive. That single phrase dismantles both pride and envy. Pride says, "My gift makes me important." Envy says, "My gift makes me lesser." Paul says, "The Spirit distributes as He wills" — which means your gift is neither your achievement nor your deficiency. It is your assignment.

Here is the contrast: the gift-centred believer builds a platform; the stewardship-centred believer builds a body. One measures by visibility; the other by benefit. Which measure have you been using on your own gift?

## The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **Romans 12:6** — "Having then gifts differing according to the grace that is given to us, let us use them." Grace is the measure, and use is the assignment. Difference is assumed; faithful use is commanded.
- **1 Peter 4:10** — "As every man hath received the gift, even so minister the same one to another, as good stewards of the manifold grace of God." Gifts are received, then ministered — stewardship language throughout.
- **1 Corinthians 13:1** — "Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, and have not charity, I am become as sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal." Gifts without love become noise. Character is the channel that makes the gift intelligible.

Three witnesses, one assignment: differing gifts, received grace, and love as the channel.

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop comparing and start stewarding.** Your gift is assigned, not awarded. Ask what your sphere needs and how your gift serves it, rather than how it compares to someone else's.

**2. Identify your gift honestly.** Ask trusted believers what they see in you, notice where service bears fruit, and test opportunities in the church. Gifts become visible in use.

**3. Let love govern the gift.** Paul insists that gifts without love become noise. Practise the fruit alongside the gift, or the gift will eventually wound the people it was meant to serve.

**4. Faith and wisdom use gifts responsibly.** Faith steps out in the gift; wisdom trains, prepares, and serves within order and accountability. Declaring grace never excuses sloppiness; it calls for excellent stewardship.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 1 Corinthians 12:1–11 slowly.** Note every gift named, then underline "to every man" and "as he will." Let the distribution settle your heart.
- **Ask two trusted believers what gift they see in you.** Listen without defending, and record what they say.
- **Serve in one concrete way this week.** Choose a need you can meet with what you have — encouragement, practical help, prayer, teaching — and meet it.
- **Practise love alongside the gift.** Before you use your gift, pray for the people who will receive it, by name if possible.

**The central truth, said plainly:** Gifts are given, not earned, and they are given for others. Steward what you received.
