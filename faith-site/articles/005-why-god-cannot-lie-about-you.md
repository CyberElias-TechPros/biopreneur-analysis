What do you do when your circumstances say one thing about your future, your memory says another thing about your worth, and Scripture says something completely different about who you are in Christ? Every week, a quiet courtroom convenes inside your own mind, and you have to decide whose testimony carries final weight.

Paul opens his letter to Titus by naming a bedrock attribute of the Father we worship: He is "God, **that cannot lie**" (Titus 1:2). Notice it does not merely say God *chooses* not to lie on most occasions, the way an honest neighbour tries to keep promises when nothing unforeseen gets in the way. Here is the central truth of this teaching: **what Scripture says you are and have in Christ is unshakably reliable because falsehood is contrary to God's very nature — He cannot lie.**

## The Holy Impossibility Behind Every Promise

Look at the single Greek adjective Paul uses in Titus 1:2: *apseudes* — literally, free from all deceit, untainted by falsehood, incapable of untruth. Human beings lie for two main reasons: either out of malice to deceive someone, or out of weakness because circumstances overtook what they sincerely intended to do. Neither flaw can ever touch the Almighty. His holiness forbids deceit, and His omnipotence forbids failure.

That is why **the Word of God** — God's authoritative self-revelation, supremely in Jesus the living Word and written in the inspired Scriptures — carries the exact reliability of God's own character. You cannot trust God deeply while treating His written Word lightly; a person's word is worth precisely as much as their character and ability to back it up.

Imagine holding a bar of pure gold stamped by a national sovereign mint with its certified weight and `.9999` purity mark. Suppose you carry that bar into a dimly lit hallway and a nervous passerby squints at it and mutters, "In this shadow it looks dull — I doubt it is worth much." Did the dim hallway lower the purity of the gold? Not by a fraction of a gram! The assay stamp testifies to what the refiner verified in the furnace, not to how bright the hallway happens to be at dusk. In Christ, God's Word is the Refiner's stamp on your redeemed spirit. Hard seasons may dim the lighting around you, yet they never alter what God has certified in His Son.

### From Moses to Hebrews: One Unbroken Testimony

Watch how both Testaments build a fortress of assurance around this single truth:

- **Numbers 23:19** — "God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent: **hath he said, and shall he not do it? or hath he spoken, and shall he not make it good?**" Spoken in the wilderness when enemies tried to hire a curse against Israel, this verse reveals why no outside threat could undo God's covenant blessing: God does not revise His promises like a fickle human negotiator.
- **Hebrews 6:17–19** — Willing more abundantly to show the heirs of promise the immutability of His counsel, God confirmed His promise with an oath, "that by two immutable things, **in which it was impossible for God to lie**, we might have a strong consolation… Which hope we have as an **anchor of the soul**, both sure and stedfast." Why did God add an oath to a promise that was already true? Not for His sake — for *yours*, so your soul would have an iron anchor in rough water!
- **1 John 5:10–11** — "He that believeth not God hath made him a liar; because he believeth not **the record that God gave of his Son**. And this is the record, that God hath given to us eternal life, and this life is in his Son." Calling yourself forsaken, unredeemed, or spiritually bankrupt when you belong to Jesus is not modesty; it inadvertently contradicts the Father's sworn record concerning His Son's work.

## What This Means for You

**1. Distinguish false humility from biblical humility.** Why do so many believers think shrinking back from Scripture's bold statements sounds humble? Real humility does not argue with the Creator when He tells you what Christ's cross accomplished. Real humility bows low enough to say, "Lord, I never could have earned this, yet because You said I am forgiven, accepted, and made new in Christ, I believe You."

**2. Practise genuine Confession of the Word.** **Confession of the Word** (*homologia* — speaking the same thing God says in Scripture) is not pretending a flat tyre is inflated or denying a practical problem that needs wise action. Rather, it is aligning your mouth with **Spiritual Realities** — the unseen covenantal facts established by Scripture — while you address earthly responsibilities with sober wisdom and diligence.

**3. Let God's truth outrank yesterday's memory.** Memory is an archive of where you have been, not a prophet of who you are in Christ. Whenever memory replays an old failure that has already been washed under Christ's blood, answer with Romans 3:4: "Let God be true, but every man a liar" — even when the doubting voice is your own!

**4. Mirror your Father's truthfulness in everyday speech.** Children of the God who cannot lie are called to become people whose word can be trusted at home, in business contracts, and in private commitments (Ephesians 4:25).

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Conduct a two-column truth check.** Write down one recurring fearful sentence you have been repeating this month on the left side of a page; on the right side, write a plain, contextually faithful Scripture promise in Christ. Choose deliberately which column gets your agreement this week.
- **Anchor your prayer in Hebrews 6:18–19.** When facing a demanding decision at work, in family life, or in health, pray from God's promise of wisdom (James 1:5) while taking responsible practical steps — seeking sound counsel, doing thorough preparation, and trusting God's faithful hand.
- **Eliminate casual exaggeration from your own speech.** Honour the God of truth by making your "yes" mean yes and your "no" mean no in schedules, finances, and promises to your household.
- **Close the day with 1 John 5:11.** Before sleep, thank the Father that eternal life in His Son is a present record, not a distant wish.

**The central truth, said plainly:** Your feelings have changed a thousand times this year; God's Word has never misspoken once. When your mood and your Maker disagree about who you are in Christ, side with the One who cannot lie.
