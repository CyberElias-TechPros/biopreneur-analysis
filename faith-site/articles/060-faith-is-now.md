Have you ever prayed about something and then spent the following days trying to work out whether God had heard you? That uncertainty usually comes from praying in one tense and believing in another.

Mark 11:24 says, "What things soever ye desire, when ye pray, **believe that ye receive them**, and ye shall have them." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **faith works in the present tense — you believe you have received at the moment of prayer, not after the evidence appears.**

## The Tense of Faith

Jesus uses three tenses in one sentence, and their order is everything. You desire — present. You pray — present. You believe that you receive — present. You shall have — future. The future is the last thing to arrive, and it arrives because the present believing came first.

**The Life of Faith** — a life ordered by God's declarations rather than appearances — begins here, in the tense of prayer. **Confession of the Word** — speaking in agreement with God's declarations (*homologia*, saying the same thing) — keeps the present tense alive after the prayer is finished. **The Name of Jesus** — His person, work, and authority — is the ground on which you ask at all.

Think of a man who signs a contract and then waits for delivery. He does not wait before signing; he signs, and then he expects. His expectation is not presumption because the contract is signed. Faith signs at prayer and expects afterwards.

### Why the Present Tense Is Hard

Believing you have received before you see anything feels like pretending, and that is why many believers slide into a future tense: they believe they will receive someday. But that is not faith in Mark 11:24; it is hope dressed in prayer language.

Jesus anchored the instruction in something remarkable: "Have faith in God" (Mark 11:22), and He immediately spoke of a mountain being removed. The point is not the size of the obstacle but the source of the confidence. Faith in God is not confidence in your own confidence; it is trust in a Person whose word cannot fail. That is why the present tense is possible at all. You are not believing in your ability to believe; you are believing in the One who promised.

The difficulty is not intellectual; it is emotional. Your senses report absence, and your feelings argue for delay. Faith does not deny the senses; it chooses a higher authority. You are not claiming that nothing is wrong; you are claiming that God's word is truer than your circumstances.

Have you ever noticed how quickly a prayer turns into worry once you stop praying it? Consider what each one actually is. The future-tense believer prays and then re-prays the same request in anxiety, treating prayer as repetition rather than reception. The present-tense believer prays once in faith, gives thanks, and guards the confession.

### Now Is Not the Same as Instant

Faith is present tense, but the present tense of faith is not the same as the immediate appearance of the thing believed. Mark 11:24 is set inside a story that spans two days — Jesus curses the fig tree on one evening and the disciples notice it withered the next morning (Mark 11:20–21). The word was spoken in faith; the visible result arrived on a schedule.

That gap is not a contradiction of "now." It is the difference between the time of believing and the time of seeing. Faith operates in the first; patience operates in the second. Confusing them produces either presumption (demanding instant evidence) or despair (concluding the prayer failed).

**The Life of Faith** — a life ordered by God's declarations — holds both without strain. It can say "it is done" about the verdict and "it is not yet" about the circumstances, because the verdict and the circumstances are not the same category of fact.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **1 John 5:14–15** — "If we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us: And if we know that he hear us, whatsoever we ask, we know that we have the petitions that we desired of him." John moves from asking to knowing — the same present-tense confidence Jesus commands.
- **James 1:6** — "But let him ask in faith, nothing wavering. For he that wavereth is like a wave of the sea driven with the wind and tossed." Wavering is a tense problem as much as a doubt problem.
- **2 Corinthians 1:20** — "For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen." God's promises are already affirmed in Christ; faith simply agrees with an established yes.

Three witnesses, one tense: knowing we have, asking without wavering, and agreeing with a settled yes.

## What This Means for You

**1. Pray once, then thank.** After you have prayed in faith, shift from asking to thanksgiving. Repeating the request in anxiety is not persistence; it is unbelief in religious clothing.

**2. Guard what you say afterwards.** Your confession after prayer either confirms or cancels your believing. Speak in agreement with God's promise, not with your fear.

**3. Refuse the pressure to manufacture feelings.** Faith is not a feeling of certainty; it is a decision to stand on God's word. Feelings can follow later; they cannot lead.

**4. Faith and wisdom pray and prepare.** Faith believes the answer is given; wisdom still prepares, works, and waits appropriately. Declaring receiving never excuses presumption; it produces steady, obedient expectation.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Mark 11:22–26 slowly.** Mark the sequence of desire, prayer, believing, and receiving, then write one sentence about which tense you usually live in.
- **Pray once about one specific matter.** Write the request down with the date, then stop re-asking and begin thanking God daily for the answer.
- **Watch your confession for seven days.** Note every time your words contradict what you prayed, and correct them on the spot.
- **Thank God before you see.** Each morning this week, thank Him for the answer as though it were already delivered, because in His word it is.
