Romans 12 ends with one of the most counter-intuitive commands in Scripture, and it is addressed to people who had real grievances.

"Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord" (Romans 12:19).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **revenge is an attempt to be the judge, and it always costs the plaintiff more than the defendant — which is why handing the case to God is not surrender but the only way to actually win.**

## The Command

### Avenge Not

And the Greek is *ekdikeō* — to vindicate, to exact satisfaction. It is courtroom vocabulary, not emotional vocabulary.

Paul is not saying "do not feel angry." He is saying "do not take the bench."

**The command is about jurisdiction, not about feeling.**

### Give Place

And *give place* is the same phrase as Ephesians 4:27 — *topos*, room. Leave room.

Room for whom? For wrath — and the next clause identifies whose.

**Leaving room for God is the opposite of leaving room for the devil.**

### The Reason

And the reason is not that revenge does not work. It works, briefly, and then it costs.

The reason is that vengeance is a divine prerogative, and it has been reserved.

**Not because God is greedy for it, but because only He can administer it without becoming unjust.**

## Why We Want It

### Because It Feels Like Justice

And often it is a genuine desire for justice, which is not a sin. The problem is that we want to be both the injured party and the magistrate.

**A plaintiff cannot also be the judge, however right the plaintiff is.**

### Because It Feels Like Power

And revenge offers a momentary sense of control — the fantasy of the perfect comeback, the imagined devastation of the other person's face.

That is why it is so addictive: it is a form of power available without any risk.

**Revenge is the cheapest power available to a powerless person, and it is also the most expensive.**

### Because the Wound Is Real

And it would be dishonest to skip this. Some of the people reading this have been genuinely wronged, and the wrong was severe.

The command is not a denial of the injury. It is an offer of a better way to carry it.

**God does not ask you to pretend the wound is small. He asks you to hand over the case file.**

### What Surrender Is Not

### Not Denial

And handing over vengeance does not mean calling evil good. Romans 12:9 has already said "abhor that which is evil."

You can name the wrong plainly and still refuse to sentence.

**Forgiveness is not a verdict of "no harm done." It is a transfer of jurisdiction.**

### Not Passivity

And it does not mean refusing all boundaries. A person may need to be reported, resisted, or removed from your life.

The difference is the motive: protection rather than punishment.

You can protect yourself without prosecuting the person.

### Not Forgetting

And it does not require amnesia. God does not forget; He chooses not to remember against us.

Forgiveness is a decision not to keep the account open, not a deletion of memory.

You will remember. The question is whether the memory still has a court attached.

### What Victory Looks Like

### Overcome Evil With Good

And Paul's alternative is not neutrality. "Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good" (Romans 12:21).

*Overcome* is the victory word. He is promising a win, not a truce.

The gospel does not tell you to lose gracefully. It tells you how to win.

### Coals of Fire

And the strange image of verse 20 — feeding an enemy heaps coals on his head — is drawn from an Egyptian ritual of public penance.

The picture is not torture. It is shame that leads to change.

Kindness is sometimes the most disarming weapon in the arsenal.

### David's Restraint

And David is the model. Twice he had Saul in his hand and refused (1 Samuel 24, 26) — and his argument was jurisdictional: "the LORD forbid that I should do this thing unto my master, the LORD's anointed" (1 Samuel 24:6).

He would not take a throne that was not his to take.

David's refusal to avenge is why he could rule without being ruled by revenge.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a command to remain in danger. Leaving an abusive situation is not vengeance.

Second, this is not a doctrine that excuses injustice in the world. Christians are to seek justice for others; the command is about personal retaliation.

**The **The Ministry of Reconciliation is what we were given instead of a gavel; Spiritual Warfare is fighting without becoming the thing we fight; and the Faith and Wisdom of a settled believer knows the difference between a wound and a warrant.

## What This Means for You

**1. Name the case.** Who owes you, and what do you want done to them?

2. Say the sentence out loud: "I hand this to You."

**3. Pray for them** — specifically, not vaguely.

4. Do one good thing for someone who wronged you, or for someone like them.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Forgiveness is a decision; safety is a plan. **The Ministry of Reconciliation** is the calling; wisdom decides what boundaries the situation requires.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Romans 12:14–21 every day.**
- **Read 1 Samuel 24 in full.**
- **Write down the wrong you are holding.**
- **Pray for that person by name, five days.**
- **Note what changes in you** — not in them.
