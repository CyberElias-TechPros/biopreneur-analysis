Jesus's disciples asked Him to teach them to pray, and what He gave them begins with two words rather than a technique.

"Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name" (Matthew 6:9).

Before a single request is made, there is an address. Before anything is asked for, something is stated about who is being addressed.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **prayer begins with who you are addressing and only then moves to what you are asking — which means the first work of prayer is not the request but the relationship the request is brought into.**

## The Setting

Matthew 6 is a chapter about religion done for an audience.

Jesus has just warned against praying to be seen: "And when thou prayest, thou shalt not be as the hypocrites are: for they love to pray standing in the synagogues and in the corners of the streets, that they may be seen of men" (Matthew 6:5).

And then, in the same breath, He gives this prayer. Which means the prayer was never designed as a performance. It is the opposite of one.

**Sonship** — the standing of a child before a Father — is what the first word establishes. **Communion With the Holy Spirit** — the ongoing conversation that standing makes possible — is what prayer actually is. **The Life of Faith** — living from that relationship — is what follows.

Consider a child who bursts into a room mid-conversation and starts with a demand, and a child who walks in and says "Dad." The second has not delayed the request. He has put it where it belongs. Which child are you when you pray?

### Why "Father" Is the Whole Revolution

It is easy to lose how startling this was.

The disciples were Jews. The name of God was not casually spoken, and the temple had courts marking how near a person might come. And Jesus says: begin with *Father*.

Not "Sovereign" first, though He is sovereign. Not "Judge," though He is just. Father.

And then He adds "which art in heaven" — not to distance Him, but to say that this Father is not limited by geography. He is near enough to be Father and great enough to be heard.

### What "Hallowed Be Thy Name" Does

The first petition in the prayer is not for anything you need. It is for God's name to be treated as holy.

That is a deliberate ordering. Before "give us," there is "hallow." Before the bread, there is the name.

Which means the prayer is not primarily a request list at all. It is a reorientation — a placing of God's honour ahead of my appetite, so that the appetite can then be brought honestly.

### The Two Errors in Prayer

Most prayer failures are not failures of technique. They are failures of the first word.

**The error of the distant God.** A believer prays as though he were approaching a reluctant official. He is cautious, formal, apologetic for taking up time. Every request is hedged. He has the theology of grace and the manner of a stranger.

**The error of the familiar God.** Another believer prays as though God were a service. He arrives with a list, expects delivery, and is offended by delay. He has the language of sonship and none of the reverence.

Set them side by side. One prays to a Father he treats as a judge. The other prays to a Father he treats as staff. Both have missed the same verse — which holds majesty and nearness in one sentence: *Our Father, which art in heaven, hallowed be thy name.*

### "Our" and Not "My"

The word is plural, and it matters more than a grammar note.

Prayer is personal but it is not private in the sense of being isolated. A believer who prays only as an individual misses the family dimension the address assumes: we come as children among children.

That is why confession, forgiveness, and provision in this prayer are all stated in the plural — "give *us*", "forgive *us*". You are praying as a member of a household, not as a customer.

### Relationship Does Not Cancel Request

There is a wrong way to take this teaching, and it is worth naming.

Some believers, hearing "relationship before request," stop asking. They reason that asking is immature, that a real relationship is content with presence. That sounds spiritual and it contradicts the prayer Jesus taught, which is full of requests — daily bread, forgiveness, deliverance.

The order does not remove the asking. It changes what the asking is.

Set the two kinds of asking side by side once more. One man asks as a client — and every delay feels like a failure. Another asks as a son — and the asking is part of the conversation rather than the whole of it.

### Where the Prayer Goes Next

It is worth reading the whole prayer as one movement: hallowed name, coming kingdom, done will, daily bread, forgiven debt, kept from evil.

Petitions about God's name and kingdom come first, and take up half the prayer. Petitions about my needs come second.

That proportion is the instruction. It is not that God does not want your requests. It is that He wants your requests placed inside a relationship where He is first, because that is the only place where they can be answered without ruining you.

### The Honest Boundary

A word for anyone who has prayed and felt nothing.

The reality of the relationship is not measured by the warmth of the moment. Some of the faithful prayers of Scripture were prayed in darkness, complaint, and tears. David says, "Hear my prayer, O LORD, and let my cry come unto thee" (Psalm 102:1) — and he was heard.

Feeling is a poor instrument for measuring a Father. Obedience and persistence are better ones. What else have you been using to measure His nearness?

## What This Means for You

**1. Change your first word.** Begin prayer with the address, not with the problem. Say "Father" and mean it before you say anything else.

**2. Put the hallowing first.** Before you list needs, name one thing about Who He is. It reorders the whole conversation.

**3. Bring the list anyway.** Relationship does not cancel request. Bring the bread, the debt, the protection.

**4. Pray "us" sometimes.** Pray for the household you belong to, not only for yourself.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom work together here.** Faith says come to the Father with boldness. Wisdom says that sonship includes the manner of a son — honesty without presumption, persistence without entitlement, and a willingness to accept an answer you did not want.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Matthew 6:5–15** in one sitting, and mark the order of the petitions.
- **Begin every prayer this week with the address** and one sentence of praise before any request.
- **Pray the whole prayer slowly once a day**, not as a recital but as a conversation.
- **Write one sentence about who God is** and read it before you ask for anything.
