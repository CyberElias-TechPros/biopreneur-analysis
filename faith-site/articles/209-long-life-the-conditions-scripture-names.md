There are two equal and opposite mistakes made about long life, and both of them are popular.

The first says length of days is purely sovereign — God decides, you receive, and nothing you do changes the number. The second says length of days is a technique — say the right things, take the right supplements, and death will politely wait.

**Psalm 91:16** holds both concerns together: "With long life will I satisfy him, and shew him my salvation."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Scripture promises length of days, and it attaches that promise to a way of living — not as a wage to earn, but as a path that tends toward life.**

## The Promise Itself

### "Satisfy," Not Merely Extend

Notice the verb. God does not say He will *extend* him. He says He will *satisfy* him with long life.

The Hebrew is *sābaʿ* — to be full, to have enough, to be satiated. It is a word used of a meal that leaves nothing wanting.

That reframes the whole subject. **The promise is not primarily about quantity; it is about a life that arrives at its own fullness.** A long life that was never satisfied would not fulfil this verse. What would it profit a man to reach ninety and never once be full?

### The Context of Psalm 91

Verse 16 does not stand alone. It is the last line of a psalm that has been describing a dwelling place: "He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty" (Psalm 91:1).

And the promise is spoken to a particular person: "Because he hath set his love upon me, therefore will I deliver him" (Psalm 91:14).

So the length of days belongs to someone who has *set their love* on God and stayed in the shelter. **The promise is relational before it is numerical.** So whose company have you been keeping?

### The First Commandment With Promise

Paul is explicit about one of the conditions: "Honour thy father and mother; (which is the first commandment with promise;) That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth" (Ephesians 6:2–3).

He is quoting the fifth commandment, and he calls attention to its structure: this one comes with an attached outcome.

Honour is not obedience to a parent's every whim; it is the posture of weight — treating someone as significant. **And Scripture says that posture tends to lengthen a life.**

### The Proverbs of Length

Proverbs returns to this again and again, and always with a condition attached.

"My son, forget not my law; but let thine heart keep my commandments: for length of days, and long life, and peace, shall they add to thee" (Proverbs 3:1–2).

"The fear of the LORD prolongeth days: but the years of the wicked shall be shortened" (Proverbs 10:27).

"By humility and the fear of the LORD are riches, and honour, and life" (Proverbs 22:4).

**The pattern is unmistakable: wisdom and reverence are described as life-lengthening, and folly as life-shortening.**

### What the Conditions Actually Are

#### Keeping, Not Merely Knowing

Every one of those Proverbs uses a verb of *keeping* — forget not, let thine heart keep, hold fast. The promise is not attached to information.

James says the same thing about the Word: "But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves" (James 1:22).

**Faith and Wisdom** are not two separate tracks. Wisdom is faith that has been worked into the ordinary decisions of a life.

#### Reverence, Not Fearfulness

The fear of the Lord is the most repeated condition in the length-of-days passages, and it is chronically misunderstood.

It is not terror of God's mood. It is the settled recognition that He is God and you are not — which produces humility, and humility produces a life that does not burn itself out.

**The proud man shortens his own days.** He works as though he were indestructible, speaks as though consequences were for other people, and refuses correction until his body insists.

#### Rest as Obedience

There is a condition people rarely connect to longevity, and it is in the fourth commandment: the Sabbath was given so that "thy manservant and thy maidservant may rest as well as thou" (Deuteronomy 5:14).

Rest is not in the list of suggestions. It is in the list of commands, and a life that never rests is a life being spent faster than it is being replenished.

#### The Honest Limit

Two limits, and they must be said clearly.

First, this is a proverb, not an actuarial table. Proverbs describes how life generally works under God, not a mechanical guarantee for every individual case. Job lived righteously and suffered terribly; the book exists precisely to correct the arithmetic that says every suffering is a symptom of sin.

Second, longevity is not the highest good. Paul could say, "For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain" (Philippians 1:21), and he was not being morbid. **The Blessed Hope** makes death an enemy rather than a terror, and a person who has settled that can hold long life gratefully without clutching it.

#### The Prayer That Gets the Balance Right

Psalm 90 gives the prayer that holds all of this together.

"Teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom" (Psalm 90:12). Then, astonishingly, the psalm ends by asking God to establish the work of their hands — not to extend the count, but to make the count worthwhile.

**The prayer is not "make me live longer." It is "make my days count, and let the work stand."**

## What This Means for You

**1. Receive the promise.** Long life is in the covenant, and you are allowed to ask for it in faith.

**2. Walk the path.** Honour, keep, reverence, rest. These are not the price of the promise; they are the road it runs along.

**3. Guard your pace.** Most people who shorten their days do it slowly, through overwork, resentment, and sleeplessness rather than through one dramatic decision.

**4. Hold it with an open hand.** Ask for length of days without making length of days your god. Christ is better than the years.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Pray for long life and live like someone who expects it — sleep, movement, food, and the care God has provided through ordinary means.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Psalm 91 in full**, then read verse 16 again.
- **Write out Proverbs 3:1–8** and mark every verb.
- **Read Ephesians 6:1–3** and consider what honour looks like in your family now.
- **Read Psalm 90** and pray verse 12 each morning.
- **Take one long rest** and call it obedience, not indulgence.
