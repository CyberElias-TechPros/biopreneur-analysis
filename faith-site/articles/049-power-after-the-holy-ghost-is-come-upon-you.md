Have you ever wondered what spiritual power is actually for — and why so many believers seem to have so little of it? The answer lies in what Jesus attached to the promise.

Acts 1:8 reads, "But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be **witnesses unto me**." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the power of the Holy Spirit is given for witness.**

## Power With a Purpose Attached

Jesus does not promise power as a status symbol or a spiritual trophy. He attaches it to a mission: witness. The Greek word for power is *dunamis* — dynamic ability, miracle-working force. It is real power, and it is directional.

**The Power of the Holy Spirit** — the Spirit's supernatural enablement for godly living, witness, and service — is the promise. **The Authority of the Believer** — the God-given right and ability to stand and act in Christ's name — is what accompanies it. **The Supernatural Life** — life lived in conscious dependence on the Spirit's power — is the result.

Notice the geographical expansion: Jerusalem, Judaea, Samaria, uttermost parts. Why would the Spirit's power need to cross a road, a border, and an ocean if it were only for private enjoyment? The power is meant to move outward, from the familiar to the foreign, from the comfortable to the costly. Pentecost was not a private experience; it was a commissioning.

Think of a power station and a grid. The station does not generate electricity for its own satisfaction; the current flows along lines to homes and businesses. If the lines are cut, the power has nowhere to go. The Spirit's power in you is meant to flow along the lines of relationship, speech, and service to people who need Jesus.

### Boldness, Not Spectacle

Read Acts and you find the earliest evidence of this power was boldness. In Acts 4:31, after prayer, "they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and they spake the word of God with boldness." In Acts 4:33, "with great power gave the apostles witness of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus."

Boldness is unspectacular but undeniable. It is the courage to speak when silence is safer, to stand when compromise is easier, and to keep speaking after opposition. Miracles accompanied the apostolic witness — and still can — but the primary evidence of power was a church that would not stop talking about Jesus.

Here the two diverge. Power pursued for display produces spiritual theatre; power received for witness produces transformed courage. One draws attention to the vessel; the other draws attention to Christ.

### Power Is for a Purpose You Did Not Choose

The disciples asked a question about timing and received an answer about assignment: "Lord, wilt thou at this time restore again the kingdom to Israel? And he said unto them, It is not for you to know the times or the seasons... But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me" (Acts 1:6–8).

They wanted a crown; He gave them a commission. And the power was tied to the commission, not to their curiosity.

Acts 4 shows what that looks like once it is working: "And with great power gave the apostles witness of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus: and great grace was upon them all" (Acts 4:33). Power, witness, grace — the three travel together. Where power is severed from witness it curdles into performance.

Luke's Gospel gives the same shape at the end: "that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations... And ye are witnesses of these things" (Luke 24:47–48). The Spirit's power is not a personal upgrade. It is an equipment for a message.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **Luke 24:48–49** — "And ye are witnesses of these things. And, behold, I send the promise of my Father upon you: but tarry ye in the city of Jerusalem, until ye be endued with power from on high." Jesus links witness and power directly, and tells them to wait for it.
- **2 Timothy 1:7** — "For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind." Power, love, and a sound mind together: power without love is harsh, and power without soundness is reckless.
- **Acts 2:4** — "And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance." The first result of filling was speech, which in context became witness to a crowd.

Three witnesses, one purpose: witness promised, power with love and soundness, and speech given.

## What This Means for You

**1. Ask for power with witness in mind.** Pray for the Spirit's power, and include the purpose in the request: courage to speak of Jesus where you are.

**2. Prepare your testimony.** Boldness works best with readiness. Know how to say in a few sentences what Jesus has done for you. Keep it honest and specific.

**3. Serve where your power meets a need.** Power expresses itself through practical service and spiritual gifts that meet real needs. Look for needs around you and expect the Spirit to supply.

**4. Faith and wisdom speak and live.** Faith expects power; wisdom chooses words, timing, and conduct that make the witness credible. Declaring power never excuses fanaticism; it produces steady, credible boldness.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Acts 1:8 and the surrounding verses.** Mark the promise, the purpose, and the places. Then write one sentence summarising your own assignment.
- **Write your testimony in five sentences.** Practise saying it naturally, and ask the Spirit for an opportunity to use it this week.
- **Pray for boldness daily.** Not for a spectacle, but for courage — and then act when the opportunity comes.
- **Squeeze one habit that contradicts your witness.** Identify a behaviour that undermines your credibility with those who know you, and take a concrete step to change it.
