Many believers have prayed for healing and then wondered whether anything happened, because nothing happened in them — no warmth, no release, no wave of certainty, just the same room and the same body.

**2 Corinthians 5:7** speaks directly into that silence: "For we walk by faith, not by sight."

Notice what the verse does not say. It does not say we walk by feeling on the good days and by faith when things get hard. It draws one line and keeps it: we walk by faith.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **faith is not a sensation you generate in your body; it is an agreement you keep with God's word, and it works as well on the days your body reports nothing back.**

That sentence has to be said plainly because a whole culture of Christian expectation has trained people to grade their prayers by the tingle factor.

## Feelings Are Real, and They Are Not Officers

### What Feelings Are For

Feelings are not the enemy of your faith. They are a legitimate part of being human, and God made you able to feel joy in worship, grief at a funeral, and fear at a threat.

The problem is the promotion of feeling to a position it cannot hold. **Feelings are excellent messengers and appalling judges.**

How feelings behave: they arrive late, they lag behind facts, they are shaped by sleep and blood sugar and weather and memory. None of that is moral. But none of it is capable of deciding what is true.

### Why "I Don't Feel Healed" Is Not Evidence

Consider a simple test. You buy a plane ticket, and the day before the flight you feel doubtful about your seat assignment. Did the doubt change your seat? No — the booking does.

Or think of the woman in the crowd, who had no idea what had happened until Jesus stopped her to say so. She *felt* the touch she gave His garment, but she had no idea that healing had already occurred.

**The evidence for your healing was never going to be a feeling. It was always going to be a verse.**

### The Psalmist Who Argued With His Own Mood

Look at how David handled this in Psalm 42, because it is one of the most honest passages in the Bible.

"Hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him" (Psalm 42:11) — a man ordering his own soul around, refusing to let the low mood have the last word.

Notice the shape of the reasoning: "My tears have been my meat day and night... All thy waves and thy billows are gone over me" and then, "Yet the LORD will command his lovingkindness in the daytime" (Psalm 42:3, 7–8).

He had feelings. He did not give them rank. And if a man after God's own heart had to talk to his own soul like that, why would you expect yours to behave any better on its own?

### How Faith Works Without Feeling It

#### Agreement, Not Sensation

Faith at its core has nothing to do with how you feel. It has to do with what you are agreeing with.

Abram was told to look at stars and count them. He was old and had no children. Scripture records one sentence: "And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6). No statement about how he felt. Even the wives' laughter elsewhere in Genesis 18 shows how the feeling often disagrees with the promise. Faith is a decision about whose report stands, and it functions whether your body is excited or your stomach is in knots.

#### The Word in Your Mouth

When nothing is happening in you, the thing to do is not wait for it to start. Do what the Word says.

"This is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith" (1 John 5:4). Notice whose word is spoken in 1 John 4:4 — "If we receive the witness of men, the witness of God is greater."

**Confession of the Word** — *homologia*, saying the same thing — is not a magic formula; it is you, unmoved by your feelings, taking the side of what God said.

#### The Part the Holy Spirit Plays in Silence

Here is the quiet half of the truth, and it matters for the person who has been disappointed.

The Spirit does more in your body than you can feel. "The Spirit of truth... will guide you into all truth" (John 16:13). And in the body, Romans 8:11 says the Spirit quickens your mortal body, and it does not say you will feel it.

Think of healing in a surgical room. A patient is anaesthetised. Feeling is entirely absent. Yet the work is real and completed. Your awareness was never the measure of the thing being done.

That illustration is not a promise that all healing is silent and invisible. Some bodies recover with a surge of life; some are repaired slowly and quietly. But it does mean anxiety is not evidence of judgment, and relief is not the proof that the transaction happened.

#### The Honest Boundary

Scripture does not promise "I will feel the quickening the day I pray." It promises that wholeness is God's will for His children and that the Spirit is at work. The absence of a feeling is not a denial.

But do not think feelings never matter at all. Feeling in church is no substitute for confessing an unaddressed sin or a cancelled appointment. Faith moves hands and feet.

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop using your body as a referee.** If your feelings could rule correctly, God would never have told you to walk by faith.

**2. Keep your habit fixed when the appointment is disappointing.** A fixed time in God's Word is not a mood; it is a choice.

**3. Speak truth before, not because of, feeling.** Saying it when you feel nothing is not hypocrisy — it is faith, refusing to let the feeling write the sentence.

**4. Guard your body from what feeds low mood.** Sleep, food, exercise, and daylight are not unspiritual. Faith and wisdom work together — the Spirit-filled life is not a life that ignores how God made the body.

**5. Let the Word be your evidence.** When "I do not feel healed" rises, answer with "Here is what God has said," and be specific.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 2 Corinthians 5:1–10** and note how often Paul grounds confidence in what is unseen.
- **Write down three things you believe that you do not feel** — and read them aloud.
- **Read Psalm 42** in one sitting and follow the mood swing.
- **Say 1 Peter 2:24 aloud** every morning before you assess your body.
- **Read Hebrews 10:19–25** and notice the confidence language.
