Have you ever noticed how easily your words drift from what you say you believe? You declare God's promise on Sunday and rehearse the problem all week, and then wonder why your faith feels thin.

Mark 11:23 links believing and speaking: "whosoever shall **say** unto this mountain... and shall not doubt in his heart, but shall believe that those things which he **saith** shall come to pass; he shall have whatsoever he **saith**." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **faith speaks what God has said, and the words you keep speaking shape what you keep receiving.**

## Say, Believe, Have

Jesus gives a sequence: you say, you believe, you have. The saying and the believing must agree, and Jesus names the failure mode — a heart that doubts while the mouth declares. Faith is not a formula for manipulation; it is integrity between heart and mouth.

**Confession of the Word** — speaking in agreement with God's declarations (*homologia*, saying the same thing) — is the discipline Jesus describes. **The Life of Faith** — living by God's word rather than appearances — expresses itself through speech. **The Word of God** — the standard for every confession — keeps the practice anchored in truth rather than wishful thinking.

Think of a farmer sowing seed. He does not sow rocks and expect wheat. Every word you speak is a seed that grows something, and the harvest always matches the seed.

### Words That Build and Words That Break

Proverbs 18:21 says, "Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof." James devotes an entire chapter to the tongue's disproportionate power, comparing it to a rudder that steers a ship and a spark that ignites a forest.

Jesus' own example is instructive. He stood before a fig tree and spoke to it, and His disciples marvelled that it withered. He did not negotiate with the tree, complain about the tree, or describe the tree to His Father at length. He addressed it with a word and moved on. That pattern does not make every inconvenience a demon or every illness a failure of speech; it simply shows that words are meant to be used with authority rather than merely with emotion. The believer who talks about the mountain all day is feeding it; the believer who speaks to it briefly and then speaks of God's faithfulness is starving it.

This is not a call to denial. Faith does not pretend the mountain is not there; it speaks to the mountain. Jesus said "say unto this mountain," not "pretend there is no mountain." Faith names the obstacle honestly and then addresses it with God's authority.

Have you ever counted how many times you described a problem before you prayed about it? Watch what separates the two: The fearful believer describes the problem in detail and calls it realism. The believing believer states the problem briefly and then speaks God's word over it.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **Romans 10:10** — "For with the heart man believeth unto righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation." Belief and confession cooperate in salvation itself — the pattern continues into daily faith.
- **2 Corinthians 4:13** — "We having the same spirit of faith, according as it is written, I believed, and therefore have I spoken; we also believe, and therefore speak." Paul names the mechanism: belief produces speech.
- **James 3:5** — "Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth!" Small words carry large consequences in either direction.

Three witnesses, one pattern: belief confessed, faith spoken, and a small fire that spreads.

## What This Means for You

**1. Listen to yourself for a day.** Notice how often your speech agrees with fear rather than with God. Awareness is the first step to change.

**2. Replace complaints with confessions.** When you catch yourself rehearsing a problem, stop and say what God's word says about it. Do not deny the problem; address it.

**3. Keep the confession specific and scriptural.** Ground your words in a verse. Faith speaks God's promises, not vague optimism.

**4. Faith and wisdom speak and act.** Faith declares God's word; wisdom still takes responsible action — medical care, work, reconciliation. Declaring truth never excuses passivity; it directs action.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Mark 11:22–24 and James 3:1–12 together.** Note the connection between Jesus' teaching and James' warnings, then write one sentence about your mouth.
- **Record your words for one hour.** Write down the statements you make about your future, your health, your finances, and your relationships, then test them against Scripture.
- **Choose one confession for the week.** Write a single verse-based declaration for your current situation and say it each morning.
- **Speak to one mountain.** Name one obstacle honestly, then speak God's word over it daily rather than describing it to everyone you meet.
