Have you ever feared that the sin you keep battling proves your new life is not real — or worse, that it might eventually defeat you? Many sincere believers live with quiet dread that their old nature will win in the end.

First John 3:9 speaks with unusual boldness: "Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin; for **his seed remaineth in him**: and he **cannot sin**, because he is born of God." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the nature God birthed in you cannot sin, because His seed remains in you.**

## Two Natures, One Decisive Birth

John is not claiming that believers never stumble. He already wrote in 1 John 1:8 that anyone claiming to be without sin deceives himself. What John describes is the nature of the new life itself: the seed of God planted in you cannot produce sin, because it is God's own incorruptible life.

**The Divine Nature** — God's own nature imparted to your reborn spirit — is the explanation. **The New Creation** — the brand-new spiritual person God made in Christ — is that nature in person. **The New Birth** — the spiritual regeneration wrought by the Spirit when you believed — is when it began. The old nature was not reformed; it was crucified with Christ. The new nature was not improved; it was created.

Think of a healthy tree grafted into an old stump. The stump may still sprout old shoots from below, but the grafted branch carries new life with its own fruit. When shoots appear from below, the gardener does not conclude the graft failed; he prunes what does not belong to the new life. John says your graft is divine, and its fruit is inevitable.

### Cannot Sin — What It Does and Does Not Mean

John's phrase is precise: the one born of God *cannot sin* because the seed remains. The incapacity belongs to the new nature, not to your unrenewed thinking. Your spirit, born of God, has no capacity for sin; your mind and body, still being renewed, can be drawn into it — which is why Paul describes the inward conflict in Romans 7 and calls for renewed thinking in Romans 12:2.

So how do you live? You stop identifying with the failures and start agreeing with the nature. When you sin, your Advocate intercedes (1 John 2:1), you confess and are cleansed (1 John 1:9), and you return to who you are. You do not conclude, "I am a sinner who sometimes does right." You conclude, "I am a child of God who slipped, and my Father restores me."

Note where the two part company: The old-identity believer treats sin as evidence of what he is; the new-identity believer treats sin as an intrusion into what he is. One descends into identity crisis; the other returns to identity clarity.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **1 Peter 1:23** — "Being born again, not of corruptible seed, but of incorruptible, by the word of God, which liveth and abideth for ever." Peter names the seed incorruptible and permanent. Whatever is born of an incorruptible seed cannot decay into its opposite.
- **Galatians 5:17** — "For the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh: and these are contrary the one to the other." Paul explains the conflict without denying either reality: two natures oppose each other, and you choose whom to feed. Conflict is not proof of failure; it is proof of life.
- **2 Corinthians 5:17** — "Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new." Paul states the verdict in the past tense. The old has passed; the new has come. Live from the verdict.

Three witnesses, one nature: incorruptible seed, opposing flesh, and a new creature — your new life cannot fail.

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop using failure to define yourself.** Sin is an intrusion into your new identity, not a revelation of it. Confess quickly, receive cleansing, and return to who you are in Christ.

**2. Feed the nature, not the flesh.** What you give attention to grows. Scripture, prayer, worship, and fellowship strengthen the new life's expression; unchecked appetite, gossip, and compromise strengthen the flesh's pull.

**3. Expect conflict and interpret it correctly.** The presence of struggle does not mean your new birth failed. It means two natures are contesting your choices — and in Christ the stronger nature is yours to yield to.

**4. Faith and wisdom guard the new life practically.** Faith declares your nature cannot sin; wisdom builds boundaries, accountability, and habits that give the flesh no footholds. Declaring grace never excuses sin; it removes sin's claim to define you.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 1 John 3:6–10 and 1 John 1:8–9 together.** Notice how John holds both truths — no sin in the new nature, and cleansing for the believer who stumbles — without contradiction.
- **Answer one condemning thought with your nature.** When you hear, "You will never change," answer with 1 John 3:9: His seed remains in me, and I am born of God. Then take the next faithful step.
- **Cut one foothold this week.** Identify a specific habit, input, or relationship that feeds the flesh, and take one concrete action to remove or limit it.
- **Feed the new nature daily.** Choose a daily practice this week — Scripture reading, prayer, or worship — and keep it consistently, not to earn anything but to strengthen the life that is already yours.
