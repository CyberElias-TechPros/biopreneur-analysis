Hebrews has spent nine and a half chapters explaining that the old system could not do it, and then it says something that sounds almost reckless.

"Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus" (Hebrews 10:19).

*Boldness* — for everyone in the room. Not for the high priest. Not for the exceptional.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the word boldness in Hebrews 10:19 is not a personality trait and not a mood; it is a right of access purchased for the family — which makes timidity in prayer a confusion about your surname, not your temperament.**

## The Word

### Parresia

The Greek is *parrēsia*, and it is a beautiful word: literally *all speech*, the freedom to say everything.

In classical usage it was the right of a citizen to speak freely in the assembly. It is political vocabulary — the franchise of a free person. So who told you the franchise was for others?

**Boldness is not loudness. It is the freedom of someone with standing to speak.**

### Not Presumption

And it is careful to say *by the blood of Jesus*. The access is not a mood of familiarity; it is a purchase.

The boldness is bold because of what it cost, not because of who you are on your own.

**The door was opened at an expense that forbids casualness.**

### The Veil

And the next verse explains the picture: "Through the veil, that is to say, his flesh" (Hebrews 10:20).

At the temple, a curtain separated the holy place from the holiest, and only one man entered, once a year. At the crucifixion, that curtain tore from top to bottom (Matthew 27:51).

**The tearing started from the top, because nobody on the floor opened it.**

## The Birthright

### Access by Family, Not by Rank

The word *brethren* opens the sentence, and everything else hangs on it.

A servant enters a room when summoned. A son enters because he lives there.

**You are not a visitor who has been granted an appointment. You are family who has been given a key.**

### The Spirit's Own Word

And Romans makes the intimacy explicit: "ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father" (Romans 8:15).

*Abba* is the household word — closer to *Dad* than to *Sire*. The Spirit does not teach you to approach formally.

**The One who leads you into the room uses a family word, and He put it in your mouth.**

### What Was Purchased

And Ephesians 2 says the access was specifically built by the cross: "through him we both have access by one Spirit unto the Father" (Ephesians 2:18).

Access is not a sentiment available to anyone; it is a passage built at a cost.

The road into the holiest was constructed out of something expensive.

### The Timidity

### Why Believers Pray Small

Most believers pray below their access — hesitant, apologetic, brief, and vague.

That is not humility. Humility is accurate self-assessment, and an accurate assessment of a child of God includes the door.

A person who has been given the palace and asks only for the doormat has not understood the gift. Which room have you been standing outside of?

### Where the Hesitation Comes From

It comes from a standing that has not been believed. If you think you are tolerated, you ask carefully.

Timidity in prayer is almost always a verdict problem, not a courage problem.

### What It Costs

And the cost is not only to you. A timid church asks for little, receives little, and then teaches others that God is small.

The way you approach the throne teaches everyone watching what you think of the Throne.

### Coming Boldly

### Confidently, Not Carelessly

Hebrews 4:16 tells us how: "Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace." Not to a throne of sentiment, but of grace — the character of the throne is the reason for the courage.

You are not talking your way in. You are being welcomed in.

### Specifically

And access without specificity is a wasted invitation. Ask for what you actually need (Philippians 4:6).

Vague prayers suggest a person who is not sure he is being listened to.

### Persistently

And keep asking. Luke's parable commends the man who keeps knocking (Luke 11:5–8), not because God is reluctant, but because persistence is what a person does who believes the door is open.

### With Others

And Hebrews 10:24–25 goes straight from boldness to the assembly — because the access is individual in its application and corporate in its design.

**A family boldness is meant to be practised in a family room.**

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, boldness is not a claim on outcomes. "If we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us" (1 John 5:14). The confidence is about being heard, not about controlling the answer.

Second, this is not a rejection of reverence. The same book calls God a consuming fire (Hebrews 12:29). Boldness before a holy God is possible only because of the blood; it is never casual about the blood.

**The **The Blood of Jesus** is the entry; **Sonship** is the standing; the **The Presence of God** is the room you are standing in.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Read Hebrews 10:19–25 and mark every reason given for the boldness.**

**2. Ask for something specific today.**

**3. Stop apologising for the interruption.** You are not interrupting.

**4. Pray out loud**, because vagueness thrives in silence.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Access is a gift; use is a discipline. **Sonship** is the position, and wisdom is the practice of showing up in the room that was opened.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Hebrews 10:19–25 every morning.**
- **Memorise verse 19.**
- **Write down what you have been afraid to ask.**
- **Read Romans 8:14–17** and mark the household words.
- **Pray one specific request** and note the date.
