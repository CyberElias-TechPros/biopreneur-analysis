Hebrews was written to people who had started well and were in danger of drifting. So the writer gives three "let us" instructions in a row: draw near, hold fast, consider one another.

"Let us hold fast the profession of our faith without wavering; (for he is faithful that promised;)" (Hebrews 10:23). Here is the central truth of this teaching: **confession is a thing you hold rather than a thing you say once — and it can be held steadily because the faithfulness behind it is not yours.**

### Why a Confession Needs Holding

You do not "hold fast" something that is weightless. The word picture is of a hand gripping under strain — the way a man holds a rope in a storm.

**The Life of Faith** — a life ordered by God's declarations — is maintained, not merely begun. **Confession of the Word** — speaking in agreement with God — is the rope in your hand. And **The Name of Jesus** — His person, work, and authority — is the anchor at the other end.

The pressure is real. Circumstances speak, bodies weaken, and friends grow tired of hearing the same hope expressed. Drifting is not usually a decision; it is a failure to keep holding.

The word translated "profession" is the same *homologia* Paul uses — saying the same thing. Hebrews is not asking for a slogan; it is asking for sustained agreement with the truth you already affirmed. In the first century that agreement could cost a man his property, which is why the writer has just reminded them of the plunder they had already endured joyfully (Hebrews 10:34).

## The Parenthesis That Changes Everything

Most of the weight of this verse sits in the brackets: "for he is faithful that promised." The writer does not say hold fast because your grip is strong. He says hold fast because His character is reliable.

That distinction is the difference between white-knuckled religion and restful faith. You are not generating the certainty; you are leaning on a Person who has never broken a promise.

Think of a climber clipped to a bolt that has held for decades. He still holds the rope — but his confidence is in the anchor, not in his hands.

There is also a corporate dimension to holding. Israel's confessions were rehearsed in assembly — the same words, out of many mouths, at the same feasts, year after year. Truth repeated by a body becomes harder to abandon than truth held privately, because abandoning it now costs more than reputation; it costs relationship.

### What Wavering Actually Looks Like

Wavering is rarely dramatic denial. It is the slow edit. A man stops saying "God will provide" and starts saying "we'll see what happens." Nothing sounds heretical; the confession has simply gone soft at the edges.

James describes the alternative and warns about the double-minded man who is "unstable in all his ways" (James 1:8). Instability is not a personality trait here; it is the consequence of a divided confession.

Have you ever caught yourself saying something about your situation that you would not want to say in front of Jesus? What did that sentence reveal?

### The Context of Drawing Near

The verses around our text matter. Verse 19 says we have "boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus." Verse 22 says draw near with a true heart. Then verse 23 says hold fast. Then verse 24 says consider one another.

Trace the difference to its root: A confession held alone eventually weakens; a confession held in a community of believers is sustained by shared memory and shared prayer.

One more pastoral note. Holding fast does not mean refusing to process pain. The same chapter that says hold fast says draw near and consider one another. Tears and confession are not competitors; the psalmists hold both in the same verse — telling God their trouble while insisting on His word.

### The Confession Has Content

"Let us hold fast the profession of our faith" — the word is *homologia*, and it has substance. Hebrews has just spent nine chapters specifying what that confession contains: a Son who is better than angels, a priest after a different order, a sacrifice offered once, a sanctuary not made with hands.

So holding fast is not holding fast to a mood. It is holding fast to a set of settled facts about a Person, and refusing to let the pressure of circumstances redefine them. That is a far more stable thing to grip than an experience.

## What This Means for You

- **Write the confession down.** Precision protects against drift.
- **Say it when nothing is happening.** The unremarkable day is the training ground.
- **Bring it to the assembly.** "Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together" (Hebrews 10:25) is the next verse, and it is not accidental.
- **Refuse to relearn unbelief from your circumstances.** Circumstances are not teachers of doctrine.
- **Faith and wisdom hold the right confession.** Not every positive sentence is faith; wisdom ensures your confession matches the promise God actually made.

### Community Is Part of the Mechanism

Hebrews does not treat holding fast as a solo discipline. The very next verse supplies the means: "And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works" (Hebrews 10:24). The word "provoke" is almost aggressive — a stirring up, an irritation toward action.

Then comes the practice: "Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching" (Hebrews 10:25). So much the more — the nearer the pressure, the more necessary the gathering.

That is a mechanism, not a sentiment. A confession held in isolation has one witness and no correction. A confession held among believers is repeated back to you on the week you cannot say it yourself.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Write your confession in one paragraph** and read it aloud daily.
- **Note one place you have doubted.** Address it with a specific scripture.
- **Share the confession with a believer** and ask them to hold it with you.
- **Read Hebrews 10:19–25** and count the "let us" instructions.
