Luke's version of the two builders contains a phrase Matthew's does not, and it is the phrase that makes the parable practical.

"He is like a man which built an house, and digged deep, and laid the foundation on a rock" (Luke 6:48).

*Digged deep.* Luke names the labour. Matthew tells you what the difference was; Luke tells you what it cost.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **depth is the one part of a building nobody sees and the only part that decides what survives — and in the life of faith, depth is dug by obedience to what has already been heard.**

## The Three Verbs

Look at the sequence in the verse, because the order is the teaching.

**Built.** He had a plan and began. Nothing wrong with that.

**Digged deep.** Before he went higher, he went lower. This is the step everybody skips, because it produces nothing visible and consumes the most time.

**Laid the foundation on a rock.** He did not build on the surface and hope. He found the rock and started there.

**The Word of God** — what He has said — is the rock. **The Life of Faith** — acting on it — is the digging. **Faith and Wisdom** — believing and building deliberately — is what keeps a person from pouring concrete on sand.

Consider a man who is proud of his new extension until a surveyor tells him the footings are sitting on made-up ground. The building is fine. The ground is not, and the ground decides the future.

### Why Depth Is Skipped

Depth is skipped for an obvious reason: it is invisible, slow, and unrewarded.

Nobody compliments excavation. There is no photograph of footings. The neighbour who started building in March has walls by June, and the man still digging looks like he is behind.

That is the pressure of the Christian life in a single image. The visible things — the platform, the vocabulary, the appearance of maturity — can be assembled quickly. The invisible things — the obedience done in private, the prayers nobody hears, the years of not quitting — cannot.

Set the two builders side by side once more, and notice that they are not distinguished by intelligence or sincerity. Both heard. Both built. One went lower before he went higher.

### The Flood and the Stream

Luke's description of the weather is worth reading closely: "when the flood arose, the stream beat vehemently upon that house."

*Flood* and *stream* together — the river rising out of its bed, the water arriving with force rather than seeping.

Note what the house did: "could not shake it." Not "was undamaged," but could not be shaken. There is a stability that the water could not argue with, and Luke attributes it to one thing: "for it was founded upon a rock."

### The Rock Is a Person, Not a Principle

It is possible to read this parable and conclude that the rock is a technique — read more, pray earlier, journal consistently. All good things, and none of them the rock.

Paul makes the identification explicit: "And did all drink the same spiritual drink: for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ" (1 Corinthians 10:4).

And Peter, on the same stone: "Behold, I lay in Sion a chief corner stone, elect, precious: and he that believeth on him shall not be confounded" (1 Peter 2:6).

So the foundation is a Person, and the digging is the process by which a life is actually transferred onto Him rather than merely onto ideas about Him.

### What Digging Looks Like

Since the metaphor must become Monday, here is what digging actually is.

It is the obedience that costs something when nobody is watching. It is the honesty about a habit you have been managing rather than killing. It is the reconciliation you keep postponing. It is the prayer that continues when there is no answer and no audience.

Each of those is a spadeful. None of them photographs well.

### The Other Man Was Not Lazy

Luke says the foolish builder "without a foundation... built an house" (Luke 6:49). He built. He probably worked hard.

His failure was not effort. It was direction — he put his labour into what could be seen, and none of it into what could be stood on.

That is a very uncomfortable sentence for anyone who measures his spiritual life by activity. How much of your labour this month went below the surface?

### The Collapse Was Immediate

"And as soon as it was touched, it fell; and the ruin of that house was great" (Luke 6:49).

*As soon as.* Not gradually. The house had no reserve, because a house without a foundation has no way to absorb a blow.

Which means the storm is not the tragedy in this parable. The missing foundation is, and it was missing long before anybody noticed.

## What This Means for You

**1. Choose one dig.** Not a renovation of your whole life. One act of obedience that goes lower rather than higher.

**2. Measure your week by depth, not by visibility.** What did you do this week that nobody saw?

**3. Stop comparing progress with the man who started later.** He has walls. You have footings. Only one of you will still be standing.

**4. Build on the Person, not the technique.** Ask whether your confidence is in Christ or in your system.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom work together here.** Faith says dig, and dig deep, because the storm will come. Wisdom says digging is planned rather than frantic — a realistic pace, a season for rest, help where the ground is rocky, and no shame in a foundation that takes longer than a neighbour's wall.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Luke 6:46–49** and Matthew 7:21–29 together, and list what each adds.
- **Name one dig** you will do this week — unseen and costly.
- **Read Colossians 2:6–7**, and note the three participles of a rooted life.
- **Thank God for one foundation** that has already held you.
