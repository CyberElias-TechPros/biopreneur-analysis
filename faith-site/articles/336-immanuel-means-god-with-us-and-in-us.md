Matthew 1:23 is a quotation of Isaiah 7:14, and the way Matthew uses it is one of the boldest interpretive moves in the New Testament.

"Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Immanuel was spoken to a frightened king as a sign of judgment and rescue, and Matthew applies it to a baby in a stable — but the promise does not stop at proximity, because the One who came to be with us then chose to be in us.**

## The Original Sign

### Ahaz Was Afraid

And the context is a terrified king facing a coalition (Isaiah 7:1–2). His heart shook like trees in the wind.

The sign was given to a man who did not want it and would not ask for it.

**God offered a sign to a man who had already decided to trust Assyria instead.** And the sign came anyway.

### A Sign of Both

And the sign was double-edged. Before the child knew to refuse evil, the two kings would be gone (Isaiah 7:16) — rescue.

And Assyria would come anyway (Isaiah 7:17) — judgment.

**Immanuel was announced in a crisis, not a carol.**

### The Name

And *ʿImmānû Ēl* is a sentence compressed into a name: with us is God. Not "God is near" or "God is willing."

God, with us.

**The name is a claim about geography.** So what would change if you believed the map?

## Matthew's Use

### A Name for a Person

And Matthew does something startling. He takes a sign-name and attaches it to a baby, and then the angel gives the baby a different name — Jesus, because He will save His people (Matthew 1:21).

Both names belong to Him. Emmanuel describes who He is; Jesus describes what He does.

**The presence and the rescue are the same person.**

### Fulfilled in a Body

And the fulfilment is not merely that a prophecy matched an event. It is that the abstract claim "God is with us" became a person you could bump into.

**"With us" now had a mother and a hometown.**

### And It Did Not End

And the last words of the same Gospel are the same promise: "lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world" (Matthew 28:20).

Matthew bookends his Gospel with presence.

**The Gospel opens with God arriving and closes with Him refusing to leave.**

### From With to In

### The Promise at the Table

And Jesus made the transition explicit: "If a man love me, he will keep my words: and my Father will love him, and we will come unto him, and make our abode with him" (John 14:23).

*Monēn* — a dwelling place. The same word family as John 1:14's tent.

The tent becomes a permanent address.

### Christ in You

And Paul names the mystery: "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (Colossians 1:27).

Not alongside. In.

The Incarnation was God entering a body. Redemption is God entering yours.

### The Power of the Indwelling

And Paul treats the indwelling as the engine of everything: "that he would grant you... to be strengthened with might by his Spirit in the inner man; that Christ may dwell in your hearts by faith" (Ephesians 3:16–17).

The *katoikēsai* there is the same word family again — to settle down as at home. Not a visit; a residence.

The presence is not the decoration of a life. It is the load-bearing wall.

### The Two Mistakes

And the two errors are equally common. One treats God as distant — a doctrine to be believed at a distance. The other treats Him as merely alongside, a helpful companion outside the door.

Scripture says He is within, and that is a more alarming thing.

An indwelling God is nearer than your own thoughts. Have you been praying to somebody further away than that?

### Living as an Address

### Speak From Inside

And the first practice is a change of reference point. When you pray, you are not shouting across a universe; you are talking to somebody in the room of your own spirit.

The intimacy is not presumption. It is accuracy.

You are not calling long-distance.

### Practise Presence

And the second is the ordinary discipline of acknowledging Him during the day. Brother Lawrence's kitchen practice is simply the theology of Colossians 1:27 applied to a Tuesday.

A sentence, a glance, a thank-you, at intervals.

Presence is a habit of attention before it is a feeling.

### Let the House Be His

And the third is a searching question. If He is in residence, the house is not yours to decorate any way you like.

That is the uncomfortable edge of the doctrine, and it is the honest one.

An occupied house is a different kind of house.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not pantheism. God dwelling in a believer does not make the believer divine; the creature remains a creature.

Second, this is not a private mysticism that ignores the church. The same presence is promised to a gathered body (Matthew 18:20).

**The **The Presence of God** is the thing promised; **The New Creation is the house it entered; and Communion With the Holy Spirit is the conversation of someone who knows he is not alone.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Matthew 1:18–25 twice.

**2. Read Isaiah 7:1–17** for the original setting.

**3. Say "God with me" on the way to work.**

**4. Change one thing** in the house He occupies.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Presence is a gift; the day still needs planning. **The Presence of God** is company; wisdom decides what company does with an afternoon.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read John 14:15–23.**
- **Read Colossians 1:24–29.**
- **Set two reminders a day** to acknowledge Him.
- **Write down one thing that must change** because He is in the house.
- **Breathe.** He is not far.
