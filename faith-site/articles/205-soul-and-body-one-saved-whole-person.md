A great deal of Christian thinking quietly assumes the body is a temporary inconvenience. The soul is the point; the body is packaging. Under that assumption, sickness is spiritually irrelevant and health is a hobby.

**1 Thessalonians 5:23** does not allow that assumption: "And the very God of peace sanctify you wholly; and I pray God your whole spirit and soul and body be preserved blameless unto the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ."

Paul names all three — spirit, soul, body — and he puts them under the same verb.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **God does not save a soul and discard a body; He sanctifies the whole person and intends to keep the body in the deal, which is why caring for it is part of obedience rather than a distraction from it.**

## Three That Are One Person

### Not Three Separate Pieces

The verse is not a diagram of three stacking compartments. Scripture uses spirit, soul, and body to describe one life from different angles.

The spirit is the part of you that is regenerated and joined to the Lord (Romans 8:16). The soul is your inner life — intellect, will, emotion. The body is the frame you answer the door in.

**The vocabulary is plural. The person is singular.** When God deals with you, He deals with the whole.

### The Word That Refuses to Break

The New Testament verb for salvation is *sōzō*, and it is the same word used for healing. When the paralytic's friends lowered him through the roof, Jesus said, "Man, thy sins are forgiven thee" (Luke 5:20), and then, "Arise, and take up thy bed, and go unto thy house" (Luke 5:24).

One act. One word behind it. **Forgiveness and healing are not two separate errands God runs; they are two directions of the same rescue.**

### The Body as a Site of Honour

Paul's argument in 1 Corinthians is direct and almost startling: "What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body" (1 Corinthians 6:19–20).

Notice what he does *not* say. He does not say the spirit is the temple and the body is scaffolding. He says the body is the temple — the physical location of the Spirit's residence.

**You do not desecrate a temple to prove your devotion to the God inside it.**

### The Body Is Going Somewhere

The strongest argument for the body's dignity is where it ends up.

"Who shall change our vile body, that it may be fashioned like unto his glorious body" (Philippians 3:21). That word *vile* in the KJV is *tapeinōsis* — humbled, lowly, not "disgusting." Paul is not insulting the body; he is saying it has been brought low and will be transformed.

The resurrection is not the escape from the body. It is the body's glorification. **The Blessed Hope** is a body-shaped hope.

### What This Changes

#### Sickness Is Not a Sign of Spiritual Failure

If the body is included in salvation, then sickness is an intruder to be resisted, not a verdict to be accepted — and not a punishment to be explained.

Which is why Jesus healed without first asking whose sin had caused it. And when the disciples asked that exact question, He rejected the premise: "Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents" (John 9:3).

**You are allowed to fight your illness without first accusing yourself.**

#### Care Is a Form of Obedience

If the body is part of the sanctified whole, then sleep, food, movement, and rest are not distractions from holiness. They are part of it.

That cuts against two opposite errors. One is the ascetic who treats the body as an enemy to be punished. The other is the careless believer who treats health as irrelevant because "it's all temporary."

Both are answered by the same verse: the God of peace is sanctifying *all three*, and He is not embarrassed by the body.

#### Why the Spirit Still Leads

The body is not the boss either. Paul is blunt in Romans: "For if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die: but if ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live" (Romans 8:13).

So there is a plain hierarchy. The spirit is regenerated, the soul is renewed, the body is presented — and every one of the three is answerable to the Spirit of God.

**Divine Wholeness** is not the body taking charge. It is the whole person coming under one Lord.

#### The God Who Attends the Body

One more layer, and it is the tenderest.

Romans 8 makes the Spirit's relation to the body explicit: "But if the Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you, he that raised up Christ from the dead shall also quicken your mortal bodies by his Spirit that dwelleth in you" (Romans 8:11).

The same Spirit who raised Jesus dwells in you — and the phrase "quicken your mortal bodies" means life is available to the frame now, not merely later.

**God is not waiting at the far end of history to care about your body. He is in it already.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop apologising for caring about your health.** It is part of the whole person God is sanctifying.

**2. Give the body its due without making it the boss.** Rest, eat, move, and sleep — then present it, willingly, to the Spirit's direction.

**3. Confess the whole package.** When you pray, bring the anxiety in your mind and the pain in your chest into the same prayer. They belong to the same Father.

**4. Refuse both errors.** Do not punish the body and do not ignore it.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** The temple's care and the temple's medicine are not rivals. Present the body, and take the help God has provided for it.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 1 Thessalonians 5:23–24 daily** this week.
- **Write down one thing** you have neglected about your body and address it.
- **Read 1 Corinthians 6:12–20** and underline "your body."
- **Read Luke 5:17–26** and count how many things the word "saved" could mean in that scene.
- **Read Romans 8:9–11** and note what the Spirit does with a mortal body.
