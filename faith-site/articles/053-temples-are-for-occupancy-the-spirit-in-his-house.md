Have you ever thought of your body primarily as your own property — a private space where you set the rules? Paul asks a question that overturns that assumption entirely.

First Corinthians 6:19 reads, "What? know ye not that **your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost** which is in you, which ye have of God, and **ye are not your own**?" Here is the central truth of this teaching: **your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit — a dwelling place God chose.**

## A Temple, Not a Warehouse

A temple exists for one purpose: to house a deity and host worship. A warehouse exists for storage and convenience. The difference is not architectural but directional — a temple is oriented toward the one who dwells there, a warehouse toward the one who owns it.

Paul says your body is a temple. **The Presence of God** — His manifest nearness, now resident in you by the Spirit — makes it so. **Communion With the Holy Spirit** — ongoing fellowship — is the activity of that temple. **Divine Wholeness** — God's restoration of spirit, soul, and body — is the wellbeing of the house He inhabits.

In the Old Testament, God's glory filled the tabernacle and later the temple. The people did not treat those structures casually, because the presence of God made them sacred. Paul says that same logic now applies to your body: the presence has moved in.

Think of a family that moves into a house and makes it a home. The house is no longer neutral space; it carries their presence, their rhythms, their name. God has done something similar in you — He has made your body a home for His presence.

## Not Your Own — The Freedom of Ownership

The verse's second half is just as important: "ye are not your own... for ye are bought with a price" (verses 19–20). Paul grounds stewardship in purchase. You were bought, and the price was Christ's blood.

At first, "not your own" sounds like a restriction. In context, it is the opposite of slavery — it is the end of being ruled by appetite. The person who owns himself is at the mercy of his own cravings; the person who belongs to God has a Lord who governs for his good.

Paul also warns that defiling the temple invites serious consequences: "If any man defile the temple of God, him shall God destroy; for the temple of God is holy, which temple ye are" (1 Corinthians 3:17). The warning is not petty legalism; it reflects the value of what God has made sacred. A believer who understands the dwelling place becomes careful without becoming fearful.

Here is the contrast: the self-owned life negotiates every boundary by preference. The temple life asks what honours the Resident. One is governed by appetite; the other by reverence.

## The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **1 Corinthians 3:16** — "Know ye not that ye are the temple of God, and that the Spirit of God dwelleth in you?" Paul says it of the church corporately and of the believer personally — the same Spirit, the same sacredness.
- **Romans 12:1** — "Present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service." Body stewardship is described as worship, not merely discipline.
- **John 14:23** — "If a man love me, he will keep my words: and my Father will love him, and we will come unto him, and make our abode with him." Jesus promised an abiding residence, which is exactly what Paul assumes.

Three witnesses, one dwelling: temple of God, living sacrifice, and abiding presence.

## What This Means for You

**1. Treat your body as sacred space.** Sleep, food, movement, and rest are not merely personal preferences; they are stewardship of a dwelling place. Honour the house.

**2. Refuse what defiles the temple.** Paul's context in 1 Corinthians 6 is sexual immorality, but the principle extends to anything that enslaves or defiles — substance abuse, gluttony, pornographic consumption, reckless neglect.

**3. Let ownership bring freedom, not fear.** Belonging to God releases you from the tyranny of appetite. When craving speaks, answer with "I am not my own" — and then choose what honours Him.

**4. Faith and wisdom honour the house practically.** Faith declares your body is God's temple; wisdom builds habits of rest, health, and self-control. Declaring sacredness never excuses neglect; it makes care a form of worship.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 1 Corinthians 6:15–20 slowly.** Mark temple, bought, and glorify, then write one sentence about what changes in your daily habits.
- **Choose one habit to reform.** Pick sleep, nutrition, movement, or media, and take one concrete step this week toward honouring the temple.
- **Ask the Resident before one choice.** Invite the Spirit's opinion about something ordinary — a purchase, a meal, a late night — and act on what you sense.
- **Thank God for your body.** Name three things your body enables you to do and thank Him for the house He chose to inhabit.
