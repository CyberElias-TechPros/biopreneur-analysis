In 2 Kings 6, Elisha's servant walked out at dawn and found the city surrounded. Horses, chariots, and a host of soldiers on the hillside, and only two men inside the walls. He ran back with the news every one of us would have brought: "Alas, my master! how shall we do?" (2 Kings 6:15).

Elisha did not count. He prayed a different prayer, and it was not for reinforcements. "LORD, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see" (2 Kings 6:17). The young man looked again, and the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire. Nothing had changed except the number he could see.

That is not a story about optimism. It is a story about arithmetic.

You have been given the same accounting lesson: "Ye are of God, little children, and have overcome them: because greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world" (1 John 4:4). Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the majority that decides your life is resident inside you, not gathered around you — and it was settled before the world formed its opinion.**

## The Election That Was Never Close

John writes "little children," and the tenderness is deliberate. This is not instruction for spiritual elites. It is a letter to people who were being frightened by louder voices — teachers who had left the fellowship, denied that Christ came in the flesh, and taken a confident tone with them.

Look at the verb tense. "Ye have overcome them." Not "you will overcome them if you are strong enough." John is reporting a result. That is either extraordinary authority or extraordinary arrogance, and the whole letter rests on which one you think it is.

The word "greater" is doing heavy lifting. John does not say the One in you is *equal* to the spirit of the age and that you must tip the balance by your zeal. He says greater — a comparison, and a decisive one. **The Divine Nature** — God's own moral and spiritual character planted in your spirit — is not a small deposit you must top up. **The Power of the Holy Spirit** — the Spirit's supernatural enablement — is not a resource you must earn access to again each morning. And **the Authority of the Believer** — the right to stand in His name — is not a loan that can be recalled mid-argument.

Think of an election that has already been decided while the counting continues in one distant district. The counting matters for the district. It does not change the government. That is the proportion John is insisting on. There is a vote being held about you every day, in conversations, headlines, doctor's rooms, and your own head — and the result was certified before the polls opened.

### Why the Internal Majority Is Different

Three things are true of every other majority you will ever face, and none of them is true of this one.

An external majority can be counted, and so it changes. Every crowd shifts. The crowd that shouted "Hosanna" on Sunday shouted something else by Friday (Matthew 21:9; 27:22). A resident does not shift because a room does.

An external majority has to be persuaded. You argue with it, negotiate, present evidence, and wait for agreement. The Spirit in you is not waiting for your agreement before He is God. He is not a candidate; He is a fact already in residence.

And an external majority can outvote you but cannot change you. It can pass laws over you and leave your nature untouched. What lives in you does the opposite: it changes the person being outvoted. That is why John's answer to the spirit of antichrist is not a debate strategy but a residence.

### The Test That Keeps This Honest

There is a danger here, and John sees it coming. A man can claim an internal majority while actually following his own appetite. So the letter gives a test one verse earlier: "Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world" (1 John 4:1).

Then the test itself: "Every spirit that confesseth that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is of God" (1 John 4:2). The Spirit who lives in you will never deny the incarnate Christ, never minimise the cross, never treat sin lightly, and never promote the speaker's ego. Where a voice does any of those, it is a loud voice from outside, however religious its vocabulary.

Set them side by side and the gap shows. The inner witness produces humility and honesty, because it has nothing to defend. The counterfeit produces defensiveness and a need to be right, because it is a mere voice in a room and knows it. Discernment is not cynicism applied to God; it is courtesy applied to strangers who claim His name.

## What This Means for You

**1. Change your arithmetic before you change your tone.** Most discouraged believers are not weak; they are counting the wrong column. They weigh the problem, the prognosis, the opposition, and the tone of the last conversation — then place God somewhere in the balance. Count the Resident first, last, and in the middle, and let the other figures find their proportion.

**2. Test the voices you have been respecting.** Some of the strongest opposition you will ever face speaks fluent religious language, and some of it speaks in your own voice at three in the morning. Run every voice through 1 John 4:2 and ask what it says about the incarnate Christ and your standing in Him. If a voice urges you toward Christ as He is revealed, it is at least a candidate for the Spirit; if it urges you toward despair of the Father's kindness, what exactly is it urging you toward?

**3. Stop auditioning for the Spirit's agreement.** He is not waiting to be convinced by your fasting or your tears. He already lives where you are praying toward. Prayer becomes restful when it stops trying to secure the presence and starts enjoying the residency.

**4. Take the ground in the sphere where the war actually is.** When Elisha's servant found the army, the battle had never been out there — it was in his perception. Your fight is far more often about sight than about strength. Ask for opened eyes rather than louder defence.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom handle the practical terrain.** Faith counts the greater One who is in you; wisdom still locks the door, keeps the appointment, asks for the second opinion, and tells a friend the truth about what is happening. Understanding that the majority is resident does not make you invulnerable. It makes you sane while you keep your ordinary diligence.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 2 Kings 6:15–17 three times this week**, each time asking God for the servant's second look.
- **Write the phrase "greater is He that is in you"** where you do your most anxious thinking, and read it aloud before you open the situation.
- **Test one recurring voice** against 1 John 4:2 and write down your finding.
- **Open your eyes for someone else this week.** Speak to a frightened person from the resident majority rather than from the shared panic.
