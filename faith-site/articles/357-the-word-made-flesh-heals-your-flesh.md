There is one word in John's prologue that settles a very practical argument, and the word is the one most people read past.

"And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us... full of grace and truth."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **John says the Word became *sarx* — flesh, the very substance that gets sick — and the same Spirit that formed that flesh lives in yours, which means the Incarnation makes your body a legitimate territory for redemption.**

## The Word He Chose

### Sarx

And John did not write *sōma*, a body, or *anthrōpos*, a man. He wrote *sarx* — flesh, the word the New Testament uses for the vulnerable stuff, the part that ages and bleeds and tires.

That is not a footnote. That is a decision.

### Why It Matters

And plenty of religious systems want to save you out of your body. The gospel does not; it saves you into the resurrection of it.

The Word did not become a spirit. He became a body with a bloodstream.

### The Flesh Was Not the Enemy

And this is the crucial correction. Our flesh-as-morality is a different question; *sarx* as the substance of a human life was assumed by God.

**He did not come to escape the body. He came to redeem it.** So why do we treat sickness as though the incarnation never touched the subject?

## What the Body Received in Christ

### Healed

And Matthew, quoting Isaiah, gives the summary of one evening: He cast out the spirits with His word, and healed all that were sick, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias, saying, Himself took our infirmities and bare our sicknesses (Matthew 8:16–17).

The healing ministry was not a side project. It was fulfilment.

### Bled For

And Isaiah says, "he was wounded for our transgressions... and with his stripes we are healed" (Isaiah 53:5). Peter quotes the same line and applies it to his readers (1 Peter 2:24).

The body was not left out of the atonement.

### Raised

And the resurrection is the final answer. He did not leave the body in the tomb and go somewhere spiritual; He took the body up.

**What God raised is the guarantee of what God will heal.**

### The Life You Now Carry

### The Same Spirit

And Paul says if the Spirit of Him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you, He that raised up Christ from the dead shall also quicken your mortal bodies by His Spirit that dwelleth in you (Romans 8:11).

That is the *sarx* word again — mortal, dying, ordinary flesh. Quicken means make alive.

### He Touched Them

And the Gospels are full of a detail we skim: He touched the leper, He took the hand of the dead girl, He spat and touched the blind man's eyes (Mark 8:23). He did not heal from a safe distance when a hand would do.

That is the incarnation applied to skin. The Word made flesh ministered with flesh, and the church that carries that Word is meant to carry the same willingness to come close.

### The Body as a Member of Christ

And Paul asks, "know ye not that your bodies are the members of Christ?" (1 Corinthians 6:15). Not your soul only; your body is in union.

### The Temple

And he says your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you (1 Corinthians 6:19). A temple is a place where God is present and therefore a place where disease is an intruder, not a tenant.

### Living in a Redeemed Body

### Receive Healing as a Gift

And the posture is not demanding; it is receiving. Healing flows from what Christ already accomplished, not from a bargaining session.

### Use the Word

And Proverbs says the words of God are life to those who find them, and health to all their flesh (Proverbs 4:22). *Marpeh* — medicine, healing.

Read it, speak it, chew it.

### Guard It With Wisdom

And this is where faith and wisdom become one thing. Sleep, food, exercise, rest, boundaries, and the doctor are not a failure of faith; they are stewardship of a temple (1 Corinthians 6:19–20).

**Trusting God for your body and caring for your body are not opposites. They are the same obedience.**

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, healing is not a formula with a guaranteed schedule. Paul left Trophimus sick at Miletum (2 Timothy 4:20) and told Timothy to use a little wine for his stomach (1 Timothy 5:23). Real faith and real bodies coexist.

Second, do not turn care of the body into idolatry. Steward it without worshipping it, and keep the kingdom first.

## What This Means for You

1. Read John 1:1–18 and Mark every physical detail in the incarnation.

2. Write out Romans 8:11 and read it over your body this week.

3. Thank God for your body out loud, out of the mouth of a temple.

4. Take one practical step of health this week — sleep, food, or movement.

5. **Receive and steward, with faith and wisdom together.** Pray with confidence, speak the word, see the doctor, and rest. All four belong in the same week.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Matthew 8:14–17.** Note the quotation of Isaiah.
- **Read Isaiah 53:1–6.** Note what was borne.
- **Speak Proverbs 4:22 over a symptom.**
- **Take one night of proper sleep and call it obedience.**
- **Notice whether you have been treating your body as a problem to be managed or a temple to be kept.**
