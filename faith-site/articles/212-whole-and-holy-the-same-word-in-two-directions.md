A month of teaching on divine health ends with a question that has been waiting at the door the whole time.

If God heals you, what is the healing *for*?

The answer is not "so you can be comfortable," and it is not "so you can testify" — though both are true. Scripture's answer is more demanding and more beautiful than either.

"Sanctify you wholly" (1 Thessalonians 5:23).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **God does not heal a person so they can go back to the same life; He makes a person whole so that a person can be holy, and the two works are the same work seen from two directions.**

## The Word Behind Both

### Holiness as Being Set Apart Whole

The Hebrew for holy is *qadosh* — set apart, consecrated, dedicated. An object was holy when it was given entirely to God's use, with nothing held back for another purpose.

Notice what that definition requires: **wholeness.** A thing partly dedicated is not holy; it is shared.

So the connection is not decorative. To be holy is to be *whole* in your belonging — one thing given to one Lord rather than scattered among many.

### The Greek That Holds Both

The New Testament word for salvation, *sōzō*, means to save, to heal, to rescue, to make whole. It is used for forgiveness, for bodies, and for the final rescue of a person.

And *holos* — whole, entire, complete — is the root of *holoklēria*, the word Paul uses in this very verse for being preserved *whole*.

**The vocabulary of healing and the vocabulary of holiness overlap because they describe the same person returning to the same Owner.**

### The God of Peace Who Sanctifies

One detail in 1 Thessalonians 5:23 is easy to miss. Paul does not invoke the God of power to sanctify. He invokes "the very God of peace."

Why peace? Because a fractured person cannot be consecrated. **The God who makes peace is the God who makes whole, and only a whole thing can be handed over.**

### The Finished Work Already Done

One guardrail, and it matters more than any other in a month like this.

You are not being made whole in order to earn acceptance. You are already accepted: "For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified" (Hebrews 10:14).

**The Finished Work** is the ground, not the goal. Everything in this month has been about learning to live out of a wholeness that was purchased, not manufactured.

### What Wholeness Produces

#### A Body That Can Be Offered

Romans 12 joins the two ideas in a single instruction: "I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service" (Romans 12:1).

*Living sacrifice* is an impossible image — a sacrifice that stays on the altar and keeps walking around. That is the Christian life. **The body is not offered once and left; it is offered again every morning.**

And notice the order in the verse: "by the mercies of God." The offering comes after the mercy, never before it.

#### A Life With No Reserved Rooms

Here is the practical shape of holiness, and it is not a list of prohibitions. It is an inventory.

Is there a room in your life you would rather God not enter? A habit, a grudge, a plan, a relationship, a way of speaking about someone? That room is the actual subject of sanctification.

**Holiness is not perfection of behaviour; it is completeness of surrender.** The temple was holy not because every stone was flawless but because the whole building belonged to God.

#### A Peace That Can Be Seen

Hebrews adds the relational dimension: "Follow peace with all men, and holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord" (Hebrews 12:14).

That verse is often used as a threat. Read it as a description instead. A person who is whole tends to be a person at peace — and a person at peace makes God visible in a way an argument never does.

**Divine Wholeness** is evangelism with a body attached to it.

#### The Testimony That Ends in Worship

Matthew records a woman who had been bleeding for twelve years, who touched the hem of His garment and was healed. Jesus turned and said, "Daughter, be of good comfort; thy faith hath made thee whole" (Matthew 9:22).

*Whole* there is *sōzō* again — saved, healed, made whole. And what did she do with it? The account says she was healed from that hour, and Mark adds that Jesus told her, "go in peace" (Mark 5:34).

**She did not receive a treatment. She received a life, and she walked away in it.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Ask what the healing is for.** If God restores your strength, what will you do with the strength? Answer that now, before you need to.

**2. Offer the body again tomorrow.** The sacrifice is living, which means it is repeated. That is not a burden; it is a mercy.

**3. Find the reserved room.** One. Name it. Let Him in.

**4. Refuse both errors about holiness.** It is not grim rule-keeping, and it is not optional. It is a whole life given to a good Owner.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together to the end.** Pray for healing, take the medicine, sleep, and offer the body — all of it is one act of stewardship under one Father.

### Closing This Month

Month seven began with the legal ground of healing and ends here. Along the way: understanding, rest, unbelief, covenant, feelings, symptoms, thoughts, forgiveness, oil, elders, presence, stress, the whole person, stewardship, testimony, order, long life, and the river.

Every one of them pointed the same direction. Not a body that is merely fixed, but a person who is wholly His.

**Divine Wholeness** was never the destination. It is the doorway you walk through into a life that belongs entirely to God.
