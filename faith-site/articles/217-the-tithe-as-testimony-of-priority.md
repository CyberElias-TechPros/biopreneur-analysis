A testimony is normally something you say. The tithe is a testimony you *do* — and it is heard by more people than you think.

"But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you" (Matthew 6:33).

*First* is the whole verse. Not mostly, not after the essentials are covered. First.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the tithe is a testimony before it is a transaction; it declares, in a form nobody can argue with, that the kingdom is first in this house.**

## Three Hearers of One Act

### Your Own Heart

Jesus said the heart follows the treasure (Matthew 6:21). Which means every giving decision is also a self-teaching moment.

You are not merely parting with money. You are telling yourself, again, what you believe about security, about the future, and about who is actually holding the month together.

**A person who gives first is preaching to their own heart, and the sermon sticks.**

### Your Household

Children learn theology from arithmetic long before they learn it from a catechism.

A family that prays before meals and never gives is teaching two different things, and the arithmetic is usually what is remembered. A family that sets aside the first portion — visibly, cheerfully, without treating it as a loss — has taught a lesson no sermon can replace.

**Your children are watching what leaves your hand, and they are drawing conclusions about God.**

### The Watching World

Paul could say to the Ephesian elders, "I have coveted no man's silver, or gold, or apparel" (Acts 20:33), and the whole church knew it was true because they had watched him work.

David made the same point when he refused Araunah's threshing floor: "Nay; but I will surely buy it of thee at a price: neither will I offer burnt offerings unto the LORD my God of that which doth cost me nothing" (2 Samuel 24:24).

**A gift that costs nothing testifies to nothing.** The cost is what makes it a testimony rather than a gesture. So how much has your giving actually said?

### Why Priority Needs a Public Form

#### Because Intentions Are Invisible

Everyone intends to put God first. Almost nobody can tell you whether they actually did, last month.

A tithe is falsifiable. It either left the account or it did not. **That is its genius: it converts a noble intention into a dated fact.**

#### Because Faith Needs Repetition

The word *first* is easy to say once. It becomes a conviction only when it is repeated across many months, in good ones and bad.

Philippians records a church that had done exactly this: "Now ye Philippians know also, that in the beginning of the gospel, when I departed from Macedonia, no church communicated with me as concerning giving and receiving, but ye only" (Philippians 4:15).

They gave when he was with them and when he was gone. That is a habit, and habits testify.

**A testimony is a pattern, not an episode.** And what would one month of that pattern say to the people who share your table?

#### Because the Kingdom Runs on It

Paul's promise to the Philippians is often quoted for personal supply, and it is a real promise: "But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus" (Philippians 4:19).

But read what it answers. It answers a church that had *sent* to his necessity. The promise is about the supply of a giver, in the context of the gospel's work being funded.

**The kingdom is not funded by the surplus of the careless. It is funded by the priority of the deliberate.**

#### The Boundary

One guardrail, and it is a serious one.

A testimony is spoiled by the desire to be seen giving it. Jesus addressed this with unusual sharpness: "Take heed that ye do not your alms before men, to be seen of them... But when thou doest alms, let not thy left hand know what thy right hand doeth" (Matthew 6:1, 3).

**The tithe testifies to God and to your household. It does not need an audience beyond that.**

### What a Testimony Costs

There is a reason the tithe is controversial in so many households, and it is not theological. It is arithmetic.

Ten per cent is a real number. In a lean month it is a real sacrifice, and every person who has done it knows exactly what it feels like the first few times — the small flash of alarm when the figure leaves the account, followed by the quiet surprise when the month still works.

That sequence is the testimony. **A God who can be trusted with the first tenth is a God a household can build a life on.**

### The Witness of Consistency

One generous gift is a gesture. A year of them is a witness.

Anyone can be generous once, especially when the giving is visible or the mood is high. What cannot be faked is the twelfth consecutive month, in a difficult year, with no audience and no applause.

Consistency is what turns giving from an event into a character trait — and character traits are what children inherit.

### When the Giving Is Hard

There is a particularly honest kind of testimony that only appears in a hard season.

If a household gives the first portion through a redundancy, a bereavement, or a failed harvest, the lesson learned by everyone in that house is not "we are generous." It is "God is real." That is a different and far better lesson.

The testimony of priority is loudest when the priority costs the most.

## What This Means for You

**1. Make the giving visible at home, in the right way.** Not with an announcement, but without a secret. Let children see that the first portion leaves first.

**2. Decide the priority before the pressure.** Priority decided in a crisis is not priority; it is negotiation.

**3. Do not tie it to how the month went.** The witness is strongest in a lean month.

**4. Refuse the applause.** Give quietly. The testimony is not for the room.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** A testimony is not a substitute for a budget. **Kingdom Stewardship** plans the month and gives from its first line.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Matthew 6:25–34** and count how many times Jesus says "first" and "seek."
- **Read 2 Samuel 24:18–25** and mark what David refused to give.
- **Tell your children** what the family gives and why, at their level.
- **Read Philippians 4:15–20** and note who the promise is addressed to.
- **Give first this month**, before any other line item.
