Most people who pray for healing have also been wronged by somebody, and the two facts are rarely kept in the same room. We treat the body as one subject and the grudge as another, and we ask God to fix the first while carefully preserving the second.

**Ephesians 4:31–32** refuses to keep them apart: "Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger, and clamour, and evil speaking, be put away from you, with all malice: and be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you."

Notice the list. Bitterness comes first, and it is named alongside shouting and slander — not as a mild personality quirk but as something to be *put away*.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **forgiveness is not a favour you do for someone who hurt you; it is the removal of a blockage in your own channel, and Scripture treats the blockage as a matter of the body as well as the soul.**

## What Bitterness Does Inside a Person

### The Root That Grows Downward

Hebrews gives the image, and it is a farming image: "Looking diligently lest any man fail of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up trouble you, and thereby many be defiled" (Hebrews 12:15).

Three things about roots, and all three are true of a grudge.

**A root is invisible for a long time.** It grows beneath the surface, unnoticeable, while the person believes they have moved on.

**A root goes down, not up.** It draws from a depth most people never inspect.

**A root produces more than itself.** "Thereby many be defiled." The writer does not say the bitter man suffers; he says many are defiled. Bitterness is contagious, and the first person infected is the one carrying it.

### Where the Body Keeps It

Scripture does not draw an anatomy chart, but it does not leave the connection implicit either.

"Envy the rottenness of the bones" (Proverbs 14:30). "A broken spirit drieth the bones" (Proverbs 17:22). The inner life is described as writing itself into bone and marrow, and Psalm 32 records a man whose unconfessed state left him with a body that felt dried out: "When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long" (Psalm 32:3).

Whatever medical mechanism you attribute to it, the pattern in Scripture is consistent. **What we will not release, we end up carrying in the body.**

### Why "I've Forgiven Them" Sometimes Isn't True

Here is where the honesty has to land, because this is one of the most common forms of self-deception.

The test is not the sentence you said once. The test is what happens when the person's name comes up.

If the thought arrives with a reheated argument, a prepared speech, and a small satisfaction at their difficulty, then a decision may have been made but the root is still drawing water. **The tongue said it; the interior life has not been told.**

That is not a reason to despair. It is a reason to be specific with God about the root instead of managing the fruit.

### How Forgiveness Actually Works

#### The Debt Was Real

Forgiveness is not pretending an injury did not occur. The whole vocabulary of forgiveness assumes a debt.

Jesus told a parable about a servant forgiven an enormous sum who then refused to forgive a small one (Matthew 18:23–35). The master's verdict at the end is precise: "shouldest not thou also have had compassion on thy fellowservant, even as I had pity on thee?" (Matthew 18:33).

Your forgiveness of another is not a new standard invented at the moment of the offence. It is a **flow-through**. You were forgiven an unpayable sum; what you are being asked to release is a small one, and the point of the parable is the absurdity of the dam.

#### Why "Seventy Times Seven" Is About Arithmetic

Peter tried to be generous: "Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times?" (Matthew 18:21).

"Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven" (Matthew 18:22).

That is not a tally to be tracked. It is a number so large it stops being a number. The moment forgiveness becomes a ledger, it has become its opposite.

#### Forgiving While You Pray

Mark records the instruction with no cushioning at all: "And when ye stand praying, forgive, if ye have ought against any: that your Father also which is in heaven may forgive you your trespasses" (Mark 11:25).

Notice the timing. *When you stand praying.* Forgiveness is not a separate project you will get to once you feel stronger. It is part of the prayer itself, and Jesus puts it in the middle of the prayer about mountains being moved.

**The faith that moves a mountain is a faith that has released a person.**

#### The Party Who Has Not Changed

One boundary, because this teaching is sometimes abused in ways that harm people.

Forgiveness and reconciliation are not the same event. Forgiveness is what you release before God; reconciliation requires a change on the other side, and Scripture does not pretend otherwise: "If thy brother trespass against thee, rebuke him; and if he repent, forgive him" (Luke 17:3).

You can release the debt and still keep a boundary. You can forgive and still not put your child in the room. That is not unforgiveness; it is wisdom, and the two are not enemies.

## What This Means for You

**1. Name the person, not the principle.** Healing rarely comes to the man who forgives "everybody" in a general way. Name them, to God, and release them by name.

**2. Ask about the root, not the fruit.** The anger is the fruit. Ask the Spirit to show you the root and be willing to hear the answer.

**3. Pray blessing over them, deliberately.** This is the hardest step and the one that dissolves the grip fastest. You cannot nurse a grudge and ask God to bless someone in the same breath for long.

**4. Guard the boundary after you release.** Forgiveness is not the abolition of wisdom. **The Ministry of Reconciliation** is offered freely, but a contract of trust is rebuilt only on evidence.

**5. Let forgiveness sit beside the appointment.** Releasing a grudge and seeing a doctor are not competing answers. Faith and wisdom work together — the body that has been carrying something for years may well need care as well as release.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Matthew 18:21–35 in one sitting** and notice how the parable ends.
- **Write down one name** you have not released, and pray for them by name for seven days.
- **Read Hebrews 12:14–17** and mark the word "root."
- **Read Psalm 32:1–7** and note the body language.
- **Say the sentence:** "I release the debt. I do not release the boundary."
