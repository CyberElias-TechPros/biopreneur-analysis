There are hundreds of years between the last Old Testament prophet and the first cry in Bethlehem, and the silence is the part of the story we skip.

"For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen, unto the glory of God by us."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Paul does not say the promises are *true* in Christ but that they are *yea* — yes, affirmed, made good — which means the manger is not a hopeful beginning but a payment already countersigned by centuries of prophecy.**

## The Word Paul Uses

### Yea

And *nai* is the Greek affirmative. Paul is saying that in Christ, every divine promise has been answered with a yes.

Not a maybe. Not a conditional. An affirmative.

### Amen

And *amēn* is the word of confirmation — so be it, it is firm. The yes is spoken and then it is sealed.

Prophecy gave the yes. The incarnation was the amen.

**God did not promise and then hope. He promised and then came.** And if He was that exact about a village, what makes you think He has mislaid your address?

### Unto the Glory of God

And Paul adds the purpose: so that glory comes to God through us. The fulfilment is not merely for our comfort; it is for His reputation.

## The Specifics That Were Certified

### A Virgin Shall Conceive

And Isaiah gave a sign to a frightened king seven hundred years early: the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel (Isaiah 7:14).

The sign was given to a man who did not want it. It was fulfilled anyway.

### Bethlehem

And Micah named the town while it was still an insignificant village: "But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel" (Micah 5:2).

Not a region. A village. Not a century. A place.

### The Line of David

And the genealogies in Matthew and Luke are tedious until you realise what they are doing: they are tracing a legal claim through a family tree across a thousand years.

Prophecy is not a mood. It is a paper trail.

### The Time

And Paul's phrase elsewhere is that He came in the fullness of time (Galatians 4:4) — after the roads were built, the language was common, and the exile had done its work.

Even the calendar obeyed. He was not early and He was not late; He was exactly on time, and the wait had been doing work nobody could see.

### What Prophecy Does for Your Faith

### It Makes Faith Reasonable

And this is the point of the fulfilled prophecies. They are not decoration; they are evidence. Faith is not a leap in the dark; it is a step onto a floor that has held before.

Peter says we have a more sure word of prophecy (2 Peter 1:19). More sure — than even an eyewitness experience.

### It Shows the Character Behind the Promise

And a God who keeps His word about a village will keep His word about your life. The pattern is the evidence.

Joshua put it plainly at the end of a conquest: not one good thing failed of all that the Lord had spoken (Joshua 21:45).

**The God who remembered Bethlehem will remember you.** Which of His promises have you quietly stopped expecting Him to keep?

### It Sets the Order of Faith

And the pattern is always word first, then fulfilment, then testimony. God speaks, we believe, He performs, we tell.

Skip the believing and you have the word without the entrance.

### Living Between the Two

### Where You Are Now

And most of us live in the silence between the last promise and the first fulfilment. That is not a failure of faith; it is the ordinary location of it.

The four hundred years were not an absence. They were a wait.

### Holding the Word

And Mary "kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart" (Luke 2:19). She did not have the whole story either.

Pondering is not passivity. It is what faith does while it waits.

### Testing the Promise

And not every strong impression is a prophecy. Faith and wisdom test what you believe you heard against scripture, against counsel, and against the fruit it produces over time.

A promise that makes you frantic is probably not from the God of peace.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a claim that every desire you have is a prophecy. Wishing is not hearing, and the promises of God are found in His word and confirmed by His Spirit.

Second, waiting is not inactivity. Faith and wisdom work while they wait — Joseph served in a prison, David kept sheep, Mary made a home — and the fulfilment found them at their post.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Micah 5:2 and Luke 2:4–7 side by side.

2. Write down the promises you are currently standing on.

3. Check each one against scripture and honest counsel.

4. Thank God for the ones already kept in your own history.

5. **Hold the word, and keep faith and wisdom together.** Do not manufacture a fulfilment and do not abandon a promise. Keep the ordinary obedience and let God keep the calendar.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Joshua 21:43–45.** Note the summary of a whole lifetime.
- **Read Luke 24:13–35.** Note how Jesus handled the prophecies.
- **Keep a list of answered promises and read it on a hard day.**
- **Do the next right thing while you wait.**
- **Notice whether you have been doubting the promise or doubting the Promiser.**
