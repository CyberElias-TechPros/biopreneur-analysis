Notice that the accuser in Revelation 12 is not described as a tempter but as a speaker.

"The accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night" (Revelation 12:10).

Day and night. Not occasionally, not in moments of weakness. He has a standing complaint and a full calendar.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the gospel does not train you to ignore the accusation; it teaches you to answer it — and the answer is not a defence of your record but a citation of His.**

## The Nature of the Voice

### It Is Prosecutorial, Not Informational

Accusation is not the same as information. Information gives you a fact you can act on. Accusation gives you a fact with a verdict attached.

The difference is what it does after it speaks. Information leaves you with a decision. Accusation leaves you with a sentence.

**The accuser never tells you something useful. He tells you something final.**

### It Uses True Facts

And this is the part that makes it hard to answer: the accusations are frequently **accurate**.

You did say that. You did fail there. He is not required to lie — an old file is enough.

**A true fact presented with a false verdict is still a lie, and that is precisely the tool.**

### It Speaks Day and Night

There is no scheduling courtesy. It comes at 3 a.m., in the car, on the walk back from a good day.

Paul knew this terrain: "I find then a law, that, when I would do good, evil is present with me" (Romans 7:21).

**You will not win this by being unavailable. You will win it by having an answer ready.**

## The Answer Given in Scripture

### The Blood

Revelation gives the method, and it is threefold: "And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death" (Revelation 12:11).

The blood comes first, and it is not a defence of innocence. It is a plea of **substitution**.

**The blood does not argue that you were right. It argues that the penalty was already paid for the wrong.**

### The Word of Their Testimony

The second element is not a description of their feelings. It is a spoken statement of what God says.

That is *homologia* — saying the same thing as God. Confession is not confessing sin repeatedly; it is agreeing with the verdict out loud.

Faith has a voice, and the voice is the answer.

### Not Loving Their Lives

And the third element ends the accusation's leverage entirely. A man who does not need to preserve his reputation cannot be blackmailed by an accusation against it.

The accuser's power depends on your need to look good. Remove that and you remove his microphone.

### Paul's Answer in Romans

And Paul answers in legal form: "Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect? It is God that justifieth. Who is he that condemneth? It is Christ that died" (Romans 8:33–34).

Notice the shape of the reply. He does not say "no one, because I am innocent." He says "no one, because God has justified."

The answer is not a claim about your behaviour. It is a claim about God's action.

### Why Silence Does Not Work

**Ignoring is not answering.** There is a school of teaching that says to simply ignore the accusations, or to rebuke them without engagement.

But a defendant who says nothing is not released. He is simply unheard.

Scripture does not model silence before the accuser. It models an answer.

### What the Answer Sounds Like

**Rebuking without reasoning.** There is a version of rebuking that is all volume and no content — a nervous habit rather than a settled stand.

Jude is careful about the difference: even Michael the archangel, contending with the devil, did not bring a railing accusation but said, "The Lord rebuke thee" (Jude 9).

Authority is not volume. It is the citation of a name that is not yours.

**Not denial.** The answer is never "I never did that." It is "that was dealt with at the cross, and it is not on my account."

**The believer's defence is a confession, not a denial.**

**Not explanation.** And it is not a rehearsing of reasons, either. Explaining an accusation to the accuser is a conversation he is not entitled to.

Job tried to argue his case and ended up with his hand over his mouth (Job 40:4). The answer is not the argument; it is the verdict.

**Stated, not discussed.** The answer is spoken and then left standing. Three short clauses, then silence.

**You do not need to win the argument. You need to state the fact.**

### The Habit of Answering

**Prepare it in advance.** 
An answer prepared in the moment of attack is an answer spoken under pressure. Prepare it now, in the calm.

That is why Paul tells believers to take the two-edged sword of the Spirit in their armour (Ephesians 6:17) — a weapon carried, not improvised.

#**Say it out loud.** There is a difference between remembering an answer and saying one. The accuser is a speaker; he is answered by a speaker.

#**Use the same answer every time.** Consistency is part of the testimony. A changing answer sounds like a defendant improvising. The same blood, the same testimony, on every occasion.

**The verdict does not change with the accusation, which is why the answer does not need to.**

#### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, answering the accuser is not the same as resenting him. The stand is already won, and the tone should be a man reading a settled order, not a man shouting in a fight.

Second, this is not a technique to banish unpleasant feelings. The voice may return tomorrow. So may the answer.

**The **The Blood of Jesus** is the same yesterday and today; the **Confession of the Word (*Homologia*)** is simply saying so, every time it becomes necessary.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Write your answer down.** Three sentences: the blood, the verdict, the justification.

**2. Notice the verdict.** Next time, separate the fact from the sentence attached to it.

**3. Say it audibly** — not angrily, not at length.

**4. Stop arguing.** If you have already confessed it, the conversation is over. Do not reopen it to defend yourself.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Answering the accuser is not the same as learning from the failure. **Righteousness** settles the verdict; wisdom takes the lesson and moves on.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Revelation 12:7–12** and note the three elements of the overcoming.
- **Write your three-sentence answer** on a card.
- **Read Zechariah 3:1–5** again, and watch the order of the rebuke.
- **Use the answer once this week**, out loud.
- **Read Ephesians 6:10–18** and identify which piece you have been missing.
