Have you ever suspected that God is waiting for you to grow into something before He can fully use you — as though maturity might finally make you acceptable? Many believers spend years trying to become what Scripture says they already are.

Peter opens his second letter with an astonishing declaration: God's "divine power **hath given unto us all things** that pertain unto life and godliness" (2 Peter 1:3). Here is the central truth of this teaching: **God already gave you everything pertaining to life and godliness — so spiritual growth is not becoming someone new, but learning to express the new someone you already are.**

## Given, Not Yet Attained

Notice the tense. Peter does not say God will give; he says God *hath given*. The supply is past, complete, and resident. Everything pertaining to life — the God-kind of life in your spirit — and godliness — the character that flows from it — has already been granted to you through knowing Christ.

**The Divine Nature** — God's own nature imparted to your reborn spirit, making you a partaker of His life and character — is the reason Peter can speak this way. You are not a natural person trying to add divine qualities from outside. You are a new creation carrying divine nature from inside. **The New Creation** — the brand-new spiritual person God made in Christ — is not an improved version of the old; it is a different kind of being altogether.

Think of a mango seed and a mango tree. The seed does not grow into something foreign to itself; it grows into the expression of what it already is. Everything the tree will ever be is already resident in the seed. Your new nature works the same way: growth is expression, not addition. You are not adding divinity; you are unfolding it.

## Growth Is Expression, Not Attainment

This distinction changes how you read every exhortation in the New Testament. When Peter tells you to add virtue, knowledge, temperance, patience, and godliness (2 Peter 1:5–7), he is not asking you to manufacture qualities you lack. He is asking you to bring your conduct into agreement with the nature you possess.

Peter's own language in verse 4 makes this unmistakable: you are made "partakers of the divine nature, having escaped the corruption that is in the world through lust." The escape is past, the partaking is present, and the adding of verses 5–7 is the daily outworking of both. He is not describing a ladder up to divinity; he is describing the conduct that divinity produces when it is understood.

**Spiritual Understanding** — the God-given capacity to perceive and grasp spiritual truth, seeing reality as God sees it — is the mechanism of this growth. As your understanding enlarges, your expression matures. You do not become more righteous; you become more aware of the righteousness you have, and your behaviour follows your awareness.

Here is the contrast: the attainment mindset asks, "What must I do to become acceptable?" The expression mindset asks, "How do I live out what I already am?" One is endless striving; the other is progressive discovery. Both involve effort — but only one has a finished foundation.

## The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **Colossians 2:9–10** — "For in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily. And ye are complete in him." Paul places Christ's fullness and your completeness side by side. Complete is not a future goal; it is a present position.
- **Ephesians 1:3** — "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who hath blessed us with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ." Paul also uses the past tense: hath blessed. Every spiritual blessing is already yours in Christ, not pending your improvement.
- **Philippians 3:12–16** — "Not as though I had already attained... but I follow after... forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before." Paul holds both together: a completed position and a pressing pursuit. He presses toward the full expression of what Christ already apprehended him for.

Three witnesses, one pattern: complete in position, growing in expression.

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop treating maturity as qualification.** You do not need to become more acceptable to be used by God; you need to become more aware of the acceptance you have. Maturity deepens expression; it does not create standing.

**2. Pursue growth as discovery, not desperation.** Read Scripture to discover who you already are in Christ, not to negotiate who you might become. Discovery produces joy; desperation produces burnout.

**3. Measure growth by expression, not by comparison.** Growth shows up in patience under pressure, honesty in difficulty, love for the unlovely, and consistency in private. Compare yourself to your former self in Christ, not to another believer's season.

**4. Faith and wisdom grow you in the right order.** Faith rests on what God has already given; wisdom builds the daily habits — Scripture, prayer, fellowship, diligence, rest — through which that gift is expressed. Declaring completeness never excuses passivity; it directs effort toward expression instead of attainment.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 2 Peter 1:3–8 in one sitting.** Underline what God has given, then list what Peter tells you to add: notice the supply precedes the pursuit.
- **Replace one "becoming" prayer with a "revealing" prayer.** Instead of asking God to make you patient or holy, thank Him that His nature is in you, then ask for wisdom to express it in a specific relationship.
- **Choose one quality to express deliberately.** Pick patience, kindness, or self-control this week and practise it in one named situation, consciously drawing on the divine nature within you.
- **Journal one area of growth you can see.** Write down where your conduct has changed since you believed, and thank God that the change was expression of His life, not manufacture of your own.
