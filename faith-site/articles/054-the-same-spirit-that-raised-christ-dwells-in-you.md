Have you ever considered that the power which raised Jesus from the dead is currently resident in your body? Most believers believe the resurrection historically and live as though it were geographically distant.

Romans 8:11 makes the connection explicit: "if the Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead **dwell in you**, he that raised up Christ from the dead shall also **quicken your mortal bodies** by his Spirit that dwelleth in you." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the same Spirit who raised Jesus lives in you.**

## The Same Spirit, Not a Lesser One

Paul's argument rests on identity: it is *the same Spirit*. Not a similar Spirit, not a diminished version, not a junior assistant. The Spirit who hovered over the tomb and produced the resurrection is the Spirit who lives in your spirit.

**The Power of the Holy Spirit** — the Spirit's supernatural enablement — is therefore resurrection power by definition. **Divine Wholeness** — God's restoration of spirit, soul, and body — rests on the promise that He will quicken your mortal body. **The Supernatural Life** — life lived in dependence on the Spirit — is life powered by the same energy that defeated death.

Have you ever wondered why the New Testament speaks of the resurrection so often as a present experience? Notice the word "quicken" — to make alive, to give life to. Paul applies it to your mortal body, the very part of you that still feels weakness, fatigue, and mortality. The resurrection is not only a future event; it is a present impartation.

Think of a house wired to the same power station that lights a whole city. The house does not generate its own electricity; it draws from the grid. Your mortal body is wired to the resurrection grid, and the Spirit is the connection.

### Power for Living, Not Only for Raising

Paul immediately applies this to sanctification: "Therefore, brethren, we are debtors, not to the flesh, to live after the flesh. For if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die: but if ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live" (Romans 8:12–13).

Resurrection power is given so that you can put sin to death. The same energy that raised Christ is available to break habits, heal wounds, and strengthen weakness. Paul's prayer in Ephesians 1:19–20 asks that believers would know "the exceeding greatness of his power to us-ward who believe, according to the working of his mighty power, which he wrought in Christ, when he raised him from the dead."

The distinction could not be sharper. The believer who does not know this power fights sin in his own strength and loses. The believer who knows it fights from resurrection resources and prevails.

### Resurrection Power Has a Present Address

The verse is often read as a promise about the future, and it is that: "But if the Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you, he that raised up Christ from the dead shall also quicken your mortal bodies by his Spirit that dwelleth in you" (Romans 8:11).

But the word "dwell" is doing present work. The Spirit who accomplished the greatest act of power in history is not waiting at the end of your life; He is resident in it now. The resurrection engine is already installed.

Paul prays for the Ephesians to know this specifically: "the exceeding greatness of his power to us-ward who believe, according to the working of his mighty power, which he wrought in Christ, when he raised him from the dead" (Ephesians 1:19–20). The measure of the power available to you is the measure that emptied a tomb.

Ezekiel's valley gives the picture: "Thus saith the Lord GOD unto these bones; Behold, I will cause breath to enter into you, and ye shall live" (Ezekiel 37:5). Dry bones, a prophet's word, and a living army — the same sequence Paul assumes when he tells believers to walk "after the Spirit" rather than after the flesh.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **Ephesians 1:19–20** — "The exceeding greatness of his power to us-ward who believe... which he wrought in Christ, when he raised him from the dead." Paul measures available power by the resurrection itself.
- **Philippians 3:10** — "That I may know him, and the power of his resurrection, and the fellowship of his sufferings." Paul longs to know resurrection power experientially, not merely doctrinally.
- **Ezekiel 37:5** — "Thus saith the Lord GOD unto these bones; Behold, I will cause breath to enter into you, and ye shall live." The vision of dry bones previews the Spirit's life-giving work — the same pattern Paul applies to believers.

Three witnesses, one power: exceeding greatness, experiential knowledge, and dry bones breathing.

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop underestimating the power within you.** When you face a habit, a fear, or a weakness, remind yourself who lives in you. Resurrection power is not a metaphor.

**2. Apply the power to the flesh.** Paul says to mortify the deeds of the body *through the Spirit*. Name the deed, then rely on the Spirit rather than willpower alone.

**3. Bring your body into the promise.** Pray for the quickening of your mortal body — strength, healing, endurance — while walking in wisdom regarding rest and care.

**4. Faith and wisdom rely and care.** Faith draws on resurrection power; wisdom still stewards the body through rest, medical care, and healthy habits. Declaring power never excuses neglect; it honours the vessel through which it flows.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Romans 8:9–13 slowly.** Mark every reference to the Spirit, then write down which area of your life most needs quickening.
- **Pray over your mortal body daily.** Ask the Spirit to strengthen and quicken you, and thank Him for the resurrection power already resident in you.
- **Mortify one deed through the Spirit.** Choose a specific habit, and each time it surfaces, pray in dependence rather than fighting alone.
- **Meditate on Ephesians 1:19–20.** Read it each morning this week and let the measure of available power reshape your expectations.
