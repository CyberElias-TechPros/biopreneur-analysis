There is a Greek idea that has quietly entered the church and done more damage to Christian health than any doctrine of demons: the idea that the body is a disposable container and only the soul matters.

Paul answers it in six verses, and he answers it with a resurrection.

"What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you" (1 Corinthians 6:19).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **your body is not a temporary container for a soul that matters more; it is a member of Christ and a temple of the Spirit, which is why God's interest in your health is not cosmetic but covenantal.**

## What Paul Was Answering

### The Slogan

The Corinthians had a phrase, and Paul quotes it twice before he demolishes it: "All things are lawful unto me" (1 Corinthians 6:12).

And then a second one, and this is the one that concerns us: "Meats for the belly, and the belly for meats" (1 Corinthians 6:13).

Read that as a slogan and you hear the philosophy: the body is a set of appetites, the appetites have corresponding objects, and the whole arrangement is meaningless — so do as you like. Food for the stomach, stomach for food, and none of it touches what really matters.

**That is the first-century version of "it's just my body."**

### Paul's Two-Part Answer

And Paul answers it in a way that has been the church's doctrine of the body ever since.

First: "but God shall destroy both it and them" (1 Corinthians 6:13). The belly is temporary. Fine — the slogan is right about that much.

Second, and here is the turn: "Now the body is not for fornication, but for the Lord; and the Lord for the body."

Look at the symmetry. Not "the body is for the Lord" only, but **"and the Lord for the body."** The relationship runs in both directions.

That is an astonishing sentence. God has committed Himself to a body. Not to a soul that happens to have one.

### "Fornication" Is Not the Whole Subject

The immediate application is sexual sin, and Paul is blunt about it: "Every sin that a man doeth is without the body; but he that committeth fornication sinneth against his own body" (1 Corinthians 6:18).

But a careful reader notices that Paul's argument is not confined to that subject. His premises are general: the body is a member of Christ. The body is a temple. The body will be raised. Every one of those premises applies to every use of the body, which is why this passage is the church's charter for bodily stewardship and not only for sexual purity.

### "Members of Christ"

"Know ye not that your bodies are the members of Christ? shall I then take the members of Christ, and make them the members of an harlot? God forbid" (1 Corinthians 6:15).

The word is *melē* — limbs, parts of a body. Paul is not saying your body belongs to Christ in some legal sense. He is saying it is attached to Him the way a hand is attached to a wrist.

You cannot separate the two and take one to a place the other would not go.

### The Resurrection Argument

And here is the argument that kills the container idea outright.

"God hath both raised up the Lord, and will also raise up us by his own power" (1 Corinthians 6:14).

Why does that matter to what you do with your body now? Because it tells you what the body is for. If the body were a disposable husk, God would not bother raising it. The fact that He intends to raise this body means He never regarded it as disposable.

And Paul expands it in chapter 15: "It is sown a natural body; it is raised a spiritual body" (1 Corinthians 15:44). Sown, not discarded. A seed is not thrown away; it is planted, and what comes up is the same thing transformed.

### Bought, Not Rented

And then the transaction clause, which is the reason for everything he has said.

"For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body" (1 Corinthians 6:20).

The word is *ēgorasthēte* — you were purchased, from the marketplace. A slave bought in the market did not decide his own use; his owner did.

That is a hard image for modern ears, so hear it as what it actually is: **the purchase is the proof of value.** Nobody pays for what they intend to throw away. And the price paid for your body was not silver.

"Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold... but with the precious blood of Christ" (1 Peter 1:18–19).

**The blood is the appraisal.** Your body was valued at the life of the Son of God.

### The Temple and the Tenant

And the second image, which is the one that reframes health.

"Know ye not that ye are the temple of God, and that the Spirit of God dwelleth in you? If any man defile the temple of God, him shall God destroy; for the temple of God is holy, which temple ye are" (1 Corinthians 3:16–17).

A temple is a house built for a resident. And the resident is the Holy Spirit — the **Presence of God**, not visiting occasionally, but dwelling.

Which means the question of how you treat your body is not primarily a question of self-image. **It is a question of hospitality.** You are not managing an asset; you are maintaining a home where Someone lives.

### The Body and the Word

And John gives the doctrine its deepest root, in a sentence of five words: "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us" (John 1:14).

God did not disdain flesh. He took it. The incarnation is the permanent answer to every philosophy that treats the material world as a mistake.

If flesh were beneath God, He would not have worn it.

### The House You Live In

One illustration, and then the practicals.

Consider a man who inherits a house with a permanent resident who is also his closest friend. The friend will never leave. Ask what the man's attitude to maintenance becomes.

He does not fix the roof because property values matter. He fixes the roof because somebody he loves is under it. **The maintenance is hospitality.**

That is the posture this teaching asks for, and it is a different posture from vanity, from fear, and from the grim duty that treats the body as a project to be disciplined.

### What Tending the House Looks Like

So the practical shape, and none of it is exotic.

It means food is a matter of stewardship, not merely appetite — Paul's own conclusion, in a passage about the belly. It means sleep is not laziness; it is repair. It means exercise is not vanity; it is the ordinary upkeep of a dwelling.

It means you take the medication. If the Spirit lives in this body, then the means of healing are not a secular intrusion; they are care for His house. Faith and wisdom work together, and a man who prays for his body while destroying it with neglect has contradicted his own prayer.

And it means you refuse the other error, which is worshipping the house. Paul is not asking you to become obsessed with your body. He is asking you to remember who lives in it. **The temple's glory is never the architecture; it is the occupant.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop treating your body as a rental.** You will not be handing it back at the end of a lease. You will be raised in it.

**2. Ask what your habits are feeding.** Not only "is this allowed" but "who lives here, and is this good for His house?"

**3. Let the price settle your worth.** **The Inheritance of the Believer** includes a body that has been bought, and nobody pays that much for something they intend to discard.

**4. Bring your body into your worship.** Not merely your thoughts. Kneeling, singing, fasting, resting — the body participates, because the body is where the **New Creation** is lived out in time.

**5. Get help when the house needs a builder.** A doctor is not a rival to the Healer. See the previous article on where sickness comes from; the same logic applies to repairs.
