Everybody is laying up treasure. That is not a decision anyone is allowed to opt out of — only a decision about which location.

"But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust doth corrupt, and where thieves do not break through nor steal: for where your treasure is, there will your heart be also" (Matthew 6:20–21).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Jesus does not ask you to have no treasure; He asks you to hold it in the one account that cannot be broken into, because the location of your treasure quietly relocates your heart.**

## What Jesus Actually Compared

### The Moth and the Rust

The contrast is not heaven versus earth as places. It is two accounts with very different security.

*Moth* is *sēs* — the clothes-moth. *Rust* is *brōsis* — literally "eating," the corrosion of metal. And then thieves, who break through (literally "dig through") mud-brick walls.

So Jesus selects the three great destructors of the ancient world: the insect, the corrosion, and the thief. **He is not saying earthly wealth is evil. He is saying it is uninsurable.**

### The Heart Follows the Money

Verse 21 is the most important sentence in the passage, and it is usually read backwards.

We assume that prudent people put their treasure where their heart already is. Jesus says the opposite: the treasure is dropped first, and the heart travels to wherever it lands.

So this is not a lecture about generosity. **It is instruction about how to move your own heart** — because you cannot change what you love by deciding to, but you can change what you invest in.

### "For Yourselves"

The phrase *lay up for yourselves* is worth noticing. This is not a counsel of self-poverty.

Jesus is telling a person to their face that they are permitted to be motivated, and that the smartest thing they can do for themselves is to put the money somewhere it survives. **It is the one investment where self-interest and obedience point the same direction.**

### The Sound Eye

The passage ends with the strangest verse: "But if thine eye be evil, thy whole body shall be full of darkness" (Matthew 6:23), and then, "No man can serve two masters... ye cannot serve God and mammon" (Matthew 6:24).

*Mammon* is not money in itself; it is money considered as a rival deity. The image is a person with a divided vision — an eye that cannot settle. **Single vision is a condition for a single heart.**

### What Makes a Treasure Heavenly

#### The Test of Permanence

The first test is simple: will it survive the next thousand years?

Most of what we call wealth fails this test in a decade, not a millennium. A currency can vanish. A business can be sold. A house changes hands.

Whereas what was given away is recorded. What was taught is still in someone's head. What was done in secret is known to God and not lost.

#### The Test of the Thief

Second test: can it be taken from you without your permission?

Your reputation can be taken. Your position can be removed. **What you have sent ahead, no one on earth has access to.**

#### The Secret Account

Jesus names the secret account explicitly earlier in the same sermon: "But when thou doest alms, let not thy left hand know what thy right hand doeth: that thine alms may be in secret: and thy Father which seeth in secret himself shall reward thee openly" (Matthew 6:3–4).

So a treasure in heaven is often a thing nobody on earth knows about. **The secrecy is not modesty; it is security.**

#### The Rich Commandment

1 Timothy gives the practical version, and it is refreshingly concrete: "Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy; that they do good, that they be rich in good works, ready to distribute, willing to communicate, laying up in store for themselves a good foundation against the time to come, that they may lay hold on eternal life" (1 Timothy 6:17–19).

Note that Paul does not tell rich believers to liquidate. He tells them to **reposition** — and he calls it "laying up in store for themselves."

Good works are not merely duties. They are deposits.

#### The Steward's Accounting

And Luke adds the promotion logic: "If therefore ye have not been faithful in the unrighteous mammon, who will commit to your trust the true riches?" (Luke 16:11).

What you have now is a trial balance. What you are doing with the small, temporary account is the evidence about your readiness for the true one.

## What This Means for You

**1. Name your actual treasure.** Not what you say is most important — where the money and the hours both went last month.

**2. Move something this month.** Purpose be specific: this sum, this student, this family, this work.

**3. Do one thing in secret.** No receipt, no mention, no Instagram. Let the Father see it.

**4. Refuse the fear that hoards.** **The Blessed Hope** makes hoarding illogical: if the future is certain, you can afford to be generous now.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Budgeting is not unbelief; laying up treasure in heaven requires a plan as much as any other investment. **Kingdom Stewardship** reads both columns.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Matthew 6:19–24** every morning this week.
- **Write down your last month's spending** and mark what it was investing in.
- **Send one sum ahead** deliberately, without telling anyone.
- **Read 1 Timothy 6:17–19** and mark every verb.
- **Read Luke 16:1–13** and note the phrase "true riches."
