Have you ever felt like a guest in God's house — grateful to be forgiven, yet unsure whether you truly belong? Many believers carry a quiet sense of spiritual outsider status, as though faith made them better servants but not family members.

John 1:12 announces a family transfer: "to them gave he power to **become the sons of God**, even to them that believe on his name." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **receiving Christ gave you more than forgiveness — it gave you His family name.** You now carry the identity, honour, and inheritance of God's children.

## Power to Become — Legal Family Status

John's word is *exousia* — power, right, authority. Receiving Christ gave you the legal right to become God's children. This is **Sonship** — the legal and relational status of being God's child in Christ, with a Father's love, a family name, and an inheritance. Sonship is not a metaphor for feeling close to God; it is a covenantal reality established by faith in His name.

Notice the condition: "even to them that **believe on his name**." The name of Jesus — His person, work, and authority — is the doorway into the family. You did not earn the name by performance. You received it by believing on the Name. And John immediately adds in verse 13 that this birth is "not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." The family name is God's gift, not man's achievement.

Think of a marriage certificate that changes a surname. The person does not merely act like a spouse; legally, she carries the family name, with all its rights and responsibilities. Your new birth did something greater: it gave you God's family name in Christ. Wherever you go — home, work, market, hospital — you carry that name. It opens doors that talent cannot open and closes doors that shame once opened.

### The Honour and Inheritance of the Name

A family name carries honour. To bear God's name means your conduct reflects His character: truth, love, integrity, and mercy. What could be more worthy of your days than representing a Father whose name is love itself? It also carries inheritance. **The Inheritance of the Believer** — the covenantal estate that belongs to every child of God in Christ — includes forgiveness, acceptance, sonship, supply, and eternal life. Romans 8:17 calls you "heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ." Heirs do not beg for scraps; they receive an estate.

This is **the New Creation** — the brand-new spiritual person God made in Christ — now bearing the family name. The new creation is not an anonymous convert; it is a named child. Heaven knows you by your new name in Christ, not merely by your old record.

Here the two diverge. The stranger visits the house; the child carries the name. The stranger hopes to be tolerated; the child knows he belongs. The stranger protects his reputation; the child protects the family honour. Same house. Entirely different standing.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **1 John 3:1** — "Behold, what manner of love the Father hath bestowed upon us, that we should be called the sons of God: and so we are." John moves from called to actual: called God's children — and so you are. The name is present reality, not future hope.
- **Galatians 3:26** — "For ye are all the children of God by faith in Christ Jesus." Paul confirms the family status is by faith in Christ: all who believe carry the name. No one is a second-class child in Christ.
- **Ephesians 3:15** — "Of whom the whole family in heaven and earth is named." Paul says the whole family — in heaven and on earth — is named from the Father. Your family name is heaven's name.

Three witnesses, one name: you carry the name of God's family in Christ.

## What This Means for You

**1. Live with family honour.** Bearing God's name means your speech, work, and relationships reflect His character. Honour is not perfectionism; it is consistency — truth in small things, love in hard moments, integrity when no one watches.

**2. Receive your inheritance with confidence.** Heirs do not approach the Father as beggars. You approach Him as a child with an estate: forgiven, accepted, supplied, and secure in Christ. Prayer becomes family conversation, not desperate negotiation.

**3. Refuse old names that contradict the new one.** Old labels — failure, outsider, unworthy, forgotten — belonged to the old household. Your new name in Christ outranks them. When old labels speak, answer with John 1:12 and 1 John 3:1.

**4. Faith and wisdom honour the name daily.** Faith declares you God's child in Christ; wisdom shows it in diligent work, honest speech, kept promises, and generous living. Declaring the family name never excuses carelessness; it motivates careful, honourable conduct.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read John 1:12–13 aloud and mark the family words.** Received, power, sons of God, believe on his name, born of God — then thank your Father for the name you now carry.
- **Answer one old label with your new name.** When shame or accusation speaks this week, answer with 1 John 3:1: called God's children — and so you are. Then walk in the honour of that name.
- **Honour the name in one hidden place.** Choose a private decision this week — money, speech, or work — and act explicitly as a bearer of God's family name, even where no one will applaud.
- **Pray as an heir this week.** Bring one need to your Father as a child with an inheritance, thanking Him for supply in Christ while asking for wisdom to steward it well.
