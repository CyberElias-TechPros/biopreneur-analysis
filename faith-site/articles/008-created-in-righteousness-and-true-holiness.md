Why does the word *holiness* strike so many people as rigid, joyless, and slightly artificial — like wearing a stiff borrowed coat two sizes too small? Whenever holiness is preached apart from the New Birth, that is exactly what it becomes: external pressure trying to force an unchanged heart to imitate God's manners. People can keep up a religious performance for a few weeks, yet sooner or later exhaustion pulls off the mask.

Ephesians 4:24 turns that exhausting picture upside down: "And that ye put on the new man, which **after God is created in righteousness and true holiness**." Look at the order of verbs! God *created* the new man first; you *put on* what He already created. Here is the central truth of this teaching: **holiness is not an unnatural costume forced onto an unholy person — it is wearing in visible everyday conduct the New Creation whom God fashioned after His own likeness.**

## "After God" and "Holiness of the Truth"

Examine the precision of Paul's language in Ephesians 4:22–24. Two phrases carry startling weight. First, the new man is created *kata Theon* — literally, **according to God**, patterned after God's own moral likeness, echoing Genesis 1:26 on a higher, resurrection plane. Second, the phrase translated "true holiness" is literally **holiness of the truth** (*hosioteti tes aletheias*), standing in direct contrast to verse 22, where the old man was corrupted "according to the **deceitful lusts**" — literally, *lusts of deceit*.

Do you see that razor-sharp contrast? Sin always operates by *deceit*: it promises satisfaction and delivers emptiness, promises freedom and builds a cage, promises shortcut gain and ruins trust. True holiness, on the other hand, springs directly out of *the truth* — seeing God, yourself, and your neighbour as they really are in the light of Christ!

Picture a craftsman redeemed out of debt and adopted into a royal household. Clean, custom-tailored garments suited to his new station hang ready in his bedroom every morning. Yet if he wakes up half-asleep and pulls yesterday's grease-stained workshop rags back over his shoulders out of sheer habit, everyone on the street will see rags even though a royal wardrobe hangs five feet behind him. Notice that when the household steward says, "Take off those rags and put on your clean garments," the steward is not telling him to spin wool or weave cloth! You can only *put on* (*enduo*) a garment that already exists.

Between "put off the old man" (verse 22) and "put on the new man" (verse 24), Paul places the hinge of the entire passage in verse 23: "And **be renewed in the spirit of your mind**." **Renewing of the Mind** — replacing old, deceit-trained patterns of thought with Scripture's truth — is the daily dressing room where inward creation becomes outward character.

### How the Rest of Scripture Unpacks This Wardrobe

- **Ephesians 4:25–32** — Immediately after saying "put on the new man," Paul shows what that wardrobe looks like on Tuesday afternoon: putting away lying to speak truth, refusing to let anger harden overnight, working diligently with your hands so you have something to give to those in need, speaking words that minister grace, and forgiving one another "even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you." True holiness is wonderfully down-to-earth!
- **Colossians 3:9–10, 12** — Seeing you have put off the old man with his deeds and "have put on the new man, which is renewed in knowledge **after the image of him that created him**… Put on therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, meekness, longsuffering." Every virtue you wear in relationships flows from the image of the Creator stamped on your reborn spirit.
- **Titus 2:11–12** — "For the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men, **Teaching us** that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly, in this present world." Legalism threatens; grace *teaches* — training you from the inside out to love sobriety, fairness, and godliness right here in the present world.

## What This Means for You

**1. Righteousness and holiness belong together.** In Ephesians 4:24, righteousness governs uprightness in your standing and dealings, while true holiness governs purity of devotion and separation unto God. Together they reflect the Father's character in **the New Creation** — the believer as a brand-new spiritual person in Christ.

**2. Expose temptation's lie before fighting its pull.** Because old desires run on *deceit* (verse 22), the fastest way to disarm a temptation is not white-knuckle willpower alone; it is turning on the light of truth. Ask plainly: "What is this shortcut promising me that only Christ actually gives, and what hidden bill will it leave behind?" Truth starves deceit.

**3. Walk in Faith and Wisdom across practical life.** **Faith and Wisdom** — the biblical partnership between wholehearted trust in God's grace and responsible daily action — shine clearly in Ephesians 4:28–29. Spiritual people do not neglect honest work, budgets, healthy boundaries, or courteous speech; they bring the new man into the office, the kitchen, and traffic.

**4. Measure holiness by love and truth, never by coldness.** Counterfeit holiness makes people stiff, critical, and unreachable. Truth-born holiness makes you honest, warm, diligent, self-controlled, and quick to forgive — just like Jesus.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Practise the Ephesians 4 "put off / renew / put on" triad on one habit.** Pick one concrete area this week — reactive irritation, procrastination, or careless speech. Name the deceit behind the old habit, read the matching verse in Ephesians 4:25–32 aloud, and deliberately practise the opposite grace today.
- **Check the sunset rule on unresolved anger.** Ephesians 4:26–27 gives a remarkably practical boundary: "let not the sun go down upon your wrath." Settle misunderstandings before bedtime rather than carrying resentment into tomorrow.
- **Bring your work under the new man's ethic.** Approach your job, studies, or business today not merely to pay your own bills, but with diligent excellence so you become a channel of generosity to others (Ephesians 4:28).
- **Speak one deliberate sentence that builds up.** Before ending a conversation with a spouse, child, or colleague today, ask whether your words imparted grace to the hearer (Ephesians 4:29).
