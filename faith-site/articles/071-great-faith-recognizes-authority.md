Have you ever noticed that Jesus was amazed by faith only twice — and both times it belonged to a foreigner? The first was a Roman centurion, a man with no religious pedigree at all.

Matthew 8:8 records his words: "Lord, I am not worthy that thou shouldest come under my roof: but **speak the word only**, and my servant shall be healed." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **great faith is the settled recognition that Jesus has authority.**

## Authority Understood From Experience

The centurion explained his reasoning: "For I am a man under authority, having soldiers under me: and I say to this man, Go, and he goeth; and to another, Come, and he cometh" (Matthew 8:9). He understood chain of command. He knew that a word carries weight when the speaker has the right to speak it.

**The Authority of the Believer** — the God-given right and ability, in Christ, to stand and act in His name — begins with this recognition. **The Life of Faith** — a life ordered by God's declarations — rests on the same logic. **The Name of Jesus** — His person, work, and authority — is where the chain of command terminates.

Jesus marvelled and said, "Verily I say unto you, I have not found so great faith, no, not in Israel" (Matthew 8:10). The man's theology was simple and his confidence was total: distance is no obstacle to authority.

Think of a general's written order. The order travels far from the general's presence, yet it carries his authority because the authority belongs to the office, not the location. Jesus' word works the same way — it does not need to be physically near to be effective.

Luke's account adds a detail Matthew leaves out. The centurion first sent Jewish elders to plead for him, and they argued that he deserved a hearing: "he loveth our nation, and he hath built us a synagogue" (Luke 7:5). Then, as Jesus drew near, the centurion sent friends with a different argument entirely: "I am not worthy." The first message rested on merit; the second rested on authority. Notice which one drew the commendation.

That shift is the whole journey of faith in miniature. We begin by presenting our record, our service, our sincerity. Then we discover that the ground we actually stand on is not our worthiness but His word. What has been your argument before God lately — your record, or His word?

### Why Great Faith Looks Small

The centurion did not ask for a sign, a sensation, or a personal visit. He asked for a word. That is what made his faith great: it needed very little from Jesus because it expected very much from His authority.

Contrast that with the demand for evidence that so often passes for devotion. "If I could just feel something," we say, or "If God would show me a sign." The centurion had understood something we easily forget: the word of a person in authority is already the sign.

The difference is this: The small-faith believer asks for a spectacle and grows anxious without one. The great-faith believer asks for a word and rests because the word is enough.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **Matthew 28:18** — "All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth." Jesus states the authority plainly, and everything the centurion inferred is confirmed.
- **Philippians 2:9–10** — "God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name: That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow." The name's authority is universal and final.
- **Ephesians 1:22** — "And hath put all things under his feet, and gave him to be the head over all things to the church." Authority is not shared with rivals; it is comprehensive.

Three witnesses, one authority: all power given, a name above every name, and all things under His feet.

## What This Means for You

**1. Settle who is in charge.** Much of your anxiety is a quiet dispute about authority. Reaffirm daily that Jesus holds all authority, and let your prayers rest on that.

**2. Ask for a word rather than a sign.** Bring your need to the word of God and stand on what He has said. The word is the sign.

**3. Speak from authority, not desperation.** When you pray in Jesus' name, you are not begging a distant official; you are appealing to a settled authority.

**4. Faith and wisdom recognise and act.** Faith honours authority; wisdom acts within it — including submission to legitimate human authority and responsible use of what has been delegated. Declaring authority never justifies presumption; it produces ordered confidence.

One more thing deserves attention. When Jesus said, "Go thy way; and as thou hast believed, so be it done unto thee," the servant was healed in the selfsame hour (Matthew 8:13). The healing followed the faith, and the faith rested on authority. Nothing in the story suggests that the centurion manufactured certainty by willpower. He simply reasoned correctly about who Jesus was, and his confidence followed his conclusion.

That is a great mercy for anyone who has ever felt their faith was too thin. You do not have to work up a feeling large enough to impress heaven. You have to think clearly about the One you are addressing, and let the reasoning do its work.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Matthew 8:5–13 slowly.** Note the centurion's reasoning and Jesus' response, then write what you would have asked for in his place.
- **Pray "speak the word only" over one need.** Bring a specific situation to Jesus and ask for His word rather than a sign.
- **Examine one area of dispute.** Notice where you have been arguing with authority — in prayer, in obedience, in submission — and settle it.
- **Use the name deliberately.** In prayer this week, name Jesus specifically and consciously, remembering whose authority you are invoking.
