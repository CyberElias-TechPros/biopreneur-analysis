Nearly every prayer we have ever been taught has a request in it. And there is a prayer, recorded in Acts, that has none at all — and it changed the map of the first-century world.

"As they ministered to the Lord, and fasted, the Holy Ghost said, Separate me Barnabas and Saul for the work whereunto I have called them" (Acts 13:2).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **worship is the prayer that has stopped asking for anything, because it has found something more interesting than its own needs — and it is exactly where God tends to give direction.**

## The Ministry That Asks for Nothing

### A Word With a Job Description

"Ministered" is *leitourgeō* — from which we get "liturgy." In secular Greek it described a public service performed at one's own expense: a wealthy citizen funding a chorus or a games.

It is the word the Greek Old Testament uses for the work of the priests in the tabernacle. And here in Acts, it is applied to prophets and teachers in a church in Antioch, "ministering to the Lord."

Notice what the word implies about cost. This is not the passive enjoyment of a nice atmosphere. It is service rendered in the presence of God, and it is rendered *to Him* — not staged for the congregation.

**That is the difference between worship and atmosphere. Atmosphere is something you receive; ministry to the Lord is something you give, and it has a direction.**

### To Whom, Not For Whom

The preposition is the whole teaching. They ministered *to the Lord*.

Very little of our corporate prayer life is directed that way. We pray *for* people, *about* situations, *against* obstacles, and *for* ourselves. All of that is legitimate — this series has spent many days on it. But it is all prayer with an object in view other than God Himself.

And that is why the hour can be so tiring. Every request has a stake attached, and a stake produces anxiety. Worship has no stake. It has only a Person.

**Worship is the only kind of prayer in which you cannot be disappointed in the outcome, because the outcome is Him and He is already here.**

### The List Is Not Cancelled

Let me be careful here, because this teaching is easily turned into a false spirituality that despises asking.

The Lord's Prayer contains petitions, and Jesus taught it as a model. Paul asked for things repeatedly. Blind Bartimaeus shouted a request and got his sight. Asking is not immature; asking is a child's privilege and God invites it.

What worship does is not cancel the list. It **reorders the subject**. You still bring the needs — you simply stop arriving with them as your entire identity.

Think of the difference between a child who enters a room only to ask for money, and one who climbs onto the lap just to be there. Both are welcome. Only one of them knows the father.

### The Silence That Feels Like Failure

Practical honesty, because this is where most people quit.

The first time you try to pray without asking, it feels like a waste. Ten minutes of saying "You are good" with no request attached, and the mind starts casting about for something useful to do.

That discomfort is diagnostic. Something in you has learned to treat God as a means, and the silence is showing you how thoroughly.

The remedy is not to force feeling. It is to keep showing up with nothing. **Maturity in prayer is not measured by how much you have to say; it is measured by how long you can stay without needing to say it.**

### David's One Thing

The psalms are full of this posture, and David states it as a deliberate narrowing.

"One thing have I desired of the LORD, that will I seek after; that I may dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the LORD, and to enquire in his temple" (Psalm 27:4).

Read the content of the one thing: to dwell, to behold, and to enquire. Not to be delivered, provided for, or vindicated — those requests fill many other psalms and are not disowned here.

And notice the order. Beholding comes before enquiring. **He looks first, and only then does he ask.** That is the sequence worship restores, and it is why so many of our enquiries feel like guessing: we skipped the beholding.

The same man wrote it from a wilderness: "O God, thou art my God; early will I seek thee: my soul thirsteth for thee... my soul shall be satisfied as with marrow and fatness; and my mouth shall praise thee with joyful lips" (Psalm 63:1, 5).

Satisfied with what? Not with the answer to a request. With Him.

### The Elders' Reason

The book of Revelation gives the same posture at its most concentrated, in the song sung before the throne.

"Thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honour and power: for thou hast created all things, and for thy pleasure they are and were created" (Revelation 4:11).

Look at the logic of the reason. Not "because You answered us." Not "because You delivered us." **Because You made us, and because You wanted to.**

That is worship's ground, and it is unshakeable precisely because nothing can happen to you that alters it. You can lose the job, the health, and the argument, and He is still worthy by the same reason He was worthy this morning.

### Where Direction Comes From

So why does the article keep coming back to Acts 13:2?

Because of what happened next, and how it happened. The church in Antioch had five named leaders with different backgrounds — Barnabas, Simeon called Niger, Lucius of Cyrene, Manaen who had been raised with Herod the tetrarch, and Saul. They were ministering and fasting.

And the Holy Ghost spoke. Not during a strategy meeting. Not during a needs assessment. Not while they were discussing the shortage of workers.

They were not asking where to send anyone. They were occupied with Him, and the direction interrupted the worship.

**When you make God the subject, the assignment becomes obvious.** That is not a formula, and it does not work as one — but it is the pattern Scripture keeps showing. Cornelius was praying when an angel came (Acts 10:3). Peter was praying on a rooftop when the vision fell (Acts 10:9). The church at Antioch was worshipping when the Spirit spoke.

### The Honest Boundary

Two things need saying, because this can be turned into a discouraging rule.

First, not every worship time produces a word. Most will not, and that is not a deficiency. The point of ministry to the Lord is ministry to the Lord; if you begin worshipping in order to extract direction, you have quietly turned it back into a request and lost the thing itself.

Second, **The Presence of God** is not a technique for a better mood. Some of the most real worship happens in a flat emotional register with nothing to show for it — and the absence of sensation is not evidence of absence. Worship that depends on goosebumps will be as reliable as your circumstances.

And practical wisdom still applies: worship is not a substitute for the counsel you owe your family, the doctor's appointment you have postponed, or the work you have been avoiding. **Faith and wisdom work together** — the altar does not cancel the ordinary duty, and a man who stays in worship to avoid his responsibilities has found a very religious hiding place.

## What This Means for You

**1. Set aside a requestless time.** Ten minutes. Not a technique for getting answers; a place to stand.

**2. Say what He is.** Both belong, but the second is easier and the first is the one that steadies you when the second stops.

**3. Watch where your mind goes when there is nothing to ask for.** That reveals what you have been treating God as. It is uncomfortable and it is useful.

**4. Stay in communion, not performance.** The Spirit is not an audience for your language. He is the One in whom this fellowship happens.

**5. Let the Kingdom set the scale.** You are not the centre of the story; the Kingdom is. Worship is simply the moment you stop pretending otherwise.
