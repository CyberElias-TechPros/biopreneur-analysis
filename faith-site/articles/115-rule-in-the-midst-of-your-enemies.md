Psalm 110 is the most quoted Old Testament passage in the New Testament, and its second verse contains a detail that has been quietly ignored for centuries.

"The LORD shall send the rod of thy strength out of Zion: rule thou in the midst of thine enemies."

Not after the enemies. In the midst of them.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **God's pattern is not to remove the opposition before giving you the throne; it is to establish your rule in the middle of the opposition, so that the enemies become the setting rather than the obstacle.**

## The Order Nobody Wants

We would prefer a different sequence, and it is worth admitting it plainly. We would like the enemies dealt with first, then the throne, then the reign. Clean up the situation, remove the difficult people, settle the finances, resolve the diagnosis — and then, in the quiet, begin to reign.

God's order is the reverse, and it is consistent through Scripture. Israel was given the land while it was still occupied. David was anointed king while Saul was still on the throne and hunting him. The disciples were given authority while the opposition was still active, and they were told to expect it.

The reason is not that God enjoys difficulty. It is that a reign exercised in a quiet room is not a reign; it is a preference. Where exactly would the throne be tested if the room were empty? The throne of a believer is displayed precisely where there is something to be ruled over.

**The Kingdom of God** — His rule, established and advancing — is not a territory you escape to. It is an authority that travels with you into contested ground. **The Authority of the Believer** — the right to stand in His name — is the rod of strength in the verse. **Spiritual Warfare** — the conflict over what you will believe and say — is the environment in which the rod is held.

Consider a lighthouse. Its whole purpose is the rocks. A lighthouse on a quiet harbour wall would be a decoration. The light is not diminished by the danger around it; the danger is why the light exists. So what does it tell you about the enemies you would prefer removed?

### The Rod, Not the Sceptre

The word "rod" here is worth attention. It is not the golden sceptre of a palace ceremony; it is the staff of a ruler's strength — the same word family that gives us the shepherd's rod in Psalm 23.

That is a strange thing to send out of Zion as a symbol of rule. A rod is what a shepherd uses to guide, defend, and sometimes discipline. It is a working tool, and it is not impressive to look at.

The message is that the reign God establishes is not a display. It is a function. A shepherd with a rod is doing something, not posing. So the believer's rule over their circumstances is not a throne room performance; it is the daily work of guiding, guarding, and correcting what has been entrusted to them.

### Reigning Is Present Tense

Paul uses this language in a way that should stop us: "For if by one man's offence death reigned by one; much more they which receive abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness shall reign in life by one, Jesus Christ" (Romans 5:17).

Reign in life. Not after life. Not in the next age. In this one, by Christ.

And notice what qualifies a person to reign in that verse: receiving. Not achieving, not earning, not proving. The reign belongs to those who receive abundance of grace and the gift of righteousness. Which means the crown is not the reward of the strong; it is the inheritance of the accepted.

That reorders everything. A believer who is trying to earn the right to reign will spend their life waiting for permission they already have.

### Where the Enemies Actually Are

Here is a pastoral observation that the text supports. The enemies that most often prevent believers from reigning are not principally external.

They are internal: a verdict of unworthiness that has never been answered, a fear that has been obeyed so long it feels like personality, a bitterness that has been nursed into a resident, an appetite that has been fed daily. These are enemies in the midst, and they do not leave because you change your job.

"They shall reign in life" is not a promise about your circumstances being pleasant. It is a promise that the person in the middle of the circumstances is no longer ruled by them.

Set them side by side and the gap shows. One believer waits for the enemies to be removed before he will live like a son — and he waits his whole life, because there is always another enemy. The other believer, surrounded by the same enemies, begins to reign in the middle of them, and the scene changes not because the opposition vanished but because the government did.

### The Vindication Is Not Yours to Stage

One more thing needs to be said, because this psalm contains a warning for anyone who loves authority too much.

Psalm 110 does not tell the king to destroy his enemies. It tells him to rule while they are present. The destruction is God's business: verse 5 says "The Lord at thy right hand shall strike through kings in the day of his wrath."

There is an entire industry of Christian leadership that has reversed this and appointed itself the striking arm. That is not the psalm. The believer holds a rod; God holds the sword.

Which means the shape of your reign looks like character under pressure, not conquest. Joseph did not overthrow Potiphar, the jailer, or Pharaoh. He governed faithfully in every place he was placed, and God moved him. Daniel did not organise a coup against Babylon. He served with such integrity that the only charge that could be brought against him was his prayer life (Daniel 6:4–5).

That is what ruling in the midst of enemies looks like. Not triumphant over them yet; unmolested by them.

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop waiting for the right conditions.** The reign is not scheduled for when things calm down. If you wait for quiet, you will not rule; you will postpone.

**2. Identify your actual "midst."** Name the enemies that surround you right now — the external pressure and the internal pattern. The psalm is not an abstraction; it is a charge given to you in a specific place.

**3. Rule your own spirit first.** "He that is slow to anger is better than the mighty; and he that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh a city" (Proverbs 16:32). The proverb locates the harder conquest indoors.

**4. Govern what has been assigned to you.** Your household, your work, the responsibilities in your hand. Faithfulness in a small assigned sphere is the apprenticeship for anything larger.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom hold the rod together.** Faith says the throne has been given; wisdom says a ruler does not spend their authority on the wrong battle. Faith and wisdom work together: faith refuses to abdicate, wisdom refuses to occupy territory that was never assigned. The rod is for guiding your sphere, not for beating everyone outside it.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Psalm 110 in full** and mark every action God does versus every action the king does.
- **Write down your current "midst"** — three things you would have to have removed before you could feel like a son.
- **Take one act of authority over one of them this week** — a decision, an apology, a refusal, a step.
- **Read Daniel 6:1–10** and note what his enemies could not find against him.
