Jesus chose an accounting word for sin, and He chose it in the prayer He taught His disciples to pray every day.

"And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors" (Matthew 6:12).

The Greek is *opheilēma* — that which is owed. Not a stain, not a sickness, not a mistake. A debt, with a creditor, an amount, and a due date.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **guilt is a debt, and a paid debt cannot be paid twice — which is why so much of what we call repentance is really an instalment sent to a creditor who has already closed the account.**

## What the Word Debt Means

### Why Scripture Chooses It

Debt is the most precise metaphor available, because it holds two things that shame cannot hold together: a real obligation and a possible settlement.

A stain can only be cleaned. A debt can be **paid** — by someone else, in full, and legally.

The moment sin is called a debt, the cross becomes the only sensible ending to the story.

### Remission Means Cancellation

The word behind forgiveness here is *aphiēmi* — to send away, to let go, to remit. It is the word used of cancelling a debt and of releasing a prisoner.

It does not describe a discount, a restructuring, or a new payment plan.

Remission is not the reduction of the amount owed. It is the removal of the obligation.

### The Record Was Nailed

Paul describes the transaction in the same commercial terms: "Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us... nailing it to his cross" (Colossians 2:14).

*Handwriting* is *cheirographon* — a handwritten IOU, a signed acknowledgement of debt.

**The document that proved you owed it was posted, publicly, on the instrument of your release.**

### What God Does With the Amount

Psalm 103 is unembarrassed about the arithmetic: "As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us" (Psalm 103:12).

And Romans 4:8 adds the accounting entry: "Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin."

Not imputed means the charge never reaches your account. It is not written off later; it is not written at all.

### The Instalments We Keep Paying

### Guilt and Guilt-Feelings

Here is where most believers live, and it is worth naming exactly.

Objective guilt is a fact about your account. Guilt-*feelings* are a fact about your nervous system. After the debt is paid, the feelings can continue sending invoices.

Feelings are a poor auditor. They report a balance that no longer exists.

### Penance, Rumination, and Self-Punishment

Three familiar instalments, and all three feel like devotion.

*Penance* is doing something extra to make up for it. *Rumination* is thinking about it repeatedly, which masquerades as being serious. *Self-punishment* is refusing good things, which masquerades as humility.

None of these is repentance. All of them are payments to a closed account.

### The Creditor Who Never Closes the File

And notice who benefits from the arrears. Not God — He cancelled the debt. Not you — you are the one paying.

Only the accuser gains anything from a believer who keeps paying interest on a principal that was settled at Calvary.

**A Christian who lives in debt to God is funding the prosecution's favourite story about Him.**

### The Clause We Forget

### Forgive Us Our Debts, As...

The prayer has a second half, and it is the part that explains why so many believers feel stuck.

The petition is not "forgive my debts" in isolation. It is *as we forgive our debtors* — a comparison woven into the request itself.

**You are praying, every time you say it, for your own release to be measured by the release you have given.**

### The Two Debtors

Jesus told a story that makes the mechanism visible: two men owed a creditor, one five hundred pence and one fifty, and "when they had nothing to pay, he frankly forgave them both" (Luke 7:42).

Then the question, and the answer: which will love him most? The one forgiven most.

**The size of your gratitude is set by the size of the cancellation you have actually believed in.**

### The Unforgiving Servant's Real Error

And the harder parable follows close behind: a servant forgiven an enormous debt, who then throttled a man over a small one (Matthew 18:23–35).

His error was not that he forgot the amount. It was that he never believed it had been remitted — because a man who believes his own debt is cancelled does not need to collect from anyone else.

**Refusing to release others is usually evidence that you have not yet received your own release.**

### What a Paid Debt Asks of You

Not repayment. Acknowledgement.

The only fitting response to a cancelled debt is to stop behaving like a debtor — and to become the kind of person who cancels.

**Freedom is not a reward for good behaviour. It is the correct response to a settled account.**

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, the debt is paid, but the harvest is still real. David was forgiven and still reaped (2 Samuel 12:13–14). Remission removes the penalty; it does not reverse every consequence in time.

Second, "the debt is paid" is not a licence to add to it. Paul's answer to that instinct is blunt: "God forbid" (Romans 6:2). A cancelled debtor who returns to the same lender has not understood the cancellation.

**The **The Blood of Jesus** settles the account; **Righteousness** is what it looks like to live as though it is settled.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Name the instalment.** Which of the three — penance, rumination, self-punishment — is yours?

**2. Check the balance sheet, not the mood.** When the feeling says you owe, read Romans 4:8 out loud.

**3. Cut rumination short.** Set a limit, and when it is reached, say the verdict.

**4. Forgive one person this week.** Not as a favour to them; as the second half of your own prayer.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Where restitution is possible and owed to a person, make it (Luke 19:8). **Justification** settles what you owe God; wisdom settles what you owe people.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Matthew 6:9–15** and stop at verse 12.
- **Read Luke 7:36–50** and count the creditor's words.
- **Write down what you think you still owe God.** Then write Colossians 2:14 under it.
- **Name one person you have not released.**
- **Pray the Lord's Prayer slowly**, and mean the second half.
