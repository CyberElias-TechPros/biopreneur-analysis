There is a version of Christian teaching that treats planning as unbelief — as though a person with real faith would never open a spreadsheet. But is that what Scripture teaches?

James is not that teacher. Read his sentence carefully and the target is not the calendar. It is the confidence.

"Go to now, ye that say, To day or to morrow we will go into such a city, and continue there a year, and buy and sell, and get gain" (James 4:13).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **James is not against a calendar; he is against a calendar with no room in it for God — the sin he names is presumption, not planning.**

## What James Actually Objects To

### Not the Journey, the Certainty

The merchants in view were doing something entirely legitimate: a one-year trading trip to a named city, with a business model and a projected profit.

James does not call the plan wicked. He calls the *boasting* wicked: "But now ye rejoice in your boastings: all such rejoicing is evil" (James 4:16).

**The offence is not the itinerary. It is the tone in which the itinerary is spoken.**

### The Missing Clause

And he names the correction precisely: "For that ye ought to say, If the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that" (James 4:15).

Notice that this is not a formula to be recited. It is a posture to be held — the difference between a plan and a presumption is whether the plan has a place in it where God could say no.

**A goal that cannot be surrendered is not a plan. It is a demand with a timeline.**

### The Fragility He Points At

The reason for the clause is not religious etiquette. It is physiology: "whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapour, that appeareth for a little time, and then vanisheth away" (James 4:14).

The *vapour* image is deliberately unflattering. Not a flame, not a river — steam off a kettle, present and then not.

**James is not asking you to doubt your future. He is asking you to notice how short your lease on it is.**

### Why Planning Is Still Commanded

#### Wisdom Counts

Jesus told two parables in one breath that assume careful planning: a builder who sits down and counts the cost, and a king who assesses the opposing army before he marches (Luke 14:28–31).

He did not criticise the counting. He used it as an illustration of seriousness.

**Counting the cost is obedience, not doubt.**

#### The Ant

Proverbs sends the lazy man to the ant, "which having no guide, overseer, or ruler, provideth her meat in the summer, and gathereth her food in the harvest" (Proverbs 6:7–8).

An ant is a planner. Scripture treats foresight as a virtue so ordinary that it can be learned from an insect.

#### The Paradox Stated Plainly

Proverbs holds both halves in one verse: "A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps" (Proverbs 16:9).

The heart devises — that is your job. The steps are directed — that is His. Neither half is cancelled by the other.

**The **Faith and Wisdom** that Scripture commends does not choose between planning and trust; it plans with its hand open.**

### How a Plan Stays Under Grace

#### Write It Down, Then Hold It Lightly

A plan you have not written is a wish. A plan you have written and cannot revise is an idol.

Write the goal, the number, and the date. Then write the clause beneath it: *if the Lord wills.*

#### Pray Before the Spreadsheet

Most of us pray after the decision, asking God to bless what we have already settled. The order matters.

"Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established" (Proverbs 16:3). The establishing follows the committing.

**Prayer is not the dedication of a finished plan. It is part of the drafting.**

#### Build In a Review

The one-year trip is a real period. A year is long enough to see fruit and short enough to change course.

**Goals under grace have review dates, because the Lord who directs steps is also the Lord who redirects them.**

#### Expect the Detour

Paul planned to go to Asia and was forbidden, then redirected to Macedonia by a vision (Acts 16:6–10). The plan was not wrong; it was simply not final.

A surrendered plan is the only kind that can be redirected without being destroyed.

#### Distinguish the Goal From the Way

The goal — to be faithful, to provide, to give — is usually stable. The way is usually negotiable.

Hold the purpose tightly and the method loosely, and most of the friction disappears.

### The Guardrails

Two errors sit on either side of this teaching.

The first is presumption: plan as though God were a formality, then be destroyed when the plan fails and call it a spiritual attack. That is what James names as evil — not the plan, but the boasting.

The second is paralysis: refuse to plan at all, call it faith, and then blame God for the lack of provision. That is not faith either; it is the servant who buried the talent and called it caution (Matthew 25:25).

**The **The Life of Faith** is not a life without a plan. It is a life whose plan belongs to Someone else.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Name the goal.** Write it in numbers and dates.

**2. Add the clause.** Not as superstition — as a real transfer.

**3. Pray first, not after.** Before the decision, not only over it.

**4. Set a review date.** A quarter is enough.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Count the cost *and* submit the count. **The Word of God** gives you both the ant and the vapour, and expects you to live with both in view.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read James 4:13–17** and underline every verb the merchants used.
- **Write your plan for the next twelve months** in three lines.
- **Add the clause** in your own words beneath it.
- **Read Proverbs 16:1–9** and mark who does what.
- **Read Acts 16:6–10** and note how quickly the plan changed.
