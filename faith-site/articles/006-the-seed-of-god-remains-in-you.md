Have you ever paused over 1 John 3:9 and wondered how to read it honestly without falling into panic on one side or presumption on the other? Two chapters earlier, the very same apostle wrote, "If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves… If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins" (1 John 1:8–9). Yet in chapter 3 he writes boldly: "Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin; for his seed remaineth in him." How do both statements sit side by side in the same inspired letter without contradiction?

When you look closely at what John is unveiling, anxiety gives way to awe. Here is the central truth of this teaching: **at the New Birth, God planted His own incorruptible seed permanently inside your spirit — an abiding inward nature that never makes peace with sin and continually empowers you to walk in holiness.**

## Reading the Seed and the Tense Rightly

Two Greek expressions unlock the harmony of John's letter. First, the verbs translated "doth not commit sin" and "cannot sin" stand in the Greek *present continuous* aspect — describing settled, habitual practice, a person's chosen trade and native lifestyle. Second, look at the reason John gives: "for **his seed** (*sperma*) **remaineth** (*menei* — abides, stays, takes up permanent residence) in him."

Faithful Bible teachers read this rich verse through two complementary lenses, and this is the understanding of the Word we teach: because **the New Birth** (the Holy Spirit's regeneration of your human spirit) implants **the Divine Nature** — God's own moral and spiritual character — as an abiding seed within your reborn spirit, the new man in Christ cannot make sin his settled home or native element, even while the believer on earth must still renew the mind, mortify fleshly habits, and receive cleansing whenever he stumbles (1 John 1:9; 2:1). That honours both chapters of John's epistle without excusing carelessness or crushing tender consciences!

Think of a small brown acorn pressed into dark soil. On the day it is planted, it does not yet tower over the fence or shade the yard; frost, wind, and weeds still press around it. Yet locked inside that single seed is the complete genetic blueprint of an oak tree. Ask yourself a simple question: under any weather on earth, can that acorn ever grow up into poison ivy or a thornbush? Never. Its seed fixes its kind. Give it water, sunlight, and time, and it can only express what was coded into its origin.

In the exact same way, God did not drop a temporary pep talk into your heart when you trusted Christ; He planted *His seed* — incorruptible, resurrection life — into your spirit, and John declares that this seed **remains** in you. It does not evaporate when a week feels heavy.

### Three Passages Showing What That Seed Produces

- **1 Peter 1:23** — "Being born again, **not of corruptible seed, but of incorruptible**, by the word of God, which liveth and abideth for ever." Everything born of Adam's fallen line carries decay inside its seed. What God planted in you through the gospel is *aphthartos* — imperishable, undying, immune to rot.
- **2 Peter 1:3–4** — Through His exceeding great and precious promises, you become "**partakers of the divine nature**, having escaped the corruption that is in the world through lust." Notice that sharing in God's moral nature never turns a creature into deity; rather, it imparts Christ's likeness and desires so you escape the gravitational pull of corruption.
- **Galatians 5:16–17, 22** — "Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh… the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance." An apple tree does not strain and groan to manufacture apples; healthy roots in watered soil naturally bear the fruit of their seed.

## What This Means for You

**1. Temptation is an intruder, not your landlord.** Before you knew Christ, sin spoke as an owner giving orders to its own house. Now that God's seed remains in you, temptation is an outside trespasser trying to talk a child of God into acting out of character. Knowing that difference gives steel to your refusal.

**2. Cultivate the soil through Renewing of the Mind.** If the seed inside every believer is incorruptible, why do Christians display different levels of visible maturity? Jesus answered that in the Parable of the Sower: stones, thorns, and crowded cares choke outward fruitfulness unless the soil is tended. **Renewing of the Mind** — steadily replacing old thought patterns with Scripture — clears away the weeds so the seed already within your spirit bears thirty, sixty, and a hundredfold.

**3. Never call a besetting struggle your identity.** Modern culture loves to paste permanent labels onto people based on their hardest temptation. Scripture refuses. If you belong to Christ, your deepest identity is "born of God," carrying His abiding seed — while wise accountability, prayer, sound counsel, and disciplined boundaries help you walk free in daily practice.

**4. Expect steady growth, not overnight perfectionism.** Seeds grow organically: blade, then ear, then full corn in the ear (Mark 4:28). Celebrate real progress in patience, honesty, purity, and generosity as evidence of God's seed at work in you.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Water the seed daily with unhurried Scripture.** Choose one paragraph from 1 John 3 or 2 Peter 1 this week and read it aloud each morning, asking the Holy Spirit to turn its truth into today's attitudes.
- **Pull one obvious thorn out of your schedule.** Identify one habit, media diet, or environment that routinely crowds your affection for God, and replace it with something that nourishes peace and clarity.
- **Answer temptation from your nature.** When enticed toward impatience, deceit, or impurity, say quietly and firmly: "I am born of God; His seed remains in me, and that path does not belong to who I am in Christ."
- **Pair prayer with wise practical guardrails.** Faith and wisdom work together: pray boldly for inward strength, and set sensible, honest guardrails around your time, finances, and relationships.
