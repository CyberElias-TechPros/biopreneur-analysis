Most giving happens after the fact. The harvest comes in, the accounts are settled, and something is offered from what is safely in hand.

First fruits work the other way round. They are brought *before* — before the harvest is complete, before the outcome is known, before there is anything to be safe about.

"Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase" (Proverbs 3:9).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **first fruits are not the largest gift but the earliest one, and their whole power lies in being given before anyone knows how the harvest will turn out.**

## What the Practice Was

### A Sheaf Before the Reaping

Leviticus gives the ordinance with unusual precision: "Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When ye be come into the land which I give unto you, and shall reap the harvest thereof, then ye shall bring a sheaf of the firstfruits of your harvest unto the priest" (Leviticus 23:10).

A single sheaf — not the whole field. And it was waved before the Lord "to be accepted for you: on the morrow after the sabbath" (Leviticus 23:11).

**The offering preceded the general harvest.** The rest of the crop was not touched until the first sheaf had been presented.

### Given Before the Barn Was Full

That timing is the theology. The farmer had to release the first sheaf while the field still stood, believing that the rest would come.

**First fruits are an act of faith expressed in agriculture.** They say, in a tangible form, "the harvest is not mine until God has had the first of it."

### The Confession That Came With It

Deuteronomy 26 attaches a spoken declaration to the practice, and it is one of the most beautiful liturgies in the Old Testament.

"And thou shalt speak and say before the LORD thy God, A Syrian ready to perish was my father, and he went down into Egypt, and sojourned there with a few, and became there a nation, great, mighty, and populous" (Deuteronomy 26:5).

Then the basket is set down, and the giver rejoices "in every good thing which the LORD thy God hath given unto thee" (Deuteronomy 26:11).

**The first fruit is not merely a gift. It is a story told with produce: this came from somewhere, and it was not my own hand alone.**

### The Best, Not the Leftover

Malachi names the failure that makes the whole practice pointless: "And if ye offer the blind for sacrifice, is it not evil? and if ye offer the lame and sick, is it not evil? offer it now unto thy governor; will he be pleased with thee?" (Malachi 1:8).

The prophet's argument is devastating in its ordinariness. **If you would not hand it to a human official, why hand it to God?**

### The Pattern Beyond Agriculture

#### The Firstborn

The principle runs through Scripture: the firstborn belonged to God (Exodus 13:2), and the firstborn of Egypt was the final sign of the exodus.

**God has consistently claimed the first as a way of claiming the whole.** The firstborn was a representative; the first sheaf stood for the field.

#### The First Fruit That Is a Person

And then the New Testament makes the image enormous.

"But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept" (1 Corinthians 15:20).

**The resurrection of Jesus is a first sheaf.** It is the first of a harvest that is still coming, and every believer's resurrection is the rest of the field.

That single verse changes how a Christian reads Proverbs 3:9. The practice was always a rehearsal for a Person.

#### The Lump and the Root

Paul uses the same logic for a different purpose: "If the firstfruit be holy, the lump is also holy: and if the root be holy, so are the branches" (Romans 11:16).

**A holy first sanctifies what follows it.** That is the whole promise attached to first fruits, and it is why the practice is not a transaction but a dedication.

#### The Boundary

One honest guardrail. First fruits are not a formula that guarantees that every business venture succeeds or that a gift produces a fixed return.

The point is honour, and honour cannot be purchased. A person who gives the first sheaf in order to force God's hand has misunderstood the practice entirely — and Malachi's whole complaint was against people who treated worship as a transaction with a cheap price.

**Kingdom Stewardship** gives because God is worthy, not because the return is calculated.

## What This Means for You

**1. Give something before you know.** The first income of a new job, the first month of a new year, the first profit of a new venture.

**2. Give the best, not the remainder.** The test is simple: would you give this to someone you respected?

**3. Say the story out loud.** Like Deuteronomy 26, name where you came from. Gratitude given a voice becomes faith.

**4. Let it dedicate the rest.** The promise is that a holy first sanctifies the lump. Give the first, and the rest is consecrated.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Honour God with the first and steward the rest. **Faith and Wisdom** are two hands on the same basket.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Proverbs 3:1–10** and mark every promise attached.
- **Read Leviticus 23:9–14** and note the timing.
- **Read Deuteronomy 26:1–11** and write your own "a Syrian ready to perish" verse.
- **Read 1 Corinthians 15:20–23** and see the firstfruits in a Person.
- **Pick your next "first"** — a bonus, a month, a job — and give from it before anything else.
