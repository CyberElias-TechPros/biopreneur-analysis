God could have delivered Israel without a sign. He chose a sign instead, and then He told them to keep it forever.

"And it shall come to pass, when ye be come to the land... that ye shall keep this service. And it shall come to pass, when your children shall say unto you, What mean ye by this service?" (Exodus 12:25–26).

"Through faith he kept the passover, and the sprinkling of blood, lest he that destroyed the firstborn should touch them" (Hebrews 11:28).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the sign was never a one-night measure but a standing declaration — which means the blood on the door is not a memory of deliverance but the continuing basis of it.**

## A Sign With a Calendar

### Annual, Not Once

And the passover was instituted as an ordinance *for ever* (Exodus 12:14). Every year, the same lamb, the same blood, the same story.

The sign was designed to remain in use.

**God did not give a rescue. He gave a memorial that functioned as a rescue.**

### Children and the Question

And the ritual was built for a question. The whole ceremony was a set-up for a child to ask why.

The answer was not a doctrine. It was a narrative: "it is the sacrifice of the LORD's passover, who passed over the houses of the children of Israel in Egypt" (Exodus 12:27).

**A sign that prompts a question is a sign that teaches.** When did anyone last ask you what your faith means?

### Faith and the Sign

And Hebrews says Moses kept it *by faith* — which means the blood was not a magic potion. It worked through obedient trust.

**The sign did not save anyone who despised it, and it did not fail anyone who trusted it.**

## Why It Still Works

### The Blood Has Not Aged

And the first reason is that the substance it points to does not decay. The shadow was temporary; the reality is eternal.

"What the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God sending his own Son..." (Romans 8:3).

**The sign has not expired, because the thing signified was never temporary.**

### The Slain Lamb Was Foreordained

And Exodus 12 antedates Sinai. It is the earliest feast Israel got, before the law, before the tabernacle, before the priesthood — because it was never about the system that came after it.

It was always about the Lamb.

**The blood was the original arrangement, not a later amendment.**

### The Name Was on the Covering

And the mechanism was not the blood as a substance but the blood as a marker pointing to the One whose covenant it ratified.

The destroyer did not inspect the blood's composition. He read the sign of a covenant.

The mark worked because of whose covenant it announced.

### The Memorial That Still Speaks

### It Speaks of Protection

And the annual reading kept one sentence in front of Israel: from him "[who] shall not suffer the destroyer to come in unto your houses to smite you" (Exodus 12:23).

The sign said: this house belongs to Him.

### It Speaks of Exchange

And the substitution is not hidden. A lamb died. Its blood was the mark. Inside, the family ate and lived.

Someone else died for this house to be safe — and that is the whole gospel in one evening.

### It Speaks of the Lamb

And the New Testament does not develop this dogmatically. It simply names it: "Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our Passover is sacrificed for us" (1 Corinthians 5:7).

Paul did not say Christ is like the passover. He said Christ *is* it.

The feast was never about Egypt. It was about Him.

### Living Under the Sign

### Communion Is the Same Structure

And Jesus took the third cup and made it permanent: "This cup is the new testament in my blood: this do ye, as oft as ye drink it, in remembrance of me" (1 Corinthians 11:25).

*Remembrance* — *anamnēsis*, a living re-presentation, not nostalgia.

He did not remove the memorial. He upgraded it.

### Do Not Sanitise It

And a church that is squeamish about the blood ends up with a gospel that is squeamish about the cross.

The passover was not a tasteful story. It was an evening of killing, blood, and bread eaten with travel clothes on.

Precision about the blood is not gore. It is accuracy.

### The Household Claim

And the sign was on the house. Which means a believer should pray it deliberately: this room, this table, this address belongs to Him and is covered.

**Claim the covering over the building you actually live in.**

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, the sign is not a superstition. It is not a phrase that repels harm between uses. It is faith's expression of trust in Christ's finished work.

Second, the covering is not an exemption from obedience. The Hebrews still had to stay inside and leave in the morning. Faith obeyed; it did not merely believe.

**The **The Blood of Jesus** is the mark; **The Finished Work** is what it announces; and the **The Name of Jesus** is whose covenant it is.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Read Exodus 12:21–28 and mark who was inside the house.**

**2. Pray over your home this week**, room by room.

**3. Tell the story** to whoever will listen — especially children.

**4. Take communion thoughtfully.**

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** A marked house is still your house to keep in order. **The Blood of Jesus** is your security; wisdom keeps the doors, the ledger, and the peace inside.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Exodus 12 once more.**
- **Write down the answer to a child's question** in plain words.
- **Pray over each room** in your house.
- **Read Hebrews 11:23–29.**
- **Read 1 Corinthians 11:23–26** before your next meal.
