John writes as an eyewitness, and the thing he says he saw was not a miracle or a crowd but a glory, and he describes its shape.

"And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **John says *etheasametha* — we beheld, we gazed as spectators — and the glory he describes is the glory of *monogenēs*, the unique one, which means the glory was not a display around Jesus but the character of Jesus Himself, full of grace and truth.**

## The Word for Seeing

### Etheasametha

And the verb is from *theaomai* — to gaze, to view as a spectacle, the root behind our word theatre. It is not a glance; it is sustained looking.

John is saying: we watched, for years, and this is what we concluded.

### Not a Vision

And this was not a trance on a mountain. It was the glory seen in a man eating fish, weeping at a grave, and washing feet.

The incarnation relocated glory from the temple to the table.

### Beheld and Handled

And John's first letter pushes it further: that which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life (1 John 1:1).

The eyewitnesses are staking everything on physical contact.

**This is not a legend that grew. It is a testimony that touched.**

## The Word for Glory

### Doxa

And in the Greek Old Testament, *doxa* translates the Hebrew *kabod* — weight, heaviness, substance. Glory is not shine; it is the weight of who someone is.

Moses asked to see God's glory and got a proclamation of His character: merciful, gracious, longsuffering, abundant in goodness and truth (Exodus 34:6).

### Weight, Not Glitter

And that reframes everything. The glory of Christ is not a halo. It is the sheer substantiality of a person who is exactly what God is.

### Of the Only Begotten

And *monogenēs* means one of a kind, unique — not "manufactured" as the older English might suggest. The same word is used of Isaac, Abraham's unique son (Hebrews 11:17).

The glory John saw was the glory of the only one of His kind.

### The Two Words That Describe It

### Full of Grace

And *charis* — favour, kindness, unearned generosity. When people met Jesus, they kept receiving things they had not earned.

### And Truth

And *alētheia* — reality, the unconcealed. He never softened a hard word to keep a crowd.

### Both Together

And this is the miracle, because most of us can only manage one at a time. Grace without truth is sentimentality; truth without grace is cruelty.

He was full of both, at the same time, in the same conversation.

**His glory looked like grace, and it told the truth.** Which of the two do you tend to drop when you are under pressure?

### Beholding and Becoming

### The Mirror

And Paul draws the practical conclusion: we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory (2 Corinthians 3:18).

Beholding is not passive. It is the mechanism of transformation.

### What You Look At, You Become

And that is a warning as much as a promise. A mind steeped in outrage becomes outraged. A mind fixed on Christ becomes like Christ.

Your feed is a spiritual formation programme, and you chose it.

### The Face of Jesus

And Paul says the light of the knowledge of the glory of God is in the face of Jesus Christ (2 Corinthians 4:6). Not in a concept. In a face.

That is why the Gospels matter. You cannot behold an abstraction.

### Practising the Beholding

### Slow Reading

And the first discipline is unhurried reading of the Gospels. Not for information but for observation — what did He do with His hands, how did He answer that man, where did He look.

### Asking the Right Question

And the second is to stop asking only "what does this mean for me" and start asking "what is He like".

The first question produces a religion. The second produces a relationship.

### Carrying It Into the Day

And the third is to bring one thing you saw into one hour you are about to live.

**Beholding that never reaches your Tuesday has not yet reached you.**

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, beholding is not mysticism that bypasses scripture. The glory is beheld in the gospel record, not in whatever impression arrives unexamined.

Second, transformation is not instant perfection. Faith and wisdom keep growing, keep repenting, keep showing up — and let the change be gradual without being optional.

## What This Means for You

1. Read John 1:1–18 once a day for a week.

2. Read Exodus 33:12–34:7 and note what God called His glory.

3. Ask of one Gospel scene: what is He like here?

4. Change one input this week — a feed, a habit, a voice — that does not show you Christ.

5. **Behold Him, and keep faith and wisdom together.** Contemplation is not escape. Let what you see in Him shape how you work, speak, and spend.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 2 Corinthians 3:7–18.** Note the direction of the change.
- **Read Colossians 1:15–20.** Note how much is claimed for Him.
- **Sit with one verse for ten minutes without a phone.**
- **Write down three things you have seen in Jesus.**
- **Notice what you have been beholding most this month.**
