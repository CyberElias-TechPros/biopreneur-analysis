There is a particular kind of tenant who keeps living in a house after the lease has run out, and the only reason he stays is that nobody has told him to leave.

Colossians says something like that about the powers.

"And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it" (Colossians 2:15).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the enemy's claim on your life expired at the cross, and what remains is not a legal tenure but a habit of occupation — which means the answer is not negotiation but notice.**

## What "Spoiled" Means

### Stripped

And the Greek is *apekdyomai* — to strip off, to divest, as a soldier strips a defeated enemy of his armour.

It is not damage. It is disarmament.

**He was not wounded at the cross. He was undressed.**

### The Triumph

And *thriambeuō* is the Roman triumph — the parade in which a victorious general led his captives through the city for everyone to see.

Paul is saying the powers were marched.

**What happened at Calvary was not a narrow escape. It was a public humiliation with witnesses.**

### The Context

And the surrounding verses say the same thing in legal language. The record was blotted out (verse 14). The debt was nailed. Only then does Paul say the powers were stripped.

The order matters: the record first, then the disarmament.

**The cross did not merely beat him. It removed his grounds.**

## Why He Still Acts Like an Owner

### Habit

And a tenant who has occupied a house for years does not move out because the paperwork changed. He moves out when he is told.

He has no legal standing and enormous practical familiarity.

**He is relying on your ignorance, not on his authority.** Which is a very different problem to have.

### Bluff

And a great deal of what the enemy does is bluff. He claims what he does not own, in a loud voice, hoping nobody checks the deed.

Jesus called him a liar from the beginning (John 8:44), which means his native language is a claim he cannot substantiate.

**A bluff works exactly once per person who never reads the contract.**

### Ignorance

And the church's unbelief is his greatest asset. Hosea's lament is the same problem: "My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge" (Hosea 4:6).

Not lack of power. Lack of knowledge.

**An heir who does not know the will lives like a servant.** So how well do you know yours?

### What the Expiry Means Practically

### No Condemnation

And the first consequence is forensic. There is no case to answer (Romans 8:1).

The accusation may continue. The verdict does not.

**He can still shout. He cannot still sentence.** So why do we still flinch at the shouting?

### No Ownership

And the second is possessive. You are not his, and you were not his at any point in your life — even the years you were serving him were years of trespass.

Your old life was not a legitimate tenancy. It was an occupation.

You were never his property. You were his territory, which is a very different thing.

### No Final Word

And the third is about outcomes. He cannot determine your destiny. He can annoy, delay, and discourage.

The verdict is signed elsewhere.

He has influence. He does not have authority.

### Serving Notice

### Know the Deed

And the first step is to know what the covenant says (Day 294). A person who is unsure of the contract negotiates when he should be asserting.

### Resist

And the second is the plain command: "Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you" (James 4:7).

*Resist* is *anthistēmi* — to stand against, to take a position. It is not a wrestling match. It is a stance.

You do not fight for ground. You stand on it.

### Do Not Negotiate

And the third is to stop bargaining. No believer was ever delivered by making concessions to a squatter.

Deliverance is not a deal.

He has nothing you want and no right to anything you have.

### Fill the House

And the fourth is occupation. Jesus' warning about the swept house is that the empty one gets re-let (Matthew 12:44–45).

Eviction without habitation is a temporary victory.

The best defence against a returning tenant is a landlord who never leaves.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a doctrine of shouting at the devil for every inconvenience. Authority is not volume, and most of life is not a confrontation.

Second, the authority is delegated and never personal. Peter, full of the Spirit, refused to be credited (Acts 3:12).

**The **The Finished Work is the eviction notice; the The Name of Jesus is the authority that serves it; and the The Authority of the Believer is the right to stand in a house that is not yours.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Colossians 2:11–15 slowly. Note the order.

2. Name one area where the enemy acts like an owner.

**3. Say out loud what the covenant says about it.**

**4. Fill the space** with the Word and with worship.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Authority does not replace responsibility. **The Name of Jesus** is your standing; wisdom still pays the rent, keeps the accounts, and does the work.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Colossians 2 eleven to fifteen each morning.**
- **Read Matthew 12:22–30.**
- **Write down the area of occupation.**
- **Serve notice in prayer.**
- **Put something of God in the space** — a verse, a song, a habit.
