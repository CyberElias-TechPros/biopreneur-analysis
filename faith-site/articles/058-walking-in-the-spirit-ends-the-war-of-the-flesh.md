Have you ever resolved to change a habit, succeeded for a while, and then collapsed back into it with more shame than before? Most believers have, and the cycle usually reveals a strategy problem rather than a sincerity problem.

Galatians 5:16 offers a different strategy: "This I say then, **Walk in the Spirit**, and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **walking in the Spirit is not a single victory but a continuous step-by-step dependence.**

## Walk, Not Wrestle

Paul does not say "fight the flesh and you will not fulfil it." He says walk in the Spirit — a metaphor of movement, direction, and companionship. A walk is made of steps, not leaps, and it continues rather than concludes.

**The Life of the Spirit** — the Spirit's own life ordering your thoughts and conduct — is the path. **The Supernatural Life** — God's own life expressed through daily conduct — is where the walk happens. **The Divine Nature** — God's own nature imparted to your spirit — is the life that makes walking possible.

The contrast Paul draws is between two directions, not two equal powers. The flesh and the Spirit are contrary to one another (verse 17), and you choose which to follow in each moment. Walking is a series of choices, not one heroic decision.

Think of a man walking uphill against a strong wind. He does not defeat the wind once and for all; he keeps facing the right direction, step after step, and the wind never determines his destination. The flesh is the wind. The walk is your direction.

### Why Willpower Loses

Paul's own testimony in Romans 7 makes this plain. He describes wanting to do good and failing, delighting in the law of God in the inward man while another law warred in his members. His conclusion is not a better technique but a person: "I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord" (Romans 7:25). The deliverance is relational, which is why a walk is the right metaphor. You are not managing a defect; you are accompanying a Person who is stronger than the defect.

Willpower alone is a finite resource that depletes under pressure. It can restrain behaviour for a while, but it cannot change desire. That is why resolutions produce temporary compliance and lasting frustration.

Paul's alternative is supply rather than suppression. When you walk in the Spirit, the flesh's desires are not fulfilled because a stronger desire is occupying you. You are not merely saying no; you are saying yes to something better.

Have you ever noticed that temptation is strongest when you are emptiest — tired, hungry, lonely, or discouraged? Trace the difference to its root: The willpower believer white-knuckles his way through temptation and eventually breaks. The walking believer is occupied with the Spirit's agenda and finds the temptation losing its grip.

### The War Is Between Two Minds

Paul locates the conflict precisely: "For to be carnally minded is death; but to be spiritually minded is life and peace" (Romans 8:6). The battlefield is not the body and it is not the environment. It is the mind.

That is why his remedy in Romans 13 is not asceticism but attention: "But put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not provision for the flesh, to fulfil the lusts thereof" (Romans 13:14). Provision — the Greek is *pronoia*, forethought. You do not defeat the flesh by fighting it in a moment of crisis; you defeat it by refusing to stock its pantry.

He says the same in Galatians with a different image: "This I say then, Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh" (Galatians 5:16). The promise is stated negatively but the mechanism is positive. The way to stop fulfilling one desire is to be occupied with another.

Peter frames it as a war against the soul itself: "Dearly beloved, I beseech you as strangers and pilgrims, abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against your soul" (1 Peter 2:11). The soul is the casualty, not the culprit.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **Romans 8:6** — "For to be carnally minded is death; but to be spiritually minded is life and peace." Paul locates the battle in the mind's orientation, not merely in behaviour.
- **Romans 13:14** — "But put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not provision for the flesh, to fulfil the lusts thereof." Walking involves removing provision — cutting the supply lines that feed the flesh.
- **1 Peter 2:11** — "Abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against the soul." Peter names the warfare honestly while calling for decisive abstinence from what feeds it.

Three witnesses, one strategy: spiritual-mindedness, no provision, and honest warfare.

## What This Means for You

**1. Choose direction each morning.** A walk begins with a decision. Before the day starts, declare that you are walking with the Spirit and ask for His leading.

**2. Cut provision, not just behaviour.** Identify what feeds the flesh — apps, places, relationships, times of day — and remove the provision. Starve what you cannot control.

**3. Practise the next step rather than the whole staircase.** You do not need to fix your whole life today. Take the next faithful step in the Spirit and let the walk continue.

**4. Faith and wisdom walk and plan.** Faith depends on the Spirit; wisdom builds structures — accountability, boundaries, rest, and honest community — that keep the walk steady. Declaring grace never excuses passivity; it makes deliberate walking possible.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Galatians 5:13–25 in one sitting.** Mark every reference to walking, leading, and the flesh, then note which verse addresses your current struggle most directly.
- **Name one provision to cut.** Identify a specific source that feeds a temptation and take one concrete step to remove it this week.
- **Pray a walking prayer each morning.** Ask the Spirit to direct your steps, and thank Him that His presence breaks the flesh's grip.
- **Tell one trusted person about your walk.** Invite accountability for the specific area you are working on, and check in with them at the end of the week.
