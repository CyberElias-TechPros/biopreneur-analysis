There are two unhelpful ways to talk about debt, and both of them are common in church.

The first treats it as a spiritual failure — evidence of unbelief, a sign that something is wrong with you. The second treats it as totally normal and not worth discussing.

Scripture does neither. It treats debt as a **symptom** worth reading.

"Owe no man any thing, but to love one another: for he that loveth another hath fulfilled the law" (Romans 13:8).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **debt is not primarily a money problem; it is a wisdom problem wearing a money problem's clothes, and treating the symptom without the cause simply moves the debt somewhere else.**

## What Scripture Says About Borrowing

### The Servant Clause

Proverbs is blunt, and it is not a threat — it is an observation: "The rich ruleth over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender" (Proverbs 22:7).

That is not a moral judgment on the poor or a curse on the borrower. It is a description of a relationship. **A borrowed sum creates a standing obligation, and obligations constrain choices.**

Ask anyone who has carried a large loan through a job change and they will describe exactly that: the freedom they thought they had was partly on credit.

### The Instruction to Owe Nothing

Paul's instruction is short and absolute: *owe no man any thing.*

The context is paying what is due — taxes, revenue, respect, honour (Romans 13:7). He is not inventing an economy with no lending; Scripture elsewhere regulates lending and forbids interest on the poor (Exodus 22:25).

What he is doing is setting the direction of a believer's life. **The trajectory is toward owing nothing but love.**

### The Wisdom of the Surety

Proverbs has an entire passage about a particular kind of debt that has wrecked more friendships than any bank loan: going surety for someone else.

"My son, if thou be surety for thy friend, if thou hast stricken thy hand with a stranger, thou art snared with the words of thy mouth" (Proverbs 6:1–2).

Then the advice is urgent and undignified: "Deliver thyself as a roe from the hand of the hunter, and as a bird from the hand of the fowler" (Proverbs 6:5).

**Do not cosign for someone you cannot afford to pay for.** That is not lack of faith; it is the book of Proverbs.

### The Widow's Oil

But here is the other side, and it matters enormously.

2 Kings 4 records a widow whose husband had died in debt, and whose sons were about to be taken as servants. She came to Elisha, and he asked, "What hast thou in the house?" (2 Kings 4:2).

The answer was a pot of oil. He told her to borrow vessels — not money — and pour. The oil filled every vessel, and she sold it and paid the debt (2 Kings 4:7).

**God's answer to her debt began with what she already had.** Not with shame, and not with a lecture.

## Reading the Symptom

### What Debt Often Reveals

Debt is a symptom, which means the question to ask is not only "how do I pay this off?" but "what produced it?"

Four common causes, and they are worth naming honestly.

**A gap between income and expectation** — living at the level you want rather than the level you are at.

**A crisis** — medical bills, a bereavement, a redundancy. Not a moral failure; a hard season.

**An emergency with no reserve** — the same crisis becomes a loan because there was nothing set aside.

**A hope deferred** — the purchase made to feel something, or to prove something.

**The cure addresses the cause; paying the balance and keeping the habit only resets the clock.**

### The Reserve as Wisdom

Proverbs assumes a store: "There is treasure to be desired and oil in the dwelling of the wise; but a foolish man spendeth it up" (Proverbs 21:20).

And Joseph's story assumes it on a national scale — seven years of plenty, then seven of famine, with a store built in between (Genesis 41:48–49).

**A reserve is not a lack of faith. It is the practical form faith takes when it thinks past next week.**

### The Ant

Proverbs 6 points at an insect for the whole subject: "Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways, and be wise: which having no guide, overseer, or ruler, provideth her meat in the summer, and gathereth her food in the harvest" (Proverbs 6:6–8).

**The ant has no one telling her what to do, and she still prepares.** That is the definition of a person who does not need debt to survive a winter.

### The Honest Boundary

Two guardrails, because this subject causes real harm when handled badly.

First, some debt is not a moral failure. A medical emergency, a bereavement, a stolen season — these are not signs that you are spiritually deficient, and anyone who tells you otherwise is adding affliction to affliction.

Second, debt freedom is not the gospel. You are justified by faith, not by a zero balance. Wisdom turns a financial decision around; shame has never once helped anyone repay a loan.

## What This Means for You

**1. Write the number down.** Every debt, the balance, the rate, the minimum. Unnamed debt grows in the dark.

**2. Find the cause.** Ask what produced it. The cure lives there.

**3. Start a reserve, even absurdly small.** A small buffer is the difference between a bad week and a new loan.

**4. Attack the highest rate first, or the smallest — but pick one and be consistent.** Method matters less than momentum.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Pray over your finances and then make a plan. **Faith and Wisdom** are the same Spirit's work in the same person.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Romans 13:1–8** and mark the instruction.
- **Read Proverbs 22:1–7** and note what verse 7 says about the borrower.
- **List every debt** on one page this week.
- **Set a reserve target** — one week of expenses, then one month.
- **Read 2 Kings 4:1–7** and ask what is already in your house.
