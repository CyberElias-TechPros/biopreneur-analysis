Two verses before this one, Hebrews makes a claim that would have astonished a Jewish reader: "Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do" (Hebrews 4:13).

Everything is exposed before God. Nothing is hidden. No covering works.

And the very next instruction is: come boldly.

That sequence is the whole gospel. The God who sees everything invites the people who cannot hide to approach anyway — and the basis is not their record but the High Priest who has passed into the heavens.

"Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need" (Hebrews 4:16). Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the believer's access to the throne is a standing right purchased by Christ, not a privilege earned by good behaviour — and it is meant to be used most when you are least presentable.**

## The Word "Boldly"

The Greek word is *parrhesia*, and it has a rich history. In classical Greek it meant the freedom to speak everything — the right of a citizen to say his whole mind in the assembly without fear.

It is not rudeness. It is not familiarity that forgets who is on the throne. It is the freedom of speech that belongs to someone with the right to be in the room.

**The Presence of God** — His manifest nearness — is what you are approaching. **Legal Position** — where and who you are before God's throne in Christ — is the right of access. **The Authority of the Believer** — the right to stand in His name — is what makes the approach possible.

Consider a child walking into a father's study in the middle of the night. He does not knock tentatively and wait for a servant to announce him. He has an address, he has a relationship, and he has the father's own permission. That is boldness — not insolence, but belonging. Have you been approaching the throne as a visitor instead?

### Why the Throne Is Called Grace

Notice the name. It is not called the throne of judgment here, though it is a judgment seat. It is not the throne of requirements. It is the throne of grace.

That naming is deliberate, and it is the answer to the guilt that keeps believers at a distance. Grace means the basis of your welcome is God's goodness rather than your performance. Which means arriving unimpressive is not a disadvantage at this throne. It is the normal condition of everybody in the queue.

The verse then names the two things you obtain: mercy and grace. Mercy for what you have done. Grace for what you must do next. Both are given at the same address.

### The "In Time of Need" Detail

There is a phrase in the verse that is often rushed: "in time of need."

The Greek is *eis eukairon boetheian* — help at the right time, or help in the opportune moment. It is describing a specific kind of aid: timely help.

That is worth noticing, because it means the throne is designed for the moment when you are already out of resources. Not the moment you have prepared your case and tidied yourself. The moment of need, which is usually the moment of embarrassment.

Set them side by side and the gap shows. One believer comes to God when the situation is presentable and the sin has been cleaned up, and quietly stays away when it is not. Another comes first, precisely because the situation is not presentable. One treats the throne as a place to bring results; the other as a place to bring needs.

### The High Priest Beside the Throne

The reason given in the previous verse is not abstract: "Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God" (Hebrews 4:14), and then, "For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin" (Hebrews 4:15).

Two facts, joined. He is great enough to represent you, and He is sympathetic enough to understand you. The throne is not an office; there is a person beside it who has been in the queue.

That is the answer to the two fears that keep believers from prayer. The fear that God is too holy to receive me, and the fear that He is too distant to understand me. Hebrews answers both in one sentence.

### Access Has a Letter of Admission

Hebrews 10 makes the mechanism explicit, and it is worth reading alongside this verse: "Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh" (Hebrews 10:19–20).

Boldness to enter, by the blood. Not by the day's performance. Not by the quality of the prayer you are about to make.

A letter of admission is what a hospital gives when a person has been accepted for treatment. It is not given because the patient is healthy. That is the whole point. Who told you the letter was conditional on your health?

## What This Means for You

**1. Come first, not last.** The throne is not the place you approach once you have made yourself presentable. It is the place you come because you are not.

**2. Bring specifics.** Boldness without content is noise. Ask for the mercy you actually need and the grace for the actual next step.

**3. Watch the two things you obtain.** Mercy and grace. Many believers come asking only for directions and leave without receiving the mercy on offer. Take both.

**4. Do not let a bad week become a period of absence.** Absence is where shame turns into habit. If anything, let a bad week shorten your distance rather than lengthen it.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom shape the visit.** Faith says the door is open and walks in. Wisdom says come honestly, name the situation, and also make the phone calls and take the practical steps the conversation implies. Faith and wisdom work together: one opens the throne room, the other opens the hospital, and both are God's provision for the need.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Hebrews 4:14–16, then Hebrews 10:19–25**, and note the reasons the writer gives for confidence.
- **Come to the throne each morning this week before anything else**, and name one need plainly.
- **Write down the thing you have been keeping out of prayer**, and bring it deliberately.
- **Ask for both mercy and grace** each time you pray this week.
