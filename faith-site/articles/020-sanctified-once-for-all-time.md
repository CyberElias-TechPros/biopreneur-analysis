Have you ever measured your spiritual life by how holy you felt this week — strong on good days, doubtful on hard ones? Many believers live on that emotional see-saw, assuming sanctification is a standard they must somehow keep up.

Hebrews 10:10 speaks a settled word into that restlessness: "we are **sanctified** through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ **once for all**." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **you were sanctified once for all through Christ's single offering — your holiness is a finished work you received, not a fragile standard you must maintain.**

## Sanctified Once, Not Sanitised Daily

To be sanctified is to be set apart as holy to God — claimed, dedicated, and belonging to Him. The writer of Hebrews does not describe sanctification as a goal you chase but as a status you possess: "we **are** sanctified," past action with continuing result, accomplished by one offering.

This is **the Finished Work** — the complete, once-for-all redemptive accomplishment of Christ's death and resurrection, lacking nothing and needing no repetition. Under the old covenant, priests offered sacrifices repeatedly because the offerings could never perfect the worshipper. Christ offered one sacrifice and sat down. Sitting down was the sign of completion — no more work to do.

The cleansing agent is **the Blood of Jesus** — His life given for you, the sufficient payment that removes guilt and sets you apart to God. Blood in Scripture is life poured out in covenant. Christ's blood did not merely cover your sin temporarily; it cleansed you permanently and consecrated you to God.

The Spirit is not idle in this. Peter writes that believers are sanctified by the Spirit unto obedience (1 Peter 1:2), and Paul tells the Thessalonians that God chose them through sanctification of the Spirit and belief of the truth (2 Thessalonians 2:13). The same Spirit who applied Christ's offering to your spirit now works in your thinking, appetites, and habits, conforming your daily life to the position you already hold. His work is not a second sanctification competing with Christ's; it is the application of Christ's finished sanctification to your lived experience.

Think of a house legally transferred to your name. The deed is signed once, but you live in the house every day. The signing was once for all; the living is daily. Sanctification works the same way: the transfer happened once at Calvary and once in you at the New Birth; the living out happens daily.

## Position and Practice — Both Are Real

Does this mean your conduct does not matter? Far from it. Scripture teaches two dimensions of sanctification, and confusion between them causes both legalism and licence. **Positional sanctification** is what Christ accomplished: you were set apart to God once for all, and nothing can reverse it. **Practical sanctification** is your daily growth in expressing that position: purity, honesty, love, and obedience worked out in ordinary life.

**Righteousness** — right standing with God, received as a gift in Christ — is the foundation of both. You do not become holy to be accepted; you are accepted, and therefore you pursue holiness. Positional truth gives practical practice a home.

Here is the contrast: the legalist pursues sanctification to secure standing; the believer pursues sanctification because standing is secure. The legalist's holiness is anxious and brittle; the believer's holiness is grateful and steady.

## The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **Hebrews 10:14** — "For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified." The same writer joins perfection and sanctification: those set apart are perfected forever by one offering. Your standing is complete, not pending.
- **1 Corinthians 6:11** — "But ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God." Paul lists sanctification among accomplished realities, not aspirations — and places it beside washing and justification.
- **John 17:17** — "Sanctify them through thy truth: thy word is truth." Jesus prays for practical sanctification through the Word — the daily dimension flowing from the finished dimension. His prayer proves both are His work in you.

Three witnesses, one sanctification: accomplished at the cross, applied at the New Birth, expressed through the Word each day.

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop measuring your acceptance by your week.** Your sanctification rests on Christ's offering, not on your emotional or moral performance. On hard days, return to Hebrews 10:10: sanctified once for all. Then rise, confess, and continue.

**2. Pursue holiness from security, not for security.** Because you are set apart, you practise purity, honesty, and love as family conduct. Pursuing holiness for security produces fear; pursuing it from security produces freedom.

**3. Let the Word do its sanctifying work.** Jesus prayed for sanctification through truth. Daily Scripture is not a box to tick; it is the Spirit's instrument for shaping your thinking, speech, and choices into agreement with your position.

**4. Faith and wisdom express sanctification in daily stewardship.** Faith declares you sanctified once for all; wisdom stewards your body, time, money, speech, and relationships as belonging to God. Declaring grace never excuses carelessness; it motivates careful, grateful holiness.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Hebrews 10:10–14 and mark the once-for-all words.** Once, for all, one offering, perfected for ever — then thank your Father that sanctification is His completed work in you.
- **Replace one anxious holiness check with a positional truth.** When you feel unsure of your standing this week, answer with 1 Corinthians 6:11: washed, sanctified, justified in the name of the Lord Jesus.
- **Practise one deliberate act of purity.** Choose a specific area — speech, media, money, or relationships — and act this week as someone set apart, not to earn standing but to express it.
- **Let the Word sanctify one thought pattern.** Take one recurring thought that contradicts your position in Christ and replace it daily with John 17:17 and a specific verse until your mind agrees.
