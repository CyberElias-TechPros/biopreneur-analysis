Acts 11:23 records a visit that changed a church, and the man who made it is worth studying because his whole method is in his name.

"Who, when he came, and had seen the grace of God, was glad, and exhorted them all, that with purpose of heart they would cleave unto the Lord."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **encouragement in the New Testament is the same word as the Spirit's own name — *paraklēsis*, from the Comforter — which means it is not a nice thing said to a sad person but a Person's ministry being done through a body.**

## The Man

### A Renamed Man

And his name was Joseph, and the apostles named him Barnabas — *son of consolation*, or better, son of encouragement (Acts 4:36).

The church gave him a name that described a function. That is a great deal better and a great deal heavier than a compliment.

**A nickname given by a community is a job description they have already watched you do.** So what would they name you?

### A Costly Encouragement

And the first thing he did under that name was sell a field and lay the money at the apostles' feet (Acts 4:37).

Encouragement started as generosity. It was not a personality; it was a disposition that cost something.

**Nobody accumulates a name like Barnabas by saying nice things.**

### A Bold Encouragement

And the second was more expensive. When nobody would believe that Saul of Tarsus had been converted, Barnabas took him by the hand and vouched for him (Acts 9:27).

He staked his own reputation on another man's story.

**Encouragement is sometimes a legal guarantee.**

## The Ministry

### Seeing the Grace

And the phrase worth noticing in Acts 11:23 is *had seen the grace of God* — that is what he was looking for. He arrived in Antioch and his instinct was to find evidence of God rather than evidence of error.

A person who is looking for grace tends to find it.

**What you are looking for in people is usually what you will see.** So what have you been looking for?

### Glad, Not Suspicious

And his reaction was to be glad. The Greek is *echarē* — he rejoiced.

Not "he withheld judgment." He was delighted. In a church that was brand new and full of people nobody expected to be there.

**Joy at another person's growth is a working of the Spirit, not a personality trait.**

### The Content

And what he exhorted them to was specific: *with purpose of heart to cleave unto the Lord* — *prostēnai*, to remain attached, and *thesis kardias*, a settled purpose.

He did not tell them to feel better. He told them to hold on.

**Encouragement is mostly direction, and it usually points to a Person.**

### Why It Is a Ministry

### Because It Is the Spirit's Name

And Jesus names the Spirit *Paraklētos* — the one called alongside, the Comforter, the Advocate (John 14:16).

Barnabas was doing by nature and grace what the Spirit does by Person.

**Wherever encouragement is happening, the Comforter has a representative in the room.**

### Because It Keeps People In

And the reason it matters is that faithfulness is fragile. Paul warns that a heart can be hardened by sin's deceitfulness (Hebrews 3:13) and prescribes exactly this remedy: encourage one another daily, while it is called today.

Encouragement is the maintenance crew of discipleship.

Most people do not leave the faith in one decision. They drift out through a season nobody steadied.

### Because It Recovers the Lost

And Mark is the case study. He abandoned the first journey (Acts 15:38), and Paul would not take him again; the disagreement split Paul and Barnabas.

And Barnabas took Mark and worked with him. Decades later Paul wrote, "Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry" (2 Timothy 4:11).

Encouragement is a bet placed on a future nobody else can see. And somebody once placed it on you.

### Doing It

### Look for the Grace

And the first practice is to arrive somewhere looking for evidence of God rather than a list of what is wrong.

It changes the room, and it changes what you can see.

Critics find what they are looking for too.

### Stand Publicly

And the second is costly and rare: say the good thing you believe about somebody where it can be overheard.

Barnabas vouched in front of the apostles. That is the ministry's full strength.

Silent endorsement is only half an endorsement.

### Stay With the Ones Who Fail

And the third is the hardest. Encouragement that only supports success is just admiration.

The Barnabas ministry is measured in Mark's.

The person who needs it most will usually be the one whose last attempt went badly.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not flattery or the avoidance of hard truth. Barnabas's encouragement was *cleave to the Lord*, which is a directive, not a compliment.

Second, this is not a calling to rescue everyone. Even Barnabas had a conflict he could not resolve (Acts 15:39), and it did not undo his life's work.

**The **The Ministry of Reconciliation is the other ministry given to a body; Communion With the Holy Spirit is where the Comforter trains His representatives; and Kingdom Stewardship is the room you are posted to.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Acts 4:32–37 and Acts 11:19–26.

2. Name somebody whose future is in doubt.

**3. Say the good thing you have been thinking privately.**

**4. Give something that costs you.**

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Encouragement does not require pretending. **The Ministry of Reconciliation** is the substance; wisdom knows how to bless somebody without lying to them.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Hebrews 3:12–14.**
- **Read 2 Timothy 4:9–11.**
- **Write one note to somebody struggling.**
- **Say one good thing in public.**
- **Stay with one person who failed.**
