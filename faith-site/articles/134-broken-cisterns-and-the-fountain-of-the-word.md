Jeremiah 2 contains one of the sharpest accusations God ever makes against His own people, and it is not about idolatry in general.

"For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water" (Jeremiah 2:13).

Two evils, not one. Leaving the fountain, and building the replacement.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **every substitute for the living Word is a cistern — something you built, holding water you collected, leaking at a rate you cannot see — and the fountain is not further away than the turn.**

## The Two Things in the Verse

A fountain and a cistern are both water sources in the ancient world, and the difference is total.

A **fountain** is a spring — water that comes from underground, rising on its own, with no effort from anyone. It is the word *maqor*, and in Jeremiah it is used of God Himself: the spring of living water.

A **cistern** is a pit dug into rock to catch rainwater. It must be hewn, maintained, dragged into place, and filled. It has no source of its own.

**The Word of God** — what He has spoken — is the spring. **The Life of the Spirit** — His life at work in you — is the rising water. **Communion With the Holy Spirit** — the ongoing relationship — is what keeps you near the source rather than at the tank.

Consider a man with a good spring at the bottom of his land who spends his weekends digging a tank, then carts water up the hill in buckets and wonders why he is tired.

### Why "Hewed" Matters

The word "hewed" is doing heavy work. *Ḥatsav* means to dig out, to quarry, to cut into stone.

A cistern is not found. It is made. Which is the diagnosis of every substitute: it is something you constructed, and it therefore says something about what you believe you need to do for yourself.

That is the deep mistake. Not that the water is bad, but that the arrangement is inverted. The spring has no priest, no quota, and no maintenance. The tank has all three.

### What the Cisterns Look Like Now

It is easy to read this and file it under "ancient idolatry." But the mechanism transfers perfectly, and the modern cisterns are respectable.

A cistern is any practice that produces a spiritual feeling without the Word and without the Spirit — the productivity system that promises you will be fine if you are disciplined, the relationships you depend on instead of God, the constant news cycle, the consultation of everyone else's opinion before you have prayed.

None of these is a scandal. That is precisely the danger. A cistern is dangerous because it is useful at first.

Set the two things side by side. One believer starts his morning with a chapter and a prayer and has a spring in the house. Another starts with alerts and a friend's voice and has a tank that must be filled again by noon. Both are drinking. Only one is being supplied.

### Broken, and Holding Nothing

The verse adds the detail that makes the accusation so pointed: the cisterns are broken. They can hold no water.

That is not a moral failure of the water. It is the nature of the construction. Every substitute leaks at the joints, and the leak is usually visible only in hindsight — you fill it, you feel better, and three days later you are thirsty in the same place.

There is a particular despair that comes with knowing exactly what your cistern is and filling it again anyway. Jeremiah is describing that despair before it is felt.

### The Two Evils Are One Problem

Notice that God calls it two evils but describes one movement: leaving and building. The building is the evidence of the leaving.

Nobody digs a cistern in a garden with a working spring unless he has forgotten, or refused, what the spring is. The tank is not the primary sin. It is the symptom.

### Jesus at the Well

Which is why Jesus takes this exact image and offers it to a woman drawing water by hand.

"Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again: But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life" (John 4:13–14).

He is answering Jeremiah 2:13 in person. The cistern is the well of Sychar, and the fountain is standing in front of her talking.

And He uses *pēgē* — the same Greek word the Septuagint uses for Jeremiah's fountain — and He puts it *inside* her.

### The Turn

The last thing Jeremiah 2 says to a people with broken tanks is not "dig better."

It is the accusation itself, which is also an invitation. God names the fountain as *me*. The spring has not moved. Cisterns are dug by people who have walked away from water they could still go back to.

That is the whole mercy of the verse. The distance is one decision, and nothing has to be rebuilt to get there.

## What This Means for You

**1. Name your cistern.** Which practice do you reach for when you are thirsty that is not the Word and not the Spirit? Name it without euphemism.

**2. Notice the leak.** How long does the relief last? That duration is diagnostic.

**3. Go back to the fountain before you do anything else.** Turn, then build nothing. Just return.

**4. Drink daily rather than tank-up weekly.** A spring is unlimited; the tank has a lid. The daily habit is not legalism. It is being supplied.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom work together here.** Faith says come to the fountain and drink freely. Wisdom says that water still comes through ordinary means — the local church, honest friendship, sleep, and time — and that a person who is deeply dry may need others to carry him to the spring rather than be told to try harder.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Jeremiah 2:1–13**, and note the kindness that precedes verse 13.
- **Write the name of your cistern**, and one thing you will do differently this week.
- **Read John 4:1–14**, and mark what Jesus says is inside the believer.
- **Read Isaiah 55:1–3**, and notice the price of the water.
