A father had just watched nine disciples fail. He brought his son to Jesus with the least confident prayer in the Gospels: "If thou canst do any thing, have compassion on us, and help us" (Mark 9:22). Jesus turned the doubt back on him — "If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth" (Mark 9:23).

"If ye have faith as a grain of mustard seed... nothing shall be impossible unto you" (Matthew 17:20). Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Jesus locates impossibility in the size of your means, not in the size of your God — a mustard seed of real faith outranks a mountain of fact.**

The same measure shows up twice in Luke. When the disciples asked for more faith, Jesus answered with a sycamine tree: "If ye had faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye might say unto this sycamine tree, Be thou plucked up by the root, and be thou planted in the sea; and it should obey you" (Luke 17:6). Twice the Lord meets the request for *more* with a statement about *what a little can do*. Whatever else that means, it means the shortage He identifies is never quantity.

## Why the Disciples Failed

The story is not a rebuke of weak people. These were the men who had cast out demons and healed the sick earlier in the same Gospel. Something had shifted. Jesus names it plainly: "Because of your unbelief" (Matthew 17:20).

**The Life of Faith** — a life ordered by God's declarations — is not measured by past success. **The Word of God** — His settled utterance — is where a believer's confidence must live, not in yesterday's results. And **The Power of the Holy Spirit** — God's own ability at work — is what a mustard seed connects you to.

Notice what Jesus did not say. He did not say they needed more authority, a better technique, or a louder voice. He said they needed faith, and He compared it to a seed — which is to say, something small, alive, and planted.

### A Seed, Not a Stone

A mustard seed is unimpressive. It has no mass to speak of and no visible strength. But it is alive, and life in the right place moves more than weight ever could.

Think of a crack in a pavement. Nobody breaks concrete with brute force from above; a root does it from beneath, quietly, over months, with a force drawn from sunlight. Faith works like the root, not the hammer.

Set them side by side and the gap shows. Unbelief asks how big the mountain is, while faith asks how alive the seed is. One measures the obstacle; the other measures the God who spoke.

### "Nothing Shall Be Impossible" Is a Category, Not a Blank Cheque

We should read this promise as Scripture intends. "Nothing shall be impossible unto you" is spoken in the context of a kingdom, a Father's will, and a word already given. It does not license a believer to demand whatever imagination suggests.

The promises of God are the ground on which faith stands. Where He has spoken, no mountain has standing; where He has not spoken, faith has nothing to lean its weight on. This is where **Faith and Wisdom** matter again: faith takes God at His word, and wisdom makes sure it is His word we are taking.

Two Old Testament questions stand behind this promise. "Is any thing too hard for the LORD?" (Genesis 18:14) — asked in the very conversation where Sarah laughed. And Jeremiah's prayer: "Ah Lord GOD! behold, thou hast made the heaven and the earth by thy great power... is there any thing too hard for thee?" (Jeremiah 32:17). The answer has never been in doubt. The only variable in the room is whether anyone will believe it.

Have you noticed that in Scripture the hard question is never aimed at God's capability? It is always aimed at a human heart.

## What This Means for You

Mark gives us the sentence every honest believer eventually prays: "Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief" (Mark 9:24). He did not clean himself up first. He brought the mixture and asked for help with the mixture.

And the boy was healed. Jesus did not wait for a tidy heart. He honoured a man who was honest about the mess and asked for more.

**1. Name the mountain.** Vagueness is unbelief in disguise; be specific about what stands against you.
**2. Check whether God has spoken.** Faith rests on a word. Find the promise, then stand on the promise.
**3. Start where your faith actually is.** A seed is a place to begin, not a reason for shame.
**4. Speak, but do not perform.** Faith speaks because it is convinced, not because words are magic.
**5. Address unbelief as a decision.** Repent of it the way you would repent of any other sin — and ask for help.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Write your mountain in one sentence.** Keep it before you each morning.
- **Find three scriptures** that speak to it and read them aloud daily.
- **Pray Mark 9:24** every time doubt rises this week.
- **Thank God for the answer** before you can see it move.
