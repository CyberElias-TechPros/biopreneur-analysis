Any lawyer will tell you that an argument is only as good as its evidence, and the accuser of the brethren has always understood this better than most believers.

He does not invent. He reads.

"Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, which was contrary to us, and took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross" (Colossians 2:14).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the accuser's only real weapon is a legitimate debt — and the cross was God's own answer to His own record, which means his appeals now go to a court that has already published the verdict.**

## What the Loopholes Were

### A Real Record

And *handwriting* is *cheirographon* — the Greek word for a handwritten IOU, a bond signed by the debtor.

Paul is describing a document with your name on it and a debt you could not deny.

**His case was not fabricated. It was documented.**

### The Ordinances

And the record was the law — the commands that named the debt and required its payment.

"Which was contrary to us" — because a standard you cannot meet only ever works against you.

**The law was never unfair. It was simply unmixed with mercy and impossible to satisfy.**

### The Legitimate Access

And this is why the enemy had standing. He could point to the record. He was not lying.

That is what makes accusation so effective: it contains enough truth to be unbearable.

**The enemy rarely lies about your sin. He lies about what it means now.**

## The Loopholes Closed

### Blotted Out

And Paul's verb is *exaleiphō* — to wipe out, to erase, the way you would erase writing from a papyrus.

Not forgiven in a ledger and still visible. Wiped.

**There is nothing left to read. Not a strike-through. An erasure.**

### Nailed to the Cross

And the mechanism: it was taken out of the way and nailed to His cross.

The debt was not cancelled by a legislator's pen but by an execution.

**Someone paid it in person, and the receipt is His body.**

### The Triumph

And the very next verse completes it: "And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it" (Colossians 2:15).

The accuser is not merely answered; he is disarmed and displayed.

**The court has finished. The parade has started.**

### The Loopholes He Still Tries

### Circumstances

And the first tactic now is inference. If bad things are happening, then God must be angry.

But the cross settled the anger question permanently, and circumstances are not a verdict.

**He has no evidence, so he produces images.**

### Feelings

And the second is sensation. A low mood is offered as proof of distance from God.

But 2 Corinthians 5:7 is the answer: walk by faith, not by sight — and a feeling is a species of sight.

A sensation is not a summons.

### Other People's Sin

And the third is the sin of others. If a leader falls, the accuser invites you to conclude something about your own standing.

That is a category error and a distraction.

He will use someone else's failure to accuse you if he cannot find yours.

### The Pre-emptive Turn

And the fourth is the pre-emptive accusation: he accuses you to others so that you will be defensive, and accuses you within yourself so that you will be silent.

He cannot win in the court, so he works the corridor.

His last remaining power is reputational, and it works only if you agree with it.

### How Grace Closes Them

### The Verdict

And the closing argument is Paul's own: "Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect? It is God that justifieth. Who is he that condemneth? It is Christ that died" (Romans 8:34).

The judge has justified. There is no higher court.

An accusation that contradicts the bench is not a case. It is noise.

### The Advocate

And there is one who answers: "My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous" (1 John 2:1).

An advocate, not an accuser, in the same courtroom.

The defence attorney is the judge's own Son.

### The Record Kept Clean

And confession keeps the record clean, not by adding evidence but by agreeing with the court.

1 John 1:9 is the procedure, and it is short.

You cannot give the enemy a document that has already been erased.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not the doctrine of "don't apologise." Confession is real, and it is commanded.

Second, the closed case is not permission to live carelessly. Romans 6 answers that in advance: the grace that closes the record also changes the man.

**The **Justification is the verdict that shuts the door; the The Finished Work is the evidence destroyed; and Righteousness is the standing nobody can re-litigate.

## What This Means for You

1. Notice what he quotes. It is usually true.

**2. Answer with what it means now**, not with denial.

**3. Memorise Romans 8:33–34.**

**4. Confess quickly.** The record is short, and the wrong path is long.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Peace is not built by arguing with every accusation. **Justification** is the standing; wisdom is the habit of not going back to a courtroom you have already been acquitted in.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Colossians 2:11–15 every morning this week.**
- **Read Zechariah 3:1–5** and note who commands the change of clothes.
- **Write Romans 8:33 on a card.**
- **Identify the accusation you hear most often.**
- **Answer it once, out loud, from the Word.**
