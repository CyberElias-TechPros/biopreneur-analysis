Hebrews opens with the most concentrated sentence about Christ in the New Testament, and it includes a clause that most readers skim.

"Who being the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person, and upholding all things by the word of his power, when he had by himself purged our sins, sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high" (Hebrews 1:3).

Sustaining the universe sits between describing His nature and describing His finished work — as though it were the ordinary thing He does.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the same Word that spoke the worlds into being is the Word that keeps them from falling apart — which means sustaining is not a different power from creating, and neither is a struggle for Him.**

## The Word Behind "Upholding"

The Greek is *pherōn* — bearing, carrying, bringing along. It is the verb you would use of a man carrying a load, or of a ship being carried on a current.

It is present tense. Not "having upheld," but upholding — continuously, right now, while you read this sentence.

And the means is "the word of his power" — *rhēmati tēs dynameōs autou*. A spoken word, in the instrument case.

**The Word of God** — what He has spoken — is not only a message He sent but the force that keeps things in place. **The Name of Jesus** — the authority attached to that person — is where this upholding power is located. **The Power of the Holy Spirit** — the energy by which God acts — is the same power described here.

Consider a man holding a heavy door shut against a crowd. His strength is real, and it is strained. That is how we tend to imagine God holding the world. The verse does not say He strains.

### Creating and Sustaining Are One Act

The Old Testament makes exactly this connection: "By the word of the LORD were the heavens made; and all the host of them by the breath of his mouth... For he spake, and it was done; he commanded, and it stood fast" (Psalm 33:6, 9).

*He commanded, and it stood fast.* Note the sequence — the word is spoken, and the standing is the consequence of the speaking.

Which means the world is not held by a mechanism God wound up and walked away from. It is held by a word being spoken.

Set the two pictures side by side. One imagines a clockmaker who built a good machine and left it running. Another reads a God who is speaking now — and if He stopped, the machine would not merely wind down; it would have nothing to stand on.

### Psalm 104 and the Breath

Psalm 104 puts it in the language of living creatures: "Thou hidest thy face, they are troubled: thou takest away their breath, they die, and return to dust. Thou sendest forth thy spirit, they are created: and thou renewest the face of the earth" (Psalm 104:29–30).

The sustaining is not a distant management. It is a continual sending forth. Every breath you took today was supplied.

That is either a very uncomfortable thought or an enormous comfort, depending on what you believe about the One doing the supplying. Which is it for you?

### Colossians Says It About Christ Directly

Paul's parallel is even more explicit: "And he is before all things, and by him all things consist" (Colossians 1:17). *Consist* — *synestēken* — hold together, are constituted.

And then Colossians adds the thing Hebrews is also doing: this cosmic upholder is the head of the body, the church (Colossians 1:18).

The two facts sit together in the same letter on purpose. The One holding the stars is not too great to be near the sick, the frightened, and the ordinary.

### Why This Matters for a Shaking Life

Everything so far is doctrine. Here is where it becomes personal.

If the universe is held by a word rather than by strain, then the things in your life that are being held are being held the same way. Not by your grip.

That is a sentence worth memorising for the week when the finances, the diagnosis, or the family all shake at once. The thing you are white-knuckling was never held by your knuckle.

### The "Sat Down" Detail

There is a detail at the end of the verse that makes the point unforgettable.

"when he had by himself purged our sins, sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high."

*Sat down.* In the tabernacle, the priests never sat, because the work was never finished (Hebrews 10:11–12). Christ sat, because His was.

So the One holding the universe together is seated. Not pacing. Not anxious about the outcome. That is what sustained authority looks like — the calm of a finished work.

### The Honest Boundary

One caution, because this doctrine can be turned into a slogan that ignores real pain.

"God is holding it all together" is true, and it is not an instruction to deny that things break. Bodies fail, marriages end, work disappears. Hebrews 1:3 is written to people who were being persecuted, not to people who needed reassuring that nothing bad happens.

The claim is not that nothing falls. It is that nothing falls out of His hand — and that distinction is the difference between a platitude and an anchor.

## What This Means for You

**1. Loosen your grip where you have been straining.** What are you holding up by main force? Name it, then hand it over in prayer.

**2. Read Psalm 33:6–9 aloud.** Let the sequence of speaking and standing register.

**3. Trust the seated posture.** If He is seated, you do not need to be frantic.

**4. Let the sustaining reach your ordinary life.** Notice what has held: breath, work, sleep, the friend who called. Those are not accidents.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom work together here.** Faith says He upholds all things and rests in it. Wisdom says resting in that is not the same as doing nothing — you still work, plan, save, and ask for help, because the One who upholds ordinarily upholds through means He has given you.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Hebrews 1:1–4** each morning, and mark every claim about the Son.
- **Read Colossians 1:15–20**, and note where the upholding sits in the poem.
- **Name one thing you are straining to hold**, and pray it into His keeping.
- **Read Psalm 104:24–30**, and thank Him for breath.
