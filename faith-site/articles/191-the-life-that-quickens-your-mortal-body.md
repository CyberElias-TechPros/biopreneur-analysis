Most believers have a doctrine of the resurrection and almost no expectation of its power in their joints, their lungs, or their blood.

"But if the Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you, he that raised up Christ from the dead shall also quicken your mortal bodies by his Spirit that dwelleth in you" (Romans 8:11).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the resurrection is not merely the doctrine you hope in at a funeral; it is the power already resident in your body, and the Spirit who raised Christ is at work on the same project in you now.**

## The Words That Carry It

### "Quicken"

The word is *zōopoiēsei* — from *zōē*, life, and *poieō*, to make. To make alive, to give life to, to vitalise.

It is the same verb used of God "who quickeneth the dead" (Romans 4:17), and of Christ who "was quickened by the Spirit" (1 Peter 3:18), and of the last Adam who will be "a quickening spirit" (1 Corinthians 15:45).

So this is not a metaphor for encouragement. It is the word for the thing that happened in the tomb, applied to a body that is still on this side of it.

### "Mortal Bodies"

And the object of that verb is the most important part of the verse.

*Thnēta sōmata* — mortal bodies. *Thnētos* means subject to death, dying, perishable. Paul is not describing a glorified body; he is describing yours, with its blood pressure and its medication and its tiredness.

The verse does not promise life to something already immortal. It promises life to the exact body you are worried about.

### "Dwell" — Twice

And notice that the word "dwell" appears twice in a single sentence.

"if the Spirit... **dwell** in you... by his Spirit that **dwelleth** in you."

The Greek is *oikei* and *enoikountos*, from *oikos*, a house. The Spirit is not visiting; He has moved in.

Read the logic, because the whole verse rests on it. The condition is indwelling. The consequence is quickening. **If He lives there, He maintains the property.** An occupant does not sit in a house watching it decay without acting.

### The Argument From the Tomb

And there is a second logical hinge that most readers pass over: "the Spirit *of him that raised up Jesus*... he that *raised up Christ*."

Paul is arguing from an accomplished fact. The Father raised Jesus by the Spirit. That Spirit lives in you. Therefore the power that produced the resurrection is present in your body as an available resident, not a distant memory.

Which puts the verse in the same family as Ephesians 1:19–20, where Paul prays that the church would know "the exceeding greatness of his power to us-ward who believe, according to the working of his mighty power, which he wrought in Christ, when he raised him from the dead."

The measuring stick for what is available to you is the resurrection. Not your energy level; not your history.

### The Body in Romans 8

It is worth noticing how much of Romans 8 is about the body, because the chapter is often remembered only for its ending.

Verse 10: "if Christ be in you, the body is dead because of sin; but the Spirit is life because of righteousness."

Verse 11: the quickening.

Verse 13: "if ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live."

Verse 23: "waiting for the adoption, to wit, the redemption of our body."

Paul's great chapter of assurance is also a chapter about bodies, and it does not treat the body as a distraction. It treats the body as the arena.

### The Present Tense of It

Now the question every reader has: is this future or now?

The answer is both, and the verse is constructed to say so.

The final completion is future — verse 23 says we are "waiting for the adoption, to wit, the redemption of our body," and Philippians 3:21 says Christ "shall change our vile body, that it may be fashioned like unto his glorious body."

But the verb in verse 11 is *zōopoiēsei* — a future indicative, and Paul's use of it elsewhere includes present action. And the condition is present: the Spirit *dwells* in you now.

So the honest answer is: the full redemption of the body is ahead, and the quickening power of that redemption is not. You are not waiting for the Spirit to come; He is in the house.

### Ezekiel's Valley and the Two-Stage Miracle

The Old Testament gives the picture, and it is instructive that it happened in two stages.

Ezekiel saw a valley of dry bones, and God asked him, "Son of man, can these bones live?" (Ezekiel 37:3). He prophesied, and there was a noise, and bone came to bone, and sinew and flesh came up.

But then: "and there was no breath in them" (Ezekiel 37:8).

And God said, "Come from the four winds, O breath, and breathe upon these slain, that they may live" (Ezekiel 37:9).

**Structure and then life.** The framework comes first and the breath comes second, and both are God's. That is not a discouragement about slow healing; it is a description of how life is organised.

### The Tent and the Treasure

One illustration, drawn from Paul's own vocabulary two chapters earlier.

"But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us" (2 Corinthians 4:7).

*Ostrakinois skeuesin* — clay pots. Cheap, ordinary, easily cracked.

Now put that with Romans 8:11. The pot is not the point, and the pot is not abandoned. The treasure is in the vessel, and the presence of the treasure is the reason the vessel keeps being repaired.

Paul goes on: "though our outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day" (2 Corinthians 4:16). The outward is under strain and the inward is being renewed, and the same Lord is in charge of both.

### What This Does Not Promise

Two honest boundaries, because this passage can be overworked.

It does not promise that a mortal body never dies. It is called *thnēta* — mortal. The verse says the Spirit quickens a mortal body, not that the body becomes immortal here. All of us will die unless the Lord returns first, and Romans 8:11 is not a contradiction of that.

And it does not promise instant results. The word is "shall quicken," and the tense includes everything between the promise and its arrival. Ezekiel's bones were assembled before the breath came.

But neither boundary cancels the plain claim: the Spirit in you is the resurrection Spirit, and His work is aimed at your flesh.

### What to Do With It

So the practice, and it is a practice rather than a feeling.

**Know who lives in you.** Most of the weakness in the church's expectation comes from a vague view of the Spirit as an influence rather than a resident.

**Pray this verse over your body.** Not as a charm, but as agreement with what is written. "Father, Your Spirit raised Jesus; He lives in me; quicken my mortal body."

**And cooperate with the quickening.** The Spirit does not quicken a body being actively destroyed, and here faith and wisdom work together — a rested, fed, treated body is a body the indwelling life can reach. Sleep, food, exercise, medicine, and rest are not the opposite of this verse; they are the ordinary means through which the indwelling life reaches the flesh.

**The Power of the Holy Spirit** is not a category reserved for crusades. It is the same power, resident in a clay pot, doing quiet repairs.

## What This Means for You

**1. Move your expectation from distant to resident.** The resurrection is not only your destination; it is your indwelling.

**2. Pray Romans 8:11 this week.** Say it until it is not a strange thing to say.

**3. Keep the body in the plan.** **Divine Life** is *zōē*, and it is aimed at *thnēta sōmata*. The Spirit is not embarrassed by flesh.

**4. Give the quickening something to work with.** A body that is rested, fed, and treated is a body the Spirit can quicken. **The Life of the Spirit** is not the enemy of the life of the body; it is its source.

**5. Trust a slow process.** Ezekiel's valley was not a failure because the breath came second.
