Proverbs 13:22 has been quoted at offering times for a century, and almost always in one direction: the wealth of the wicked is coming to you, so stay ready.

The first half of the verse rarely gets quoted with as much energy. A good man leaves an inheritance to his grandchildren.

Those two halves belong together, and together they mean something considerably more sober than a transfer of funds.

"A good man leaveth an inheritance to his children's children: and the wealth of the sinner is laid up for the just" (Proverbs 13:22).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Proverbs 13:22 is not a promise that you will inherit a wicked man's estate; it is a statement about the moral instability of hoarded wealth, and that instability is a warning before it is a hope.**

## What the Verse Is Actually Saying

### Two Men, Two Directions

Look at the grammar. The good man is *leaving* something — the verb is active, and the object is the next two generations. The sinner is not described as giving anything; his wealth is *laid up*, stored, held in reserve somewhere he does not control.

One man's wealth travels down. The other's travels away.

**The proverb is a contrast between wealth with an exit and wealth under arrest.**

### The Hebrew Word for "Laid Up"

The verb behind *laid up* is *tsāphan* — to hide, to treasure up, to store away secretly. It is the word used of hidden treasure and of things concealed for safekeeping.

It is not a neutral word. It carries the sense of a stockpile that has been kept back from use — which is exactly the charge Scripture brings against unjust gain everywhere else.

**What is hidden cannot be stewarded, and what cannot be stewarded cannot be kept.**

### Why It Cannot Stay

Proverbs assumes a moral order in the world, not merely a moral order in the heart. Wealth gathered by oppression has a defect built into it, and the defect is not in the amount but in the title.

The Preacher watched this happen and despaired over it: "Yea, I hated all my labour which I had taken under the sun: because I should leave it unto the man that shall be after me" (Ecclesiastes 2:18). He had earned it honestly and still had to hand it over.

**If even honest wealth changes hands, how much more certainly will dishonest wealth?**

### The First Half Is the Condition

And here is the part that should stop us. The proverb does not say the wealth comes to anyone who wants it. It says it is laid up *for the just*.

The Hebrew for *just* is *tsaddîq* — the righteous man. The verse is identifying a kind of person, not offering a sweepstakes.

**The verse describes where wealth ends up. It does not promise you will be the one standing there.**

### How the Release Happens

#### Egypt Paid for the Exodus

The pattern is described plainly: "And the LORD gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians, so that they lent unto them such things as they required. And they spoiled the Egyptians" (Exodus 12:36).

Generations of unpaid labour walked out of Egypt carrying gold. The wealth had been laid up in the wrong treasury for four hundred years, and God settled the account on the way out.

God has a longer ledger than the one you are reading.

#### Cyrus Funded the Temple

Isaiah named a pagan king a century and a half before he was born, and called him God's shepherd (Isaiah 44:28). When the time came, Cyrus released the temple vessels and ordered his treasury to pay for the rebuilding (Ezra 1:4–8).

A empire's surplus was requisitioned for a house of worship it did not believe in.

When the release comes, it often comes through people who have no idea what they are funding.

#### Naboth's Vineyard Did Not Last

The counterexample matters just as much. Ahab and Jezebel took a vineyard by false witness and murder, and the property stayed in the family for a season.

But Elijah met Ahab in that vineyard with a sentence about dogs (1 Kings 21:19), and within a generation the dynasty was gone and the land had changed hands.

The release is not always quick. It is always certain.

### The Warning Comes First

#### Do Not Read It as a Windfall

The temptation with this verse is to turn it into a waiting room: hoarded wealth is coming, so position yourself.

That reading produces exactly the disposition the proverb condemns. A person watching for a transfer is a person with the sinner's relationship to money and the righteous man's vocabulary.

You cannot desire another man's hoard and call it hope.

#### Check Your Own Storehouses

The verse indicts any wealth that is *tsāphan* — hidden, withheld, stockpiled against a need it could meet.

James is blunt about the mechanism: "Your gold and silver is cankered; and the rust of them shall be a witness against you" (James 5:3). The hoard itself testifies.

**Before you ask where the wicked man's wealth is going, ask where yours is sitting.**

#### The Inheritance You Are Actually Building

And the first half of the proverb is a command in disguise. A good man *leaves* an inheritance — which means he arranged his life so that something survived him, in the right hands.

That is **Kingdom Stewardship**: not merely accumulating well, but building something that is still working two generations after you stop touching it.

**The question is not how much you will leave. It is whether what you leave is worth inheriting.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Audit your title, not just your total.** Is there any part of what you hold that came through pressure, omission, or advantage?

**2. Make restitution where you can.** The law of the trespass offering required it (Leviticus 6:4–5). **Righteousness** is not a feeling about your money; it is a set of claims that can be checked.

**3. Give the hoard a door.** Decide what portion of your reserve exists to be released, and to whom.

**4. Build for grandchildren, not for a year.** Ask what your work will still be doing in forty years.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Trust God for the release, and do not go looking for a wicked man's estate. **The Inheritance of the Believer** is secured by Christ, not by the failure of anyone else's portfolio. The believer's confidence is in a title deed, not a foreclosure.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Proverbs 13:11–25** and note how often wealth and the tongue are linked.
- **Read Exodus 12:35–36** and count the generations.
- **Read Ezra 1:1–11** and see who paid for the temple.
- **Write down one thing you hold** whose title you are not sure about.
- **Read James 5:1–6** slowly. Then read it again as a believer, not as a spectator.
