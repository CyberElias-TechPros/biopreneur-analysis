Most believers assume the enemy knows everything about them, and the assumption does more damage than the reality.

He does not. He is not omniscient, and he is not present everywhere. What he has is a very long file and a lot of patience.

"Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices" (2 Corinthians 2:11).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **you are being studied, and that is not a reason for fear but for study of your own — because the enemy's knowledge of your patterns is finite and often wrong, while the Word you take up is neither.**

## He Has a File

### Observation Over Time

And a devil who has been watching the human race for six thousand years has data. He watched the same three plays work in Genesis, in Eden, in Gethsemane.

He knows what worked on you last time.

**The file is real, and it is compiled from your own responses.**

### The Weakest Entry Point

And he is not a gentleman. He attacks where the wall is lowest, at the hour when resistance is thinnest.

Peter's denial happened at a fire, at night, after a long evening — not in a cathedral at noon.

**He does not ask whether you are strong. He asks when you are not.** Which hour of your week would you attack, if you were him?

### Tailored Temptation

And Jesus adds a striking detail: "Behold, the devil shall cast some of you into prison, that ye may be tried" (Revelation 2:10). And to Peter: "Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat" (Luke 22:31).

Sifting — a process designed for a specific crop.

**The pressure is usually shaped to you, which is why generic advice often fails.**

## What He Does Not Know

### Not Omniscient

And the file has limits. He cannot read your thoughts, and he cannot know the future — he works by observation and inference.

That is why prophecy frightened him so much in the ancient world: he was watching a plan he only partly understood.

**He predicts. He does not know.**

### Not Irresistible

And more importantly, his data does not determine your outcome. He knows your history; he does not determine your next choice.

That gap between the file and the decision is where faith lives.

**The file is a prediction. Faith is the variable he cannot model.** So why live as though the file were the verdict?

### Often Simply Wrong

And he miscalculates. He miscalculated at the cross — "Which none of the princes of this world knew: for had they known it, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory" (1 Corinthians 2:8).

The triumph was delivered through what he was sure was a victory.

**The wisest thing he ever did was his greatest strategic error.**

### What You Should Study

### Not Him

And the instruction is not to study the enemy. Indeed, James says to be sober and vigilant, but then says *resist* — not investigate (James 4:7).

There is a fascination with the darkness that is a form of self-harm.

**Curiosity about the enemy is not the same as preparedness for him.**

### The Word

And the study is of the Word: "Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee" (Psalm 119:11).

*Hid* is *ṣāpan*, to store up — the way a household keeps what it cannot buy in a crisis.

What is stored in you will surface under pressure, and nothing else will.

### Your Own Patterns

And a third study is honest self-examination. Know where you fall and when. That is humility, not morbidity.

The only way to close a gap is to have looked at it.

### Fighting With What Is Stored

### Jesus' Method

And in the wilderness, the Son of God answered a personal assault with three quotations (Matthew 4:1–11).

Not arguments. Not rebukes. The Scripture he had stored.

The weapon was not creativity. It was storage.

### The Spirit and the Word

And the sword of the Spirit is explicitly the Word of God (Ephesians 6:17) — a strange combination, a weapon named for the Spirit but identified as a text.

The Spirit will bring it up, but He will only bring up what is there.

The one who gave you the sword does not swing it for you.

### The Engaged Mind

And this is why reading is not a spiritual luxury. John's letters keep tying love for God to keeping His commandments, and you cannot keep what you do not know.

A believer who does not read is a soldier who does not drill.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, studying the Word is not a way to control God. The Word is not a phrase to be deployed for outcomes; it is a Person to be obeyed.

Second, this is not a call to isolation. Paul says the assembly is where the body is built up and where each member supplies what the others lack (Ephesians 4:16).

**The **The Word of God** is the store; **Meditation in the Word is how it gets into the store; and Spiritual Warfare is what happens when it comes out under pressure.

## What This Means for You

1. Stop trying to out-think the enemy. Study the Word.

2. Learn your own patterns honestly.

**3. Memorise three verses** for your three favourite temptations.

4. Read a Psalm aloud each day this week.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Truth applied is more use than truth stored. **The Word of God** is the deposit; wisdom is knowing how to take it out and use it in a conversation.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Matthew 4:1–11 and mark the three quotations.**
- **Write down your three recurring temptations.**
- **Find a verse for each.**
- **Memorise one this week.**
- **Read Psalm 119:97–112 and count the vocabulary of love for the Word.**

**The central truth, said plainly:** You are being studied, and that is not a reason for fear but for study of your own — because the enemy's knowledge of your patterns is finite and often wrong, while the Word you take up is neither.

**Declare this today:**

> "Father, give me a mind filled with Your Word rather than a mind obsessed with the enemy. Let Your truth be the store I draw from in the hour of pressure, and let no scheme of darkness find me unarmed, in Jesus' Name. Amen."
