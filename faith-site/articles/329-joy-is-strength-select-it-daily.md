Nehemiah 8:10 is one of the strangest pastoral instructions in Scripture, delivered at the most emotionally inappropriate moment imaginable.

"Neither be ye sorry; for the joy of the LORD is your strength."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Nehemiah spoke to people who were weeping under conviction and told them to eat, share, and stop grieving — because joy in this verse is not a reward for the strong, it is the supply that makes anybody strong.**

## The Scene

### A Weeping Congregation

And the people were crying because the law had been read aloud for hours and they had understood it (Nehemiah 8:9). It was conviction, and it was honest.

Their reaction was the correct one. And Nehemiah told them to stop.

**Sometimes the most spiritual thing you can do is eat something.** So who told you sadness was the holier posture?

### The Instruction

And the instruction is physical: eat the fat, drink the sweet, and send portions to those who have nothing prepared.

It was a feast with a delivery service.

**Repentance that never reaches the table has not finished its work.**

### The Reason

And the reason given is not "because you deserve it." It is "for this day is holy" and "the joy of the LORD is your strength."

The joy is the reason they can stand, not the proof that they have.

**Strength is the gift. Joy is the channel.** And a blocked channel is a weak person.

## The Word

### Ḥedwāh

And the word is *ḥedwāh* — gladness, joy, the joy that is His. Not a manufactured cheerfulness but His own gladness.

The phrase means the joy that belongs to the Lord, and it is being lent to them.

**You are being strengthened by somebody else's happiness.**

### Strength

And *māʿôz* — a place of strength, a fortress, a refuge. Not "energy," but a safe place.

Joy is shelter.

**The glad person has somewhere to stand.**

### Not Sorrow

And the command is *al-tēʿāṣēbû* — do not grieve. It is an imperative, addressed to people who had every emotional reason to be sad.

Which tells you that grief in this context is a choice they were being asked to lay down for a day.

**There is a sorrow that is honest, and a sorrow that has become a habit.**

### Why Joy Is a Decision

### Because It Is Commanded

And you cannot command a feeling. Yet Scripture commands rejoicing repeatedly (Philippians 4:4; 1 Thessalonians 5:16).

Which means joy is an act of the will before it is a sensation.

**A command implies a choice. A choice implies a chooser.** So who is choosing yours?

### Because It Is an Assessment

And joy is a verdict about reality, not a reaction to circumstance. Habakkuk proves it: though the fig tree does not blossom and the fields yield nothing, "yet I will rejoice in the LORD" (Habakkuk 3:18).

He names the loss first, and then chooses.

Joy is what you do with the facts, not what the facts do to you.

### Because It Is Contagious

And there is a reason Nehemiah attached a delivery to the feast. Joy that stays in one house is a mood; joy that is sent to somebody with nothing prepared becomes a supply line.

The instruction was not only "be glad." It was "make somebody else's day holy too."

Gladness multiplies when it is carried, and evaporates when it is hoarded.

### Because It Is Fruit

And Galatians calls it fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22) — which means it grows rather than being manufactured, but it also means the tree is involved.

You cannot produce fruit. You can stay in the conditions where it ripens.

Joy is not achieved. It is cultivated.

### Choosing It

### Rehearse What Is True

And the first practice is to state the reasons. Joy that has no content is a mood; joy that has reasons is a foundation.

Name three things that are true today regardless of how you feel.

**Gladness needs a text.**

### Eat and Share

And the second is Nehemiah's own method, which is embarrassingly practical. Food and generosity are part of the prescription.

The body is not the enemy of the spirit.

There is a reason the kingdom is described as a feast.

### Do It Before You Feel It

And the third is the order: obey first. Sing before the feeling arrives, and the feeling frequently follows.

David's psalms are full of a man telling his own soul what to do.

"Bless the LORD, O my soul" is an instruction, not a report.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not the suppression of grief. Jesus wept, and Nehemiah himself wept earlier in the book. Lament is biblical; chronic gloom is what is being addressed.

Second, this is not a demand to perform happiness for other people's comfort. Nobody should be told to smile at a funeral.

**The **The Kingdom of God is a kingdom of gladness; The Life of the Spirit is where fruit grows; and The Life of Faith is the choice to act on what is true before it feels true.

## What This Means for You

**1. Read Nehemiah 8:1–12 twice.**

**2. Name three true reasons for gladness today.**

3. Eat something good and share something.

**4. Sing before you feel it.**

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Joy is not a substitute for grief work or care. **The Kingdom of God** is joy; wisdom knows when to laugh and when to sit with somebody who is weeping.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Habakkuk 3:17–19.**
- **Read Psalm 16:5–11.**
- **Write down your three reasons.**
- **Do one glad thing that costs nothing.**
- **Give something away** and watch your face.

**The central truth, said plainly:** Nehemiah spoke to people who were weeping under conviction and told them to eat, share, and stop grieving — because joy in this verse is not a reward for the strong, it is the supply that makes anybody strong.

**Declare this today:**

> "Father, let the joy of the Lord be my strength today and not my reward for having a good week. Teach me to choose it before I feel it, to eat and share and rejoice, and to be strengthened by Your gladness, in Jesus' Name. Amen."
