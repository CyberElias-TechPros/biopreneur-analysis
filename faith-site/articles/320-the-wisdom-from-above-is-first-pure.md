James 3:17 is a list, and lists in Scripture are almost always ordered on purpose.

"But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **James gives seven marks of heavenly wisdom and puts purity first because the order is a test — a plan that is clever, effective, and slightly dirty fails at the first word, no matter how well the rest of the sentence reads.**

## The Contrast

### Three Kinds of Wrong Wisdom

And James has just named them: "earthly, sensual, devilish" (James 3:15) — *epigeios*, *psychikē*, *daimoniōdēs*.

Earthly: it works only in this world's terms. Sensual: it comes from appetite. Devilish: it is willing to do what heaven would not.

**Wisdom that only needs to work is not wisdom yet.**

### The Evidence of the Wrong Kind

And James gives the diagnostic in verse 16: "where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work."

The test is relational. Wisdom from below leaves a mess in the room.

**You can tell the source by the aftermath.**

### The Question

And verse 13 asks the question that sets up the whole passage: "Who is a wise man and endued with knowledge among you? let him shew out of a good conversation his works with meekness of wisdom."

The proof is a manner, not an argument.

**Wisdom is demonstrated, not debated.**

## The Seven Marks

### Pure

And *hagnē* — chaste, clean, undivided. The word is used of moral purity, and the root idea is single-mindedness.

It is not primarily about sexuality; it is about a plan with nothing hidden in it.

**The first question is not "will this work?" but "is this clean?"**

### Peaceable

And *eirēnikē* — peace-making, not merely peace-keeping. It produces peace rather than preserving a nervous quiet.

A plan that requires constant conflict management is not from above.

**Peace is a product here, not a mood.**

### Gentle

And *epieikēs* — equitable, forbearing, reasonable. The word that describes somebody who does not insist on their rights.

It is the quality of a person who could win and chooses to be kind.

**Gentleness is strength with the brakes on.**

### Easy to Be Intreated

And *eupeithēs* — compliant, open to persuasion. Literally "easily persuaded."

Which is a strange mark for wisdom, until you notice that the wise person is the one who can still be talked out of a plan.

**A mind that cannot be corrected is not a wise mind, however accurate it is.**

### Full of Mercy and Good Fruits

And the fifth and sixth are a pair: mercy as a disposition and good fruits as the evidence.

Mercy that never produces anything is sentiment. Fruit without mercy is administration.

**Heavenly wisdom leaves a trail of people who were helped.**

### Without Partiality, Without Hypocrisy

And the last two are *adiakritos* and *anypokritos* — undivided and unpretended. No favouritism, no performance.

The plan does not change depending on who is watching.

**Wisdom is the same in every room.**

### Why Purity First

### Because Order Is a Test

And James does not say "pure and also peaceable." He says *first* pure, *then* the rest. The sequence is deliberate.

A plan that is peaceable, gentle, and fruitful but not clean is not wisdom. It is a very well-managed compromise.

The first word governs the list.

### Because Motive Contaminates Method

And a mixed motive eventually shows up in the execution. The plan that needed a small dishonesty at the start will need a larger one later.

Purity is not a decoration on wisdom. It is the load-bearing wall.

Nothing built on a lie stays level for long.

### Because God Tests the Heart

And the reason is simply that God looks at the heart (1 Samuel 16:7). A strategy that is impressive and impure is being evaluated by a different examiner.

Men grade the plan. He grades the planner.

### Testing Your Plans

### Ask the First Question

And the practical discipline is to apply the list in order. Purity first, and do not go on to the others until the first one is settled.

Most of our bad decisions would have been caught at the first word.

If the answer to "is this clean?" is anything but yes, stop there.

### Ask Who It Makes Peace With

And the second question is whether the plan produces peace or merely postpones a fight. Some plans buy quiet with a compromise.

Deferred conflict is not peace. It is a debt.

### Ask Whether You Could Be Talked Out of It

And the third is the humility test. If nothing could change your mind, you are not exercising wisdom; you are defending a preference.

A plan that cannot be questioned is a plan that cannot be corrected.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a call to indecision. *Eupeithēs* is teachability, not paralysis; wisdom still decides.

Second, this is not a demand for sinless strategy. It is a call to honest motive, which means being willing to abandon a plan the moment you see the stain in it.

**The **Spiritual Understanding is the faculty that receives this wisdom; Faith and Wisdom is carrying it into a decision; and Renewing of the Mind is how the thinking gets clean enough to hold it.

## What This Means for You

1. Read James 3:13–18 once a day this week.

2. Take the biggest decision in front of you.

3. Apply the seven marks in order.

4. Stop at the first one that fails.

5. Keep faith and wisdom together. Purity does not guarantee a good outcome in this life, and a clean plan still needs counting. **Spiritual Understanding** is the discernment; wisdom is the spreadsheet.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Proverbs 2:1–9.**
- **Read 1 Corinthians 1:18–31.**
- **Write out the seven marks.**
- **Score one plan against them.**
- **Abandon whatever fails the first.**
