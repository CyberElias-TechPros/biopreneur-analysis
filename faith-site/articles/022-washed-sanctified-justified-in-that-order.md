Have you ever wondered whether God changes your behaviour first and then accepts you, or accepts you first and then changes your behaviour? The order matters more than most believers realise, because a wrong order produces a lifetime of striving.

Paul answers with three past-tense verbs in 1 Corinthians 6:11: "but ye **are washed**, but ye **are sanctified**, but ye **are justified** in the name of the Lord Jesus." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **God's order is washed, sanctified, justified — cleansing, setting apart, and right standing all accomplished in Christ, so your identity is settled before your behaviour is changed.**

## Three Verbs, One Finished Work

Paul writes to a church that had come out of serious sin, and he does not soften the contrast: "and such were some of you." Then he pivots with the strongest possible conjunction — *but*. Everything that follows is stated in the past tense, accomplished, not pending.

**Washed** speaks of cleansing: the defilement of the old life removed, not managed. **Justification** — God's legal act of declaring you righteous in Christ, pronouncing you not guilty and granting you right standing — settles your record before God. Sandwiched between them, **sanctified** speaks of being set apart as holy to God. Three acts, one work, all accomplished "in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God."

**The Name of Jesus** — His person, work, and authority — is the ground of all three. **Righteousness** — right standing with God, received as a gift in Christ — is the outcome of justification. You were not made clean to be justified; you were justified in the One who is clean, and His cleansing reaches your whole life.

Think of a patient wheeled into surgery already declared eligible for treatment. The declaration of eligibility comes before the healing, and the healing unfolds because the declaration was made. God's order is similar: the verdict precedes the transformation, and the transformation flows from the verdict.

### Why the Order Never Reverses

Try reversing the order and see what happens. If sanctification came before justification, your holiness would have to earn your verdict — and no holiness of yours could ever be sufficient. If justification came before washing, you would be declared righteous while still defiled, which would make God's verdict dishonest.

God's order is both gracious and honest. He washes because cleansing is needed. And what would a verdict be worth if it rested on a holiness you manufactured yourself? He sanctifies because belonging is required. He justifies because a verdict must be pronounced. And He does all three in Christ, so none depends on your performance and all rest on His.

This is **Justification** at work — a legal declaration, not a moral improvement programme. The judge has ruled; the family has received you. Your behaviour now grows into a verdict already announced, rather than straining to produce one.

Watch what separates the two: The self-improvement life tries to become acceptable and hopes for a verdict later. The gospel life receives the verdict now and grows into it daily. One lives under uncertainty; the other under a settled sentence of righteousness.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **Romans 3:24** — "Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus." Paul uses *freely* — without cause in you, without cost to you — and grounds it in Christ's redemption. Justification is a gift, not a wage.
- **Titus 3:5–7** — "Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration... that being justified by his grace, we should be made heirs." Paul's order matches: washing, renewing, justifying, heirship — all by grace.
- **Hebrews 10:10** — "By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all." Sanctification is accomplished by Christ's offering, not by your resolve, which is why the order can be stated in the past tense.

Three witnesses, one order: washed, sanctified, justified — cleansing, belonging, and verdict, all in Christ.

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop trying to reverse God's order.** If you are working to earn a verdict, you are attempting what Christ already accomplished. Receive the verdict, then let your conduct grow from it. Identity first; behaviour follows.

**2. Treat your past as past.** Paul's phrase "such were some of you" is deliberate: it is a former identity, not a current one. When old sin patterns resurface, answer with the past tense of verse 11 — washed, sanctified, justified.

**3. Let all three verbs shape your self-talk.** When shame says dirty, answer washed. When aimlessness says nobody's, answer sanctified. When accusation says guilty, answer justified. Each answer is a Scripture, not a sentiment.

**4. Faith and wisdom walk out the three verbs practically.** Faith declares what God has done; wisdom stewards the body, speech, money, and relationships as belonging to a washed, sanctified, justified person. Declaring grace never excuses sin; it makes holiness the natural family conduct.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 1 Corinthians 6:9–11 in full.** Notice the contrast between "such were" and "but ye are," then write the three verbs somewhere you will see them daily.
- **Answer one accusation with the right verb.** This week, identify whether shame is attacking your cleansing, your belonging, or your verdict — then answer with washed, sanctified, or justified accordingly.
- **Practise one act of purity as family conduct.** Choose a concrete area this week and act as someone already washed and set apart, not to earn standing but to express it.
- **Thank God for the order.** Each morning this week, thank the Father that He settled your identity before He asked for your behaviour, and let gratitude fuel your obedience.
