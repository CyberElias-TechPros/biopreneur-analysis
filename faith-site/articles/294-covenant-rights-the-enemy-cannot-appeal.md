Most believers pray as though they were applying for something. The New Testament describes them as holding a deed.

"But now hath he obtained a more excellent ministry, by how much also he is the mediator of a better covenant, which was established upon better promises" (Hebrews 8:6).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **a covenant is a binding legal instrument, not a warm sentiment — which means the believer's standing is not a favour to be begged for but a contract with a mediator who has already signed.**

## What a Covenant Was

### A Cutting

And the Hebrew idiom for making a covenant is *kārat bĕrît* — to cut a covenant. The ceremony involved slaughtered animals and a walk between the pieces.

Genesis 15 is the decisive scene: Abraham prepared the animals, and then a deep sleep fell on him, and God alone passed between the pieces (Genesis 15:17).

**In the covenant that mattered most, only one party walked. God took both sides of the risk.**

### Binding on Both

And ordinarily a covenant bound both parties with obligations and penalties. It was witnessed, recorded, and public.

It was closer to a treaty than to a promise.

**A covenant was the most serious instrument the ancient world knew how to make.** So when God wanted to be believed, He did not merely speak. He cut.

### Not Disannulled

And Paul's argument in Galatians assumes its legal force: "Though it be but a man's covenant, yet if it be confirmed, no man disannulleth, or addeth thereto" (Galatians 3:15).

Even a human contract, once ratified, cannot be edited.

**If men honour their contracts, how much more does God honour His?** And the covenant He swore is not written on stone tablets in a distant archive. It is written on the heart, and it is administered by a living Mediator who never stops representing His people.

That is why the writer to the Hebrews calls it better. Not newer simply because it came later, but better in kind: better promises, better sacrifice, better mediator, better permanence.

### One Covenant, Two Administrations

And there is a further clarity worth holding. The new covenant is not a repair of the old; it is its fulfilment. The law told a man what he owed. The covenant in blood tells him what he has been given, and then writes the same law inside him so that he wants it.

**The old covenant demanded. The new covenant supplies.**

## The Better Covenant

### Better Promises

And Hebrews' comparison is not between a bad covenant and a good one, but between a provisional one and a permanent one.

The first was ratified with the blood of animals; the second with the blood of Christ (Hebrews 9:12).

**Better promises require a better sacrifice, and a better sacrifice secures better promises.**

### A Better Mediator

And the mediator is not a priest who offers for his own sins first. He is the Son who offered Himself once.

"Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them" (Hebrews 7:25).

**The Mediator is not a third party. He is the reason the contract exists.**

### An Oath

And God added an oath to the promise, "that by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation" (Hebrews 6:18).

Two immutable things: the promise and the oath.

God did not merely promise. He swore, which is a strange and wonderful condescension.

### What the Covenant Secures

### Righteousness

And the first term is standing. "For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him" (2 Corinthians 5:21).

Not a loan of righteousness. An exchange.

### Access

And the second is entry. "By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil" (Hebrews 10:20).

The terms of the covenant include the right to come in.

### Inheritance

And the third is property. "And if ye be Christ's, then are ye Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise" (Galatians 3:29).

An heir does not apply. An heir already owns.

### Protection

And the fourth is covering, which the passover established and the cross confirmed.

A covenant does not merely define a relationship. It defines an estate.

### Living From the Terms

### Read the Document

And the practical instruction is to know what the covenant actually says. Most believers live on rumours of it.

The New Testament is the covenant document. Read it as one.

### Do Not Renegotiate

And do not offer to earn what the covenant grants. That is not humility; it is an insult to the Mediator.

You cannot improve a contract that God Himself drafted.

### Appeal to the Mediator

And when the accuser quotes the terms, quote the Mediator. "Who is he that condemneth? It is Christ that died" (Romans 8:34).

The defence is not your performance. It is His.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, a covenant has obligations as well as benefits. The new covenant writes God's law on the heart (Hebrews 8:10) — it does not abolish obedience; it internalises it.

Second, this is not a doctrine of entitlement. The covenant is a gift, and a gift received with a demand is no longer received as a gift.

**The **The Blood of Jesus** is the ratification; the **The Inheritance of the Believer is the estate; and the The Authority of the Believer is the right to act on the terms.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Hebrews 8 in full.

2. Write down three things the covenant secures for you.

**3. Replace one begging prayer with a believing one.**

**4. Read Genesis 15 and note who walked between the pieces.**

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** A deed is a fact; living in the house is a practice. **The Inheritance of the Believer** is yours; wisdom decides what you do with the property.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Hebrews 8:1–13 three times.**
- **Read Galatians 3:15–29.**
- **Write out Hebrews 8:6.**
- **List what you have been asking for that is already deeded.**
- **Thank God for one of them out loud.**
