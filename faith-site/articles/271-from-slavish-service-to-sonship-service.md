The parable of the two sons is usually preached as a story about a wild younger brother, and it is really a story about two employees.

The younger one wanted the estate without the father. The older one served in the estate without the relationship.

"Wherefore thou art no more a servant, but a son; and if a son, then an heir of God through Christ" (Galatians 4:7).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the work does not change when a servant becomes a son; the reason does — and that single change alters the durability, the joy, and the outcome of everything he does.**

## The Older Brother

### He Was in the Field

And Luke is careful about where he places him: "Now his elder son was in the field: and as he came and drew nigh to the house, he heard musick and dancing" (Luke 15:25).

He was doing the work. He was not in the party.

**It is possible to be near the house and absent from the joy of it.**

### His Complaint

And when he heard the reason, "he was angry, and would not go in" (Luke 15:28). And then the sentence that gives away the whole condition: "Lo, these many years do I serve thee, neither transgressed I at any time thy commandment: and yet thou never gavest me a kid" (Luke 15:29).

*Do I serve thee* — the Greek is *douleuō*, to serve as a slave. He had been a servant in his own father's house.

**The older brother had a slave's contract and a son's address.**

### What He Did Not Know

And the father's answer is one of the tenderest lines in Scripture: "Son, thou art ever with me, and all that I have is thine" (Luke 15:31).

He had never asked. He had access to everything and had been negotiating for a goat.

**The tragedy of the servant heart is not what it does. It is what it never asks for.**

## Two Kinds of Service

### Service for Acceptance

And slavish service is always transactional, even when it is sincere. It works to secure a standing it does not believe it has.

Its marks: comparison, resentment, exhaustion, and a strange anger at grace shown to others.

**A servant serving for acceptance will always resent a son who is accepted for free.**

### Service From Acceptance

And sonship service begins after the standing is settled. It works because the relationship is secure, not in order to make it so.

Its marks: willingness, honesty about limits, joy, and the capacity to celebrate someone else's blessing.

**Sons work differently because they are not paying anything.**

### The Same Tasks

And here is the important part: from the outside, the two can be indistinguishable for years. Same field, same hours, same calluses.

**God is not primarily measuring output. He is measuring the heart the output came from.** So what would your service look like if nothing were owed?

### Why the Difference Matters

### It Changes Durability

And slavish service burns out, because a debt that never clears produces labour that never stops.

Sonship service can rest, because the work is not what is holding the house up.

Only a son can take a day off without guilt.

### It Changes Honesty

And a servant hides failure, because failure threatens the arrangement. A son reports it, because the relationship is not on the line.

Sons can say "I got this wrong" without fearing the consequences of saying it.

### It Changes Reward

And the inheritance is not wages. A wage is proportionate to work; an inheritance is a share of a family.

Which is why the reward of sonship is not "more work, better paid."

The son does not get a bigger goat. He gets the estate.

### It Changes Joy

And the father's house had music playing, and the older brother could hear it from the field and did not know what it was for.

Joy in God is difficult for anyone who thinks God owes him a wage.

### Living as a Son

### Start From the Standing

And the practical instruction is to begin every day from the finished position, not toward it.

What you do is a response to what you have been given.

### Ask

And learn to ask. The older brother's whole problem surfaces in a sentence he never said: he wanted a feast and never requested one.

Sons are not shy about the family accounts.

### Serve Without an Audience

And do something nobody sees, and notice how it feels. Servants need witnesses, because service is their currency.

The test of sonship is whether you can work unobserved without resentment.

### Rest Without Guilt

And take the rest. The Sabbath was given to sons, not extracted from servants (Mark 2:27).

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, sonship is not passivity. Hebrews calls us "labourers" and Revelation calls us overcomers. Sons work hard; they just do not work scared.

Second, sonship is not familiarity without reverence. The same Galatians that says "a son" also says God sent the Spirit of His Son (Galatians 4:6). Family, not equality.

**The **Sonship** is the standing; **The Inheritance of the Believer is what the standing holds; and The New Birth is the reason neither was ever a wage.

## What This Means for You

1. Listen to your language about serving. Does it sound like a contract or a family?

2. Name one thing you have been working for that has already been given.

**3. Ask for something this week** you have been quietly resenting not having.

**4. Do one task with no recognition** and no mention of it.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Sonship changes the motive; discipline still builds the habit. **Sonship** gives the security, and wisdom decides how a secure person spends his week.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Luke 15:11–32 twice** — once from the younger son's side, once from the older.
- **Read Galatians 4:1–7 daily.**
- **Write out the older brother's complaint in your own words.**
- **List the things you have never asked for.**
- **Take a genuine rest day** with no productivity attached.
