Do you ever pray as though God were still deciding whether you qualify — as though one more good week might finally complete you? Many believers love God sincerely yet live with a quiet sense of spiritual incompleteness: forgiven enough to be saved, perhaps, but not complete enough to be confident.

Colossians 2:10 answers that ache with a settled present tense: "ye **are** complete in him." Not becoming complete. Not hoping to be complete. **Are.** Here is the central truth of this teaching: **in Christ you are not partially complete or still under construction — you are complete in Him, and His fullness supplies what human effort never could.**

## The Fullness That Fills You

Paul immediately grounds the statement in verse 9: "For in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily." The Greek word *pleroma* means fullness, full measure, nothing lacking. Everything of God — His life, wisdom, grace, and power — dwells in Christ without dilution. And because you are **in Him**, that fullness is not merely available to you at a distance; it is the environment in which you live.

This is **the Inheritance of the Believer** — the covenantal estate that belongs to every child of God in Christ, including forgiveness, acceptance, sonship, and supply. An inheritance is not earned by the heir's performance after the testator's death; it is received by legal relationship. You did not inherit Christ's fullness by climbing toward it. You received it by union with Him.

Think of a young tree planted in rich soil beside a river. The tree does not manufacture water, minerals, and sunlight by straining upward. It draws from the soil and river that surround its roots. Completeness in Christ works the same way: you do not produce fullness by effort; you draw from the fullness in which you are rooted. Effort has its place in tending the tree — watering, pruning, protecting — but effort never creates the river.

### Complete Is a Position, Not a Mood

Notice the contrast Paul sets up. Religion measures completeness by recent performance: complete on good days, incomplete on hard days. The Word measures completeness by union with Christ: complete in Him on every day, because His fullness does not fluctuate with your mood. **Spiritual Realities** — unseen covenantal facts established by God's Word that endure longer than temporary circumstances — are exactly this kind of truth. Your feelings report the weather. Colossians 2:10 reports the climate.

Why does this matter so much? Because lack-consciousness quietly governs prayer, giving, and courage. A believer who thinks she is almost complete prays apologetically, gives sparingly, and shrinks from opportunities to serve. A believer who knows she is complete in Him prays boldly, gives cheerfully, and serves from supply rather than from strain. Completeness is not arrogance; it is agreement with Scripture.

And look at the title Paul gives Christ in the same verse: "the head of all principality and power." Your completeness is not in a system, a personality, or a denomination. It is in the risen Head over every power that could accuse you. Nothing missing. Nothing broken. Nothing left for darkness to claim.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **Ephesians 1:3** — "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who hath blessed us with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ." Paul's word is *all* — not some, not a portion. Every spiritual blessing God has for His children is already located in Christ, where you already are.
- **John 1:16** — "And of his fulness have all we received, and grace for grace." The fullness in Christ is not locked away from believers; it is received from Him, grace upon grace, like wave after wave from the same ocean.
- **2 Peter 1:3** — "According as his divine power hath given unto us all things that pertain unto life and godliness." Peter uses the same completeness language: *all things* pertaining to life and godliness have been given through knowledge of Him. Your supply for living godly is not pending; it is given.

Three apostles, one fullness: in Christ you lack nothing that pertains to life and godliness.

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop praying from lack; start praying from supply.** You do not approach your Father as a beggar hoping to be completed. You approach Him as a complete child in Christ, asking for wisdom and grace to express what is already yours. Prayer becomes fellowship and alignment, not negotiation.

**2. Your growth is expression, not construction.** Completeness does not mean you stop growing. It means growth is no longer construction of a missing foundation; it is the steady expression of a finished one. You renew your mind, practise holiness, and serve diligently — not to become complete, but because you are.

**3. Refuse the comparison trap.** Completeness in Christ ends the exhausting habit of measuring your spiritual worth against another believer's gifts, testimony, or platform. Their fullness is theirs; yours is in the same Christ. Comparison always produces lack-consciousness. Union with Christ produces contentment and courage.

**4. Faith and wisdom guard this truth.** Faith declares you complete in Him; wisdom stewards that completeness with diligence — honest work, careful speech, healthy rest, and faithful giving. Declaring wholeness never excuses laziness or neglect. It empowers responsible, joyful stewardship of the life God has given you.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Pray one prayer from completeness this week.** Instead of asking God to make you acceptable, thank Him that you are complete in Christ, then ask for spiritual understanding to live out one specific implication at home or at work.
- **Read Colossians 2:9–15 slowly and mark every "in Him" phrase.** Notice how Paul stacks union language — in Him, in Him, in Him — and let each one answer a specific area where you have felt incomplete.
- **Give from fullness, not from fear.** Choose one act of generosity this week — time, money, encouragement, or service — and give it as a complete person in Christ, not as a person hoping generosity will complete you.
- **Retire one lack sentence.** Whenever you hear yourself say, "I am just not enough," replace it with, "In Christ I am complete; I am learning to express His fullness in this situation."
