The psalmist asks his own soul a question, and it is a question worth stealing.

"Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted in me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him" (Psalm 42:5).

Note the structure. He does not deny the feeling. He questions it, and then he addresses it.

"For we walk by faith, not by sight" (2 Corinthians 5:7).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **your standing is a fact recorded in heaven; your feelings are a report on your nervous system — and a believer who confuses the two will spend a life being governed by the weather instead of the title deed.**

## Two Different Kinds of Thing

### A Fact

Your **Legal Position** in Christ is a fact. It was established at the cross, announced in the gospel, and recorded in a place no file cabinet rots.

It does not have a temperature. It does not have a mood.

**A fact is true whether or not you are currently experiencing it.**

### A Report

Feelings are data, and useful data — but about a different subject. They report your state, not your standing.

A man in a storm can feel seasick on a ship that is not sinking.

**Weather is real without being authoritative.** A forecast tells you what to wear. It does not tell you where you live.

### The Third Category

There is also a third thing, and it is the one most often mistaken for a verdict: the *interpretation* of a feeling. Two believers feel the same heaviness; one concludes God has withdrawn, the other concludes he needs sleep and a psalm. Same sensation, opposite lives.

**The feeling is rarely the problem. The story attached to it usually is.**

### Why the Confusion Is So Common

Because feelings are loud and facts are quiet, and the loud one usually wins the argument.

Add to that a church culture that sometimes equates spiritual intensity with spiritual condition, and the confusion becomes doctrine.

**We are not called to feel the truth. We are called to walk in it.**

## What Feelings Are Good For

### They Are Information

And they should be read. Anxiety says something is being feared. Numbness says something is being avoided. Anger often guards something loved.

Discarding feelings is not maturity.

**The error is not feeling. The error is delegating the verdict to the feeling.**

### They Are Affected by the Body

And a great deal of what we call spiritual weather is physiological — sleep, blood sugar, hormones, grief, illness.

Elijah's suicidal despair was treated with food and sleep (1 Kings 19:5–7), which is a remarkably practical piece of pastoral care.

Sometimes the spiritual problem is a nap. That is not a reduction of the spiritual life. It is an honest reading of how God made a body to work.

### They Can Be Trained

And while feelings are not the authority, they are not fixed. What you rehearse, what you worship, and what you believe reshape them over time.

That is what the renewing of the mind is doing in the emotional life.

Feelings are a sail, not a rudder. A sail can move you quickly, and a sail can be trimmed. It cannot decide the destination.

### Living From the Standing

### Speak to the Soul

And the Psalmist's method is still the right one. He addresses the downcast soul rather than obeying it.

That is not denial. It is a man taking his own hand off the wheel.

You are not your mood's secretary. You are its pastor.

### Return to the Record

And when the mood argues, the answer is a document: "Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect?" (Romans 8:33).

Not "I feel accepted" but "God has justified."

The answer to a feeling is never a bigger feeling. It is a fact.

### Expect the Gap

And accept that there will be a lag. You will often have to act on the truth for some time before you feel it.

Faith is the discipline of that gap.

Walking by faith does not mean ignoring your legs. It means trusting the map they are on.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not stoicism. Christians are commanded to feel — to weep with those who weep (Romans 12:15). The point is not numbness; it is order.

Second, this does not make feelings trivial. Persistent despair is not just a doctrine problem, and it should be brought to God and, where needed, to people who can help.

**The **The Life of Faith** walks on facts; **Righteousness is the fact it walks on, and the feelings follow at whatever pace they can manage.

## What This Means for You

1. Name the difference today. When a low mood arrives, ask: is this a report or a verdict?

**2. Answer with Romans 8:33** rather than with a bigger feeling.

**3. Check the physical first.** Sleep, food, and health are not unspiritual.

**4. Read Psalm 42** and copy the way the psalmist talks to himself.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Faith acts on the record; wisdom reads the body. **Legal Position** is the fact; the daily care of your frame is the stewardship of it.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Psalm 42:1–11 each morning.**
- **Write down five things that are true regardless of mood.**
- **Write down five current feelings.**
- **Put the two lists side by side.**
- **Act on one item from the first list today.**
