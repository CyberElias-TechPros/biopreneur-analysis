Most believers have quietly negotiated a lower standard for their bodies than the one the gospel announces, and they have done it so gradually they no longer notice the downgrade.

"Beloved, I wish above all things that thou mayest prosper and be in health, even as thy soul prospereth" (3 John 2).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **divine health is not the extraordinary exception granted to a favoured few but the ordinary baseline God established in redemption, and every doctrine is judged by what it makes normal.**

## The Verse's Own Standard

### An Apostolic Wish

Notice who is writing and what he is doing. John, the apostle who walked with Jesus and leaned on His breast, opens a personal letter with a wish for the recipient's body.

Not a command, and not a formula — a wish, *euchomai*, the language of desire. But look at the shape of it: he desires that Gaius' body would be as well as his soul.

That comparison is the key. John does not set the body's health above the soul's prosperity; he uses the soul's prosperity as the measure of what he wants for the body.

So the question becomes: what does John assume about the soul of a believer? That it is prospering. And if that is the measure, what does he want for the body? The same.

### "Be in health"

The word is *hygiainein* — to be sound, to be healthy, the root of "hygiene." It is the word used of the man with the withered hand when Jesus restored it (Mark 3:5, in the related form), and of the whole body being sound rather than full of darkness (Matthew 6:22–23).

*Hugiainō* is not a dramatic word. It is the word for **soundness** — the ordinary functioning of an ordinary body.

That is significant. The apostle's wish for Gaius is not for a spectacular miracle. **It is for normal health**, and he treats that as a thing worth asking for as a first priority.

### "Above All Things"

The phrase *peri pantōn* is better rendered "in all things" or "concerning all things," and translators have long debated whether John means "before anything else" or "with regard to everything."

Either way, his priority is on record: in a letter about a church conflict, hospitality, and a difficult man named Diotrephes, the first thing John writes is a wish for Gaius' health.

**An apostle for whom doctrine mattered did not think body-talk was beneath the gospel.**

### The Pattern in the Gospels

And John's wish is not a personal preference. It is the pattern of Jesus' ministry, and the pattern tells you what the kingdom considers normal.

Matthew summarises it: "And Jesus went about all Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, and preaching the gospel of the kingdom, and healing all manner of sickness and all manner of disease among the people" (Matthew 4:23).

"All manner." Not a selective quota. Not a demonstration case. And the crowds followed from all of Syria with their sick, "and he healed them" (Matthew 4:24).

**Ask what a kingdom looks like by what it does when it arrives.** If healing is the sign of the age to come, then health is the atmosphere of that age, and sickness is the thing being displaced.

### "The Higher Normal"

Which brings the phrase in the title, and it needs defining because it can sound like a claim to never be ill.

Divine health is not a promise that a believer will never feel a symptom. It is the **established standard** against which any symptom is measured. It is what the finished work makes available, what the covenant names, and what the gospel announces.

Put it starkly. **Is healing the miracle, or is sickness the anomaly?**

Most Christians live as though sickness is normal and healing is the exception. The New Testament reads the other way round: wholeness is the family norm, and sickness is the intrusion that requires an explanation.

### The Life That Reaches the Body

And Paul gives the mechanism, which is what stops this from being merely a mood.

"But if the Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you, he that raised up Christ from the dead shall also quicken your mortal bodies by his Spirit that dwelleth in you" (Romans 8:11).

The word "quicken" is *zōopoiēsei* — to make alive, to give life to. And the object is "mortal bodies" — *thnēta sōmata*, the very bodies that are subject to death.

Read the argument: the same Spirit who raised Jesus lives in you, and His business is raising bodies. The resurrection is not a distant event with a present doctrine attached. It is a present power with a future completion.

**Divine Life** is not only for the spirit. It is *zōē*, and it is aimed at your flesh.

### The Standard and the Doctor

One honest paragraph before the illustration, because this standard is easily turned into a weapon.

If health is the higher normal, then a believer who is unwell has not sinned his way into it, and nothing here licenses anyone to say so. Job was blameless and lost his health. Timothy had "often infirmities" and Paul prescribed wine for his stomach (1 Timothy 5:23) rather than rebuking his faith.

Note both facts in that verse: the standard is real, and the pastoral response was practical. **Paul did not tell Timothy to stop being sick; he told him what to take.**

That is faith and wisdom working together, and it is the pattern this article is asking for: hold the standard, use the means, and never turn the gap into a verdict on a suffering brother.

### The Household Scale

One illustration, because the difference between a baseline and a miracle is easiest to see in a home.

Imagine a family where the parents weigh the children every month. Now imagine one family where the expectation is growth, and another where the expectation is decline, and a good month is one where the child did not lose weight.

Both families love their children. But only one has the right normal.

**A household that expects sickness will call a mild week a blessing.** A household that expects health will call a mild week a mild week, and keep looking for the growth.

That is what a **Divine Wholeness** expectation does: it changes the vocabulary of the ordinary.

### Building the Expectation

So how do you actually set your expectation at the higher normal, without pretending?

Start where the verse starts: with the soul. John measures the body's health against the soul's prosperity, which means the standard grows as you grow. A believer whose soul is thriving has a larger expectation for his body, not a smaller one.

Then read the Gospels as a description of the normal, not a museum of exceptions. Let the sheer volume of "all manner" and "he healed them all" do its work.

Then speak it. The **Confession of the Word** is not denial — it is naming the standard in the presence of the symptom. "This is not my normal" is a different sentence from "this is not happening."

And then use every means: prayer, the laying on of hands, medicine, rest, food, sleep. **The Life of the Spirit is not an alternative to the life of the body; it is the power that animates it.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Name your normal.** Write down what you actually expect for your body. Compare it with 3 John 2. The gap is your assignment.

**2. Stop calling decline a blessing.** The gratitude you offer in a hard season is right; the resignation that settles for it is not the same thing.

**3. Pray for the body as readily as the soul.** Most believers have a developed prayer life for character and almost none for their own flesh. John's first wish was health.

**4. Let the resurrection run backward into today.** Romans 8:11 says the Spirit who raised Christ is already at work in a mortal body. **Divine Health** is not wishful thinking; it is applied resurrection.

**5. Keep your standard and your humility in the same hand.** The person beside you with a long illness is not a lesser believer, and the doctor is not the enemy of the standard.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 3 John** in full — it is twenty-five verses.
- **Write your expectation down**
- **Pray Romans 8:11 daily**
- **Read Matthew 4:23–25** and count what happened when the kingdom arrived.
