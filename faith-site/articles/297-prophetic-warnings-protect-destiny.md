Acts 11 records a famine warning delivered years in advance, and the church's response is one of the most practical passages in the New Testament.

"And there stood up one of them named Agabus, and signified by the Spirit that there should be great dearth throughout all the world: which came to pass in the days of Claudius Caesar" (Acts 11:28).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **a warning from God is not fate announced in advance but a door opened early — and the difference between a warning that saves you and one that merely frightens you is what you do in the interval.**

## What a Warning Is For

### Not Information

And the famine prophecy was not given to make the church interesting. It was given so that they could buy grain.

"Then the disciples, every man according to his ability, determined to send relief unto the brethren which dwelt in Judaea" (Acts 11:29).

**A prophecy that produces no practical action has been received badly.** So what was the last thing God warned you about, and what did you do?

### Not Fate

And the famine still came. The warning did not cancel the hardship; it prepared people for it.

Which means a warning is not an escape clause. It is a head start.

**God's warnings do not always remove the trouble. They remove the ambush.**

### A Test of Attention

And there is a further purpose that is easy to miss. A warning tests whether the hearer is listening at all. Israel was warned for centuries and did not hear, and the indictment was not that God was silent.

It was that they had stopped attending.

**The warning is not only about the danger. It is about the relationship.**

### A Mercy

And the reason to warn at all is love. A God who said nothing would still be just; a God who warns is kind.

Jonah's entire reluctance was built on the fact that warnings imply mercy (Jonah 4:2).

**Every warning is an argument for God's patience rather than His severity.**

## How Warnings Come

### Through the Word

And the most reliable warning system is Scripture, which is full of them. "All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness" (2 Timothy 3:16).

The reproofs are not decoration. They are the warnings.

**A Bible you only read for comfort will not warn you.**

### Through the Spirit

And the Spirit still speaks: "And when they had fasted and prayed, and laid their hands on them, they sent them away" (Acts 13:3). Notice how much of Acts happens after waiting.

The Spirit's warnings are usually quieter than our anxieties.

**Learn the difference between a burden and a broadcast.** Most believers receive far more impressions than they can interpret, and the discipline is not to collect them but to weigh them.

### Through the Word Preached

And ordinary preaching is a warning system that nobody counts as one. A faithful sermon "reproves, rebukes, exhorts" (2 Timothy 4:2) — sometimes years before the thing it warned about arrives.

**The most common form of prophecy is a sermon you nearly skipped.** How many have you slept through?

### Through People

And through ordinary people. "Where no counsel is, the people fall: but in the multitude of counsellors there is safety" (Proverbs 11:14).

A friend who tells you the truth is a warning system with a face.

### Through Circumstances

And sometimes through the world itself. Joseph read a dream and a famine and organised Egypt (Genesis 41).

Wisdom can read a forecast without needing a vision.

### Receiving a Warning

### Test It

And the first duty is discernment. "Despise not prophesyings. Prove all things; hold fast that which is good" (1 Thessalonians 5:20–21).

Not every alarming voice is the Spirit, and some are simply the alarmist's temperament.

A warning that produces panic is usually not from God.

### Weigh the Fruit

And a genuine warning produces sober action, not chaos. Agabus's prophecy produced a collection, not a crisis meeting.

The Spirit's warnings make people calm and busy, not frantic.

### Act in Proportion

And respond in proportion to what has been said. Joseph stored grain for seven years; the Ephesian elders were warned for three years.

Some warnings require a lifetime of attention.

### Paul's Example

And Acts 21 shows a warning that was true and yet not obeyed as a direction. Agabus acted out the chains, and the disciples begged Paul not to go, and he went anyway (Acts 21:10–14).

The prophecy was accurate; the interpretation was wrong.

A warning is information. It is not always an instruction.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not fortune-telling. The purpose is preparation for obedience, not the satisfying of curiosity about tomorrow.

Second, this is not permission for control. Using "the Lord told me" to steer another person's life is a distortion, not a gift.

**The **Revelation Knowledge of God is what a warning draws on; Spiritual Understanding is what sorts it; and The Power of the Holy Spirit is what makes the response possible.

## What This Means for You

**1. Ask what the last warning you received was asking you to do.**

2. Write it down as an action, not a feeling.

3. Test it against Scripture and one wise counsellor.

**4. Act on it this week.**

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** A warning is a mercy with a deadline. **Revelation Knowledge** gives the information; wisdom decides the schedule for acting on it.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Acts 11:27–30 and Acts 21:1–16 together.**
- **Compare the two responses.**
- Write down one warning you have been ignoring.
- **Turn it into an action.**
- **Thank God for one warning that already saved you.**

**The central truth, said plainly:** A warning from God is not fate announced in advance but a door opened early — and the difference between a warning that saves you and one that merely frightens you is what you do in the interval.
