There is a reason so much Christian effort produces patched people rather than whole ones: we work in the wrong direction.

We start with the symptom. We attack the body, then the mood, then — if there is time and energy left — the spirit. Scripture begins at the other end.

"Beloved, I wish above all things that thou mayest prosper and be in health, even as thy soul prospereth" (3 John 2).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **God's work moves from the spirit outward, which is why the order — spirit, then soul, then body — is not a preference but the way wholeness actually reaches a person.**

## The Order in Scripture

### The Inner Man First

Paul's prayer for the Ephesians names the sequence with unusual precision: "That he would grant you, according to the riches of his glory, to be strengthened with might by his Spirit in the inner man; that Christ may dwell in your hearts by faith" (Ephesians 3:16–17).

The request is not for better circumstances. It is for strength in the *inner man* — and everything else in the prayer grows out of that root: comprehension, knowledge, fullness.

**The inner man is the engine room. Everything the ship does is downstream of it.**

### The Soul as the Measure

3 John 2 is startling because of the comparison it makes: *as thy soul prospereth.*

John is not saying health is earned by spiritual performance. He is saying the soul's condition is the reference point for the whole. A prospering soul and a healthy body are meant to run at the same pace.

**Divine Wholeness** is measured from the inside, which is why two people with identical diagnoses can be in entirely different places.

### The Body Follows

Proverbs gives the practical mechanics: "My son, attend to my words; incline thine ear unto my sayings. Let them not depart from thine eyes; keep them in the midst of thine heart. For they are life unto those that find them, and health to all their flesh" (Proverbs 4:20–22).

*Health* there is *marpe* — a healing, a cure, a medicine. The words go in through the ear and the eye, are kept in the heart, and arrive at the flesh.

**Note the route. It is not spirit to body directly. It passes through attention.**

### The Renewed Mind as the Channel

That is why Romans 12 puts the mind at the hinge: "Be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind" (Romans 12:2).

The spirit has already been made new. The body will be raised. The soul — the mind, will, and emotions — is the part being brought into agreement in between.

**Renewing of the Mind** is the daily work of closing the gap, and it is where most of the Christian life is actually lived.

### What Happens When the Order Is Ignored

### Body Only

Treat the body and nothing else, and you get a person who is physically maintained and inwardly starved. The symptoms may quiet down; the person does not become whole.

This is not an argument against medicine. It is an argument against medicine as a *complete* answer, which it has never claimed to be.

### Soul Only

Work on the mind and the feelings, and you get a person who talks well and still breaks down at the joints. Real progress, real insight, a body quietly keeping score.

**Therapy and discipleship are both good. Neither is the whole person.**

### Spirit Only

And here is the error most common in the church: spirit only. Correct doctrine, lively worship, and a body nobody has been taught to steward.

Jesus did not heal people and tell them the body was irrelevant. He touched the eyes, the ears, the hand, the tongue.

**An over-spiritualised faith is not more spiritual. It is just smaller than the gospel.**

### The Psalmist's Own Diagnosis

Psalm 73 gives a portrait of someone who tried to manage the inside while the outside carried the cost: "Thus my heart was grieved, and I was pricked in my reins" (Psalm 73:21).

*Reins* is the old word for the kidneys — the deepest seat of feeling in Hebrew anatomy. Then he says, "My flesh and my heart faileth: but God is the rock of my heart" (Psalm 73:26).

The recovery begins with an inner reorientation and reaches the flesh. **The psalmist does not choose between the two; he tells the truth about both.**

## How to Work in the Order

### Begin With the Spirit

Everything starts with what is already true. You are a new creation (2 Corinthians 5:17). You have been made righteous (2 Corinthians 5:21). The Spirit lives in you (1 Corinthians 6:19).

You are not trying to become something. You are learning to live out of what you already are. And if that is the engine room, what have you been feeding it?

### Then Renew the Soul

Feed the mind on the Word until the inner commentary starts agreeing with God. That is a discipline, not a mood. It involves attention, repetition, and time.

**Spiritual Understanding** grows the same way any understanding grows: exposure plus obedience.

### Then Present the Body

Romans 12:1 follows verse 2 in the same passage: "present your bodies a living sacrifice."

The body is not the front end of the process. It is the offering, made after the mind has been renewed.

### And Keep Faith and Wisdom Together

The order is not a prohibition on the doctor. It is a description of how God works, and it never means you should reject practical help while waiting for the inner work to finish.

Take the medicine. Keep the appointment. Sleep. And in the middle of all of it, keep working the order, because **the body that is being renewed from the inside and cared for from the outside is a body being stewarded well.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Check the direction of your effort.** Are you starting with the symptom or with the source?

**2. Pray Ephesians 3:16 for yourself this week.** Strength in the inner man, before anything else.

**3. Measure by the soul, not only the scale.** Ask what your soul is prospering in — or failing to.

**4. Give the Word the route it needs.** Ear, eye, heart — attention is how the medicine arrives.

**5. Do not despise the body in the process.** Wholeness is not escape from the frame. It is the frame catching up with what God has already done.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 3 John 1–4** daily and note the comparison.
- **Pray Ephesians 3:14–21** over yourself for seven days.
- **Read Proverbs 4:20–27** and Mark every body part.
- **Write down one inner thing** and one outer thing, then start with the inner.
- **Read Psalm 73:21–26** and note the recovery.
