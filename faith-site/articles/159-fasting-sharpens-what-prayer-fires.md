Jesus's teaching on fasting is startling for what it assumes.

"Moreover when ye fast, be not, as the hypocrites, of a sad countenance" (Matthew 6:16).

*When*, not *if*. He assumes His followers will fast, and His only concern in the verse is that they do it without advertising.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **fasting does not move God to a different position; it moves a person's attention off the appetite and onto the One being sought — which is why Scripture assumes it rather than commanding it loudly.**

## What Fasting Is Not

The boundaries have to come first, because this subject has been used badly.

**It is not a lever.** Nothing in Scripture presents fasting as a way to obligate God. The prophets are scathing about fasts that expect a reward: "Wherefore have we fasted, say they, and thou seest not?" (Isaiah 58:3). God's answer is not "you did it wrong technically" but "you did it while oppressing your workers."

**It is not a punishment.** Some believers fast to atone for something. But atonement is finished and belongs to Christ alone.

**It is not a hunger strike.** The point is not to suffer impressively. Isaiah's whole chapter redirects the fast away from performance and toward loosing bonds and feeding the hungry.

**It is not a display.** Matthew 6:16–18 is explicit — no sad face, no announcement. "But thou, when thou fastest, anoint thine head, and wash thy face; That thou appear not unto men to fast, but unto thy Father which is in secret" (Matthew 6:17–18).

### What Fasting Actually Does

If it is not a lever, what is it?

**Communion With the Holy Spirit** — the fellowship fasting is meant to intensify — is the aim. **The Life of Faith** — trust expressed in a physical decision — is the shape of it. **The Divine Nature** — the life of God at work in a whole person, body included — is what a fast is making room for in a small way.

Consider a man trying to hear a quiet conversation in a noisy room. He does not accuse the speaker of being silent. He reduces the noise.

That is what a fast does. It turns down the volume of one very loud appetite, so that a voice you have been straining to hear has room.

### The Physical Honesty

Here is something that must be said plainly, because fasting teaching often skips it.

You are a body. Hunger is a real signal, and long fasts without water are dangerous. People with diabetes, eating disorders, pregnancy, medication needs, or a history of food-related illness should not fast without medical advice. That is not weak faith. It is stewardship of a body Scripture calls a temple.

**Matthew 6's secrecy covers more than showing off.** It also means you are not competing. Your fast is between you and your Father, which means its length, its form, and its wisdom are between you and Him too.

### The Kinds of Fast

Scripture records several.

A **partial fast** — Daniel ate vegetables and water rather than the king's food (Daniel 1:12). A **full fast** — no food, water only, as with Esther (Esther 4:16). A **corporate fast** — a whole nation called to repent (Joel 2:15). And a **fast from a specific thing** rather than food.

The variety tells you the form is not the point.

### Isaiah's Correction

Isaiah 58 is the most important passage on this subject and it is uncomfortable.

God rejects a fast that coexists with injustice: "Behold, in the day of your fast ye find pleasure, and exact all your labours" (Isaiah 58:3).

Then He defines the fast He wants: "to loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy burdens, and to let the oppressed go free, and that ye break every yoke... to deal thy bread to the hungry" (Isaiah 58:6–7).

Which means a fast that is only an internal religious experience and leaves your behaviour toward people untouched has missed the point.

### The Secret Reward

Matthew 6:18 promises the reward to the Father "which seeth in secret."

Not a public result. A private one. Which is fitting, because the thing fasting is meant to achieve — a quieter heart before God — is itself invisible.

### The Honest Boundary

A word for those who have never fasted or who cannot.

Fasting is not required for salvation, and it is not a mark of maturity that others can measure. Jesus said, "when ye fast" — an assumption about His people, not a condition of His love.

If your season of life makes fasting unwise, obedience may take the form of a fast from something else: a habit, a feed, a purchase. The principle of turning down the noise still holds.

## What This Means for You

**1. Decide what you are seeking.** A fast without an aim becomes a diet or a dare.

**2. Start small.** One meal, one day. The habit is built at a size you can keep.

**3. Tell nobody.** Practise Matthew 6 deliberately this first time.

**4. Replace the food with prayer.** The hunger is meant to be spent as attention, not merely endured.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom work together here.** Faith says fasting is worth doing and that God rewards what is done in secret. Wisdom says that means checking your health, drinking water, not making it a measure of yourself or others, and keeping Isaiah 58 in view so the fast reaches your conduct.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Matthew 6:16–18** and Isaiah 58:1–12 together.
- **Plan one simple fast this week** with a clear aim.
- **Spend the meal time in prayer**, not merely in discipline.
- **Do one thing from Isaiah 58** toward someone else that day.
