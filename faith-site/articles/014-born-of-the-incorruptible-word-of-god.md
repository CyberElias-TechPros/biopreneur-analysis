Have you ever made a sincere spiritual resolution — to pray more, to forgive, to live better — only to watch it fade within weeks? It is a humbling experience, and it raises an honest question: if human resolutions are so fragile, what holds your new life together?

Peter answers with a contrast that changes everything: you were born again "not of corruptible seed, but of incorruptible, **by the word of God**, which liveth and abideth for ever" (1 Peter 1:23). Here is the central truth of this teaching: **you were not born again by a fragile human decision that can spoil — you were born of God's incorruptible Word, so His life in you carries His own permanence and power.**

## Corruptible Seed Spoils; Incorruptible Seed Abides

Peter's word for corruptible is *phthartos* — subject to decay, spoilage, and ruin. Think of seed left in damp soil without life: it rots. Human resolutions, good intentions, and emotional decisions are corruptible seed. They can begin well and still spoil under pressure, distraction, or disappointment. That is not a criticism of your sincerity; it is simply the nature of corruptible things.

Incorruptible seed is the opposite: *aphthartos* — incapable of decay. **The Word of God** — God's spoken, written, and living truth, centred in Christ — is incorruptible because it carries God's own life and cannot be annulled by time, opposition, or your worst week. When that Word birthed you at **the New Birth** — the spiritual regeneration wrought by the Holy Spirit when you believed in Jesus — it planted **Divine Life** in your spirit: God's own life (*zoe*), permanent and powerful.

Think of the difference between a handwritten note and a printed banknote. A handwritten note can fade, tear, or be lost. A printed banknote carries the authority of the issuing government in every copy. Your new birth was not a handwritten note of human intention. It was issued by the living Word of God, and it carries His authority in you.

### The Word That Births You Also Sustains You

Notice Peter's description: the Word "liveth and abideth for ever." It is not merely ancient; it is alive. It does not merely survive; it abides — it remains, endures, and continues to operate. Isaiah 40:8 declares the same permanence: "The word of our God shall stand for ever." The Word that gave you birth is the same Word that sustains your walk.

That is why Scripture commands you to feed on the Word daily. You are not feeding on it to earn your birth; you are feeding on it because birth has already happened and life must be nourished. James 1:18 confirms the pattern: "Of his own will begat he us with the word of truth." God birthed you by truth, and truth continues to shape you as you receive it.

The distinction could not be sharper. Corruptible religion births believers by human effort and sustains them by human effort — and wonders why they spoil. Incorruptible revelation births believers by God's Word and sustains them by God's Word — and they abide. Same human beings. Entirely different seed.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **John 1:13** — Jesus' own birth is described as "not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." The principle extends to every believer: spiritual birth is God's work through His Word, not man's work through his will.
- **James 1:18** — "Of his own will begat he us with the word of truth, that we should be a kind of firstfruits of his creatures." The Word of truth is the instrument of your begetting. Truth birthed you; truth will mature you.
- **Hebrews 4:12** — "For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword." The incorruptible Word is not passive information. It is living, active, and discerning — it reads you even as you read it.

Three witnesses, one seed: God's Word birthed you, and God's Word abides in you.

## What This Means for You

**1. Your new life is more permanent than your worst week.** A bad week cannot spoil incorruptible seed. It can reveal areas where your mind needs renewal, but it cannot cancel your birth. Confess honestly, receive cleansing, and return to the Word that birthed you.

**2. Feed on the seed that birthed you.** Daily Scripture is not religious decoration; it is nourishment for divine life. Read slowly, meditate on one passage, and speak what you read. What you feed on grows; what you starve fades.

**3. Speak the Word that made you.** Confession of the Word — speaking the same thing God says in Scripture — aligns your mouth with the seed in your spirit. When fear speaks, answer with Scripture. When shame speaks, answer with Scripture. The incorruptible Word in you is stronger than corruptible voices around you.

**4. Faith and wisdom tend the seed.** Faith trusts the incorruptible Word; wisdom creates rhythms that protect it — regular reading, wise counsel, honest accountability, and rest. Declaring the Word never replaces diligent stewardship of your time and habits; it directs it.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 1 Peter 1:22–25 slowly and mark every permanence word.** Notice how Peter stacks incorruptible, liveth, abideth, ever — then let that permanence answer a specific area where you have feared your new life might spoil.
- **Memorise one incorruptible promise.** Choose a verse such as Isaiah 40:8 or James 1:18, write it where you will see it daily, and speak it aloud when corruptible feelings contradict it.
- **Feed on the Word before feeding on the news.** Begin one day this week with Scripture before any screen or headline. Let the incorruptible Word set the frame for the day's corruptible noise.
- **Answer one corruptible voice with the Word.** When fear, shame, or accusation speaks this week, answer it with a specific Scripture you have read and believed. Let the Word that birthed you outlast the voice that accuses you.
