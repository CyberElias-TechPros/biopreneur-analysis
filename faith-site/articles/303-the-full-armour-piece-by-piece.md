Ephesians 6 lists the armour of God, and one detail about the list is almost never mentioned from a pulpit.

Five of the six pieces are defensive. Only one is a weapon.

"Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day" (Ephesians 6:13).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **every piece of the armour is something God has already provided, and the verb attached to all of it is not attack but stand — which means the armour is issued to occupants, not to invaders.**

## The Three Verbs

### Put On

And the first verb is *endyō* — to clothe yourself. The armour is not assembled; it is worn.

Which means it is a person before it is a technique.

**You do not wield the armour. You dress in it.** So what were you wearing yesterday?

### Withstand

And the second is *anthistēmi* — to stand against, to resist. It is the word James uses for resisting the devil (James 4:7).

It is a holding action.

**The armour is built for defence, and defence is what the assignment requires.**

### Stand

And the third verb is the startling one. "And having done all, to stand" (Ephesians 6:13).

After everything — the preparation, the fight, the exhaustion — the achievement is that you are still upright.

**In this warfare, surviving is winning. The one still standing at the end has held the field.** And who told you the goal was to advance?

## The Pieces

### The Belt of Truth

And the first piece is *alētheia* — truth, worn at the waist. The belt held the soldier's tunic and everything else hung from it.

Truth here is not only honesty; it is the gospel's account of reality. Loose it and the rest of the armour slips.

**Everything you wear hangs on what you believe is true.** And the belt is the first thing fastened, which is why a believer whose grip on truth has loosened finds the rest of the armour oddly loose as well.

### Why the Pieces Are Lent, Not Earned

And there is a detail in the passage that changes the tone of the whole list. Paul calls it *the armour of God* — not the armour of the Christian. It is His equipment, issued to His soldiers.

Which means none of it is manufactured by the wearer. Not the truth, not the righteousness, not the salvation, not the faith.

**You are not forging the armour. You are being fitted for it.**

### The Breastplate of Righteousness

And the second covers the chest — heart, lungs, the vital organs. Paul borrows the image from Isaiah 59:17.

And it is *righteousness*, not *righteous behaviour*. You are guarding a standing, not a scorecard.

**The accusation aimed at your heart is answered by a gift, not by a performance.** Which is why the breastplate cannot be improved on by better behaviour.

### The Shoes of the Gospel of Peace

And the third is footwear, which is easy to skip and impossible to stand in without. *Hetoimasia* — readiness, a prepared footing.

Peace with God is the ground you stand on and the message you carry.

**A soldier who cannot move cannot hold a position.**

### The Shield of Faith

And the fourth is *thureos*, the large door-shaped shield, designed to be locked with others into a wall.

And it is used *in all things* or *above all* — a deliberate total coverage.

Faith is not a private accessory. It is a wall piece.

### The Helmet of Salvation

And the fifth protects the head, and Paul elsewhere calls it the hope of salvation (1 Thessalonians 5:8).

A mind without hope cannot hold a thought long enough to obey it.

The helmet guards the organ that decides what everything else means.

### The Sword of the Spirit

And the sixth is the only offensive piece — *machaira*, the short sword, and identified explicitly: the Word of God.

Short, held close, and used in conversation and in temptation.

One weapon, and it is a sentence.

### Putting It On

### Name Each Piece

And the practical discipline is deliberate, not automatic. Paul says *take up*, which implies a decision each day.

Name the truth you are standing on. Name the righteousness you are wearing.

Armour left in the cupboard protects nobody.

### Pray It

And Paul's own instruction continues straight into prayer (Ephesians 6:18). The armour is put on in conversation with God, not in a checklist.

### Wear It Together

And the shield's design is the clue. These are pieces meant to interlock with other believers, not to be worn alone.

A lone soldier with a door-sized shield is a target. A line of them is a wall.

The armour assumes a formation.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, the armour is not a ritual. Repeating the names of the pieces does not confer protection; trusting the Christ they describe does.

Second, it is not a substitute for wisdom. The soldier still plans, still rests, still trains, and still eats.

**The **Righteousness** is the breastplate; **The Word of God** is the sword; and **Spiritual Warfare is standing in a formation rather than charging alone.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Ephesians 6:13–18 every morning this week.

2. Identify which piece you habitually leave off.

3. Put that one on deliberately today.

4. Find one other believer to stand beside.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Armour is not a strategy. **Spiritual Warfare** is the standing; wisdom is the plan for the day you are standing through.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Ephesians 6 six to eighteen daily.**
- **Write down the six pieces.**
- **Note which is missing in your life.**
- **Pray the armour on out loud.**
- **Read Isaiah 59:15–21** and see where Paul got the imagery.
