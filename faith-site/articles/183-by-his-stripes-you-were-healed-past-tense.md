Every Christian has heard the verse, and most of us have heard it quoted in a way that empties it of its most important word.

"With his stripes we are healed" (Isaiah 53:5).

Not "will be." Not "may be." We *are*.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **healing is not a future transaction you are petitioning for but a past accomplishment you are receiving, and the whole difference between begging and believing is which tense you are living in.**

## The Tense of It

### The Verse Where It Stands

Isaiah 53 is written in a mixture of tenses, and the healing clause sits in a prophecy that is spoken from the vantage point of what the servant will accomplish. But the verbs are deliberately arranged: "wounded" for transgressions, "bruised" for iniquities, a chastisement for our peace, and stripes for healing.

Note that every clause links a suffering to a benefit, and the benefit is stated in the same register as the suffering. **The healing is not a footnote to the atonement**. It is one of its listed outcomes.

### "We Are Healed" — the Hebrew

The verb is *nirlā*, from *rāp̄āʾ* — to heal, to restore, to make whole. It is the same root as the name Jehovah-Rapha, "the LORD that healeth thee" (Exodus 15:26).

And the form is prophetic perfect. Hebrew often uses the completed tense to describe what God has determined to do, because with God a determined thing is as good as done.

So the promise in Isaiah is not optimism. It is a completed action described in advance of the event, because the event was never in doubt.

### Peter's Past Tense

And then the New Testament does the decisive work for us, because Peter quotes the passage and fixes the tense in the past.

"Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed" (1 Peter 2:24).

*Ēthēte* — you were healed. Aorist passive. Something done to you, at a point in time, already accomplished.

Look at what Peter does with the verse. He puts sin and healing in the very same sentence, in the very same body, cleared in the very same transaction. **You would not say "you were forgiven in the future." You cannot say "you were healed in the future" either.**

### What This Does to Your Praying

Which brings the practical point, and it is the hinge of this article.

If healing is a past purchase you have not yet received, your prayer is **an appropriation**, not a petition.

Those are two entirely different activities. One says, "Please would You provide this." The other says, "Thank You that You already did."

And notice that both are prayers. This is not a rebuke to anyone who has asked God for healing — see the honest section below. It is a correction of the starting point. You are not trying to get God to begin something. You are coming into the middle of something He finished.

### Matthew's Commentary

Matthew gives us permission to read Isaiah this way by doing it himself.

He records that Jesus healed many, "that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias the prophet, saying, Himself took our infirmities, and bare our sicknesses" (Matthew 8:17, quoting Isaiah 53:4).

The verb for "took" is *elaben* — took to Himself. The healing ministry of Jesus was not a preview of a future possibility. It was the visible arrival of an accomplished provision.

And Matthew does not say "that healing might be available." He says it might be fulfilled. Something in the prophet was waiting to happen, and it happened.

### What the Stripes Were

There is a physical detail worth pausing over, because it makes the metaphor concrete.

The word Isaiah uses is *chabbûrâ*, meaning a stripe or a lash-wound. And when Matthew and John record the events of that night, they record the fulfilment: "Then Pilate therefore took Jesus, and scourged him" (John 19:1).

A Roman scourging was not a beating. It was a specified procedure with a bone or metal knuckle in the cords, and it was aimed at the body rather than the will.

Have you ever wondered why the wound and the healing are described in the same verse?

**His body was a specification, so that yours could be a distribution.**

### The Blood That Finishes It

And the New Testament keeps tying this to one more past tense, which is why the article keeps returning to it.

"For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified" (Hebrews 10:14).

"For the priests went always into the first tabernacle... But Christ being come an high priest of good things to come, by a greater and more perfect tabernacle, not of this world" (Hebrews 9:6, 11).

The whole argument of Hebrews is that the old system had to repeat, because its sacrifices were not final. The new one does not repeat, because it was final. **A repeated prayer is not evidence of an unfinished work; it is the request of a child who has not yet been told it is done.** That is what **The Finished Work** means, and it is the foundation under everything in this month.

### The Receipt and the Groceries

One illustration, because the distinction between a purchase and a receipt is where most believers live.

Imagine a man who has paid for a week's groceries in advance, in full, with a receipt in his hand — and then stands in the store aisle for three days asking whether he might be allowed some bread.

The clerk would think him mad. So would the manager. **The payment is not the issue; the appropriation is.**

That is the gap between what was done at the cross and what is being enjoyed in your body. And the gap is never on heaven's ledger. It is in the aisle, asking.

### An Honest Word to Anyone Still Unwell

And here is where the article must be careful, because tense can become a weapon.

If you are reading this with a body that is not yet healed, nothing in this article makes you a failure. The tense of the cross is not a rebuke to a person still in the process of receiving it.

Consider what the Bible does not promise: it does not promise the receipt on the day you learn about the purchase. Ten lepers were cleansed "as they went" (Luke 17:14). A blind man was healed twice in stages (Mark 8:22–25) — a detail so inconvenient that some have tried to explain it away. And the man at the pool of Bethesda had waited thirty-eight years (John 5:5).

**The provision does not change because the delivery is slow.** But neither does the provision shrink if you must wait.

And wisdom: this teaching is not a reason to refuse help. Faith and wisdom work together — the doctor, the medicine, the rest, and the friend who carries you are not substitutes for the stripes; they are often the ordinary channel through which the purchase reaches you.

### What to Do With It

So how do you live in a past tense?

Say it. **Confession of the Word** is *homologia* — saying the same thing. Not "I hope", not "I'm believing for", but the same thing Peter said: *I was healed by His stripes.*

Then stop trying to get God to start. When you are tempted to plead, replace it with thanks — that is the fastest way to change the tense you are living in.

Then be patient with the gap, without being resigned to it. Standing is not passivity. Standing is what you do when the work is done and the delivery is signed.

## What This Means for You

**1. Pray from the past, not toward the future.** Change one word in your praying this week: from "please heal" to "thank You, it is done."

**2. Let the blood settle the matter you keep reopening.** **The Blood of Jesus** did not partially pay. Reopening a paid account is not humility, it is unbelief wearing a modest hat.

**3. Compare the three passages.** Three passages, one transaction, one tense.

**4. Expect the delivery in God's order, not your own.** Provision is instant; manifestation is often a walk. Both truths are in the same Bible.

**5. Say it out loud before you feel it.** **Divine Wholeness** is not a feeling that arrives to make confession possible. It is a fact that makes confession reason.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 1 Peter 2:21–25** and circle the past tenses.
- **Write "I was healed" on a card** and say it daily this week.
- **Stop re-asking for one thing** you have already received in Christ.
- **Read Luke 17:11–19**, and note when the cleansing happened.
