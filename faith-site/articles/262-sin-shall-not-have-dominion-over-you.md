Romans 6:14 is often quoted as a promise and abandoned as a puzzle, because the second half does not sound like the reason anyone would expect.

"For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace."

*Why* will sin not rule you? Because you are not under law. Most of us would have said: because you have been given power.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the promise and the reason are in the same verse, and the reason is the stronger half — sin's dominion does not break because you fight harder, but because the jurisdiction you live under has changed.**

## The Word Dominion

### It Is a Reign, Not a Visit

The Greek is *kyrieuō* — to be lord of, to exercise dominion. It is the same root family as *kyrios*, lord.

Paul is not saying sin will never be present. He is saying it will not be master.

**A king can invade a country for a season without being its sovereign.**

### The Series Paul Argues

And chapter 6 has already established the pattern: "Let not sin therefore reign in your mortal body... For sin shall not have dominion over you" (Romans 6:12, 14).

Notice the pairing. The believer is told not to let sin reign, and then assured it will not. Command and promise in the same breath.

**What is promised is what is now possible — which is why the command makes sense.**

### What It Does Not Mean

It does not mean you cannot sin. Verses 12 and 13 are imperatives precisely because the choice is live.

It means sin no longer has the right of command.

**A soldier can disobey an officer without the officer ceasing to exist. The question is which army has his oath.**

## Why "Not Under Law"

### The Law's Inability

Paul has already explained the problem in chapter 7: the law can name a thing and condemn it, but it cannot produce the obedience it demands (Romans 8:3).

The law is a mirror, an X-ray — it shows the break; it cannot set it.

**The law could tell you what sin is. It was never able to tell sin to leave.**

### The Law's Effect on Sin

And worse, the law gives sin something to work with: "For without the law sin was dead... For sin, taking occasion by the commandment, wrought in me all manner of concupiscence" (Romans 7:8).

The prohibition stirred the appetite. Being under law produced, rather than removed, the dominion.

**A command without a change of nature only gives the old nature a target.**

### Why Grace Breaks It

And therefore the change of jurisdiction is the mechanism. Under grace, the relationship is not legal performance but family.

A son who disobeys is disciplined, not disowned. And the discipline of a secure son is what trains obedience.

Grace removes the accusation that made sin attractive — and it removes it permanently.

### How Freedom Is Actually Entered

### Reckon, Do Not Wrestle

Paul's instruction is accounting, not grappling: "Likewise reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed unto sin" (Romans 6:11).

*Reckon* is *logizomai*, the word of the ledger. You write down what is already true.

Faith does not fight the old master. It files a change of address.

### Yield, Do Not Manage

And the practical instruction is a transfer of command: "but yield yourselves unto God, as those that are alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness unto God" (Romans 6:13).

*Yield* is presentational, like handing over a set of tools.

Sin loses its weapons the moment the limbs change employers.

### The Obedience of Grace

And yielding produces a different obedience from the one law produced: "But God be thanked, that ye were the servants of sin, but ye have obeyed from the heart that form of doctrine which was delivered you" (Romans 6:17).

*From the heart.* That is the difference. Not compliance, but a new allegiance.

The law can get your hands. Grace gets your heart, and the hands follow.

### The Practical Battle

### What If It Feels Like Dominion

Feelings report the weather, not the title. Many believers feel ruled by something they have the standing to refuse.

The fact does not become false because the sensation contradicts it.

You are not asked to feel no longer a slave. You are asked to reckon yourself free.

### What Repentance Looks Like Now

And when a believer sins, the response is not a period of servitude — it is a return to the Master he already serves.

That is why confession is short and repentance is fast.

A servant who knows whose he is does not need weeks to come home.

### What Habit Change Requires

And in practical terms: no slavery, no passivity. Freedom is worked out in habit by a willing man, which is precisely why it can be.

You cannot conquer a stronghold from a cell. You can demolish one from the outside.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, "not under law" does not mean the moral law is irrelevant. Paul's whole point is that grace fulfils what the law demanded (Romans 8:4), not that it was cancelled as a standard.

Second, the promise of no dominion is not permission to entertain what destroyed you. Romans 6 ends with the bluntest possible question: "What fruit had ye then in those things whereof ye are now ashamed?" (Romans 6:21).

**The **The Authority of the Believer rests on a transferred jurisdiction; the Righteousness you received is the legal ground of it, and the The Divine Nature** is what makes the new allegiance natural rather than forced.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Learn the difference between a presence and a reign.** Sin's presence is not sin's rule.

**2. Reckon daily.** Say what is true before you feel anything.

**3. Name your members.** Whatever you yield repeatedly becomes the weapon.

**4. Stop negotiating with the old master** and start reporting to the new one.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Freedom requires habit change. **The Authority of the Believer** is the standing; wisdom builds the routines that make the standing habitual.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Romans 6 three times this week.**
- **Write out verse 14 and underline the reason.**
- **Name the thing that feels like a master.**
- **Yield one specific member** — eyes, mouth, hands — to God tomorrow.
- **Read Colossians 1:13–14** and note the transfer language.
