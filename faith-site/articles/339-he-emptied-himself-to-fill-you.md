Philippians 2:7 is the verse the church has argued about for centuries, and the argument usually misses what Paul is doing with it.

"But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the word Paul uses is *kenoo* — to empty out — and he applies it not to Christ's deity but to His privileges, which means the Incarnation was a deliberate divestment of rights rather than a loss of nature.**

## The Verb

### Ekenōsen

And the Greek is *heauton ekenōsen* — He emptied Himself. The noun form, *kenosis*, has become a theological battleground.

But notice what Paul does *not* say. He does not say the Word stopped being God.

**An emptying is not an erasure.** And the difference is the whole gospel.

### Of What, Then?

And the context answers: He did not consider equality with God a thing to be grasped (Philippians 2:6) — *harpagmon*, a prize to be seized and held.

He did not cling. He let go of the advantage.

**He laid down the right to be treated as what He was.**

### The Form of a Servant

And *morphē doulou* — the outward expression of a slave. Not a servant's duties performed by a lord; a servant's visible form.

Which means the emptying was observable. People could see it.

**Humility you can see is the only kind Paul is describing.** Humility is not the absence of strength; it is strength that has stopped needing to prove itself.

## The Two Errors

### He Did Not Cease to Be God

And the first error is the ancient one: that the Son somehow downgraded His nature. The same passage has Him exalted to the highest name and worshipped by every knee.

A being who ceased to be God could not receive that.

**The throne at the end of the paragraph is the answer to the question in the middle.**

### He Did Not Merely Pretend

And the second error is the opposite: that the human life was a costume over a distant deity, that the hunger and the grief were a performance.

Hebrews says He was tempted in all points and learned obedience through suffering (Hebrews 5:8).

**A costume cannot learn anything.**

### The Mind of Christ

And Paul's purpose is ethical, not speculative. He introduces the passage with "Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus" (Philippians 2:5).

The doctrine is a template, and it is meant to be copied.

**Paul preaches the Incarnation as a way of life.**

### Downward, Then Upward

And the shape of the passage is a V. He descended — no reputation, servant, obedient unto death — and then God exalted Him (Philippians 2:9).

The path up ran through the way down.

**There is no shortcut to the high places that bypasses the low ones.**

### The Towel

And John gives the same truth as a picture: the Lord of glory, with a towel around His waist, washing feet (John 13:4–5).

He knew who He was, and that is what let Him kneel.

**Only a secure identity can afford humility.**

### Emptied to Fill

### The Emptying Was Not for Himself

And here is the turn of the title. He emptied Himself, and the result was the filling of others.

"For ye know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich" (2 Corinthians 8:9).

**His emptying is the source of your fullness.**

### What Fills You

And the filling is not merely comfort. Peter says we become partakers of the divine nature (2 Peter 1:4) — the same language of receiving what He is.

The emptied One fills.

He gave up a reputation to give you a nature.

### The Return

And so the paradox is complete: the one who empties himself is the one God fills, and the one who clings is the one who loses.

"Whosoever will save his life shall lose it" (Matthew 16:25).

Empty hands are the only ones that can be filled.

### Living the Kenosis

### Lay Down the Right

And the first practice is very concrete: name one right you have been insisting on — to be understood, to be credited, to be treated fairly — and release it for a season.

Not forever. Deliberately.

A right you cannot put down has become a master.

### Keep the Identity

And the second is the guard: emptying is not self-erasure. Jesus washed feet and still said, "Ye call me Master and Lord: and ye say well; for so I am" (John 13:13).

He served from identity, not toward it.

You cannot lay down a status you do not know you have. So whose opinion have you been borrowing yours from?

### Serve in the Form

And the third is visibility. The servant form was observable. Do something that looks like service and cannot be mistaken for strategy.

An invisible humility is often just a preference.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a call to accept abuse. Jesus laid His life down; He was not taken by surprise (John 10:18), and the laying down was voluntary at every step.

Second, this is not a demand to deny your gifts. The emptied Christ raised the dead; emptying is about status, not ability.

Three terms hold this together. **The Divine Nature** is what the emptying makes room for — His life deposited in ours. **The Finished Work** is what the emptying accomplished at the cross, complete and unrepeatable. And **Sonship** is the secure identity that lets a yielded person kneel without disappearing.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Philippians 2:1–18 once a day this week.

2. Name one right you have been clinging to.

3. Lay it down for a week.

4. Serve in a way people can see.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Humility is not the absence of boundaries. **The Divine Nature** is being formed; wisdom still names what is genuinely harmful and keeps it out of the house.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read John 13:1–17.**
- **Read 2 Corinthians 8:1–15.**
- **Do one task nobody will thank you for.**
- **Be corrected without defending yourself once.**
- **Notice what it costs, and Who it is costing you for.**
