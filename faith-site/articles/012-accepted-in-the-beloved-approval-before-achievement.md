Do you ever serve God while quietly wondering whether He is pleased with you — as though your usefulness might finally earn His approval? Many sincere believers work hard for the Lord yet live with the uneasy feeling that they are still on trial.

Ephesians 1:6 announces the opposite order: God "hath **made us accepted** in the beloved." Made — not promised to make us accepted after enough service. Accepted — welcomed, approved, received into favour. Here is the central truth of this teaching: **God accepted you in the Beloved before you achieved anything for Him — approval in Christ is the starting point of your walk, not the prize at the end of it.**

## The Greek Word Behind "Accepted"

Paul's word is *charitoo* — to grace, to endow with favour, to make accepted. It comes from *charis*, grace. You were not accepted because you became impressive; you were graced because God chose to favour you in His Son. The phrase "in the beloved" points to Christ, God's Beloved Son. Your acceptance is located in Him, not in your record.

This is **Righteousness** in its relational sense: right standing with God, both your legal right standing before His throne and the new moral nature created in your reborn spirit. **The Finished Work** — the complete, once-for-all redemptive accomplishment of Christ's death and resurrection — secured that standing. You did not negotiate it. You received it by faith in the Beloved.

Think of an adopted child welcomed into a family home. The adoption papers are signed before the child learns the family rules, earns a place at the table, or contributes to the household. The child does not live to earn adoption; the child lives from adoption, learning the family ways because the family is already theirs. That is the order of grace: accepted first, then trained as a beloved child.

### Approval Before Achievement Changes Everything

Why does this order matter so much? Because achievement-first religion produces two opposite failures. On good days it produces pride: "God must be pleased because I performed well." On hard days it produces despair: "God must be distant because I performed poorly." Both reactions assume approval fluctuates with performance. Ephesians 1:6 ends that fluctuation: your approval rests in the Beloved, whose standing never fluctuates.

That settled approval gives rise to **the Consciousness of Righteousness** — the settled inward awareness that you stand forgiven, clean, and welcomed before the Father in Christ. Sin-consciousness keeps you hiding like Adam among the trees, rehearsing failure. Righteousness-consciousness brings you boldly to the throne, confessing honestly and receiving cleansing without fear that one failure revokes your acceptance.

Watch what separates the two: The applicant serves to be accepted; the accepted child serves because he is accepted. The applicant gives to impress; the child gives to express gratitude. The applicant obeys to stay approved; the child obeys to walk in the joy of approval. Same activities — entirely different engine.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **Romans 5:1** — "Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ." Justification by faith produces peace — settled, reconciled peace — not anxious probation. Peace is the fruit of acceptance already given.
- **Colossians 1:12** — "Giving thanks unto the Father, which hath made us meet to be partakers of the inheritance of the saints in light." God *hath made us meet* — qualified us — in Christ. Qualification preceded participation; acceptance preceded inheritance.
- **1 John 4:10** — "Herein is love, not that we loved God, but that he loved us, and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins." Love came from God's side first. Your acceptance is anchored in His prior love, not your subsequent performance.

Three apostles, one order: God loved, accepted, and qualified you in Christ before you could achieve anything to deserve it.

## What This Means for You

**1. Serve from approval, not for approval.** Your ministry, giving, and obedience are expressions of acceptance, not applications for it. When you serve, you are not auditioning. You are a beloved child working in the family business.

**2. Confess quickly, without fear of rejection.** Because you are accepted in the Beloved, confession is never a plea to be readmitted. It is an honest child coming to a welcoming Father, receiving cleansing (1 John 1:9), and returning to fellowship with confidence.

**3. Let approval produce obedience, not complacency.** Acceptance in Christ never licenses carelessness. A child who knows he is loved does not use love as an excuse to disobey; he delights to honour the Father whose love secured him. Grace trains holiness — it does not cancel it.

**4. Faith and wisdom honour this approval daily.** Faith declares you accepted in the Beloved; wisdom shows it in diligent work, honest speech, kept promises, and careful stewardship. Declaring approval never replaces responsible action — it motivates it with gratitude.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Begin each morning by receiving, not earning.** Speak Ephesians 1:6 aloud before your first task: "Father, thank You that You have made me accepted in the Beloved. Today I serve from Your approval." Let acceptance set the tone for achievement.
- **Answer performance-based shame with Scripture.** When you hear, "God is disappointed in you," answer with Romans 5:1 and Romans 8:1: justified by faith, no condemnation in Christ. Then confess specifically and walk on in peace.
- **Serve one person this week as an accepted child.** Choose a task nobody will applaud and do it faithfully, quietly, and cheerfully — as someone whose approval with the Father is already settled.
- **Read Ephesians 1:3–8 slowly.** Notice how Paul stacks blessings — chosen, predestined, accepted, redeemed, forgiven — all before any command to perform. Let the order sink in before you act.
