There is a name that has been given, not earned by the speaker — a name God Himself conferred on Jesus after the cross, and a name before which every knee in three realms will eventually bow.

"Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name" (Philippians 2:9). Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the Name is not a password but a person's revealed authority — which is why it works for the believer who understands whose name he is using.**

## A Gift, Not a Slogan

Notice the grammar of Philippians 2. God exalted Him. God gave Him the name. The name is a conferral, a public verdict on the finished work. Everything about it is legal rather than magical.

**The Name of Jesus** — His person, work, and authority — is the instrument. **The Authority of the Believer** — the God-given right and ability, in Christ, to stand and act in His name — is what makes the instrument usable in your hand. **The Finished Work** — what Christ accomplished and completed — is why the name has anything behind it at all.

Think of a company seal. The seal is not powerful in itself; it is powerful because of the company it represents and the signature authority behind it.

### What the Early Church Did With It

Acts 3 is the case study. Peter and John meet a lame man at the gate and say, "Silver and gold have I none; but such as I have give I thee: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk" (Acts 3:6).

Notice what Peter says about the result: "And his name through faith in his name hath made this man strong" (Acts 3:16). Name plus faith, not name plus technique.

Then comes the confrontation before the council: "by what power, or by what name, have ye done this?" (Acts 4:7). The question is legal, and Peter's answer is the whole gospel in one sentence: "Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved" (Acts 4:12).

### Why the Name Is Not a Formula

Somewhere along the way, the Name got treated like an incantation — a phrase appended to the end of a request to make it work. That is closer to the pagan view of names than to Scripture.

A name in the ancient world stood for the character and authority of the one who bore it. To speak in someone's name was to act as his representative, within his commission, on his terms.

Have you ever used the Name as a closing formula rather than as a conscious appeal to the authority of Christ? What would change if you said it slowly and meant it?

### The Scope of the Name

"Things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth" (Philippians 2:10). That is not a narrow jurisdiction. Ephesians 1 puts the same truth in architectural terms: Christ is seated "far above all principality, and power, and might, and dominion, and every name that is named" (Ephesians 1:21).

Watch what separates the two: The believer who knows the scope of the name speaks to a situation with calm authority; the believer who thinks the name is a lucky phrase speaks nervously and hopes something happens.

### Using the Name Rightly

- **In prayer.** "Whatsoever ye shall ask in my name, that will I do" (John 14:14). The Name is a request made to the Father in the Son's credit.
- **In declaration.** Speaking to mountains, storms, and sickness in the Name is the ordinary practice of the church.
- **In baptism.** The Name is the ground of identity, not merely a ritual word.
- **In daily work.** "Whatsoever ye do, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus" (Colossians 3:17) — the whole of life under the authority.

### The Name Is Shared, Not Borrowed

Jesus does not merely permit the use of His name; He authorises it as a transfer of standing: "And whatsoever ye shall ask in my name, that will I do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son" (John 14:13). The purpose clause matters — the glory of the Father in the Son. The name is not a tool for your agenda; it is a channel for His.

That is why praying in the name is not a verbal formula appended to a request. It is the conscious decision to ask for what the Name represents, on the basis of what the Name accomplished, for the purpose the Name serves.

## What This Means for You

Acts 19 gives the cautionary tale: seven men tried to use the Name they had seen Paul use, and the spirit answered, "Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye?" (Acts 19:15). The Name does not work for strangers to Christ.

That is not a threat to sincere believers; it is a reminder that the Name is a relationship before it is a resource.

**1. Say the Name with understanding.** Pause and mean it.
**2. Keep short accounts.** Unrepented sin muddies the confidence with which we appeal to Him.
**3. Stand on the Name in prayer daily.** Make it habitual, not theatrical.
**4. Refuse superstition in both directions.** Neither magic nor neglect.
**5. Faith and wisdom speak within commission.** Wisdom asks whether this is an instruction from the Lord, not merely whether the Name is available.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Write Philippians 2:9–11** and read it aloud before prayer each day.
- **Pray one specific request** explicitly in the Name, slowly.
- **Read Acts 3:1–16 and Acts 4:5–12** side by side.
- **Note every place this week** where you used the Name — and whether you meant it.
