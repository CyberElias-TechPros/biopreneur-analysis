Every shepherd's psalm builds to one small, physical image, and it is easy to read past it.

"Thou preparest a table before me in the presence of mine enemies: thou anointest my head with oil; my cup runneth over" (Psalm 23:5).

A running-over cup is a strange thing to celebrate. It is a spill. It is waste by any accountant's measure.

And that is exactly the point.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **a cup that runs over is not a cup that is being wasted — it is a cup whose purpose has finally started, because the overflow was never meant to be drunk by the cup.**

## The Anatomy of Overflow

### Full Is Not the Same as Running Over

A full cup is a satisfied one. It has reached its capacity and stopped.

A running-over cup has exceeded its capacity while remaining in place. The excess is not for it, and it is not staying in it.

**God is not in the business of filling vessels to their limit. He fills them past it.**

### What the Table Is For

Look at the setting before the cup. There is a *table*, prepared, and it is prepared "in the presence of mine enemies."

That is a public meal. A table is not a private possession; it is a place where others are seated.

**The overflow of verse 5 only makes sense if there is somebody else at the table of verse 5.**

### The Anointing Before the Overflow

And before the overflowing cup there is oil on the head — the anointing of a guest, a priest, a king.

The order is deliberate: identity, then provision, then excess. You are anointed first, and the overflow comes as a consequence of standing, not as a reward for performing.

### The Pattern Through Scripture

#### Abraham Was Blessed to Be a Blessing

The first promise to Abraham contains an unmistakable grammar: "And I will bless thee... and thou shalt be a blessing" (Genesis 12:2).

The blessing is given with a direction attached. It is not a terminus; it is a channel.

Every blessing in Scripture arrives with an address on it.

#### The Widow's Oil Was Limited by Vessels

Elisha's instruction to the widow was to borrow vessels — "not a few" (2 Kings 4:3). The oil flowed while there was a vessel to hold it, and stopped when the vessels ran out.

The supply was never the constraint. The capacity to distribute was.

Multiplication waits on your willingness to go and get more containers.

#### Paul's Enriched-to-Be-Generous Logic

Second Corinthians puts the whole mechanism in one verse: "Ye being enriched in every thing to all bountifulness, which causeth through us thanksgiving to God" (2 Corinthians 9:11).

The enrichment is instrumental. It is *to* bountifulness. And the end product is not your comfort but other people's thanksgiving.

**If your abundance has produced no thanksgiving in anyone else, it has not yet done its work.**

### The Two Ways to Stop a Flow

#### Dam It

The first is to hold it. This is the rich fool's strategy, and the language of Scripture for it is hoarding.

But hoarding has a subtler consequence than punishment: it changes what you are. A reservoir has no outlet, and a reservoir eventually stagnates.

What you refuse to let pass through you, you stop being able to receive.

#### Redirect It

The second is subtler and more religious. It is to keep the overflow but rename it — to call the surplus "my blessing," "my season," "what I've earned."

It is possible to thank God for the overflow and then quietly dam it in the same prayer.

Gratitude that ends in possession is not gratitude; it is a receipt.

### The Boundary

Now the guardrail, because this teaching can be turned into a burden.

Overflow is not the same as abundance. Some seasons are full cups with nothing spilling, and a full cup is not a failure. The shepherd who fills the cup has not promised that everyone's cup overflows simultaneously.

And giving from overflow is not a demand that you pour out what you need to keep. **The **Faith and Wisdom** of stewardship knows the difference between surplus and supply, and does not confuse generosity with depletion.

With that said, most believers in the comfortable world have more overflow than they have identified. The question is not usually whether you have some. It is whether anyone else has received it.

## What This Means for You

**1. Identify your overflow.** What is genuinely beyond your need this month — money, time, skill, space, attention?

**2. Ask who it is addressed to.** The blessing arrived with an address. If you do not know the address, it is still in transit.

**3. Build capacity to distribute.** The widow's flow depended on borrowed vessels. Some of us need to build the container before the overflow makes sense.

**4. Watch for thanksgiving.** Paul's test is concrete: did the supply produce gratitude to God in someone?

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Distinguish *supply* from *surplus*, and give from the second so the first stays intact. **Kingdom Stewardship** is not the same as perpetual deficit. **The Inheritance of the Believer** is not diminished by distribution, because what is inherited in Christ is not a stock but a Supply.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Psalm 23** slowly and stop at verse 5.
- **Write down three things in your life that overflow.**
- **Give one of them away** this week, deliberately.
- **Read 2 Kings 4:1–7** and count the vessels.
- **Ask one person** what they need that you have.
