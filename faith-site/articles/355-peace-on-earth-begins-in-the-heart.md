The angels promised peace on earth, and most of us have spent our lives looking for it in circumstances — a quieter month, a paid-off bill, a resolved argument — while Paul locates it somewhere else entirely.

"And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to the which also ye are called in one body; and be ye thankful."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Paul's verb for peace is *brabeuō* — to act as an umpire — which means peace is not a mood to be chased but a governor to be obeyed, and the peace announced over Bethlehem is meant to hold session in a human chest.**

## The Verb That Changes the Verse

### Brabeuō

And the word is an athletic term. *Brabeuō* means to preside over the games, to award the prize, to make the call on the field.

Paul is not saying "feel peaceful." He is saying "let peace referee."

### Rule, Not Reside

And the difference is everything. A resident peace sits quietly in a corner. A ruling peace decides what counts and what does not.

Most of us want peace as a guest and don't realise it came as a judge.

### In Your Hearts

And the venue is the interior life — the place where you argue with yourself at 2am. That is where the umpire is stationed.

**Peace is not something you install in your circumstances. It is something you obey in your chest.** So which voice has actually been umpiring your last month?

## What the Umpire Does

### It Calls the Play

And when a decision is in front of you, the peace of God is a legitimate signal. If a step leaves you in turmoil that does not lift, pay attention.

Paul told the Colossians that this peace is what they were called to as one body — it is a shared instrument, not a private superstition.

### It Ends the Argument

And some fights are already settled and we keep replaying them. The umpire's whistle is final, which means the arguing is now unbelief.

### It Guards the Gate

And Paul's other great promise is that the peace of God, which passes all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus (Philippians 4:7).

*Phroureō* is a military word — a garrison. Peace posted at the door.

### Peace, in the Right Order

### Peace With God First

And this is the foundation nobody can skip: being justified by faith, we have peace with God through Jesus Christ (Romans 5:1). That is a settled legal reality, not a feeling to be obtained.

Everything else is downstream. If this one is shaky, the other two will be too.

### Then Peace With Yourself

And Paul says let it rule in your hearts. That is the interior courtroom where you keep prosecuting yourself.

If God has declared you righteous, your own verdict has been overruled.

### Then Peace With Others

And the verse adds, "to the which also ye are called in one body." Peace is not a private luxury; it is a congregational requirement.

The measure of your peace is often tested at a table with somebody irritating.

### Getting the Peace Back

### Through Thanksgiving

And Paul ends the verse with "and be ye thankful" — apparently unrelated, and completely related. Gratitude reorients a mind away from what is missing toward what is given.

Grumbling and peace have never shared a room for long.

### Through the Person

And behind all of it is not a technique but a Person. Jesus said, "Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid" (John 14:27).

Notice the grammar of the gift. He did not leave a philosophy; He left His own peace. And He said it on the night He was about to be arrested, which means the peace He gives is not the peace of good news. It is the peace of a settled Father.

**The world's peace depends on what is happening. His peace depends on who is holding you.**

### Through the Word

And David says, "Great peace have they which love thy law: and nothing shall offend them" (Psalm 119:165). Peace grows where the word is loved.

### Through Staying the Mind

And Isaiah gives the oldest promise in the book: "Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee" (Isaiah 26:3).

The word for stayed is a leaning, a being propped up. Peace is a posture, not an accident.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, peace is not avoidance. Peacemaking sometimes requires the difficult conversation, and the umpire does not award the prize for running away.

Second, peace is not passivity about injustice. Jesus slept in the storm and He also overturned tables. Faith and wisdom know which moment requires which.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Colossians 3:12–17 and identify every command in the passage.

2. Ask: what decision have I been making while my peace was screaming?

3. Write one sentence of thanks before you pray anything else today.

4. Name the argument you keep replaying and accept the umpire's call.

5. **Let peace rule, and keep faith and wisdom together.** Not every uneasy feeling is God's warning and not every calm feeling is His approval. Test the call against scripture, take the counsel, and then move.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Philippians 4:4–9.** Note the order: prayer, thanksgiving, then peace.
- **Read John 14:27.** Note what kind of peace He gives.
- **Set your mind on one promise for the whole week.**
- **Leave one unresolved argument at the throne and stop picking it up.**
- **Notice what happens in your body when you finally stop fighting.**
