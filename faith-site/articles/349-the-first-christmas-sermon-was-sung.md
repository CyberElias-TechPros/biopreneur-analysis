Before a single verse of the New Testament was written, before anybody had a doctrine to explain, there was a choir, and the first Christmas sermon came in song.

"Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the first sermon of the Incarnation had two movements — glory to God in the highest, and peace on earth — and heaven chose to sing it rather than explain it, because the announcement was too large for prose.**

## Why Singing

### Music Says What Statements Cannot

And a sentence can inform you; a song can carry you. When a truth is too big to be handled at arm's length, we make it singable so it can live inside us.

Heaven did not give the shepherds an essay. Heaven gave them a refrain.

### Singing Is the Voice of Overflow

And James says, "Is any among you afflicted? let him pray. Is any merry? let him sing psalms" (James 5:13). Singing is what happens when the heart is fuller than speech can manage.

The choir sang because the incarnation required a bigger container than exposition.

### Heaven Sings Over a Stable

And notice the location. The most extravagant music in the New Testament breaks out over a manger in an occupied country, not over a palace or a throne.

The scale of heaven's celebration tells you the scale of what was happening in that field.

**Nothing about the setting looked worth a choir. The choir disagreed.**

## The Two Movements of the Song

### Glory in the Highest

And the first movement is vertical. *Doxa en hypsistois theō* — glory to God in the highest. The finest of the goods was not for the shepherds' comfort; it was for the Father's honour.

The Incarnation begins with the glory of God, not the convenience of man.

### Peace on Earth

And the second movement is horizontal. *Eirēnē* — peace, not merely the absence of war but the arrival of wholeness, the end of hostility.

It is not a general mood over the planet; it is peace *among* those on whom His good pleasure rests.

### Good Will Toward Men

And the phrase is best read as God's good will coming down, not man's good will going up. The peace is rooted in His pleasure, not ours.

That is the difference between a ceasefire and a reconciliation.

**The song was not optimism. It was an announcement of a treaty, signed in a stable.**

### What the Peace Actually Is

### Peace With God

And Paul explains the mechanism: being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ (Romans 5:1).

The first peace in the gospel is not a feeling. It is a status.

### Peace With Others

And then Paul tells the Ephesians that Christ is our peace, who has made both one and broken down the middle wall of partition (Ephesians 2:14).

The song has a social consequence. Where the barrier was, there is now a body.

### Peace Within

And then, only then, the subjective peace: the peace of God that passes understanding keeps the heart and mind (Philippians 4:7).

Guarding your heart comes after the treaty; it is not the treaty.

### Singing It Now

### Sing When You Feel It

And the simplest application needs no sophistication. When gratitude bubbles up, open your mouth. Sing badly, sing alone, sing in the car — the scale is not the issue.

God is not grading the performance. He is listening for the overflow, and an overflow is never in tune anyway. Let it out.

### Sing When You Do Not

And the deeper application is the harder one. Paul and Silas sang at midnight, in the inner prison, with their feet in the stocks (Acts 16:25).

The song did not come because the circumstances changed. The circumstances changed because the song came.

**Singing is not always the result of breakthrough. Sometimes it is the instrument of it.**

### Sing the Gospel to Yourself

And let the content of the song be the gospel. Colossians says to teach and admonish one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs (Colossians 3:16).

What you sing, you remember. What you remember, you believe in the dark.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, singing is not a technique for manipulating outcomes. It is the voice of a heart that trusts, not a lever on the universe.

Second, singing does not replace obedience. Faith and wisdom sing all the way to the doctor, the reconciliation meeting, and the unpaid invoice — and then do what needs doing.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Luke 2:8–20 and mark every place somebody responded to the news.

2. Learn one hymn or chorus by heart this week.

3. Sing it out loud when the house is empty — or let the car be the chapel.

4. Sing the gospel over one situation that has felt dark.

5. **Sing, and keep faith and wisdom together.** Let the song carry your hope and let your hands still do the work. Praise is not a substitute for prudence; it is the atmosphere in which prudence becomes joyful.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Psalm 148:1–14.** Note who is commanded to praise.
- **Read Acts 16:22–34.** Note what the singing preceded.
- **Sing on a hard morning before you check your phone.**
- **Write one verse of your own gratitude.**
- **Notice what the song does to the room — and to you.**
