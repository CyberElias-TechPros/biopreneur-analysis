Abraham was ninety-nine and Sarah was past the age of childbearing. Romans does not soften the facts. It says he "considered not his own body now dead" (Romans 4:19) and that Sarah's womb was dead too. And then it says he did not stagger.

"He staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief; but was strong in faith, giving glory to God" (Romans 4:20). Here is the central truth of this teaching: **unwavering faith is not the absence of difficulty but the absence of double-mindedness — Abraham looked straight at the impossible and chose to give God glory anyway.**

## Staggering Is an Inner Motion

The word pictures a wavering balance, a person pulled between two positions. Staggering is not the same as struggling. A man can grieve, question, and wait without staggering; what he cannot do is set up two thrones and consult both.

**The Life of Faith** — a life ordered by God's declarations — is a life that has stopped renegotiating. **The Word of God** — His settled utterance — is the single throne. And the outcome Paul names is striking: Abraham was "strong in faith, **giving glory to God**." Faith is not merely a means of getting something; it is an act of worship.

Think of a bridge surveyor who trusts the engineer's report rather than the sway he feels mid-span. The sway is real. He does not deny it. He simply knows which report governs the crossing.

## The Anatomy, Step by Step

Paul gives four movements, in order, and the order matters.

- **He faced the facts.** "He considered not his own body now dead... nor yet the deadness of Sarah's womb" (Romans 4:19). Faith is not built by pretending the deadness is not there.
- **He fixed his attention.** The verse is a deliberate refusal to keep considering — a redirect of the mind, not a suppression of it.
- **He weighed God's ability.** "What he had promised, he was able also to perform" (Romans 4:21). The scale had one weight on it: God's power.
- **He gave glory.** Faith glorified God before the promise arrived. That is the part we so often miss: the giving of glory happened before the cradle.

Hebrews gives the same man a slightly different angle: "she judged him faithful who had promised" (Hebrews 11:11). Notice what produced the strength. It was not the size of the promise and not the quality of the couple's bodies. It was a judgement — a deliberate verdict about character. Faith is not a mood you summon; it is a conclusion you reach about someone you know.

That is why Paul can speak of being "strong in faith" without implying that Abraham never felt weak. Strength here is not bravado. It is the settled weight of a verdict that has already been returned.

## Why Staggering Is So Expensive

James explains the cost. The one who wavers "is like a wave of the sea driven with the wind and tossed... let not that man think that he shall receive any thing of the Lord" (James 1:6–7). The problem is not that God is stingy; the problem is that a double heart has nothing stable to receive with.

Here is the contrast: staggering keeps asking whether God meant it, while unwavering faith has already settled the question and moved on to obedience. One keeps reopening the file; the other files it and gets to work.

## What Sarah's Laughter Teaches

We should not read Romans 4 as if Abraham and Sarah were made of stone. Sarah laughed at the promise, and God answered her laughter not with rebuke but with a name: Isaac, "laughter." He took the very sound of her unbelief and turned it into the name of her son.

That is a mercy worth sitting with. Have you ever assumed that one wobble has disqualified you from being called strong in faith? What if the God who named laughter after your doubt is the same God who still counts your faith as righteousness? Abraham's own record includes a detour into Hagar, and God still calls him the father of faith.

**1. Answer the facts, do not suppress them.** Name the deadness honestly, then name God's ability over it.
**2. Stop re-asking a settled question.** When God has spoken, treat further doubt as a decision rather than a feeling.
**3. Give glory early.** Thank God for the answer before you can see it.
**4. Feed your attention deliberately.** Faith comes by hearing, so choose the voices that report God's ability.
**5. Faith and wisdom test the promise.** Faith does not stagger at what God has said, but wisdom checks whether God actually said it, rather than declaring whatever you want.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Write out your deadness in one sentence.** Then write Romans 4:21 underneath it.
- **Give glory before the answer.** Thank God daily this week for something you cannot yet see.
- **Name your double opinion.** Identify the second voice you have been consulting and stop it.
- **Read Genesis 21:1–7.** Watch the laughter become a name.
