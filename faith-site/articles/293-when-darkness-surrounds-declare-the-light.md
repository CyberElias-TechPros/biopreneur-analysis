The first recorded words of God in Scripture are not an explanation. They are a command, spoken into a situation that had nothing in it to work with.

"And God said, Let there be light: and there was light" (Genesis 1:3).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **God did not describe the darkness; He addressed it — and the same voice that spoke light into a void has been given to a people who carry it in earthen vessels.**

## The Situation

### Without Form and Void

And Genesis 1:2 is precise about the raw material: *tohu wabohu*, unformed and unfilled, with darkness on the face of the deep.

Not a slightly untidy room. A nothing.

**God did not begin with potential. He began with a blank.** So what are you waiting for before you speak?

### The Spirit Moved

And there was one thing present: "and the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters."

The Hebrew is a brooding, hovering image — a bird over a nest. The Spirit was already there before the word came.

**Where God is about to speak, His Spirit is already brooding.**

### The Method

And the method was speech. Not effort, not negotiation, not a gradual improvement of the darkness.

**The instrument of creation was a sentence.**

## Why Speech

### It Creates

And in Scripture, words do things. "Through faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God" (Hebrews 11:3).

The universe is not merely described by words. It is made of them.

**Speech is not commentary on reality. In God's hands, it is construction.**

### It Addresses Rather Than Reports

And the difference matters more than it sounds. Reporting says: it is dark in here. Addressing says: light, come.

One is accurate and useless. The other is an act.

**Description is honest. Declaration is effectual. Faith does both, but only one of them changes the room.**

### It Distinguishes

And Genesis 1:4 says God separated the light from the darkness — a function of naming and defining.

Light without distinction is just a bright fog.

**God's words do not only add. They sort.**

### The Light in You

### The Same Command

And Paul explicitly connects creation to conversion: "For God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts, to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" (2 Corinthians 4:6).

The voice that made the first light made the second.

**Your conversion is a creation event with the same speaker.**

### In Earthen Vessels

And the very next verse explains the delivery system: "But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us" (2 Corinthians 4:7).

The container is deliberately unimpressive.

The vessel is clay so that nobody mistakes the light for the pot.

### Therefore Declare

And Isaiah's call follows: "Arise, shine; for thy light is come, and the glory of the LORD is risen upon thee" (Isaiah 60:1).

*Arise* and *shine* — imperatives addressed to people who already have light and are behaving as if they do not.

You are not asked to manufacture light. You are asked to stop hiding under a basket.

### What Declaration Is Not

### Not Denial

And declaring light is not pretending the room is bright. Genesis does not say the darkness was imaginary.

It says the light came into a real dark.

Faith names the darkness accurately and then speaks to it.

### Not Magic

And there is no formula. The words are not incantations, and the power is not in the phonetics.

It is in the God who hears and answers.

A sentence spoken without faith is just a sentence.

### Not Volume

And shouting is not declaration. Elijah's contest was won by a still small voice after the shouting failed (1 Kings 18–19).

The light does not need you to be loud. It needs you to be honest and unashamed.

### Speaking Into Your Dark

### Name the Void

And the first step is specific: what exactly is dark? A marriage, a diagnosis, a debt, a mind, a future.

Vague declarations produce vague expectation.

### Say What God Says

And the second is to find the Word that addresses it, and say that. Not your preferred outcome — His stated will.

Declaration is agreement, spoken out loud.

### Say It Before You See It

And the third is the difficult one. Romans 4:17 describes God as the one who "calleth those things which be not as though they were."

That is the pattern. Speak first, see later.

If you wait for the evidence, you are describing. If you speak before it, you are creating a space for it.

### Keep Speaking

And the fourth is persistence. Ezekiel prophesied to dry bones, and then prophesied again to the wind (Ezekiel 37:4, 9).

Two rounds. One word was not the end of it.

Some voids require a second sentence.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, declaration is not a way to control God. It is agreement with Him, not instruction to Him.

Second, speaking light does not remove the need for action. The light shows the path; you still have to walk it.

**The **Confession of the Word** is the speech; **The Word of God** is its content; and **Revelation Knowledge is what tells you which word this darkness needs.

## What This Means for You

1. Write the dark thing down plainly.

2. Find a verse that addresses it.

3. Say it aloud, every morning, for a week.

4. Stop describing the situation to everyone who will listen.

5. Keep faith and wisdom together. Light shows the way; it does not walk it. **The Word of God** is the declaration; wisdom is the plan you execute once the room is lit.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Genesis 1:1–5 each morning.**
- **Read 2 Corinthians 4:1–7 twice.**
- **Write your three darkest realities.**
- **Match a verse to each.**
- **Say them out loud before breakfast.**
