Ask most believers whether God wants them well and they will say yes. Ask them whether they expect to be well, and the answer changes — because somewhere along the way they started treating healing the way people treat a raffle: someone wins, and you hope it is you.

**Psalm 103** does not read like a raffle. "Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits: who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases" (Psalm 103:2–3).

Notice the word. Benefits. Itemised, listed, belonging to the covenant.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **a lottery hands out prizes at random and leaves the loser with nothing to appeal; a covenant names its benefits in writing and gives every member of the family a legitimate claim.**

That is not a small shift in vocabulary. It is the difference between begging and inheriting.

## What Makes a Covenant Different

### A Covenant Names Terms

A lottery publishes odds. A covenant publishes terms.

When God cut covenant with Israel, He did not say "some of you will be blessed." He set the terms out in writing, in advance, with the blessings on one side and the curses on the other (Deuteronomy 28). Anyone in the nation could read what belonged to them.

And look at what the blessings included. "Blessed shalt thou be in the city, and blessed shalt thou be in the field... Blessed shalt thou be in thy basket and thy store" (Deuteronomy 28:3, 5). Then: "And the LORD will take away from thee all sickness, and will put none of the evil diseases of Egypt, which thou knowest, upon thee" (Deuteronomy 28:60–61).

**Health was never a bonus feature. It was in the contract.**

### The Curse Was Named, So the Redemption Is Named

Here is the hinge that most teaching skips.

Deuteronomy 28 lists the curses too, and among them "the LORD will smite thee with a consumption, and with a fever, and with an inflammation" (Deuteronomy 28:22). Sickness was not a footnote in the old covenant's penalty; it was spelled out.

Now read Paul: "Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us" (Galatians 3:13).

If the curse named sickness, and Christ redeemed you from the curse, then your freedom from sickness is not a hopeful extrapolation. It is a term of the covenant, paid for in the same transaction that paid for your forgiveness.

### Forgiveth and Healeth in One Breath

Look again at how Psalm 103 pairs them: "who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases."

Two clauses, one relative pronoun, one breath. The psalmist does not rank them or separate them by dispensations. He lists them as benefits of the same God, to the same people, in the same sentence — and then says the reason is that He is "slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy" (Psalm 103:8).

**The Blood of Jesus** is what makes both clauses legal rather than wishful. The same blood that cleanses the conscience purchased the whole package.

### The Difference Between a Right and a Guarantee

#### Why "Right" Is the Right Word

A right is something owed to you on the basis of a relationship, not something you talk God into. The word "right" is not arrogance when the covenant is the source of it — it is simply reading the document.

But a right is not a guarantee of circumstances. Two passengers can hold the same ticket to the same destination; one arrives through calm air, the other through a storm that shakes the meal tray. The ticket was never in question.

That distinction preserves both things the Bible says: healing belongs to you, and the body is still mortal until the redemption of the body (Romans 8:23).

#### Why Some Never Claim It

**Legal Position** exists whether or not the heir knows it. An unread will does not invalidate an estate.

That is why teaching matters more than most believers suspect. A person who thinks healing is a lottery prays like a gambler. A person who knows it is covenant prays like a child with a name in the will.

And what about the believer who has known and is still unwell? Two honest answers, and both are in Scripture: the body is mortal and some things await the resurrection, and some promises arrive after a delay that is not a denial. Hebrews 11 lists people who "through faith... were made strong" and, in the same chapter, people who "were stoned... were sawn asunder" (Hebrews 11:34, 37). The chapter does not apologise for the second group, and neither should we pretend they did not exist.

#### The Covenant Is Better, Not Merely Repeated

Hebrews says Christ is "the mediator of a better covenant, which was established upon better promises" (Hebrews 8:6).

Better, not merely renewed. Which means the New Covenant does not give you less access to God's healing than Deuteronomy gave Israel. It gives more — a cleansed conscience, an indwelling Spirit, and a Mediator who has already passed through death.

So if the old covenant could say "I will take away all sickness from you," what is the new one saying? Read it and see.

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop praying as a stranger.** You are not asking a distant God for a favour He might not feel like granting. You are standing in a covenant and naming its terms.

**2. Learn the benefits.** You cannot claim what you cannot name. Read Psalm 103 and Deuteronomy 28 slowly this week and write down what belongs to you.

**3. Let the cross settle the question of God's willingness.** The debate about whether God wants you well was answered at Calvary, not in your circumstances.

**4. Refuse the gambler's vocabulary.** Strike "if it is God's will" from your prayers about healing where the covenant has already spoken, and keep it where Scripture has not.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** A covenant right and a doctor's appointment are not in competition; faith and wisdom work together. The One who wrote the covenant also made the body that heals with rest, food, and treatment.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Psalm 103:1–14** and underline every benefit.
- **Read Deuteronomy 28:1–14 and 28:58–61** and note both sides of the covenant.
- **Say "I am not waiting on a draw"** over your body once a day this week.
- **Write out Galatians 3:13** and circle the word "curse."
- **Read Hebrews 8:6–13** and notice the word "better."
