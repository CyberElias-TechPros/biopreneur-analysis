Paul makes one of the boldest financial statements in the Bible, and he makes it in a chapter asking people to give money away.

"For ye know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Paul uses *ptōcheia* — beggarly poverty, not merely modest means — and he makes it the engine of a prosperity that is measured by what God gives, not what you demand, because generosity is what grace produces in a person who has been made rich.**

## The Two Accounts

### He Was Rich

And Paul says it as a settled fact. "Though he was rich." Not in a metaphor about good character; in the glory He shared with the Father before the world was (John 17:5).

He had a throne. He had worship. He had the fullness of everything.

### He Became Poor

And the word is *ptōcheia*, related to *ptōchos* — a beggar, one who crouches and cowers. Not the merely poor man who works; the destitute man who has nothing.

He was born into a family that offered two turtle doves instead of a lamb (Luke 2:24) — the offering of the poor in Leviticus 12. He said of Himself, "the foxes have holes, and the birds of the air have nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head" (Matthew 8:20).

### For Your Sakes

And the reason is not abstract. *Di' hymas* — for you. The poverty was purposeful and directed at a person.

**He did not become poor by accident. He became poor by assignment.**

## What the Poverty Purchased

### That You Might Be Rich

And Paul's purpose clause is *hina hymeis ploutēsēte* — that you might become rich. He is not embarrassed by the word. He uses it.

Which means generosity in the gospel is not funded by self-hatred about money. It is funded by an inheritance.

### Riches in What?

And Paul is careful elsewhere. He says God has chosen the poor of this world rich in faith (James 2:5), and he speaks of the unsearchable riches of Christ (Ephesians 3:8).

So the riches are not only material, and not less than material. They begin with reconciliation and reach as far as the practical need of a starving brother.

### A Distribution, Not a Luxury

And here is the sting of Paul's argument in context: he is collecting money for poor believers in Jerusalem. He uses the incarnation to motivate a transfer of resources.

The cross is not a metaphor for generosity. It is the reason for it.

### How Grace Handles Money

### Grace Makes You Rich Without Making You Greedy

And this is the miracle the world cannot copy. A man who knows his account is settled no longer needs money to tell him who he is.

That man can give. That man can wait. That man can be generous without needing a return.

### The Macedonian Example

And Paul introduces this verse right after describing churches in deep poverty who begged to be allowed to give (2 Corinthians 8:1–4).

Those were not wealthy people giving from surplus. They were poor people giving from grace.

**The generosity of the gospel is not proportional to the balance. It is proportional to the revelation.**

### Proportional, Not Equal

And Paul adds the rule: not that others be eased and you burdened, but by equality — your abundance supplying their lack (2 Corinthians 8:13–14).

Grace is not a demand for bankruptcy. It is an economy of surplus moving toward need.

### Living Rich

### Work and Give

And Paul's own rule is plain: let him that stole steal no more, but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth (Ephesians 4:28).

The point of earning is the ability to give.

### Sow Deliberately

And 2 Corinthians 9 says he that soweth sparingly shall reap sparingly, and God loves a cheerful giver (2 Corinthians 9:6–7). The measure is willingness, not pressure.

### Hold It Open

And Proverbs says there is that scattereth and yet increaseth, and there is that withholdeth more than is meet, but it tendeth to poverty (Proverbs 11:24).

The closed hand is not merely unkind; it is unwise.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not the health-and-wealth bargain that makes God a vending machine. He became poor for your sake, not so you could leverage Him into a luxury car.

Second, this is not the poverty theology that treats lack as a virtue. Faith and wisdom handle both: the atonement is not a coupon, and your bank balance is not a measure of your godliness either way.

## What This Means for You

1. Read 2 Corinthians 8:1–15 in one sitting and note who gives in the passage.

2. Ask: does my giving look like the Macedonians or like a tip?

3. Choose one specific need and meet it this month.

4. Write out the verse and put it where you will see it.

5. **Give with faith and wisdom together.** Give according to what you have, not what you owe. Budget the giving into the month, and then let it be joyful rather than a debt.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Philippians 2:5–11.** Note what "rich" and "poor" cost Him.
- **Read Proverbs 11:24–28.** Note what scattering does.
- **Give something you would rather keep.**
- **Say thank You for an inheritance you did not earn.**
- **Notice whether you have been handling money as an owner or a steward.**
