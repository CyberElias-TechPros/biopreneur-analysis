This is one of the most quoted verses in the Bible and one of the least understood, because it is almost always preached as a warning rather than a promise.

"Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap" (Galatians 6:7).

Read it again without the funeral tone. It is a description of a fixed order, and a fixed order is good news for anyone who has been treated unfairly.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **sowing and reaping is not a threat hanging over the ungodly; it is a fixed mercy, because a law that works the same for everyone means your harvest is not at the mercy of someone else's mood.**

## The Law and Its Shape

### Established Before Anyone Broke It

The principle is older than the law of Moses and older than Galatians. It was written into creation itself: "While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease" (Genesis 8:22).

God said that after the flood, with a rainbow in the sky. It is a promise about reliability. **The world runs on a dependable order, and that order includes consequences.**

### Same Kind

The first thing to notice about the law is that it is *specific*. Paul does not say "whatsoever a man soweth, something will come up." He says *that* shall he also reap.

Wheat does not produce barley. A field sown with resentment does not produce peace with a different label on it.

The harvest always matches the seed's species, even when the timing disguises it.

### Proportional

Paul states the measure explicitly in the same letter's earlier argument: "But this I say, He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully" (2 Corinthians 9:6).

Two farmers, same field, same sun. Different harvest. **The difference is not favour; it is volume.** So which would your field say you have been doing?

### With a Delay

The law's most frustrating feature is its timing. Seed goes into the ground and nothing happens. For weeks.

James uses exactly that patience as his picture of the Christian life: "Be patient therefore, brethren, unto the coming of the Lord. Behold, the husband of the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth, and hath long patience for it" (James 5:7).

**A person who cannot wait cannot farm, and a person who cannot farm cannot walk with God for long.**

### Continuous

Paul adds a boundary to the reaping that is easy to miss: "For he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting" (Galatians 6:8).

Two fields, and only two. Every hour is being sown into one of them. **There is no neutral ground where nothing is being planted.**

### Why a Fixed Law Is a Mercy

#### Because God Is Not Partial

The verse's opening phrase is the key: *God is not mocked.*

The Greek is *muktērizō*, to turn up the nose at, to sneer. Paul is saying that no one gets an exemption by pretending the rule does not apply.

**And that is precisely the comfort.** If the law could be bent for the powerful, it could also be bent against you.

#### Because No One Can Steal Your Harvest

A law without favourites means a harvest that cannot be confiscated by someone with better connections.

Whatever you have sown in secret — the kindness nobody saw, the integrity that cost you the deal, the prayer at 3 a.m. — is in a field that no one else has access to.

Sown seed is the safest asset in the world, because its owner is the law itself.

#### Because You Are Not Dependent on the Soil You Inherited

Some people start life in poor soil. The law does not promise them a good field; it promises them a fair one.

Hosea gives the invitation: "Sow to yourselves in righteousness, reap in mercy; break up your fallow ground: for it is time to seek the LORD, till he come and rain righteousness upon you" (Hosea 10:12).

*Break up your fallow ground* — there is ground in your life that has never been turned. **You are not stuck with last season's field.** And how much of your ground has never actually been turned?

#### Because Weeping Is Not the End

Psalm 126 makes the promise about the harvest in a way that speaks directly to anyone sowing through tears: "They that sow in tears shall reap in joy. He that goeth forth and weepeth, bearing precious seed, shall doubtless come again with rejoicing, bringing his sheaves with him" (Psalm 126:5–6).

Notice the phrase *bearing precious seed*. The weeping one is still carrying seed. **The grief and the sowing are happening in the same hand, and the promise is that both have a future.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Ask what you are actually sowing.** Not what you intend to plant — what you planted last week in words, money, time, and attention.

**2. Sow in the field that is inside you first.** Thoughts, appetites, and speech are the nearest soil you own.

**3. Increase the volume.** The harvest is proportional. If the return is thin, the honest first question is about the amount of seed, not God's willingness.

**4. Protect the waiting.** **Faith and Wisdom** are what patience looks like in a person: the seed is in, the work is done, and the field is being watched without being dug up every morning.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Pray over the field and plough it. Faith without work sows nothing; work without faith panics at the wait.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Galatians 6:6–10** every morning and note the phrase "in due season."
- **Write down three harvests** you would like in a year, and the seed that produces each.
- **Sow one of them this week**, concretely.
- **Read Psalm 126 aloud** if this season has been a weeping one.
- **Break up one fallow patch** — a habit, a relationship, a skill you have let go.

**The central truth, said plainly:** Sowing and reaping is not a threat hanging over the ungodly; it is a fixed mercy, because a law that works the same for everyone means your harvest is not at the mercy of someone else's mood.

**Declare this today:**
