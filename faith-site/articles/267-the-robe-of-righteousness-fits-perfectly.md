Clothing is one of Scripture's most persistent images for salvation, and it is a strangely intimate one.

"I will greatly rejoice in the LORD, my soul shall be joyful in my God; for he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness" (Isaiah 61:10).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the robe of righteousness is not a uniform issued in bulk; it is a garment measured to the person wearing it, which is why it needs neither altering to fit you nor effort from you to keep it on.**

## Why Clothing

### It Covers

The first function of a garment is the one everybody understands. It covers what we would rather not display.

"And the eyes of them both were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaves together" (Genesis 3:7). The first human reaction to sin was a wardrobe decision.

**God's answer to that instinct was not to remove the shame but to provide a better covering.**

### It Is Given

And the covering in Scripture is always received, never woven. "Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them" (Genesis 3:21).

Somebody else did the making. Somebody else did the killing, in fact.

**The first wardrobe in the Bible cost an animal its life, and it was not the guilty party's.**

### It Identifies

And clothing carries status. The robe in the parable is the sign of sonship; the ring is the seal of authority; the shoes are the mark of a free man rather than a servant.

**What you wear in the Father's house tells everyone which family you belong to.**

## The Garments God Gives

### Zechariah's Exchange

The clearest single picture: Joshua stood in filthy garments, the accuser stood at his right hand, and the command came — "Take away the filthy garments from him... and I will clothe thee with change of raiment" (Zechariah 3:4).

The removal and the replacement are both acts of the court.

**Righteousness was not improved. It was exchanged.** Which of the two have you been trying to do?

### The Best Robe

And the parable makes the same point in domestic language: "But the father said to his servants, Bring forth the best robe, and put it on him" (Luke 15:22).

*Best* — *prōtos*, the first, the principal one. Not a spare.

**The returning son was not issued work clothes. He was dressed as an heir.**

### The Wedding Garment

And Jesus' parable of the wedding feast has a hard edge: a guest without the garment is removed (Matthew 22:11–13).

The garment is provided; refusing it is the offence.

Nobody is excluded for lacking a robe. They are excluded for declining the one offered.

### Why It Fits

### Measured, Not Bulk

Isaiah's Hebrew for *covered* carries the sense of wrapping closely, of enveloping. It is not a tarpaulin thrown over a shape.

And in the gospel, the righteousness given is Christ's own — which means it is exactly proportionate to the person who is in Him.

You are not wearing someone else's coat. You are wearing the family garment, and it was made for the family you were put into.

### It Needs No Altering

And this is where most believers spend their lives: adjusting the robe.

Adding to it by effort, taking it in by penance, patching it with good behaviour after a bad week.

The garment does not need your tailoring, and every alteration is a statement about the tailor.

### It Does Not Slip Off

And it is not held on by your grip. The picture in Isaiah is God doing the clothing — the active party is not the wearer.

A garment put on you by God does not depend on you noticing it is there.

### Why We Still Feel Badly Dressed

And here is the honest pastoral point: feelings of shame persist after the robe is on, because shame was learned before it was healed.

The answer is not to sew fig leaves. It is to keep reading the wardrobe record.

You do not become clean by feeling clean. You feel clean, eventually, by believing you are.

### Wearing It

### Stop Hiding

The fig-leaf instinct does not disappear at conversion. It reappears every time we conceal a failure.

Walking in the light is simply refusing to hide what the robe already covers (1 John 1:7).

### Stop Comparing

And garments are personal. Comparing your robe to another's produces either pride or despair, and both are distractions from the One who dressed you.

### Let It Be Seen

And the robe is not for indoor use. Isaiah's chapter ends in praise that is public: "he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decketh himself with ornaments" (Isaiah 61:10).

**Dressed people go outside.** So what would it look like to stop dressing only in private?

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, the robe is not a licence to live carelessly. Peter says be clothed with humility (1 Peter 5:5) and Revelation says the fine linen is the righteous acts of the saints (Revelation 19:8). The garment produces behaviour; it does not replace it.

Second, the garment is not a costume. It is not something you put on for meetings and take off at home.

**The **Righteousness you wear is a gift; Justification is the fitting; and the The Inheritance of the Believer** is what a dressed son has been given to manage.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Read Zechariah 3:1–5 and Luke 15:11–24 together.**

**2. Name the alteration** you have been making to your standing.

**3. Stop hiding one thing this week.**

**4. Read Isaiah 61 in full** and mark every garment.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Being dressed does not remove the need to walk carefully — it makes careful walking possible. **Righteousness** is given; wisdom is what a well-dressed person does next.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Isaiah 61 every morning this week.**
- **Write down what you believe you must add to be acceptable.**
- **Read Revelation 19:6–9** and note who is credited with the linen.
- **Confess one hidden thing.**
- **Thank God for the robe** before you check the mirror.

**The central truth, said plainly:** The robe of righteousness is not a uniform issued in bulk; it is a garment measured to the person wearing it, which is why it needs neither altering to fit you nor effort from you to keep it on.

> "Father, thank You that You did not clothe me in something that nearly fits. Let me wear Your righteousness without shrinking it, stretching it, or hiding under it, and let the garment be visible in the way I live, in Jesus' Name. Amen."
