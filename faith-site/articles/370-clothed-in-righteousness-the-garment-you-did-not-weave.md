Everything in the ancient world was read through what a person wore. A robe announced a rank, a grief, a wedding, or a debt. That is why Scripture speaks of righteousness as clothing more often than almost any other image, and why the imagery is not decoration.

"I will greatly rejoice in the LORD... for he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness" (Isaiah 61:10). Here is the central truth of this teaching: **righteousness is not a garment you sew by good behaviour; it is one God puts on you — and the whole of the Christian life changes when you stop trying to weave what you were given to wear.**

## Read the Verbs

Isaiah does not say, "I have woven a robe." He says, "he hath clothed me." The verb belongs to God from beginning to end, and this is the difference between religion and the gospel in a single sentence.

**Righteousness** — right standing with God, given as a gift rather than earned as a wage — is what the robe is made of. **Legal Position** — the standing you hold before God's court because of Christ rather than because of your record — is what the garment secures. **The Consciousness of Righteousness** — a mind that knows what it is wearing — is what the garment produces when it is finally believed.

Notice the two layers in the verse. There are "garments of salvation" *and* a "robe of righteousness." Salvation is the rescue; righteousness is the standing that follows it. Both are given. Neither is requested from a wardrobe of your own making.

### Fig Leaves and the First Attempt

The Bible's first clothing crisis is in Genesis 3 — have you ever noticed how much of the gospel it already contains? Adam and Eve sin, and their instinct is to cover themselves: "they sewed fig leaves together, and made themselves aprons" (Genesis 3:7). That is the first religion in human history, and its logic has not changed. Something is exposed, so something is manufactured.

God's response is not to admire the tailoring. He clothes them Himself with coats of skins (Genesis 3:21), and the skins required a death. Which covering, then, would you rather stand in? The pattern is set at the beginning of the story: the covering God accepts is the one He provides, and it costs a life.

**The robe of righteousness is not a repaired garment, but a replacement.** Trying to improve your own covering is not a step toward the robe; it is a refusal of it. And that is where many sincere believers live — not in open sin, but in a constant, low-grade tailoring project, patching together a standing before God out of yesterday's obedience and this morning's quiet time.

### The Priest in the Filthy Garments

The book of Zechariah gives the same doctrine as a courtroom scene. Joshua the high priest stands before the angel of the LORD "clothed with filthy garments," and Satan stands at his right hand to resist him (Zechariah 3:1). Watch what happens. The LORD does not ask Joshua to go and wash. He speaks to those standing by: "Take away the filthy garments from him... Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment" (Zechariah 3:4).

The accuser's objection is answered by a change of clothes, and the change is commanded by the Judge. There is no clearer picture anywhere in Scripture of what justification is. The case is not won by arguing with the accuser. It is won by a garment.

Then comes the detail that makes the passage remarkable: "And I said, Let them set a fair mitre upon his head" (Zechariah 3:5). The high priest's turban carried the words *Holiness to the LORD*. The man is given a new standing and a new identity in a single transaction, and he contributes nothing but the standing there.

### What Being Clothed Changes

Walk into a room wearing the wrong clothes and notice what happens to your posture. It is not a moral failure to feel underdressed; it is simply what clothing does to a person, and it is exactly why God speaks in garments. That is precisely why God speaks of righteousness as clothing rather than as a concept. **What you wear shapes how you stand.**

This is the practical difference between two believers with the same theology. The first believes in justification by faith and still enters prayer like a man expecting to be searched at the door. His theology is orthodox and his posture is a servant's. The second believes the same verse and enters prayer the way a son enters his father's house. Both have the same robe. Only one is conscious of wearing it.

Jesus told a parable about this at a wedding: a guest arrives without the wedding garment and is speechless when challenged (Matthew 22:11–12). The garment was provided by the host, and refusing it was the offence. That is a sober picture, and it is the clearest reason not to spend a life trying to look acceptable in something of our own manufacture.

## What This Means for You

**1. Name the garment you are actually wearing.** When you feel accused, ask what you are standing in — His robe, or a fresh set of fig leaves sewn since morning.

**2. Stop repairing what must be replaced.** Effort spent improving your own covering is effort not spent receiving His. Confession is the act of taking off, not tidying up.

**3. Dress for the wedding, not the audition.** The robe was given for relationship, not for performance review. Let your conduct flow from the garment rather than attempting to earn it.

**4. Practise the garment language.** Say what Isaiah said: "He hath clothed me with the robe of righteousness." The **Confession of the Word (Homologia)** is not positive talk; it is agreeing out loud with what God has already done.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom together keep the garment unstained.** Faith declares the robe is on; wisdom keeps the feet clean. Faith without wisdom turns grace into carelessness, and wisdom without faith turns it into a dress code. Neither one ignores the ordinary means — honest examination, forgiveness, accountable friendship, and where needed the sound care of body and mind — because a person who is properly dressed also looks after what they are wearing.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Isaiah 61:10 aloud every morning** for seven days, in the first person, before you check anything else.
- **Read Zechariah 3:1–5 and mark every verb** God performs and every verb Joshua performs.
- **Write down your last fig leaf** — the one thing you do to feel acceptable — and name it honestly before God.
- **Read Luke 15:20–24** and notice that the robe, the ring, and the shoes were all given before the son said a word about being a servant.
- **When the accuser speaks this week**, answer with the garment rather than with an argument.
