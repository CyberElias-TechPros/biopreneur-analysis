Ephesians 5:11 contains two instructions that look contradictory until you notice they are both about light.

"And have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness, but rather reprove them."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **light does not attack the dark; it simply makes things visible — and the mandate to expose is not a licence for suspicion but an invitation for whatever is hidden to be healed.**

## The Two Commands

### No Fellowship

And the first word is *synkoinōneō* — to share with, to participate jointly. It is not merely "do not do it." It is "do not partner in it."

Which is broader than personal sin. It covers endorsing, profiting from, and quietly enabling.

**You can be in fellowship with a work you are not personally committing.** So what are you quietly funding, endorsing, or tolerating?

### Reprove

And the second word is *elegchō* — to convict, to expose, to bring to the light for the purpose of correction. It is the same verb used of the Spirit's conviction (John 16:8).

It is a judicial word, and its goal is not humiliation. It is change.

**Conviction is the Spirit's method. Condemnation is the enemy's. Both are loud about sin.**

### Both at Once

And the two belong together. You cannot expose what you are quietly participating in, and you cannot participate in what you are genuinely exposing.

**The integrity of the message depends on the separation of the messenger.** Which is why the two commands are one command.

## How Light Works

### It Does Not Struggle

And light is a strange weapon because it has no force. It does not wrestle the darkness; it simply arrives, and the darkness is not consulted.

That is why Paul's next verse is about everything becoming visible (Ephesians 5:13).

**Light is not an attack. It is a presence.**

### It Reveals Shape

And light does two things to a room: it shows you what is in it, and it shows you how to walk through it.

Exposure without direction is just a scandal.

**It is not enough to reveal the hole. Show the way around it.**

### It Heals

And here is the part that fear obscures. Things exposed to light get treated; things kept in the dark get worse.

"Confess your faults one to another, that ye may be healed" (James 5:16). The order is exposure, then healing.

**Concealment is not protection. It is an untreated wound.**

### The Fear That Stops Us

### Fear of Conflict

And most believers avoid reproof because they dread the conversation. It is easier to be quiet and call it grace.

But silence about a work of darkness is not grace toward the person in it.

**A peace that requires you to lie is not peace.**

### Fear of the Darkness Itself

And some are afraid to name what is happening, as though naming it might summon it.

But Paul's whole point is that light is stronger, and a named darkness is already being handled.

What you cannot name will govern you. What you name in the light can be dealt with.

### Fear of Being Wrong

And some are afraid of the accusation of judgmentalism. That is a real risk, and Jesus warned about it (Matthew 7:1–5).

But the warning was against hypocritical judgment, not against all discernment.

Fear of judging badly is not a reason to stop seeing clearly.

### Doing It Well

### Deal With Your Own Light First

And the plank is the prerequisite. You cannot reprove what you are practising.

That is not a demand for perfection. It is a demand for honesty about your own log.

A person who is being exposed to light themselves can safely expose others.

### Go to the Person

And the order is private first: "go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone" (Matthew 18:15).

Public exposure is the last resort, not the first instinct.

If you have not spoken to them, you have not yet reproved them. You have only discussed them.

### Speak to the Deed, Not the Worth

And the language matters. "That was wrong" is reproof. "You are worthless" is not.

Paul's phrase is *works* of darkness. The deeds are the subject.

Name the deed plainly and leave the person's dignity intact.

### Leave Room for Repentance

And the aim is restoration, which means leaving space for a person to turn without being destroyed.

"A brother offended is harder to be won than a strong city" (Proverbs 18:19).

The measure of a reproof is not whether it was true. It is whether it was received.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a commission to police the world. Ephesians is addressed to the church, and 1 Corinthians 5:12 says explicitly that judging those outside is God's business.

Second, this is not permission for cruelty. The same Paul who says "reprove" says to do it with all meekness (Galatians 6:1).

**The **The Word of God is the standard of light; the The Ministry of Reconciliation** is the goal; and **Spiritual Warfare is fought with exposure rather than accusation.

## What This Means for You

**1. Check your fellowships.** What darkness are you quietly partnering with?

2. Identify one thing you are afraid to name.

**3. Name it — in private, to the person, with the deed as the subject.**

**4. Let your own light be examined first.**

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Exposure needs timing and tact. **The Word of God** is the standard; wisdom decides who to speak to, when, and in what room.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Ephesians 5:1–21 twice.**
- **Read Matthew 18:15–20.**
- **Write down what you have been afraid to name.**
- **Pray about the person before you speak.**
- **Have the conversation** — or the part of it that is yours.
