Psalm 85 is a prayer for revival, and at its centre is one of the most beautiful lines in the Old Testament.

"Mercy and truth are met together; righteousness and peace have kissed each other" (Psalm 85:10).

The picture is of two parties who were apart, and have now embraced.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **righteousness and peace are joined in Scripture the way a kiss joins two faces — which means any peace that is bought by lowering the standard is not peace, and any righteousness defended without love is not righteousness.**

## Where They Met

### The Problem Between Them

And the tension is real and old. If God is righteous, how can He be at peace with the guilty? If He makes peace, has He compromised His justice?

Every religion in history has answered that by sacrificing one of the two.

**Either the standard bends, or the sinner is destroyed — unless a third possibility exists.**

### The Answer

And the gospel is that third possibility. "Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins... that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus" (Romans 3:25–26).

*Just, and the justifier.* Both, in the same act, at the same moment.

**The cross is where the kiss happened, and both parties were fully present.**

### What It Cost

And notice the cost to both. Righteousness did not relax; it was satisfied. Peace did not come cheap; it was purchased.

The kiss was not a compromise. It was a reconciliation.

**Cheap peace and cheap righteousness are both counterfeits, and the cross is the proof of the real price.**

## Peace With God

### Objective Before Subjective

And Paul draws the conclusion without a stumble: "Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ" (Romans 5:1).

Peace with God is not a feeling. It is a status. It is the end of hostility, declared from the bench.

**The war ended before you felt anything about it.**

### Then the Feeling

And the feeling follows — "the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts" (Philippians 4:7).

The subjective peace is real, and it is downstream.

**You do not get peace by pursuing peace. You get it by pursuing the God who made it.**

### Guarding It

And it can be lost, in the practical sense. Not the status, but the experience of it — through unconfessed sin, through unbelief, through a mind that will not stop rehearsing.

That is what Hebrews means by seeing to it that no root of bitterness springs up (Hebrews 12:15).

The treaty stands. But a believer can live like a man who has not heard the news.

### The Two Errors

### Peace Without Righteousness

The first error trades truth for calm. It refuses to name sin, redefines God's standards as cultural, and calls the resulting numbness "grace."

It is not grace. It is a cease-fire with the flesh.

A peace that required no cross is not the peace of God. It is a truce with the thing He died to kill.

### Righteousness Without Peace

And the second error is louder and more religious. It defends truth without love, corrects without gentleness, and calls the resulting tension "standing for holiness."

But the fruit of the Spirit includes both, and they grow on the same tree.

A righteousness that leaves no peace in its wake has become a personality, not a fruit.

### Both in the Same Life

And Paul's kingdom summary holds them together: "For the kingdom of God is not meat and drink; but righteousness, and peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost" (Romans 14:17).

Three. In that order. And the third is the evidence the first two are present.

Joy is the smell of a life where righteousness and peace have stopped fighting.

### Keeping Them Kissed

### Never Trade One

And in any decision, refuse the trade. Do not buy peace by lying, and do not defend truth by wounding.

If both cannot be had, you have probably misidentified one of them.

The Spirit never leads you to sin in order to produce a virtue.

### Let Peace Referee

And Colossians gives the peace of Christ a judicial role: "let the peace of God rule in your hearts" (Colossians 3:15). *Rule* is *brabeuō*, to act as umpire.

When peace leaves a decision, treat it as a signal to re-examine, not to proceed faster.

An umpire is not a suggestion box.

### Pursue the Person

And both are pursued in the same place. "Follow peace with all men, and holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord" (Hebrews 12:14).

Two imperatives, one verse, one pursuit.

They are not rival goals. They are two descriptions of one face.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, peace is not the absence of conflict. Jesus came to bring a sword in some households (Matthew 10:34), and faithfulness sometimes costs the calm.

Second, righteousness is not the absence of gentleness. The same Lord described Himself as meek (Matthew 11:29) and did not break a bruised reed.

**The **Justification is where they met; the Righteousness** is what was satisfied; and the **The Glorious Liberty of the Children of God** is the peace that resulted.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Check your last conflict.** Did you defend truth at the cost of love, or keep peace at the cost of truth?

**2. Name a place you have lowered the standard** to keep everyone comfortable.

**3. Name a place you have been harsh** in the name of being right.

**4. Let peace referee one decision this week.**

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Both virtues require skill in application. **Righteousness** is the standing; wisdom is knowing which one a moment is asking for.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Psalm 85 in full.**
- **Read Romans 5:1–11** and mark the word *peace*.
- **Memorise Psalm 85:10.**
- **Apologise to one person** you corrected without gentleness.
- **Confess one thing** you have been calling peace.
