The book of Acts does not begin with a departure; it begins with a promise about what the departure would produce, and the promise is about you.

"But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the ascension did not end the incarnation; it redistributed it — because the same Spirit who formed Christ in Mary now forms Christ in a body of believers who are sent to the same places He was sent.**

## The Same Pattern Twice

### Overshadowed Then

And Luke records the angel's answer to Mary's question: the Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee (Luke 1:35).

That is how the Word became flesh the first time.

### Overshadowed Now

And Jesus tells His disciples to wait in Jerusalem until they are endued with power from on high (Luke 24:49), and then Acts 1:8 says the Holy Ghost shall come upon you.

The same language. The same Spirit. A different body.

**Bethlehem was one body. Pentecost made many.** And the many are still the way He shows up in a town.

### The Body Continues

And Paul says the church is His body, the fulness of Him that filleth all in all (Ephesians 1:22–23). That is not flattery; it is a job description.

Where the body is, the Head is represented.

## What a Witness Is, and Where He Sends

### Not a Lecturer

And the Greek *martys* is the word behind martyr — a witness is someone who testifies to what they have seen and knows.

You do not have to be able to answer every objection. You have to be able to say what He has done.

### Not a Salesman

And a witness is not trying to close a deal. He tells the truth and leaves the verdict with the hearer.

That takes an enormous amount of pressure off, and it also takes away the excuse.

### A Life Plus Words

And a witness is credible because the life matches the testimony. Peter says to be ready always to give an answer with meekness and fear (1 Peter 3:15), and he says it right after describing a good conscience.

**The argument is the life; the words explain it.** So what is your life arguing for this week?

### The Geography of the Sending

### Jerusalem

And the list starts at home. Jerusalem was where they had been, where the opposition was, and where they had failed.

The first mission field is the place you are already known.

### Judaea and Samaria

And then the near and the disliked. Samaria was the half-breed neighbour the disciples wanted to call fire down on (Luke 9:54).

He sends you to the people you would not have chosen.

### The Uttermost Part

And then the ends of the earth — which for a first-century Galilean was an impossible sentence, and for you is a plane ticket or a WhatsApp message.

The scope is a strategy, not a slogan. Begin where you are, expand as you go.

### A Body With Many Locations

And this is the genius of the arrangement. A single man in first-century Palestine could only be in one place; a body of believers scattered across cities can be in all of them at once.

That is why the persecution in Acts 8 did not stop the message — it spread the messengers (Acts 8:4). Every displacement became a distribution.

You are not where you are by accident. The address is part of the assignment — so what would change about your street if you treated it as a sending rather than a stopping place?

### What the Power Is For

### Not for Display

And the power in Acts produced healing, deliverance, and boldness — never a show. Simon the sorcerer wanted to buy it and was rebuked (Acts 8:18–20).

Power is for the assignment, not the audience.

### For Boldness

And the first recorded prayer for power asks for exactly that: that with all boldness they may speak thy word (Acts 4:29).

The miracle they wanted most was courage.

### For the Poor and the Bound

And the summary of Jesus's ministry is good news to the poor, liberty to the captives, recovery of sight to the blind (Luke 4:18). The power follows the assignment.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, power is not a substitute for preparation or character. Faith and wisdom study, show up on time, and keep the accounts clean, because the witness is the whole life.

Second, this is not a call to a platform. Most witnesses never stand on one. Your Jerusalem is likely a kitchen, a shift, or a group chat.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Acts 1:1–11 and note what the disciples asked versus what they were given.

2. Name your Jerusalem in one sentence.

3. Name one person in it who needs a witness, not a lecture.

4. Ask for boldness specifically this week, and then speak.

5. **Be a witness, and keep faith and wisdom together.** Speak with gentleness and respect, keep the relationship open, and let the life carry the weight the words claim.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 2 Corinthians 5:14–21.** Note what an ambassador is.
- **Read Colossians 1:24–29.** Note the mystery in the sentence.
- **Write your testimony in five sentences.**
- **Tell one person one true thing about Jesus.**
- **Notice whether your life is arguing for something you have not said.**
