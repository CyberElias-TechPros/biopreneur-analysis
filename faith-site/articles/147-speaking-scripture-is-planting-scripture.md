Romans 10 is about the mouth as much as the heart, and Paul builds the whole chapter on a verse from Deuteronomy.

"But what saith it? The word is nigh thee, even in thy mouth, and in thy heart: that is, the word of faith, which we preach" (Romans 10:8).

He is quoting Moses: "But the word is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it" (Deuteronomy 30:14).

Moses put the mouth first. Paul keeps the order.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the word of faith lives in two places at once — the mouth and the heart — and speaking it is not a performance added to believing but one of the ways believing is planted deep enough to hold.**

## Why the Mouth Comes First

It seems backwards. Surely the heart believes and then the mouth speaks.

But Moses and Paul both list the mouth before the heart, and the reason is not rhetorical flourish. It is the order of experience for most people: you say a thing long before you feel it settled, and the saying is part of how it settles.

**Confession of the Word** — *homologia*, saying the same thing God says — is the practice described. **The Word of God** — what He has spoken — is the seed being planted. **The Life of Faith** — a life lived on what was said — is the crop.

Consider a man learning a language. He does not wait until he feels fluent to speak; he speaks badly, repeatedly, and the fluency grows out of the speaking. Nobody accuses him of pretending.

### What Confession Is Not

This teaching has been badly handled, so the boundaries matter.

Confession is not a spell. The words are not the power; the God who spoke them is. A person repeating a verse with no trust and no obedience is making a noise.

Confession is not denying facts. Saying "I am healed" while refusing to see a doctor, or "I am rich" while the rent is unpaid, is not faith — it is a slogan, and it is the opposite of the honesty Scripture requires.

Confession is not a substitute for the inner life. Jesus was severe about words without substance: "This people draweth nigh unto me with their mouth, and honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me" (Matthew 15:8).

So the practice is real, and it is not a mechanism.

### The Two Directions

Here is the part that makes this practical rather than merely corrective.

The traffic runs both ways. What is in the heart comes out of the mouth — "out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh" (Matthew 12:34). And what is in the mouth goes back into the heart, because you cannot say a thing repeatedly without it shaping you.

Which means your speech is simultaneously a report and a cause. It tells you what you believe, and it forms what you will believe.

Set the two believers side by side. One rehearses the difficulty all week — not lying, just narrating it — and finds his heart sinking in proportion. Another says the same true facts to God and then says what God says about them, and finds his heart steadied in the saying. Both are being honest. One is planting weeds.

### Proverbs Says the Same Thing

"Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof" (Proverbs 18:21).

*A hand* is the literal sense of the Hebrew word for "power" here — the tongue has a hand. It reaches. It takes hold of things and brings them into a life.

And the eating is the harvest imagery again: you will eat the fruit of what you have been saying.

### Paul's Own Testimony

Paul gives a personal version in 2 Corinthians: "We having the same spirit of faith, according as it is written, I believed, and therefore have I spoken; we also believe, and therefore speak" (2 Corinthians 4:13).

Note the sequence — believed, therefore spoke. And note that he says it is the same spirit of faith, the same pattern, in every believer.

### How to Speak It Without Pretending

The practice is simple, and it has three rules.

**Speak what God actually said.** Not a wish, not a slogan, not a claim He never made. Take the verse and say it.

**Speak it to God as well as about your situation.** Much confession is really a report to yourself. Turning it into prayer changes the posture from assertion to trust.

**Say it until it settles, not until it impresses.** The measure is not volume or duration but whether it has begun to shape how you actually feel and act.

### The Honest Boundary

One thing to say plainly, because confession teaching has hurt people.

If you have spoken the Word faithfully and the situation has not changed, that is not evidence that you spoke wrongly or believed weakly. Paul asked three times for a thorn to go and was given grace instead (2 Corinthians 12:8–9). Jesus asked for a cup to pass and drank it.

Confession is a way of agreeing with God. It is not a lever that obliges Him to a timetable you have chosen.

And if your speech has become a source of shame — if you hear yourself saying things you do not believe and feel like a fraud — the answer is not louder saying. It is honesty with God about where you actually are, and then the slow work of letting the verse become true in you.

## What This Means for You

**1. Choose one verse to say.** Not ten. One, said daily, understood, and prayed.

**2. Say it to God, not at your problem.** It is a conversation, not an incantation.

**3. Listen to your own reports.** What do you say about your week, your body, your money, your future? That is what you are planting.

**4. Speak it before you feel it.** Feelings follow speech far more often than speech follows feelings.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom work together here.** Faith says speak what God said. Wisdom says speak it truthfully and kindly, in context, with your whole life behind it — because the words of a believer whose conduct contradicts them are heard by nobody, including himself.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Romans 10:6–13**, and mark every mention of mouth, heart, and confessing.
- **Write one verse** and say it aloud morning and evening.
- **Catch one negative report** you repeat often, and replace it deliberately.
- **Read Deuteronomy 30:11–14**, and note the last three words of the verse.
