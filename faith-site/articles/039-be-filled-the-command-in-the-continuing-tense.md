Have you ever wondered whether you need a new experience with the Holy Spirit, or whether the one you had years ago should still be sufficient? The question usually comes from a misunderstanding of what "being filled" actually means.

Ephesians 5:18 reads, "And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but **be filled with the Spirit**." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **being filled with the Spirit is a continuous command, not a one-time event.**

## A Command in the Present Tense

In the Greek text, the verb is present imperative — literally, "keep on being filled." Paul is not describing a past experience to remember; he is commanding an ongoing practice. The filling of the Spirit is not a trophy you display on a shelf; it is a supply you keep drawing from.

**The Power of the Holy Spirit** — the Spirit's supernatural enablement for godly living, witness, and service — is not a reserve you deplete. It is a relationship you maintain. **Communion With the Holy Spirit** — ongoing personal fellowship and sensitivity — is the channel, and **the Anointing** — the Spirit's empowering presence resting within you — is what keeps the channel fruitful.

Paul's contrast is instructive. Wine fills a person and produces excess: loss of control, dulled judgment, and surrender to impulse. The Spirit fills a person and produces the opposite: self-control, clarity, worship, gratitude, and mutual submission. Both are about being filled and controlled — the question is by what.

Think of a sailboat on a lake. The wind is steady, but the sailor adjusts the sails continuously to catch it. Does a sailor "get the wind" once and then drift for the rest of the voyage? No — he stays under it, trims for it, and moves with it. Paul says the same about the Spirit: keep catching the wind.

### Why One Experience Is Not Enough

The New Testament records believers being filled repeatedly. In Acts 2:4 they were filled at Pentecost. In Acts 4:31 they were filled again after prayer. In Acts 13:52 the disciples were "filled with joy and with the Holy Ghost." The filling is repeatable, and it is connected to specific conditions: prayer, surrender, obedience, and fellowship.

That should encourage you. If your last sense of fullness was months ago, you have not missed your only chance. You can come again today, and the same Spirit is still willing to fill.

Here the two diverge. The experience-chaser depends on emotional highs and fears their absence. The continual-receiver lives in steady supply, receiving afresh each day regardless of feeling.

### The Grammar of "Be Filled"

Paul's command in Ephesians 5:18 is a present imperative, and the tense matters: "be not drunk with wine... but be filled with the Spirit." The contrast is between two kinds of influence, and the command is continuous — be being filled, keep on being filled.

The comparison to wine is not incidental. A man filled with wine is controlled by something outside himself that alters his speech, his walk, and his courage. Paul is saying there is a legitimate version of that: a life so under the Spirit's influence that its ordinary conduct is visibly different.

This is why the filling is repeatable. Acts records it more than once — "they were all filled with the Holy Ghost" at Pentecost (Acts 2:4), again after prayer in Acts 4:31, and the disciples are described as "filled with joy and with the Holy Ghost" in Acts 13:52. The Spirit is not re-given; the fullness is re-received.

**The Anointing** — the Spirit's empowering presence resting on a believer for service — is not a reservoir you drain. It is a relationship you keep returning to.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **Colossians 3:16** — "Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly... teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs." Paul's parallel passage to Ephesians 5:18 substitutes the Word for the Spirit's filling — showing that Scripture is a primary means of being filled.
- **Acts 4:31** — "And when they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were assembled together; and they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and they spake the word of God with boldness." Filling followed corporate prayer and produced bold witness. It is repeatable and purposeful.
- **Acts 13:52** — "And the disciples were filled with joy, and with the Holy Ghost." The filling is not reserved for crisis; it accompanies ordinary discipleship and produces joy.

Three witnesses, one practice: the Word indwelling, prayer answered, joy overflowing.

## What This Means for You

**1. Come daily, not just at crisis points.** Make being filled a morning practice: acknowledge the Spirit, yield your day, ask for His fullness, and receive by faith.

**2. Keep short accounts.** Unconfessed sin, unforgiveness, and clutter block the flow. Confession and forgiveness keep the vessel clean and available.

**3. Yield the controls deliberately.** Filling implies control. Name the areas where you have been steering alone — money, relationships, calendar, speech — and hand them over again.

**4. Faith and wisdom stay filled and stay steady.** Faith receives the filling; wisdom builds the rhythms and boundaries that keep you available. Declaring fullness never excuses fanaticism; it produces the self-control Paul contrasts with wine.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Ephesians 5:15–21 slowly.** Notice the results of filling: singing, thanksgiving, and mutual submission. Let the passage define what fullness looks like.
- **Pray a daily filling prayer.** Each morning this week, ask the Spirit to fill you afresh and yield one specific area of your life to Him.
- **Sing something to God once a day.** Filling and singing are linked in Scripture; let worship be a practical way of staying full.
- **Identify one control you have not yielded.** Name it, hand it over in prayer, and take one concrete action that reflects His leadership.

**The central truth, said plainly:** Being filled is a continuing command. Keep coming, keep receiving, keep yielding — stay full.
