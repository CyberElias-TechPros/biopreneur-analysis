A short letter from an old man contains the most quoted prosperity prayer in the New Testament, and it is usually quoted with the second half amputated.

"Beloved, I wish above all things that thou mayest prosper and be in health, even as thy soul prospereth" (3 John 2).

Most people stop at "prosper and be in health." The verse's actual standard is the clause at the end.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the soul is the thermostat of an entire life, which is why John prays for outer prosperity to catch up with an inner measure rather than the other way round.**

## Reading the Clause

### "Even As"

The Greek is *kathōs* — according as, in proportion to, exactly as.

So the prayer is not "prosper and be healthy, and by the way I hope your soul is fine." It is a **proportion**: may the outside match the inside.

The soul is the measurement, not an afterthought.

### What "Prosper" Means Here

The word for the soul prospering is *euodoō* — to have a good journey, to go well, to succeed. It is the same verb used of a ship making good progress.

It does not mean the soul has money. It means the soul is **moving well** — advancing, not stuck.

### The Two Other Uses

The word appears elsewhere in the New Testament in ordinary contexts: "Let all things be done unto edifying" and, more tellingly, of a journey. It is travel language.

A soul that prospers is a soul that is going somewhere.

### Why "Above All Things"

John's phrasing is strong: *above all things I wish.* It marks the priority of the request, not a ranking of values.

The old man's deepest wish for his friend was not a bigger house. It was a whole inside.

### Why the Inside Comes First

#### The Soul Decides the Taste of Everything

Proverbs says it plainly: "The spirit of a man will sustain his infirmity; but a wounded spirit who can bear?" (Proverbs 18:14).

Reverse the logic and it still holds: a prospering soul can carry a great deal, and a wounded one struggles even in good circumstances.

Two people with identical lives can have entirely different experiences of them.

#### The Psalmist Argues With His Soul

Psalm 42 records the internal dialogue that proves the point: "Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted in me? hope thou in God" (Psalm 42:11).

Notice that the psalmist addresses his own soul as a separate party. He does not argue with his circumstances; he argues with the voice inside him.

The soul is the place where the argument about a life actually happens.

#### What Fills the Soul

Romans gives the mechanism: "For they that are after the flesh do mind the things of the flesh; but they that are after the Spirit the things of the Spirit" (Romans 8:5).

*Mind* there is a verb — *phroneō*, to set one's attention on, to be interested in.

**The soul is prospered by what it is fed.** There is no shortcut and no substitute.

#### The Warning Jesus Added

And Jesus makes it stark: "For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?" (Mark 8:36).

That is the exact inversion of John's prayer — outer prosperity at the price of inner ruin.

It is possible to have a prospering account and a starving soul, and Jesus called it a loss.

### How a Soul Prospers

#### By the Word

The direct route, and there is no substitute: "Wherefore lay apart all filthiness and superfluity of naughtiness, and receive with meekness the engrafted word, which is able to save your souls" (James 1:21).

*Engrafted* — planted into you. **The Word is soul food, and it saves by being rooted.**

#### By Guarding the Inputs

Proverbs makes it a discipline: "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life" (Proverbs 4:23).

The word for guard is *mišmār* — a watch post. **Somebody has to be at the gate.**

#### By Practice, Not Only Intake

A soul can be stuffed with information and still be malnourished. It prospers by doing: forgiveness, generosity, thanksgiving, obedience in small things.

**The soul grows food only in the field of behaviour.**

#### The Boundary

One guardrail, because this phrase is sometimes spiritualised into total neglect of the body and the wallet.

The verse says *prosper and be in health* as well. John is not choosing the soul over the body; he is setting the order. **Caring for your finances and your health is obedience, not carnality — but neither can compensate for an empty inside.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Take an honest reading.** Is your soul moving well this month, or just busy?

**2. Feed it before you check the other numbers.** The first ten minutes of the day are the thermostat.

**3. Guard one gate this week.** A feed, a conversation, a programme you know is poisoning you.

**4. Practise something that grows the soul.** Forgive someone. Give something away. Thank God out loud.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Pray for outer prosperity and steward the soul that will have to live with it. **Kingdom Stewardship** covers both accounts.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 3 John 1–4** daily this week.
- **Read Psalm 42 in full** and mark the dialogue with the soul.
- **Read James 1:19–25** and note the "engrafted word."
- **Name the thermostat** — what has been setting your temperature?
- **Take one gate off your diet** for seven days.
