Proverbs 28:1 is a single verse with two observations, and the second is not a command.

"The wicked flee when no man pursueth: but the righteous are bold as a lion."

Nobody is told to be bold here. The verse simply reports what is observed — and that changes everything about how you get there.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Proverbs does not command boldness; it reports it as a consequence of righteousness — which means courage is not a personality to be worked up but a fruit of knowing where you stand.**

## Two Observations

### The Fleeing

The first half is a portrait of a person running from nothing. There is no pursuer. The fear is internal and self-generated.

That is what guilt does. It converts an empty road into a chase.

**Guilt is a pursuer that exists entirely in the mind of the pursued.**

### The Boldness

And the second half is not tension or aggression. *Bold* here is *bāṭaḥ* — to trust, to be confident, to be secure.

The lion is not angry. The lion is unbothered.

**Boldness in Scripture is closer to settled trust than to steely nerves.**

### The Contrast

And the two are presented as a single phenomenon with two expressions. The difference is not circumstance; both halves of the verse describe people in the same world.

**Same road, no pursuer, two entirely different experiences of it.** And the difference between them is not what is behind them. It is what they believe is behind them.

### The Lion's Posture

Watch what a lion does when it is unthreatened. It does not pace. It does not scan. It rests in the open, in plain sight, at the exact centre of its own territory.

That is the picture. Not aggression — occupancy.

**Boldness is what rest looks like in a dangerous world.** So why do we keep mistaking it for loudness?

## Why Righteousness Produces Courage

### Fear Needs an Accusation

And at the root of most fear is a verdict. Fear of exposure, fear of judgment, fear of being found out.

Remove the verdict and the fear loses its foundation.

**You cannot be blackmailed by something that has already been forgiven.**

### Standing Removes the Exposure

And righteousness is precisely the removal of exposure: "There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1).

The verdict came in, and it came in your favour.

**A man with no case against him has nothing to be afraid of in court.**

### Love Completes It

And 1 John finishes the argument: "Herein is our love made perfect... that we may have boldness in the day of judgment... There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear" (1 John 4:17–18).

The mechanism is *perfect love* — a love that has been completed, not a feeling that has been intensified.

**Fear is cast out by something finished, not by something felt.**

### The Boldness of the Early Church

### They Prayed for It

And when the apostles were threatened, they did not pray for protection. "And now, Lord, behold their threatenings: and grant unto thy servants, that with all boldness they may speak thy word" (Acts 4:29).

They asked for more courage, not less danger. And the building shook.

The prayer of a secure person is different from the prayer of a frightened one.

### It Was Recognised

And later, the council "took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus" (Acts 4:13) — the mark was *parrēsia*, plain unpolished courage.

They had no credentials. They had a standing.

Boldness is one of the few things that cannot be faked for long.

### It Was Not Natural

And remember who these men were. Peter had denied Christ to a servant girl weeks earlier.

The boldness was not temperament. It was the resurrection.

The same man can be a coward at one fire and a witness at another.

### Where False Boldness Comes From

### From Temperament

And some boldness is just personality, and it can be very convincing and very empty.

A loud man is not necessarily a courageous one.

Volume is not the same as conviction. A man can be fearless in an argument and terrified of a hospital corridor, and the same personality will produce both.

### From Position

And some boldness is borrowed from a role rather than a relationship. Authority in an office can feel like courage until the office is taken away.

Standing in Christ survives the loss of every other platform.

### From Ignorance

And some boldness is simply not knowing enough to be afraid — which lasts until the first real opposition.

### From Compensating

And some is a cover for fear. The most aggressive person in the room is sometimes the most frightened.

Real boldness does not need to be defended.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, boldness is not rudeness. Paul asks for boldness *as he ought to speak* (Ephesians 6:20) — courage with a governed tongue.

Second, it is not the absence of fear. Courage is not the lack of trembling; it is the decision about what to do while trembling (Psalm 56:3).

**The **Righteousness you have is the ground; the Legal Position is the security; and the The Name of Jesus is the authority that makes the boldness effective rather than merely brave.

## What This Means for You

1. Identify your fear and its accusation. What verdict are you afraid of?

2. Answer the verdict with Romans 8:1.

3. Pray Acts 4:29 for yourself this week.

**4. Do one unremarkable brave thing** — say the true thing, have the conversation.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Boldness without wisdom is recklessness. **Legal Position** gives you the standing; wisdom tells you when and how to speak.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Memorise Proverbs 28:1.**
- **Read Acts 4:23–31 three times.**
- **Write down your three biggest fears.**
- **Name the verdict under each.**
- **Ask God for boldness, not for safety.**
