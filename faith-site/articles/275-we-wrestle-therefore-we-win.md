Ephesians 6 is the church's best-known warfare passage, and its most important sentence is not about armour at all.

"For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places" (Ephesians 6:12).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **naming the enemy correctly is half the victory — because a believer who fights people will wound the very ones he was sent to save, and a believer who fights principalities from a finished triumph will not be afraid of either.**

## The Word Wrestle

### Hand to Hand

The Greek is *palē*, and it is not a battle word. It is the word for grappling — the close-quarters, exhausting, sweaty business of a wrestling match.

Paul does not describe a duel at a distance. He describes contact.

**This is not a war you can watch from a hill. It is a contest with your arms around someone.**

### Not Blood

And then he removes the obvious opponent. *Flesh and blood* is a Hebrew idiom for humanity — the ordinary, mortal, embodied stuff of people.

The enemy is never the person in the room.

**Every human being in your conflict is either a casualty or a captive, never the commander.**

### The Four Ranks

And the four terms are not likely a portrait of four distinct enemy departments. They are a cumulative description: principalities, authorities, world-rulers of this darkness, spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly places.

The force is organised, ancient, and above the earthly plane.

**Whatever the taxonomy, the point is that the opposition is bigger than biology.**

## The Errors in Both Directions

### Fighting People

And the first error is the everyday one. A believer who has not seen the unseen will always aim his weapons at the nearest visible target.

The colleague, the spouse, the critic, the brother who voted differently.

**Nobody in history ever fought a human being and won the right thing.**

### Ignoring the Enemy

And the second error is overcorrection. Some respond to a hyper-spiritual culture by refusing to discuss the enemy at all — and then the ambush finds them unarmoured.

C. S. Lewis's line is quoted too often because it is too true: the danger is two errors, disbelief and exaggerated interest.

**Deuteronomy 29:29 is the fence: enough has been revealed to arm you, not enough to satisfy your curiosity.**

### Blaming the Enemy for Everything

And the third error is the opposite of the first: blaming a demon for what belongs to the flesh. James answers that one bluntly — wars and fights come from within, from the desires that war in your members (James 4:1).

**The enemy will take responsibility for everything, if you let him. It flatters him and blinds you.**

### Wrestling From Victory

### The Triumph Already Held

And Paul's other warfare passage is not an instruction but a victory announcement: "And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it" (Colossians 2:15).

*Spoiled* is *apekdyomai* — to strip off, to disarm. The Roman triumph parade: the conquered leaders led in chains behind the general.

**The ancient powers are already in the parade. You are not fighting for a victory; you are enforcing one.**

### So What Is the Wrestling For

And this is the essential question. If the war is won, why grapple?

Because the space between the victory and its manifestation is where you live, and in that space there is opposition to faith.

The wrestling is not to change the outcome. It is to hold your ground in it.

### Jacob's Lesson

And Jacob wrestled all night and walked away with a limp and a new name (Genesis 32:24–28). He did not win by pinning the man; he won by refusing to let go.

The point of wrestling is not conquest. It is clinging.

### Practising the Discernment

### Name the Human

And the practical discipline is to name the person in the conflict, then deliberately reclassify the conflict. The person is not the problem.

That single reframe changes what weapons you use.

If you can pray for them, you have found the right enemy.

### Name What Is Actually Operative

And then name what is actually at work. Is this deception? Accusation? Fear? Division? Discernment is not naming a demon; it is identifying the tactic.

Spiritual understanding is more often tactic-recognition than rank-identification.

### Use the Right Weapon

And the weapons of this warfare are not carnal (2 Corinthians 10:4). Truth, prayer, the Word, the name of Jesus, submission to God.

Which means anger is not a weapon. Neither is sarcasm, or leverage.

If your weapon makes you more like your enemy, it is his weapon.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a doctrine of perpetual war. Christ's victory is complete, and the Christian life is not one long battlefield; it has ordinary days, ordinary work, and ordinary joy.

Second, this is never a licence to fear evil. "Neither give place to the devil" (Ephesians 4:27) is about yielding ground — not about cowering.

**The **The Authority of the Believer** is the standing; **Spiritual Warfare is grappling from it; and Spiritual Understanding is knowing whose blood the opponent is made of.

## What This Means for You

1. Take your hardest relationship. Write down who the enemy is not.

2. Name the tactic rather than the demon.

3. Pray for the person — specifically, for their good.

**4. Put down one carnal weapon** this week — the coldness, the remark.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Warfare is not an excuse to abandon ordinary responsibility. **Spiritual Warfare** is the conflict; wisdom is what you do on an ordinary Tuesday while the battle continues.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Ephesians 6:10–20 every morning.**
- **Read Colossians 2:13–15 twice.**
- **Write out verse 12 in your own words.**
- **List the flesh-and-blood you have been fighting.**
- **Pray a blessing over one of them.**
