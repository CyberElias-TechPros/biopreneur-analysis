Most people have the phrase *boldness in the day of judgment* filed under "future" — something to be sorted out at the end.

John files it under "now," and he says why in the same sentence.

"Herein is our love made perfect, that we may have boldness in the day of judgment: because as he is, so are we in this world" (1 John 4:17).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **boldness on the last day is not manufactured on the last day — it is the same confidence you are practising or neglecting right now, because the basis for it does not change when the calendar does.**

## The Reason John Gives

### Not Your Performance, Your Resemblance

The ground of confidence here is not a review of your record. It is a statement of fact about position: *as he is, so are we in this world.*

Present tense. In this world. Not "as He was" and not "as we will be."

**The basis for boldness on the last day is a resemblance that is already true today.**

### Where He Is

And where is He? Seated, at the right hand, with every authority subjected (Ephesians 1:20–22).

If that is the measure of *as he is*, then the comparison John is drawing is not modest.

**You are not being asked to feel bold about yourself. You are being asked to notice where you are seated.**

### What "Perfect Love" Is Doing

The phrase *love made perfect* — *teleioō* — is about completion, reaching an intended end.

The love John describes has finished its work when it has produced confidence rather than cringing.

**Love that has not yet cast out fear has not yet finished what it came to do in you.**

### The Fear It Removes

### Torment, Not Reverence

The next verse is the one people quote: "There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment" (1 John 4:18).

The word *torment* is *kolasis* — punishment, correction. John is not against the fear of the Lord; he is against the fear that expects a beating.

**A child who is afraid of being hit cannot enjoy the father's house, and John is describing a Father who has not raised a hand.**

### Why Fear and Love Cannot Share a Room

"He that feareth is not made perfect in love" (1 John 4:18).

That is not a threat. It is a description of two incompatible postures occupying the same space. Fear keeps a person at the door, calculating; love brings them to the table.

Fear makes you accurate about the rules and blind to the relationship.

### The Fear That Remains

And there is still a fear worth having — the reverent awe that Scripture repeatedly commends (Proverbs 9:10).

The difference is the direction of the expectation. Terror expects harm. Awe expects glory, and is safe in its presence.

**The fear of the Lord runs toward Him. Terror runs from Him.**

### Why This Is Practised Now

### Confidence Is a Habit, Not a Decision

Hebrews makes the practice explicit: "Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need" (Hebrews 4:16).

*Come boldly.* The instruction is present tense and repeated — it is a habit being formed, not a verdict being awaited.

Boldness is a muscle. It is built by the visits you actually make.

### The Throne Is Already Grace

Note what the throne is called. Not the throne of evaluation. The throne of *grace*, and the reason given is that we have a great high priest who has been through everything we face.

**You are not approaching a judge who is deciding whether to be kind. You are approaching a throne whose entire character is the kindness.**

### The Blood Gives the Access

And Hebrews 10 grounds the confidence in a fact, not a feeling: "Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus" (Hebrews 10:19).

The boldness is *by the blood*. It is not a personality trait; it is a right of access purchased at a price.

**Confidence is not arrogance when it is anchored in someone else's sacrifice.**

### Practising in the Small Room

Here is the practical centre of it: the way you pray when nothing is at stake reveals what you expect of God.

A person who prays hesitantly, apologising for the interruption, is already rehearsing a judgment-day posture.

Your prayer life is your theology of the judgment, spoken out loud.

### The Boundary

Three guardrails, because this subject is easily distorted.

First, boldness is not presumption. Hebrews 10:19 comes after ten chapters about a better priesthood — the access is real *because* the work was real. Ignoring the work and strolling in is not faith.

Second, the judgment seat of Christ is real. Paul says we must all appear before it (2 Corinthians 5:10), and he says it while also saying that there is no condemnation (Romans 8:1). Both are true: the believer's works are assessed; the believer's standing is not.

Third, this is not an exemption from repentance. A bold child still says sorry. The difference is that he says it to a Father rather than a prosecutor.

Hope does not make a believer casual. It makes a believer confident — and confident people are honest.

## What This Means for You

**1. Notice how you enter prayer.** Hesitant or at home? That is the diagnostic.

**2. Practise boldness today** in a small thing — ask for something specific.

**3. Read 1 John 4:17–18 out loud** until the second half stops contradicting the first.

**4. Distinguish awe from terror.** Awe is safe. Terror never was.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Boldness without obedience becomes carelessness; **Righteousness** gives you standing, and the **The Presence of God** is where the standing is enjoyed. Come boldly, and come honestly.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 1 John 4:13–19** every day this week.
- **Write down what you actually expect** when you pray.
- **Read Hebrews 4:14–16** and underline the word *boldly*.
- **Ask God for something specific** and note the date.
- **Read 2 Corinthians 5:9–11** alongside Romans 8:1; hold both.
