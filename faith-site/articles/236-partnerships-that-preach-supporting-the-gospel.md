The church at Philippi sent Paul money at least four times, and he never once thanked them for a donation.

He thanked them for a **fellowship**.

"For your fellowship in the gospel from the first day until now" (Philippians 1:5). The word is *koinōnia* — the same word used for the communion of the Spirit and the sharing of the saints. It is a word about a shared thing, not a transferred thing.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Paul never called a gift to his mission a donation; he called it a fellowship — because the one who sends shares the reward of the one who is sent.**

## The Word Paul Chose

### Not a Sponsor, a Partner

A donor stands beside a work. A partner stands *inside* it.

That distinction is the whole of Paul's theology of support. When he writes to the Philippians later, he is explicit about the accounting: "Now ye Philippians know also, that in the beginning of the gospel, when I departed from Macedonia, no church communicated with me as concerning giving and receiving, but ye only" (Philippians 4:15).

The phrase *communicated* is again the *koinōnia* family — and he frames it as a ledger with two columns, *giving and receiving*, as if the transaction had parties rather than a recipient.

**In Paul's arithmetic, a gift to the gospel is a joint account, not a receipt.**

### The Receipt He Asked For Instead

Here is the sentence that undoes most of our thinking about ministry support: "Not because I desire a gift: but I desire fruit that may abound to your account" (Philippians 4:17).

He says plainly he does not want the gift. Then he says what he does want — *fruit that abounds to your account.* The Greek for *account* is *logos*, a reckoning, a statement of account.

So the man receiving the money is asking that the benefit be booked to the givers.

**Paul treated their giving as an investment whose returns would be credited to them, and he cared more about their returns than about his own supply.**

### Why This Is Not Flattery

It would be easy to read that as a polite thank-you note. But Paul had already proved it: he worked as a tentmaker rather than be a burden (1 Thessalonians 2:9), and he refused support from Corinth while planting there (1 Corinthians 9:12) precisely so the gospel would not look like a business.

A man who will not take money to protect the message is not a man flattering the people who send it.

**The refusal and the rejoicing come from the same conviction: the mission is not for sale, and its fruit is shareable.**

### Why Sending Is Real Participation

#### The Chain in Romans

Paul states the mechanism in Romans: "How then shall they call on him in whom they have not believed? and how shall they believe in him of whom they have not heard? and how shall they hear without a preacher? And how shall they preach, except they be sent?" (Romans 10:14–15).

Read the chain. Believing depends on hearing; hearing depends on preaching; preaching depends on **sending**.

Faith comes by hearing — and hearing comes by someone paying for a journey.

**The sender is not adjacent to the chain. The sender is a link in it.**

#### The Same Reward

Jesus had already made the accounting rule explicit when He sent the seventy: "And in the same house remain, eating and drinking such things as they give: for the labourer is worthy of his hire" (Luke 10:7). And earlier, "he that receiveth a prophet in the name of a prophet shall receive a prophet's reward" (Matthew 10:41).

That is a startling sentence. Not a *donor's* reward. A **prophet's** reward.

**Heaven does not pay the sender a thank-you; it pays him the harvest.**

#### The Elder Who Wrote It Down

The mechanism was still being taught a generation later: "Beloved, thou doest faithfully whatsoever thou doest to the brethren, and to strangers; which have borne witness of thy charity before the church: whom if thou bring forward on their journey after a godly sort, thou shalt do well... We therefore ought to receive such, that we might be fellowhelpers to the truth" (3 John 5–6, 8).

*Fellowhelpers* — *synergoi*, co-workers.

**Support is not assistance to the work. It is a share in it.**

### The Way It Gets Distorted

#### When Giving Becomes Distance

The corruption of partnership is patronage: writing a cheque so that you do not have to know the person, the field, or the cost. That is not *koinōnia*; it is a subscription.

Paul's language in Philippians 4:10 is telling — "your care of me hath flourished again; wherein ye were also careful, but ye lacked opportunity." He had noticed their absence, and he called it a lack of *opportunity*, not a lack of love. Partnership is a relationship with logistics, not a transaction with sentiment.

#### When Giving Becomes Purchase

The other corruption is buying influence. Peter confronted it directly: "Thy money perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be purchased with money" (Acts 8:20).

**A gift that expects control has stopped being fellowship and started being a contract.**

#### When Giving Becomes a Substitute

And there is a quiet third failure, closer to home: sending money instead of going, or instead of praying, as if the cheque discharged the whole obligation. Paul asks for prayer as urgently as he asks for funds — "that utterance may be given unto me, that I may open my mouth boldly" (Ephesians 6:19).

**The **The Ministry of Reconciliation** is carried on two legs: the one who goes and the one who holds him up.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Name your partners.** Which workers, churches, or missions are you actually inside of? If the answer is none, that is the first decision.

**2. Give where you know the person.** Partnership without knowledge decays into patronage.

**3. Read the report.** Ask what happened. Paul wrote whole letters back to his senders; expect the same.

**4. Pray the other half.** Intercession is not a lesser contribution to the same work.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Give regularly rather than impulsively, decide the amount in advance, and **Kingdom Stewardship** keeps the partnership sustainable. **The Life of Faith** gives because it has a share in the outcome, not because it was moved by a story.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Philippians 1:3–11** and mark every mention of partnership.
- **Read Philippians 4:10–20** and note what Paul says he wants.
- **Choose one worker or mission** and commit to a monthly amount.
- **Write to them** and ask for a report.
- **Pray Ephesians 6:19** for them by name each day this week.
