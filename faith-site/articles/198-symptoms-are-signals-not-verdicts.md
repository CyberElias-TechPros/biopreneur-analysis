There are two ways to be wrong about pain, and most believers have lived in one of them.

The first is to ignore the body entirely, treating every symptom as an enemy report to be silenced. The second is to treat every symptom as a verdict already delivered, the first sentence of a diagnosis you have not been given.

**Genesis 1:31** matters here more than it is usually given credit for: "And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good."

The body you are reading was designed. Its signals were designed. And a designed signal is worth reading properly.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **a symptom is information about your body, not a ruling about your future, and the believer's task is to read the signal with wisdom while refusing to let it write the sentence.**

## What a Signal Is For

### The Body Keeps Records

Pain is not a moral failure. It is a nervous system doing its job. A hand that pulls back from a hot stove is obeying a signal, and that signal saves lives.

Consider what happens when the signal is missing. A body that cannot register pain — through nerve damage, for instance — is in more danger, not less. The warning system is a gift, and the one who gave it is the same One who said the whole thing was very good.

**Your body is not lying to you when it hurts; it is reporting.**

The lie is what we add on top of the report. Chest pain becomes "I am having a heart attack and dying tonight." Back pain becomes "I will never walk normally again." A symptom becomes a sentence about the rest of your life, and it never had the authority to write one.

### Report, Not Ruling

Notice the difference in the two sentences:

*"My knee is swollen and hurts when I walk."* That is a report from a body.

*"I am a person with a bad knee, and it will always be this way."* That is a verdict from fear.

Both may be spoken by the same woman in the same hour. Only one of them is her body talking. **The other is her own imagination, promoted to the status of a prophet.** Which one have you been saying out loud this month?

### When Jesus Met a Symptom

Look at the paralysed man let down through the roof, because the story says something we should not rush past.

Jesus told him, "thy sins be forgiven thee" (Matthew 9:2). The scribes objected, and Jesus answered: "whether is it easier, to say, Thy sins be forgiven thee; or to say, Arise, and walk?" (Matthew 9:5).

He then healed him and said, "that ye may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins" (Matthew 9:6).

Here is what to notice: Jesus did not dispute that the man could not walk. He saw the paralysis plainly. The visible fact was not a lie; it simply was not the last word on what the man would carry. The same Jesus went on to say, "They that be whole need not a physician, but they that are sick" (Matthew 9:12).

**He never once minimised a symptom, and He never once let a symptom define the man.**

### How to Read It Without Fear

#### Wisdom Sees, Faith Says

Proverbs puts the two together in a single verse: "A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself: but the simple pass on, and are punished" (Proverbs 22:3).

Prudent sees and acts. That is wisdom. The believer is not told to close his eyes; he is told to be faithful with what he sees.

Simply: a lump should be examined, a fever should be monitored, a persistent cough should be investigated. The Christian who treats a change in her body as a matter of unbelief is not exercising faith; she is disobeying Proverbs.

#### What to Do With the Thoughts That Follow

A symptom rarely comes alone. It comes with a chorus of interpretations, and here the apostle gives us the tool: "Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:5).

The word for *thought* is *noēma* — a mind-set, a way of seeing. **Spiritual Warfare** is not against flesh, and here the battlefield is what you are guessing about your body.

Catch the thought that has run past the evidence: that the pain proves abandonment, that the body is broken forever, that the healing was a lie. Take it captive. It did not come from the report; it came from the fear.

#### What God Says About the Body

While you are treating the signal faithfully, do not let it speak the language of rejection.

"The spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you" (Romans 8:11). "Ye are the temple of the living God" (2 Corinthians 6:16). "The body is for the Lord" (1 Corinthians 6:13).

**Divine Wholeness** is God's direction of travel for you, and a flare-up is not a reversal of direction. It is a signpost on the road.

#### The Two Errors, Named

**The first error:** treat the symptom as the whole truth. That yields despair and paralysis, and it disobeys everything the covenant says.

**The second error:** treat the symptom as irrelevant. That yields neglect, and it disobeys Proverbs.

The path between them is narrow and it is where most healing teaching has failed. **Read the signal honestly. Bring the body to a doctor. Bring the body to God. Keep the report short and the promise long.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Write the report in plain words.** "My back hurts when I bend." Not "everything is falling apart." Strip the verdict out of your description and see how much lighter it is.

**2. Get the thing checked.** People die of things that were treatable because they were afraid to know.

**3. Refuse to let a symptom speak about your future.** Your body has no access to that information.

**4. Speak faith and act wisely in the same hour.** Faith and wisdom work together: pray over the body and take the medicine; seek prayer and keep the appointment; anoint and rest.

**5. Watch your mouth when the flare comes.** What you say in the first hour of a symptom often shapes the next three weeks.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Keep a one-week log** of what your body reports, in plain factual words.
- **Read Proverbs 22:3** and note what prudence does with what it sees.
- **Identify one verdict** you have spoken about your body, and rewrite it as a report.
- **Read 2 Corinthians 10:3–6** and mark "imaginations."
- **Book the appointment you have been avoiding.**
