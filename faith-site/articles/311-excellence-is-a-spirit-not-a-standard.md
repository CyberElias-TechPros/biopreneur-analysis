Daniel 6:3 contains a phrase that deserves to be read slowly, because the wording is strange to modern ears.

"Then this Daniel was preferred above the presidents and princes, because an excellent spirit was in him; and the king thought to set him over the whole realm."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Scripture does not call Daniel's excellence a skill or a standard; it calls it a spirit — which means excellence is a quality of the person that shows up in the work, not a benchmark the work is held to.**

## The Phrase

### Ruach Yattirah

And the Aramaic is *rûaḥ yattîrâ* — an excellent spirit, or a surpassing spirit. The word *rûaḥ* is breath, wind, disposition.

It is the word for what a person is like inside, not the word for what a person can do.

**The evaluation was about his spirit, and the work followed.** So which one are you still trying to fix first?

### Repeated Twice

And it is not a one-off compliment. Daniel 5:12 says the same of him before the queen: "an excellent spirit, and knowledge, and understanding, interpreting of dreams."

And notice the order — spirit first, then knowledge and understanding. The skills are listed as consequences.

**Scripture lists the cause before the competencies.**

### Noticed by Pagans

And the man who noticed was Darius, a Mede who did not worship the God of Israel. He could not have explained the source, but he could recognise the quality.

Which means excellence is legible to people who do not share your faith.

**A spirit can be seen even when it cannot be named.** And it was seen by a man who had no vocabulary for it.

## Why It Is Not a Standard

### Standards Are External

And a standard is a measure applied from outside — a rubric, a benchmark, a grade. It can be met and it can be gamed.

A spirit cannot be gamed, because it does not depend on the observer.

**You can pass an inspection with a bad spirit. You cannot pass the King with one.**

### Standards Are Situational

And a standard changes with industry and era. What counted as excellent in one decade is ordinary in the next.

A spirit is portable across all of them.

**The standard moves. The spirit is the same in every economy.** And which standard were you measured by last year?

### Standards End

And a standard ends when the task ends. The spirit continues into the next task, the next room, and the next decade of life.

That is why Daniel is still trusted at eighty in a new empire.

**A man with an excellent spirit does not need to re-earn his reputation after a change of government.**

### What the Spirit Looks Like

### Work Without Supervisi

And the first mark is what happens when nobody is watching. Daniel's adversaries went looking for a fault and found none — except in his religion (Daniel 6:4–5).

They had to make something up.

**An excellent spirit is what you have when your work would survive an audit by your enemies.**

### Refusing the Cheap Gain

And the second is restraint. Daniel would not take the king's meat at the beginning of his career (Daniel 1:8), when nobody had asked him for anything.

The spirit is present at the beginning, or it is not excellence.

The person you will be at the top is decided by what you refuse at the bottom.

### Doing the Next Faithful Thing

And the third is unspectacular. Daniel did not campaign for promotion; he did the work he was given, in the language of the kingdom he was in, with an integrity that had nothing to hide.

Excellence is mostly the accumulation of ordinary days done properly.

### Growing It

### Ask for the Spirit

And the first move is not a career plan. It is a request. Daniel's spirit is described as being *in him* — a gift, not an achievement.

Ask for it, and expect the answer to arrive as a change of disposition rather than a change of circumstances.

The prayer for excellence is answered in your character before it is answered in your career.

### Work as Unto Him

And the second is Paul's instruction: "And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men" (Colossians 3:23).

The audience is the thing. You cannot be excellent for an audience you cannot see with an eye on one you can.

Excellence for God and excellence for men look identical and are entirely different.

### Refuse the Small Compromise

And the third is at the level of the pen. Daniel's integrity was found in his administration, not in his prayers.

The place to build an excellent spirit is the paperwork, the invoice, the small exaggeration, the shortcut.

Character is built in the tiny decisions, and it is exposed in the large ones.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not perfectionism. Perfectionism is anxiety dressed as diligence, and it produces paralysis and cruelty. Excellence is faithful and finished, not flawless.

Second, this is not a prosperity formula. Daniel's excellence eventually took him to the lion's den. Quality does not exempt you from trouble.

**The **Kingdom Stewardship** is the assignment; **The Anointing** is the enablement; and **The Power of the Holy Spirit is what makes a person capable of work that outlives him.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Daniel 6:1–10 and mark every mention of his work.

2. Audit one thing you did last week as if your enemies were reading it.

3. Ask God for an excellent spirit in those words.

**4. Refuse one small compromise** this week.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** A good spirit is not a substitute for competence. **Kingdom Stewardship** is the calling; wisdom is the training, the study, and the years of practice that a steward owes his Lord.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Daniel 5:10–16 and 6:1–10.**
- **Read Colossians 3:22–25.**
- **Pick the smallest task you do** and do it immaculately.
- **Note where you cut corners.**
- **Write one sentence about the spirit you want to be known for.**
