There are names that decide how a person sleeps at night — a diagnosis, a debt figure, a verdict from someone with power over you — and Philippians 2 puts all of them in a category.

"Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the name given to Jesus is not one item in a ranked list of names — it is the name above every name, which means whatever has been naming your future, from a diagnosis to a rejection, is operating beneath a jurisdiction it cannot overturn.**

## What the Verse Actually Grants

### Hyperypsōsen

And the word is *hyperypsōsen* — to exalt beyond measure, to super-exalt. Paul stacks the preposition on purpose; there is no higher shelf for a name to be placed on.

This is not a promotion within the ranks. It is a position outside the ranking.

### A Name, Not Just a Title

And the Greek *onoma* carries more than a label. In the Hebrew mind a name is the character and the authority of the one who bears it.

So the gift of the name is the gift of standing: who He is, and what He can be appealed to for.

**The name is not a magic word. It is a jurisdiction.** So which name has been functioning as yours?

### Every Name Means Every Name

And Paul immediately specifies: every knee, of things in heaven, in earth, and under the earth (Philippians 2:10). The scope is exhaustive because it is meant to be exhaustive.

There is no category of thing you can name that sits outside the reach of that sentence.

## The Names That Govern You

### The Diagnosis

And a medical report is a genuine fact and a false sovereign. It can describe your body accurately and still have no authority over your eternity or your assignment.

Hezekiah was told to set his house in order, and he turned his face to the wall and prayed (Isaiah 38:1–5). The report was true; it was not final.

### The Balance

And the number in the account is a fact too. It tells you what you cannot currently do, and it does not tell you what God cannot do.

Notice how quickly a balance becomes an identity — as though a spreadsheet could name a person.

### The Rejection

And the harshest names are the ones other people give you. Failure. Difficult. Too much. Not enough.

Those names were spoken by someone with a smaller jurisdiction than the One who calls you by a new name (Revelation 2:17).

**Only the One who made you has the standing to name you.**

### The Past

And the loudest name of all is usually the one written by your own history. But Paul says if any man be in Christ, he is a new creation; old things are passed away (2 Corinthians 4:17).

A name from a country you no longer live in.

### How the Name Works

### It Is Spoken, Not Chanted

And in Acts 3 Peter says, "In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk" (Acts 3:6). It was a sentence, not a spell.

The power is in the Person named, and the faith is in the one speaking.

### It Is Backed by Relationship

And the seven sons of Sceva used the name as a technique and the spirit answered, "Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye?" (Acts 19:15).

The name is not a password. It is an appeal to Someone you actually know.

### It Is a Strong Tower

And Proverbs says the name of the LORD is a strong tower, and the righteous runs into it and is safe (Proverbs 18:10).

Note the verb. Not admire it. Run into it. The name is a place.

### It Sends, It Does Not Merely Mention

And Jesus said, "In my name shall they cast out devils; they shall speak with new tongues" (Mark 16:17). The name is an authority to act with, not a phrase to decorate a prayer.

**Using the name of Jesus is not asking Him to attend. It is acting on His behalf.**

### Speaking It Right

### Address the Fear by Name

And here is where it becomes very practical. Vague dread is hard to answer; a named fear can be brought under a named authority.

Name what is frightening you out loud, and then speak the name above it.

### Speak to the Mountain, Not About It

And Jesus said to speak to the mountain (Mark 11:23). Talking about your problem is not the same as addressing it.

### Then Act Like It Is Settled

And the proof of faith is the next step taken. Peter took the man by the hand (Acts 3:7) — the name was spoken, and then the hand was offered.

**Faith speaks the name and then reaches for the hand.**

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a formula that removes the need for obedience or medical care. Faith and wisdom work together: speak the name, and keep the appointment.

Second, do not use the name as a weapon against people. The name of Jesus is not a way to win arguments; it is the name that saves, heals, and delivers.

## What This Means for You

1. Write down the three names that most govern your fear.

2. Read Philippians 2:5–11 out loud over that list.

3. Speak the name of Jesus over each one, by name, in a sentence.

4. Do one practical thing the fear has been preventing.

5. **Speak the name and take the step, with faith and wisdom together.** The name is not a substitute for the doctor, the honest conversation, or the budget — it is the authority under which you do all three.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Ephesians 1:15–23.** Note where Christ is seated.
- **Read Acts 3:1–10.** Note the order — name, hand, walking.
- **Say the name out loud when the fear arrives.**
- **Tell one person the name over their situation.**
- **Notice which names you have been repeating about yourself.**

**The central truth, said plainly:** the name given to Jesus is not one item in a ranked list of names — it is the name above every name, which means whatever has been naming your future, from a diagnosis to a rejection, is operating beneath a jurisdiction it cannot overturn.

**Declare this today:**

> "Father, thank You that the name of Jesus is above every name — including the ones I have been afraid of. Teach me to speak His name with understanding and not as a formula, and let every fear in my life bow to the authority You have given Him, in His Name. Amen."
