There is a word in Ephesians 1 that carries the sound of a slave market and the relief of a receipt, and it is the word Paul uses for what happened to you.

"In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Paul's word is *apolytrōsis* — a release secured by the payment of a price — and he says it in the present tense, *we have*, which means redemption is not a hope you are working toward but a possession you are learning to walk in.**

## The Word Behind the Word

### Apolytrōsis

And the term is a compound built for the ancient world. *Lytron* is a ransom, the price paid to free a prisoner or buy back a slave. *Apolytroō* is to release by paying it.

Paul did not choose a polite word for improvement. He chose a commercial word with a cost inside it.

**Redemption is not a rescue from a distance. It is a purchase at a price.** What does it tell you that God did not simply cancel the debt?

### Through His Blood

And Paul does not soften the means. *Dia tou haimatou autou* — through His blood. In the vocabulary of the law, blood is the life given up, the payment made visible.

Peter says it plainly: we were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, but with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish (1 Peter 1:18–19).

Silver and gold could not cover it. Only a life could.

### The Forgiveness of Sins

And Paul apposes the two ideas as if they were one: redemption, the forgiveness of sins. The release and the cancellation are the same transaction.

*Aphesis* is a letting go — a debt dismissed, a bond released, a prisoner unshackled.

## What the Present Tense Means

### "We Have"

And the verb is present active indicative. Not "we will have." Not "we hope to have."

Ephesians opens with a cascade of present-tense possessions — we have redemption, we have an inheritance, we have been blessed — all of it anchored in what Christ already accomplished.

### Not a Goal but a Ground

And this changes the direction of your Christian life. You are not working toward release; you are working from it.

The slave who does not know he has been freed will still take orders from the old master.

**Redemption is not something you are earning. It is something you are learning to inhabit.** So why do you keep reporting back to a master who no longer owns you?

### The Riches of His Grace

And Paul adds the measure: according to the riches of His grace. Not out of a modest reserve, but according to the abundance of it.

The supply is not the bottleneck. Your awareness of it usually is.

### What Redemption Changes

### Your Owner

And the first change is ownership. A redeemed slave belongs to a different master, and Paul is not embarrassed to say so — you were bought with a price (1 Corinthians 6:20).

You are not your own. That is not a threat; it is the end of an old tyranny.

### Your Standing

And the second change is legal. If the price was paid, the accusation has no claim. Paul says there is therefore now no condemnation (Romans 8:1) because the payment was complete and accepted at the resurrection.

Justification is the courtroom word for it: declared righteous, not merely pardoned.

**A debt that has been paid in full cannot be collected twice.**

### Your Future

And the third change is directional. Paul says the Holy Spirit is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession (Ephesians 1:14).

Redemption has a now and a not yet, and the same blood secures both.

### Living Redeemed

### Stop Paying Interest

And here is where it becomes practical. Many believers have been purchased and still live as though the interest on an old debt were still accruing — performing for acceptance, apologising for existing, bracing for a bill.

The account is settled. The performances are not only unnecessary; they are a misunderstanding of the receipt.

### Answer the Accuser With the Price

And the accuser's whole strategy is to make you forget the price was paid. When he brings up what you were, answer with what you cost.

Not a defence of your record. A statement of His purchase.

### Walk Like a Free Person

And freedom has a gait. The redeemed speak differently, serve differently, and are not desperate.

Mark 10:45 says the Son of Man came to give His life a ransom for many. If that was the price, the least you can do is walk like it worked.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a licence to sin because grace is abundant. Paul rejects that question in Romans 6 outright — the freed person does not return to the slave market for recreation.

Second, this is not a call to live in unexamined guilt as though the blood were only a down payment. Faith and wisdom together mean taking sin seriously and taking the blood more seriously still.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Ephesians 1:1–14 in one sitting and underline every phrase starting with "in whom" or "we have."

2. Write out 1 Peter 1:18–19 in your own hand.

3. Name the accusation you keep answering and answer it once with the blood.

4. Stop one performance you have been running to earn acceptance.

5. **Take sin seriously, and keep faith and wisdom together.** Redemption is not carelessness. Confess quickly, make the amends, see the doctor, and still refuse to drag yourself when the blood has already spoken.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Mark 10:45.** Note who the ransom was for.
- **Read Hebrews 9:11–28.** Note how the blood works.
- **Say "I have redemption" out loud when the old voice speaks.**
- **Thank Him for a price you did not set.**
- **Do one generous thing free people do.**
