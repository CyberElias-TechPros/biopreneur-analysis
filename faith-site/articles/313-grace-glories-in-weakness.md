Second Corinthians 12 contains the only place in Scripture where a believer asks God three times for something and is refused, and the refusal is recorded as good news.

"And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness" (2 Corinthians 12:9).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **God's answer to the thorn was not removal but residence — grace is not the strength to pretend you are not weak, it is the power of Christ pitched like a tent over the weakness you have stopped hiding.**

## The Request

### Three Times

And Paul's language is specific: "For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me" (2 Corinthians 12:8).

Three times is not a lack of faith. It is the persistence of somebody who believes God hears.

**Persistence is not evidence of doubt. It is evidence of relationship.** So what have you stopped asking for?

### The Thorn

And nobody knows for certain what it was. Eyes, epilepsy, a persecutor, a persistent temptation — the guesses fill volumes.

Which is probably deliberate. A specific thorn would help a few; an unnamed one helps everyone.

**God left the thorn unlabelled so that your thorn could fit in the sentence.** And yours has a sentence.

### A Messenger of Satan

And Paul calls it *angelos Satana* — a messenger of Satan (2 Corinthians 12:7), given to buffet him.

Which means the thing that drove him to his knees had an ugly origin and a holy assignment.

**God is not the author of the thorn, and He is the Lord of its purpose.** Can you hold both of those at once?

## The Answer

### Sufficient

And the answer is not "no." It is *arkei soi* — it is enough for you, it suffices. A present-tense adequacy.

Not "will be sufficient later." Sufficient now, in this.

**Grace does not remove the need. It meets it.**

### Made Perfect

And *teleitai* — is completed, is brought to its finish. The word is about a process reaching its end, not a strength being displayed.

His power does not bypass weakness. It finishes in it.

**Weakness is not the disqualification for power. It is the site.**

### Rest Upon

And Paul's conclusion is even stranger: "that the power of Christ may *rest* upon me." *Episkēnōsē* — to pitch a tent over, to dwell as a tabernacle.

The power does not stand beside him. It tents over him.

**Grace is a covering, and the covering is a Person.**

### Why Weakness

### Because It Is Honest

And the first reason is simply truth. A person who knows their weakness tells the truth about themselves, and God has always worked through truthful vessels.

Pretended strength is a closed window.

**God cannot fill what you are busy pretending is already full.**

### Because No One Can Boast

And the second is stated plainly in 1 Corinthians 1:29 — "that no flesh should glory in his presence."

The venue matters. If the power came from the vessel, the vessel would be the attraction.

**Weakness is the arrangement that keeps the glory in the right place.**

### Because It Is Where People Live

And the third is pastoral. A saviour who could only work through the strong has very little to say to most of the world.

He chooses the weak on purpose, and it is good news for everybody.

You are not disqualified by what you cannot do. You are qualified by who He is.

### The Boasting

### A Different Boast

And Paul says *most gladly* he will boast in infirmities. Not resign to them. Boast.

This is not self-loathing, and it is not a performance of humility. It is a person who has found the better treasure.

A man who boasts in the tent is a man who has seen what is over it.

### Weakness, Not Sin

And the distinction matters. Paul boasts in *astheneiais* — weaknesses, incapacity, limitations. He does not boast in his sins.

There is a difference between a limitation and a rebellion.

You can glory in a thorn. You cannot glory in a compromise.

### Pleasure in the List

And he goes further: "I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ's sake" (2 Corinthians 12:10).

Five items, and every one of them is out of his control. That is the point.

He has stopped negotiating with reality and started trusting the One who rules it.

### Living With a Thorn

### Stop Asking for the Wrong Thing

And the first discipline is honest: if God has answered "sufficient," continuing to demand "removed" is not faith. It is arguing with a settled answer.

That does not make the thorn pleasant. It makes it governed.

Some things are not healed. They are carried, and the carrying is grace.

### Tell the Truth About It

And the second is confession, in a safe room, to a safe person. The thorn stops being a power when it stops being a secret.

### Keep Working

And the third is that Paul kept travelling, planting, writing, and suffering after the answer. He did not retire to a cave.

Weakness is a condition, not a career change.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a reason to glorify illness or to refuse help. Paul's thorn was a limitation God used; it was not a calling to neglect care. Medical treatment and this verse are not in competition.

Second, this is not licence for unmortified sin. There is a difference between an infirmity you carry and a habit you coddle.

**The **The Power of the Holy Spirit** is the tent; **The Supernatural Life is a limping man walking in it; and The Anointing is most visible where the vessel is least impressive.

## What This Means for You

1. Read 2 Corinthians 12:1–10 once a week for a month.

2. Name the thorn you have asked to be removed.

3. Ask instead for sufficiency — and note what changes.

4. Tell one safe person the truth about it.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Grace is sufficient, and medicine is grace with a face on it. **The Power of the Holy Spirit** is not a substitute for rest and care; wisdom receives both.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Isaiah 40:27–31.**
- **Read 1 Corinthians 1:18–31.**
- **Write out 2 Corinthians 12:9 by hand.**
- **Say "sufficient" over your thorn.**
- **Keep one appointment** you were going to avoid.
