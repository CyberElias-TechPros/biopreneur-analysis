This is one of the strangest compliments Jesus ever paid, and it is paid to the wrong people.

"And the lord commended the unjust steward, because he had done wisely: for the children of this world are in their generation wiser than the children of light" (Luke 16:8).

A manager who has just been caught wasting his employer's goods has cut deals with the debtors to secure his own future — and the master commends the *wisdom*, not the dishonesty.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Jesus did not tell His followers to be less shrewd about earth; He told them to be as shrewd as the world is and about better things — the scandal is that the world out-plans the church.**

## What Exactly Was Commended

### The Shrewdness, Not the Fraud

Read the sentence precisely. The master commends him *because he had done wisely* — the Greek *phronimōs*, prudently, sensibly.

He does not commend the theft. He commends the foresight. The steward had looked at a coming crisis and acted in advance to prepare for it.

**Jesus is not admiring the dishonesty. He is admiring a man who took his own future seriously.**

### What the Steward Actually Did

He called the debtors and reduced their bills — a hundred measures of oil became fifty (Luke 16:6).

The mechanism is disputed by commentators, but the effect is not: he converted a position he was about to lose into relationships he would need afterwards.

**He used a temporary asset to purchase a permanent welcome.**

### The Sentence That Follows

And Jesus tells His disciples to do the same with *mammon*: "Make to yourselves friends of the mammon of unrighteousness; that, when ye fail, they may receive you into everlasting habitations" (Luke 16:9).

Money is temporary by definition. The instruction is to convert it, deliberately, into something that receives you on the other side.

**The steward's error was the direction he invested in. His method was sound.**

### Why the World Out-Plans the Church

#### Deliberateness, Not Intelligence

The comparison Jesus makes is generational — *in their generation*. The children of this world are wiser about the things of this world, which should not surprise anyone.

What should surprise us is the gap in *effort*. A business plan gets more scrutiny than a discipleship plan. A budget forecast gets more prayer than a ministry. Why is that?

**The world is not more intelligent than the church. It is often more deliberate.**

#### Serpents and Doves

Jesus makes the same point in the sending charge: "Behold, I send you forth as sheep in the midst of wolves: be ye therefore wise as serpents, and harmless as doves" (Matthew 10:16).

The pairing is the whole teaching in one line. **Wisdom without harmlessness becomes cunning; harmlessness without wisdom becomes naivety.** Jesus refuses to let you pick one.

#### The Prudent Man Sees

Proverbs describes the difference in a sentence: "A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself: but the simple pass on, and are punished" (Proverbs 22:3).

The simple are not wicked. They simply did not look ahead. And the penalty for not looking ahead is the same as the penalty for doing wrong — you get hurt.

**Foresight is a moral duty, not an optional personality trait.**

### What It Looks Like to Be Both

#### Rich Toward God

The phrase comes from the end of the parable of the rich fool: not laying up treasure for yourself, but being "rich toward God" (Luke 12:21).

That is a direction of wealth, not an amount of it. A person can be rich toward God with very little, and bankrupt toward Him with a great deal.

#### Wise Toward Earth

And being wise toward earth means the ordinary disciplines: knowing what comes in, what goes out, what the obligations are, and what the risks look like.

Paul tells the Colossians to walk "in wisdom toward them that are without, redeeming the time" (Colossians 4:5). That is a strategy for how a believer handles the outside world — not withdrawal, and not imitation.

#### The Single Master

The parable ends with the line that keeps all of this from collapsing into worldly ambition: "Ye cannot serve God and mammon" (Luke 16:13).

Notice that Jesus does not say you cannot *use* mammon. He says you cannot *serve* it. The steward's problem was never that he handled money; it was that money had become the thing he was building a future with.

**The **Kingdom Stewardship** uses money the way a carpenter uses a tool — constantly, and without loyalty.**

### The Boundary

Two cautions, because this teaching is easy to abuse.

First, shrewdness is not a licence for sharp practice. The steward was *unjust*, and Jesus says so plainly. The commendation is of a method abstracted from its ethics, and the point is that the method should be used for righteous ends.

Second, being "wise toward earth" is not a call to anxiety about the future. **Faith and Wisdom** plans and then commits. A believer who has done the arithmetic can sleep, because the outcome is not the arithmetic's to decide.

## What This Means for You

**1. Name your coming crisis.** The steward's whole advantage was that he saw it before it arrived.

**2. Convert something.** Take one asset — money, time, skill — and deliberately invest it in a relationship or a work that will outlast it.

**3. Read your own accounts.** If you cannot state your position in numbers, are you not the simple man of Proverbs 22:3?

**4. Pick the harder plan.** Ask where you have been less deliberate about a spiritual matter than a financial one, and reverse it.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Wisdom plans; faith rests. **The Kingdom of God** is not served by sloppiness, and it is not built by cunning.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Luke 16:1–13** in one sitting and mark every use of *wise*.
- **Write down your financial position** in five numbers.
- **Read Matthew 10:16** and ask which half you have been practising.
- **Choose one relationship** to invest in this month.
- **Read Ephesians 5:15–17** and note what it says about time.
