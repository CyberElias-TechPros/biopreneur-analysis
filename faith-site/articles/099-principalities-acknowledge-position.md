If a man wearing a colonel's insignia were given the power to forget his own rank, the enemy would still know it. Rank does not depend on the wearer's memory of it. The soldier might spend his whole career apologising for crossing a parade ground, dodging orders he had every right to give, and bracing for rebuke from officers junior to him — and nothing would change except the misery of his own service. His authority was never the feeling he carried into a room.

That is a fair picture of a great many sincere believers. The powers look at you and see a seat; you look at the powers and see a lion. Something in your position makes them uneasy, and something in your thinking keeps you apologising for it.

"I am seated with him in the heavenlies," writes Paul, "Set at his right hand, everything — not only this world but the world to come — put under his feet" (Ephesians 1:20–21). Then he says it of you: "and hath raised us up together, and made us sit together in heavenly places in Christ Jesus" (Ephesians 2:6). Here is the central truth of this teaching: **your rank over the powers is a seat Christ already occupies and shares with you — acknowledged in the unseen realm whether or not you feel it.**

## Where the Seat Came From

Ephesians 2 is not a guide to self-elevation; it is a record of evacuation and relocation. Look at what sits immediately before verse 6. Paul says you "were dead in trespasses and sins" (Ephesians 2:1), walking "according to the prince of the power of the air" (Ephesians 2:2). Then comes the hinge of the whole passage: "But God, who is rich in mercy... when we were dead in sins, hath quickened us together with Christ... and hath raised us up together, and made us sit together" (Ephesians 2:4–6).

Three verbs, one single act: quickened, raised, seated. You did not climb. You were carried, and the carrying began while you were still dead. This is why the position cannot be lost through a bad week. It was never yours to hold up. A dead man is not asked to contribute to his own resurrection, and that is the point Paul is making.

**Legal Position** — where and who you are before God's throne in Christ — is not a mood that rises with a morning's prayer and collapses by lunch. **The Authority of the Believer** — the right and ability to stand in His name — is simply what that position costs when it is exercised. **Spiritual Warfare** — the conflict over what you will believe and say — is what happens in the space between your seated position and your unsettled feelings.

### A Seat, Not a Rung

There is a word in Ephesians 1 worth pausing over. Paul lists the hierarchy the seat is above: "far above all principality, and power, and might, and dominion, and every name that is named, not only in this world, but also in that which is to come" (Ephesians 1:21). He is naming ranks, and then placing Christ above every one of them without exception.

The church, in turn, is not merely the audience of that passage. Verse 22 says God gave Christ "to be the head over all things to the church," and the next chapter puts you in the same seat. Position in the kingdom is not earned by climbing rank; it is inherited by being in the Person.

Set them side by side and the gap shows. A rung must be climbed, and a man on a rung is always anxious about who is above him. A seat is occupied, and a man in a seat is not competing with the room. One produces striving; the other produces rest that looks, to the nervous, almost like arrogance.

### Why the Powers Notice

There is a scene in Mark that reads like an intelligence report. Jesus enters a synagogue and a man with an unclean spirit cries out, "Let us alone; what have we to do with thee, thou Jesus of Nazareth? art thou come to destroy us? I know thee who thou art, the Holy One of God" (Mark 1:24).

Put the details in order, because they are not the details of a man thinking for himself. The voice identified Jesus before Jesus had said a word about Himself — before the sermon began, before a single claim was made. The spirit knew, and the spirit was frightened, and the congregation did not yet know. Perception ran ahead of the worship service.

That is the pattern the gospel keeps repeating. In Acts 16, a slave girl with a spirit of divination follows Paul for days, announcing to the street that these men are servants of the Most High God. Paul is grieved, and eventually turns and commands the spirit out. The spirit had announced the truth and still had to leave. Recognition is not protection. A principality can see your seat perfectly accurately and still have no power to move you out of it.

Consider a land registry. A deed is filed, stamped, and recorded, and the record stands whether or not the owner is on the property, feeling like a landowner, or even thinking about the farm that day. The registry holds the fact and does not consult the owner's mood. Ephesians 2:6 is no sentiment; it is a filed fact — written down, sealed, and written in a Person.

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop negotiating for a position you already hold.** Some believers pray for authority in the way a tenant begs a landlord for a deed. You are not petitioning for a seat; you are learning to occupy one. The prayer that changes things here is not "give me authority" but "open my eyes to where You have already put me."

**2. Answer the accuser from your position, not your performance.** When guilt speaks, it is interrogating your conduct. Your answer should not be a defence of your conduct; it should be a statement of your standing. A prisoner is judged by his record. A seated son is judged by his Father's verdict.

**3. Expect the unseen to know you before you feel known.** Rank in this war does not announce itself with a feeling. There will be times when you feel entirely ordinary and the resistance you meet is disproportionate to your size in the room. That is because the resistance is not answering your mood; it is answering your seat.

**4. Do not confuse rest with presumption.** The seat is not permission to do whatever you like. It is commission to do only what your King is doing. A man who has authority but no obedience is not seated; he is simply sitting.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom value the position correctly.** Faith receives your seat as fact; wisdom decides how a person of that rank behaves in a room. Authority is not volume. It is sober, consistent, self-controlled bearing — the kind that makes a hostile room quieter before a single word is spoken.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Ephesians 1:19–2:7 in one sitting** and underline every phrase that describes where you are rather than how you feel.
- **Speak your position aloud each morning this week**, from your seat rather than toward it.
- **Answer one recurring accusation in writing.** Write the charge down, then write the positional answer beside it.
- **Read Mark 1:21–27** and note exactly when the spirit began speaking.
