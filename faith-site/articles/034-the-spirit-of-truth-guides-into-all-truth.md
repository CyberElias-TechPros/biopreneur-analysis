Have you ever faced a decision where the facts were clear but the way forward was not — where information alone could not tell you what to do? That gap between knowing and discerning is where the Spirit of truth works.

Jesus promises in John 16:13: "when he, the **Spirit of truth**, is come, he will **guide you into all truth**... and he will shew you things to come." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the Holy Spirit is your personal Guide into truth — not merely information about God, but living revelation that shapes decisions.**

## Information Is Not Revelation

The world is drowning in information about God and about everything else. **Revelation Knowledge** — truth made alive to your spirit so that it becomes real, personal, and actionable — is a different category altogether. Information informs the mind; revelation changes the person. And what good is a map you cannot read to a traveller who cannot see the road ahead?

Jesus says the Spirit will *guide you into* truth — the picture is of a guide walking with a traveller, not a textbook handed over at a desk. **The Word of God** — the inspired, living, authoritative message of God — is the map the Guide uses. **Communion With the Holy Spirit** — ongoing personal fellowship and sensitivity — is the conversation along the way.

Notice two guardrails Jesus builds into the promise. First, "he shall not speak of himself" — the Spirit does not promote Himself or invent His own agenda. Second, "whatsoever he shall hear, that shall he speak" — He relays what belongs to the Father and the Son. And He "will shew you things to come," giving foresight that protects and prepares.

Think of a mountain guide who has walked the trail many times. He does not replace the map; he reads it with you, warns of the cliff the map marks but does not describe, and chooses the safe path around the washed-out bridge. The map is trustworthy; the guide makes it walkable.

### Truth That Agrees With the Word

Every genuine leading of the Spirit agrees with Scripture. This is not a limitation on His power; it is a guarantee of His consistency. The Spirit who inspired the Word will never contradict the Word. So when an impression, a prophecy, or a strong feeling conflicts with Scripture, Scripture governs.

The Spirit also always points to Jesus: "He shall glorify me: for he shall receive of mine, and shall shew it unto you" (John 16:14). Any voice that diminishes Christ, denies His finished work, or shifts your confidence from Him to yourself is not the Spirit of truth.

The distinction could not be sharper. The unguided believer treats every strong impression as divine and stumbles between zeal and confusion. The guided believer listens, tests against the Word, seeks wise counsel, and walks with confidence. One is led by feelings; the other by the Guide.

### The Difference Between Guidance and Gossip

John gives a test that runs the length of the letter: "But the anointing which ye have received of him abideth in you, and ye need not that any man teach you: but as the same anointing teacheth you of all things, and is truth, and is no lie" (1 John 2:27).

This is not a dismissal of teachers — the same author writes to instruct them. It is a statement about where the final authority sits. The Spirit's teaching is not one voice among many; it is the standard by which the other voices are weighed.

The Spirit's guidance has a recognisable character. He does not flatter; He convicts (John 16:8). He does not contradict the word He inspired; He takes what is Christ's and shows it (John 16:14). And He does not leave you with a vague impression and no direction; the promise is that He will "guide you into all truth."

Which means a great deal of what passes for spiritual leading can be tested. Does it exalt Christ? Does it agree with Scripture? Does it produce love, joy, peace, and self-control? Those are not decoration on the guidance; they are its fingerprints.

### The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **1 Corinthians 2:10** — "But God hath revealed them unto us by his Spirit: for the Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God." Revelation is the Spirit's work; the deep things of God are opened by Him, not by intellect alone.
- **1 John 2:27** — "But the anointing which ye have received of him abideth in you, and ye need not that any man teach you: but as the same anointing teacheth you of all things." The anointing is an internal teacher, resident and reliable, always in agreement with the written Word.
- **Acts 16:6–10** — "They were forbidden of the Holy Ghost to preach the word in Asia... and the Spirit suffered them not... And after he had seen the vision, immediately we endeavoured to go into Macedonia." The Spirit's guidance is specific enough to redirect plans — and personal enough to be obeyed.

Three witnesses, one Guide: He searches, He teaches, He redirects.

## What This Means for You

**1. Ask for guidance before you decide.** Make it a habit to ask the Spirit for wisdom at the start of decisions, not after you have already chosen. He is present and willing to lead.

**2. Test every impression against the Word.** Write down the impression, then check it against Scripture, the character of Jesus, and wise counsel. Revelation always harmonises with the Word.

**3. Train your listening ear.** **Sensitivity: The Lost Art of Listening to God** — learn to notice the quiet impressions that come during prayer, Scripture, and ordinary tasks, and keep a record so you can learn to recognise His voice.

**4. Faith and wisdom follow the Guide together.** Faith trusts His leading; wisdom confirms it with Scripture, counsel, timing, and circumstances. Declaring His guidance never excuses recklessness; it calls for tested obedience.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read John 16:7–15 and mark every action of the Spirit.** Guide, speak, hear, show, glorify — then ask Him to do each of those in your life this week.
- **Keep a guidance journal for seven days.** Record impressions, decisions, and outcomes. Patterns will emerge faster than with occasional reflection.
- **Test one major impression against Scripture.** Write the impression, then write two or three verses that confirm or challenge it, and seek one piece of wise counsel.
- **Obey one clear leading promptly.** When the Spirit directs something specific this week, do it within the day and note what followed.
