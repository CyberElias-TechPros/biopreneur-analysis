Have you ever prayed in faith and then spent the afternoon researching how impossible your situation is? That is not diligence; it is consideration of the wrong evidence.

Romans 4:19 says of Abraham, "being not weak in faith, he **considered not his own body now dead**, when he was about an hundred years old, neither yet the deadness of Sara's womb." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **faith is not strengthened by staring at the obstacle but by steadying attention on God's promise.**

## What Consideration Does

Paul's word for *considered* means to fix the mind on, to observe carefully, to take full account of. Abraham did not lack information; he had decades of it. What he refused was to let that information become his meditation and his conclusion.

**The Life of Faith** — a life ordered by God's declarations rather than appearances — depends on this discipline of attention. **Renewing of the Mind** — the continuous transformation of thought patterns through God's truth — is how consideration is retrained. **The Word of God** — the inspired, living, authoritative message of God — is the evidence faith considers instead.

Notice Paul's verdict: Abraham was "not weak in faith" precisely because he did not consider. Weakness of faith and fixation on impossibility grow in the same soil.

Think of a navigator in a storm. He knows the waves are real, but he steers by the compass rather than by the swell. The swell is information; the compass is authority. Faith navigates that way.

## Facts, Faith, and Focus

Faith is not anti-fact. Abraham knew he was a hundred and that Sarah's womb was dead. He simply did not let those facts preside over God's promise. Proverbs 4:20–23 tells us to attend to God's words, keep them in the midst of the heart, for they are life to those who find them.

There is also a communal dimension to consideration. Abraham's household knew the promise, and Sarah's laughter in Genesis 18 shows how contagious doubt can be inside a family. The people you speak with most often will either strengthen your attention on the promise or pull it back to the obstacle. That does not mean abandoning struggling friends; it means being deliberate about whose voice carries authority in your inner life.

That is why Philippians 4:8 commands a particular kind of thinking: whatsoever is true, honest, just, pure, lovely, of good report — think on these things. Attention is a steward, and what you give it authority over your heart.

Have you ever noticed that the more you explain a problem, the larger it becomes in your mind? Here is the contrast: the circumstance-centred believer prays and then studies the problem, growing more convinced of impossibility. The promise-centred believer prays and then studies God's faithfulness, growing more confident of His character.

## The Word Says the Same Thing From Three Directions

- **2 Corinthians 4:18** — "While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal." Paul commands a deliberate redirect of the eyes.
- **Hebrews 12:2** — "Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross." Endurance follows looking, and looking has an object.
- **Romans 4:20** — "He staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief; but was strong in faith, giving glory to God." The alternative to considering the obstacle is considering the promise.

Three witnesses, one focus: looking at the unseen, looking unto Jesus, and glorying in the promise.

## What This Means for You

**1. Audit your attention.** Notice what you read, watch, and rehearse about your situation. If most of it feeds impossibility, change the diet.

**2. Meditate on the promise, not the problem.** Replace problem-rehearsal with Scripture meditation. Say the verse when the obstacle comes to mind.

**3. Answer facts with the promise, not with denial.** Acknowledge what is true and then state what God has said. Both can be spoken in the same breath.

**4. Faith and wisdom consider and act.** Faith fixes attention on the promise; wisdom still gathers needed information and takes responsible steps. Declaring the promise never means ignoring reality; it means refusing to let reality have the final word.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Romans 4:18–25 slowly.** Note what Abraham considered and what he refused to consider, then write down your own two lists.
- **Set a media boundary.** Limit one input that repeatedly feeds your sense of impossibility, and replace that time with Scripture.
- **Practise the redirect.** Each time the obstacle comes to mind this week, say the promise aloud and turn your attention to God's character.
- **Thank God for His faithfulness.** End each day by naming one evidence of God's faithfulness, training your attention toward what is true of Him.
