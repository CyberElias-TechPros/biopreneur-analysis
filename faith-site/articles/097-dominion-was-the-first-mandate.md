Before the ten commandments, before the tabernacle, before Abraham was called out of Ur, God said something about human beings that has never been rescinded: "and let them have dominion."

"And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea" (Genesis 1:26). Here is the central truth of this teaching: **dominion is not a reward for spiritual maturity but a feature of the image — humanity was made to rule, and redemption restores what creation was designed for.**

### A Mandate Before a Fall

It is easy to read the Bible as a story of humanity losing ground, with a rescue appended at the end. But Genesis begins with a commission, not a warning. The very first thing God says about the creature He is about to make is that it will rule.

**The Authority of the Believer** — the God-given right and ability, in Christ, to stand and act in His name — is not a novelty added at Pentecost. It is a restoration. **The New Creation** — the recreated human spirit — is what makes the restoration personal. **The Kingdom of God** — His rule, established and advancing — is the order humanity was made to administer.

Think of a steward given a great house before he has done a single day's work. The assignment comes with the appointment.

Notice the plural: "let them have dominion." The mandate is given to a pair, and therefore to a community. Ruling was never designed to be a solo project, which is one reason the New Testament locates so much authority in the gathered church rather than in the individual hero.

### What Dominion Is Not

It is not the right to exploit. The mandate in Genesis 1 is followed immediately by a diet of plants and fruit (Genesis 1:29) — a picture of provision, not predation. Humanity was to rule as God rules: by care, order, and generosity.

It is not a licence to dominate other people. Verses 26–28 give dominion over fish, birds, cattle, and the earth — never over another human being made in the same image.

It is also not automatic. Psalm 8 marvels that God crowned humanity "with glory and honour" (Psalm 8:5) and then asks, in effect, why He would bother.

## The Fall's Damage

Sin did not remove the image; it defaced it. Human beings still rule — but now they rule badly, and they are ruled by what they were made to govern. Fear governs. Appetite governs. Circumstance governs.

That is why the gospel is described in such political terms: a transfer of kingdom, a change of citizenship, a defeat of the powers. It is not merely the forgiveness of a debt, though it is that. It is the restoration of an office.

Have you ever considered that passivity in the face of circumstances might be more than a personality trait? What if it were an abdication?

Genesis 9 repeats the commission after the flood to Noah: "Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth" (Genesis 9:1). Even after judgement, the office stands. God does not withdraw the assignment because the officeholder failed; He works toward restoration.

### Hebrews 2 and the Missing Crown

Hebrews quotes Psalm 8 and then says something startling: "But now we see not yet all things put under him. But we see Jesus... crowned with glory and honour" (Hebrews 2:8–9).

The psalm's promise looks unfulfilled until you look at Christ. In Him, the crown is worn, and those who are in Him are joined to His reign. That is why Paul can say believers are "raised up together, and made to sit together in heavenly places in Christ Jesus" (Ephesians 2:6).

### Recovering the Mandate

The Greek word behind "dominion" in the Septuagint is *archein* — to rule, to be first, to take the lead. It is the same verb family the New Testament uses for Christ as "the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, that in all things he might have the preeminence" (Colossians 1:18). Dominion, then, is not a mood of superiority; it is the act of taking the lead in a place where no one else is leading.

Watch how differently Scripture treats the two failures available to a person holding this mandate. Passivity is never praised as faith. The servant who buried his talent was not commended for humility; he was called wicked and slothful (Matthew 25:26). Yet neither is seizure commended. When Israel's elders tried to take leadership God had not assigned, the earth opened (Numbers 16). The mandate is real, the boundaries are real, and both matter.

There is also a recovery order worth noticing. God did not ask Adam to make himself an image before restoring his function, and He does not ask you to. You are not working toward a qualification; you are working from one. That is why Paul can tell the Colossians to behave in a way that matches what has already been done to them: "ye are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God" (Colossians 3:3)."

The indicative precedes the imperative, here as everywhere.

### Two Directions of Error

The line between them runs here: The abdicating believer blames circumstances and calls it humility; the domineering believer seizes what was never given and calls it faith. One is passive where it should act; the other acts where it should submit.

### The Image Is the Qualification

Genesis does not say humanity was given dominion because it was capable. It says dominion followed the image: "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion" (Genesis 1:26). The capacity for rule was built into the resemblance.

That means the fall did not remove the mandate; it corrupted the exercise of it. And it means redemption restores the qualification before it restores the performance — which is precisely the order the New Testament insists on everywhere.
## What This Means for You

- **Take responsibility before you take authority.** Dominion begins with ownership of what is actually yours to manage.
- **Rule your inner world first.** "He that is slow to anger is better than the mighty" (Proverbs 16:32).
- **Govern your sphere, not someone else's.** The earth was given; the neighbour's field was not.
- **Refuse passivity as a spiritual virtue.** Waiting on God is not the same as doing nothing.
- **Exercise dominion with skill.** Faith and wisdom govern together: competence, planning, and diligence are how authority becomes useful instead of merely loud.

- **Read Genesis 1:26–31** and write down what was actually given.
- **Name one area of your life where you have been passive.**
- **Take one concrete act of stewardship** in that area this week.
