A police officer directing traffic has no personal strength to stop a lorry. What she has is a badge, a uniform, and a warrant signed by someone with jurisdiction. Strip those away and she is simply a person standing in a road.

Luke 9:1 records something similar in the kingdom: "Then he called his twelve disciples together, and gave them power and authority over all devils, and to cure diseases." Here is the central truth of this teaching: **authority in the kingdom is never seized by the confident; it is handed to the sent — and everything a believer does in the Name flows from that handover.**

## Two Words, Not One

Luke uses both *dunamis* and *exousia* — power and authority. Power is the ability to do; authority is the right to act. A man may have the strength to break a door and no right to enter; another may have the key and no strength to carry what is inside.

**The Authority of the Believer** — the God-given right and ability, in Christ, to stand and act in His name — requires both, and both are given together. **The Name of Jesus** — His person, work, and authority — is the warrant. **The Kingdom of God** — His rule, established and advancing — is the jurisdiction.

Notice the sequence in the verse. He called. He gave. Then, in verse 2, He sent. Calling, gift, commission — never the reverse.

### What Delegation Actually Means

Delegation is a legal arrangement, not a personality trait. An ambassador does not become important by feeling important; he becomes an ambassador when a government appoints him and gives him credentials.

Paul uses exactly that language: "Now then we are ambassadors for Christ" (2 Corinthians 5:20). An ambassador speaks with the authority of the sending state while remaining personally unimportant.

Think of a power of attorney. The document does not make a person wiser or stronger. It makes his signature count.

### Why This Matters So Much

If authority is manufactured, then only the strong, loud, and confident can exercise it — and everyone else is left waiting to become someone else. If authority is delegated, then the timid believer with a Bible and a Name has the same warrant as the bold one.

Have you ever held back from praying for someone because you did not feel spiritually impressive enough? What does that hesitation assume about where authority comes from?

### The Ceiling on Delegated Authority

A delegated authority can only act within its commission. A police officer cannot search a house on a warrant for a different address. So it matters enormously what Christ actually authorized.

Matthew 28:18 gives the scope: "All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth." Then verse 19 sends. Luke 10:19 states the commission plainly: "Behold, I give unto you power to tread on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy."

The difference is this: Presumption acts where it was never sent and calls the failure a lack of faith; faith acts where it was sent and expects the warrant to be honoured.

### The Posture of the Delegated

- **Humility.** You are a representative, not the government.
- **Boldness.** A representative who doubts the credential dishonours the sender.
- **Accountability.** Delegated authority is exercised on the sender's terms, not on your preferences.
- **Dependence.** Jesus said, "without me ye can do nothing" (John 15:5) — not "without me you can do a little."

None of these is a formality. In Scripture, authority is always relational before it is functional.

### Two Men, Two Crowns

The centurion in Matthew 8 understood delegation from the inside, and Jesus called his understanding greater than anything He had found in Israel. "For I am a man under authority, having soldiers under me: and I say to this man, Go, and he goeth" (Matthew 8:9). He did not describe his own power. He described his position in a chain.

That is the whole architecture of kingdom authority, and it explains why humility and boldness are not opposites in Scripture. Both are simply accurate assessments — of yourself as sent, and of the One who sent you.

That last observation is worth one more sentence. A leader who resents every authority above him will exercise authority below him as a tyrant, because he has no experience of legitimate submission. The centurion was a good commander precisely because he was a good subordinate.

## What This Means for You

Romans 13 establishes that human authority is also delegated, which is why the centurion's insight in Matthew 8 was so sharp: he understood that a word carries weight when the one speaking stands under authority.

A believer who resents all authority will struggle to exercise any. The kingdom does not produce freelancers.

**1. Receive before you attempt.** Start the day by acknowledging what has been given, not by assessing how strong you feel.
**2. Learn what the warrant covers.** Read Matthew 28, Luke 10, and Mark 16 and write down what is authorized.
**3. Speak the Name deliberately.** Authority is exercised in His name, not in yours.
**4. Submit where submission is due.** Authority flows through order, not around it.
**5. Faith and wisdom use authority lawfully.** Wisdom asks whether this is your assignment, this hour, and this person — because an ambassador who exceeds his brief does not expand the kingdom.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Write Luke 9:1** where you will see it, and underline "gave."
- **Take one assignment** you have been declining for lack of confidence, and act on the warrant.
- **Pray for one person** out loud this week, in the Name.
- **Read Luke 10:1–20** and note what the seventy rejoiced in — and what Jesus told them to rejoice in instead.
