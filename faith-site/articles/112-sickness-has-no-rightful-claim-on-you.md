Read Isaiah 53 as a single paragraph and the grammar will stop you.

"He was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed" (Isaiah 53:5).

Four clauses, four benefits. Transgression dealt with. Iniquity dealt with. Peace secured. Healing given.

Then Matthew quotes the same passage and applies it in a way that leaves no room for treating healing as a side issue: "When the even was come, they brought unto him many that were possessed with devils: and he cast out the spirits with his word, and healed all that were sick: That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias the prophet, saying, Himself took our infirmities, and bare our sicknesses" (Matthew 8:16–17).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **healing is part of the purchase of Calvary, so sickness stands in your body as an intruder with no legal right — and the believer answers it with authority, not with acceptance.**

## The Word That Carries It

The Hebrew word behind "healed" in Isaiah 53:5 is *rapha*, the same root used when God reveals Himself as "the LORD that healeth thee" (Exodus 15:26). It is a wide word — used for physical bodies, for nations, for broken waters, and for wounded hearts. It is not a narrow clinical term, and it is not a metaphor either. It is the ordinary word for being made well.

There is a further grammatical detail worth pausing over. The New Testament applies Isaiah 53 to healing in the past tense of Christ's work and the present tense of its effect. Peter writes, "Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree... by whose stripes ye were healed" (1 Peter 2:24). Were healed — an accomplished purchase, received now.

**Divine Wholeness** — the restoration of spirit, soul, and body that redemption secures — is what the stripes purchased. **The Finished Work** — what Christ completed at the cross — is the basis. **The Authority of the Believer** — the right to stand in His name — is how a believer answers a symptom that is making a claim.

Consider a writ of possession. A court order does not remove the squatter by itself; somebody has to serve it, and the household has to be told it has a right to the property. The order establishes the truth. Serving it applies the truth.

### What Healing in Redemption Does Not Mean

Here is where honesty is required, because this teaching has been badly handled and people have been hurt by it.

It does not mean that a believer who is sick has no faith. It does not mean every sickness ends instantly on demand. And it does not mean the body will never wear out — the same Bible that promises healing also says the outward man perishes while the inward man is renewed day by day (2 Corinthians 4:16).

What it does mean is that sickness is not God's will for you, is not your portion, and has no covenant right to your body. That is a position, and it is a real one, and it changes how you face the diagnosis.

Paul is instructive here. He had a thorn in the flesh and prayed three times for it to go. The answer was not removal; it was "My grace is sufficient for thee" (2 Corinthians 12:9). A theology that promises instant removal to everyone has no place for that answer, and a theology that expects sickness as God's ordinary will has no place for Isaiah 53. Both need each other to stay honest.

### Why the Body Is Contested

If healing is purchased, why is the body so often the front line?

Part of the answer is that the body is where the visible meets the invisible. Suffering in a body is not private; it is public, slow, and instructive to everyone watching. It is also the place where a person's deepest convictions get exposed, because pain has a way of evicting theory.

The New Testament treats the body with a seriousness that both extremes miss. "What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body" (1 Corinthians 6:19–20). A temple is not a temporary shelter to be neglected; it is a dwelling to be kept.

So the same Scripture that says sickness has no right to your body also says the body is not yours to do with as you please. It was bought. That is why a Christian cannot treat prayer as a substitute for stewardship, and cannot treat stewardship as a substitute for prayer.

### Answering the Claim Without Pretending

So what does authority over sickness actually look like in a hospital corridor?

It looks like a person who has done two things. First, they have settled the legal question: sickness is not their portion, and they will not accept a verdict God did not give. Second, they have taken up the practical means God has provided: medical care, rest, the prayers of the elders, the support of the church, and the ordinary wisdom that says yes to treatment.

Set them side by side and the gap shows. One believer treats the illness as a sentence and settles into it, and it becomes a spiritual identity. Another treats every symptom as an enemy to be shouted at and refuses all help, and exhaustion follows. The believer who understands authority does neither. He disagrees with the sickness, and he does everything a sensible person with that conviction would do.

That is why James's instruction is so ordinary: "Is any sick among you? let him call for the elders of the church; and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord" (James 5:14). Oil in the ancient world was a medicinal staple. God did not give the church a choice between prayer and care; He gave them both, in one sentence.

### The Persistence This Requires

One more thing needs saying, because it is the question everyone eventually asks. What about when healing does not come quickly?

The Bible's model is persistence without accusation. The woman with the issue of blood had spent everything she had on physicians and grown worse (Mark 5:26), and she still pressed through the crowd to touch His hem. Jesus' reply was neither "you should have come sooner" nor "I see your great faith"; He called her daughter and sent her in peace (Mark 5:34).

Faith is not a way of forcing a timetable. It is a refusal to accept a condition as a final verdict, held alongside a submission to God's wisdom about when and how.

## What This Means for You

**1. Settle the position before the symptom arrives.** Authority is easier to stand in when you have already decided, in a quiet season, what your body belongs to. Read Isaiah 53 and 1 Peter 2:24 until they are familiar, not just familiar to your search history.

**2. Disagree with the sickness, not with your doctor.** Healing and medical care are not rivals. Faith says "this is not my portion"; wisdom says "take the treatment". The same Father gives both.

**3. Take your body seriously as a bought temple.** Sleep, food, movement, and rest are not unspiritual. A person who believes their body is a temple keeps it in repair.

**4. Ask for the oil and the elders.** There is a corporate dimension to healing that individualist Christianity misses. The prayer of faith in James 5 is a shared act.

**5. Keep your faith and wisdom honest about outcomes.** Never let a doctrine make you ashamed to say "I am still waiting." Faith and wisdom work together as you keep trusting, keep treating, and keep telling your Father the truth about what this is like.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Isaiah 53:1–5 and Matthew 8:14–17 back to back**, and note how the gospel writer applies the prophet.
- **Speak 1 Peter 2:24 over your body** each morning this week.
- **Take one practical step of care** you have been putting off.
- **Ask one believer** to pray with you about anything in your body that has been troubling you.
