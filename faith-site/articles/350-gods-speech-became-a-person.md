The letter to the Hebrews opens without a greeting, without a name, and without a warm-up, and its first sentence is a history of how God has been talking.

"God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Hebrews opens by contrasting *polymerōs kai polytropōs* — many portions and many ways — with a single climactic speech *en huiō*, in a Son, which means the final revelation of God is not a document to be studied but a Person to be known.**

## The Grammar of Revelation

### Many Portions

And *polymerōs* means in many parts, piece by piece. A prophet here, a dream there, a law given in stages.

Revelation came in instalments because the hearers could only carry so much at a time.

### Many Ways

And *polytropōs* means in many manners — vision, history, poetry, law, judgement, mercy, a burning bush, a still small voice.

God has never been short of delivery methods. The variety is not confusion; it is condescension to human capacity.

### In a Son

And then the contrast lands. Not *en prophetais* but *en huiō* — in a Son. The preposition changes from "by" a servant to "in" a person.

The Son is not another instalment. He is the whole.

**Prophets carried the words. The Son is the Word.** So why do we still treat Him as one voice among many?

## What the Son Shows

### The Brightness of His Glory

And verse 3 goes further: He is the brightness — *apaugasma*, the radiance — of God's glory, and the express image of His person.

You cannot see the sun and separate it from its light. That is the relationship between the Father and the Son.

### The Final Word

And the phrase "in these last days" marks a turn in history. The last era began not with a date but with a birth.

We are not waiting for another revelation. We are living inside the one we were given.

### The One Who Speaks Still

And Hebrews insists the Son is not a past event. The same letter warns, "Today, if you will hear his voice, harden not your hearts" (Hebrews 3:7–8).

The final word is still speaking.

### What This Changes About Reading the Bible

### Christ Is the Key

And Jesus Himself set the rule on the road to Emmaus: beginning at Moses and all the prophets, He expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself (Luke 24:27).

The Bible is not a collection of unrelated revelations. It is a library pointing at one Person.

### Not Information but Encounter

And this is where many of us stall. We can accumulate correct facts about God for decades and never once sit in front of Him.

The Son did not come to give you a better syllabus. He came so that you could know the Teacher.

**The point of the whole library is a face.**

### Hearing Means Obeying

And in scripture, hearing is never merely auditory. "Today, if you will hear his voice" is followed immediately by a warning about hardened hearts.

To hear the Son is to do what He says. Anything less is background noise.

### When God Seems Silent

### He Has Already Spoken

And here is the mercy in this passage for anyone in a quiet season. God has not left you without a word. He has given you His Son, and in Him everything you need for life and godliness (2 Peter 1:3).

The silence you feel is not an absence of revelation. It is often an invitation to what has already been said — and what has already been said is not thin. It is a Person with a face, a voice, and a body that was broken for you.

### The Voice at the Mountain

And at the transfiguration, Peter wanted to build three tabernacles and keep the moment. The Father's answer was a sentence that ended the conversation: "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him" (Matthew 17:5).

Hear ye him. That is still the whole instruction.

### Familiarity Is the Danger

And the greatest risk to a person with a Bible is not ignorance. It is familiarity that never becomes obedience.

The word can be on your shelf and not in your hands.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this does not make scripture optional. The Son is known through the scriptures that testify of Him (John 5:39); to set Jesus against the Bible is to invent a Jesus.

Second, this does not make every impression a revelation. Faith and wisdom test what you hear against what has been written, because the Spirit who inspired the text does not contradict it.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Hebrews 1:1–4 slowly, three times this week.

2. Read Luke 24:13–35 and note how Jesus read the Old Testament.

3. Ask: have I been collecting facts about God or coming to Him?

4. Take one passage you know well and look for Christ in it.

5. **Hear Him, and keep faith and wisdom together.** Listen with an open heart and a settled text. The voice of the Son will always agree with the words He breathed.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Colossians 1:15–20.** Note how much is said about who He is.
- **Read Matthew 17:1–8.** Note the Father's single instruction.
- **Read one Old Testament chapter looking for Jesus.**
- **Obey the last thing He clearly told you.**
- **Notice whether your study ends in a note or in a knee.**
