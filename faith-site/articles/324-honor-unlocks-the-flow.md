Matthew 13:58 is one of the most disturbing verses in the Gospels, and it is stated so briefly that it is easy to read past.

"And he did not many mighty works there because of their unbelief."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **in Nazareth the obstacle was not a lack of power but a lack of honour — and since honour is the posture that lets something flow from one person to another, its absence does not merely grieve God, it blocks the channel.**

## The Scene

### Familiarity

And the townspeople's objection is not theological. It is biographical: "Is not this the carpenter's son? is not his mother called Mary?" (Matthew 13:55).

They knew the family. They had bought furniture from him.

**Familiarity is the soil in which contempt grows best.**

### The Proverb

And Jesus names the mechanism: "A prophet is not without honour, save in his own country, and in his own house" (Matthew 13:57).

The problem is not the prophet's gifting. It is the town's assessment.

**Their estimate of him set the ceiling of the room.** And a low ceiling is still a ceiling.

### The Word

And Mark's version adds a detail Matthew omits: "And he could there do no mighty work" (Mark 6:5). *Ouk edynato* — he was not able.

That is a startling thing to say about omnipotence, which is why Matthew softened it to "he did not."

**Whatever else this means, it means honour is not optional equipment.**

## Why Honour Opens

### Because It Is a Posture of Receiving

And honour is simply the position from which something can be handed to you. A person who has already decided you have nothing to give cannot receive from you.

The channel is the estimate.

**You cannot receive from somebody you have already appraised.**

### Because It Positions You

And Elisha gives the clearest picture in Scripture. He poured water on the hands of Elijah (2 Kings 3:11) — a servant's task, done for years, before he asked for a double portion.

Honour preceded inheritance.

**The man who served the anointing received the anointing.**

### Because Honour Is Not the Same as Agreement

And there is a distinction that keeps this doctrine healthy. You can honour somebody you disagree with, and you can dishonour somebody you obey.

Honour is about the worth of the person, not the accuracy of every opinion they hold. Which is why Jesus could honour the synagogue ruler and still heal on the day he expected otherwise.

**Contempt is the thing that closes the channel. Disagreement does not have to.**

### Because God Resists the Alternative

And the reason is not merely mechanical. God resists the proud (James 4:6), and contempt is pride wearing work clothes.

**A room full of appraisals is a room where grace has nothing to work with.**

### Where Honour Is Due

### In the House First

And the proverb's second clause is the uncomfortable one: *and in his own house*. The nearest people are the hardest to honour, and they are the first test.

A man's home is where his real theology of honour is visible.

Nobody's honour is proven in an auditorium.

### The Ones Who Carry the Word

And Paul says the elders who labour in the word are worthy of double honour (1 Timothy 5:17). Not because they are impressive, but because of what they carry.

The honour is attached to the message, not the messenger's personality.

What you think of the vessel is what you think of the supply.

### The Ordinary Contributors

And the kingdom has a long list of people whose contribution looked small: the boy with loaves, the servant girl who mentioned a prophet, the widow with two mites.

Honour is not reserved for the impressive.

A channel can be narrow and still carry water.

### Practising Honour

### Watch Your Tone

And the first discipline is speech. Honour is not a claim; it is a register — how you talk about somebody when they are not in the room.

If the tone is contemptuous, the channel is closed.

You can obey somebody and still be closed to them.

### Give It Where It Costs

And the second is deliberately honouring those who cannot return the favour. That is the only version of honour that tests the heart.

Everyone honours a person who might promote them.

Honour that expects a return is a business transaction.

### Honour Without Flattery

And the third is the guard. Honour tells the truth about a person's worth; flattery tells a person what they want to hear.

One opens a channel, the other opens a bar tab.

Flattery is honour that has been for sale.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, honour is not obedience to abuse. Scripture honours parents and also requires a person to leave and cleave, and Jesus' own family thought Him mad. Honouring somebody does not require letting them harm you.

Second, honour does not make anybody infallible. You can honour a teacher and still test what they say (Acts 17:11).

**The **The Anointing is what the channel carries; Kingdom Stewardship is honouring the assignment somebody has been given; and The Presence of God is what flows once the channel is open.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Matthew 13:53–58 and Mark 6:1–6 together.

**2. Name the person you have grown familiar with.**

**3. Change your tone about them this week.**

**4. Honour someone who cannot repay you.**

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Honour is not credulity. **The Anointing** is worth receiving; wisdom still tests what is taught and refuses what is harmful.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 2 Kings 2:1–15.**
- **Read Ephesians 6:1–4.**
- **Write down three people you have undervalued.**
- **Say one true, honouring thing to each.**
- **Watch what opens.**
