There is a reason so many believers pray with a nagging uncertainty about whether they were allowed to ask for what they asked for.

"And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us" (1 John 5:14).

The verse names a condition, and the condition is the part nobody explains.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the Word does not merely tell you what to believe about prayer; it supplies the will of God you are meant to be praying, which is why praying Scripture is not a formula but a discovery.**

## The Condition and the Confidence

### "According to His Will" Is Not a Wall

Most people hear the phrase as a fence, and a fence that keeps them out. *If it happens to be His will, maybe.*

But read the grammar. John does not say "if we ask something vague enough to be safe." He says *kata to thelēma autou* — according to His will, in line with it. That is a description of alignment, not of uncertainty.

And the confidence is the point of the sentence. John is telling you how to have *parrēsia* — boldness, free speech, the confidence of a citizen who has a right to be heard. Confession uses the same noun for the boldness of a child before a father.

**A fence does not produce boldness. A map does.** That is what the will of God is in prayer: not the wall that keeps you out, but the map that shows you where the door is.

### The Chain in the Verses

Follow John's logic and it is tighter than most prayers we pray.

If we ask according to His will → He hears us. Present tense, no delay in the hearing.

And if we know that He hears us → we know we have the petitions we desired. Note the tense of "have": *echomen*, present. Not "will receive." Have.

So there are three links: ask, hear, have. And the whole chain hangs on the first one, which means the question every believer eventually faces is not "will God answer?" but "am I asking for something He has already promised?"

### Where the Will of God Is Found

And this is where the article turns, because "according to His will" is useless as an instruction unless the will is discoverable.

It is discoverable in one place: the Word. Not in a feeling, not in a coincidence, not in the intensity of your wanting.

Paul says Scripture is profitable for doctrine, reproof, correction, instruction (2 Timothy 3:16). He says the whole counsel of God was declared (Acts 20:27). He says the things written were written for our learning, "that we through patience and comfort of the scriptures might have hope" (Romans 15:4).

So when you pray the Word, you are not using a technique on God. **You are praying a request whose condition has already been met.**

### Isaiah's Guarantee

Isaiah gives the strongest statement of this in the Old Testament, and it is about the Word's own efficacy.

"For as the rain cometh down, and the snow from heaven, and returneth not thither, but watereth the earth... so shall my word be that goeth forth out of my mouth: it shall not return unto me void, but it shall accomplish that which I please, and it shall prosper in the thing whereto I sent it" (Isaiah 55:10–11).

Read what the Word is compared to. Rain. It does not negotiate with the ground, and it does not ask permission. It comes down and the ground responds.

**Which means the Word of God is not merely a statement of what God would like. It is a statement of what He is doing.**

And the immediately preceding verse is the one we like to quote separately: "For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways" (Isaiah 55:8). We quote it to explain why prayer is mysterious. In context, it is the reason we need the Word spoken to us: because our natural thinking is not going to arrive there by itself.

### Jeremiah's Almond Tree

There is a picture that changes how you read a promise, and it is a pun in Hebrew.

Jeremiah was shown a rod of an almond tree, and the LORD asked him, "What seest thou? And I said, I see a rod of an almond tree. Then said the LORD unto me, Thou hast well seen: for I will hasten my word to perform it" (Jeremiah 1:11–12).

The Hebrew word for almond is *šāqēd*, and the word for watching or hastening is *šōqēd*. The same consonants. The almond tree was the watcher's tree — the first to wake in the season.

**God said: what you are looking at is a picture of what I am doing with everything I have said. I am watching over it to perform it.**

That is what you are standing on when you pray a Scripture. Not a hope that God might act on it. An activity already in progress.

### Ezekiel, and the Order of Prayer

And then the Old Testament adds the other half, which keeps this from becoming mechanical.

Ezekiel records God promising restoration — gathering, cleansing, rebuilding the waste cities — and then says: "Thus saith the Lord GOD; I will yet for this be enquired of by the house of Israel, to do it for them" (Ezekiel 36:37).

Look at that. God declares the thing, and then says He will be asked for it.

**The promise does not make prayer unnecessary; it makes prayer possible.** What happens when you pray a promise is not that you are reminding God of something He forgot. It is that He has chosen to do the declared thing in response to the asking.

### The Blueprint

One illustration, and it is the most useful way I know to hold this.

A builder does not arrive at a site with a wish list. He arrives with approved drawings. He can build with confidence not because he is optimistic but because the drawings have been signed.

And here is the part worth noticing: the drawings also mean he cannot build whatever he likes. A wall where the client wanted a window is not boldness; it is a different building.

Praying Scripture is building from approved drawings. You have the document, and you have the freedom to build — within the shape of what was drawn.

### How to Pray the Word

So the practice, concretely.

Take a promise you need — not a verse you like, but one that addresses your actual situation — and read it until you know what it is claiming. Then ask for that, in your own words, naming your situation inside it.

"Lord, You said the prayer of faith shall save the sick. This is my brother's name. You said Your word would not return void. Here is the word; here is the need."

That is not a magic formula, and it is not repetition for its own sake. It is what **Confession of the Word** actually means — *homologia*, saying the same thing God says — applied to your asking rather than only your identity.

And when a request cannot be tied to a promise, be honest about it. "Lord, I want this, and I do not know whether it is Your will. Your will be done" is a better prayer than a confident claim you are not sure of. **The Life of Faith is not certainty about your own desires; it is trust in His character when the map has a blank space on it.**

### A Word on the "Ask What Ye Will"

John 15:7 is often quoted as a blank cheque, and it is not one: "If ye abide in me, and my words abide in you, ye shall ask what ye will, and it shall be done unto you."

Look at what has to be true first. His words abiding in you. That is not a condition you satisfy to unlock the promise; it is the process by which your wanting becomes His wanting.

**Abiding does not give you a licence to want anything. It changes what you want.** That is a better gift than a blank cheque, and it is the one He offered.

## What This Means for You

**1. Find the promise before you find the words.** Most weak praying is not weak in delivery; it is weak in foundation. Locate the verse before you locate the language.

**2. Pray with the text open.** This is not immature. It is the difference between a conversation and an improvisation.

**3. Let the Word examine the request, not the reverse.** If a verse contradicts what you are asking, the verse wins. That is what makes the confidence possible later.

**4. Watch for the blank spaces.** Where the Word is silent on your particular question, pray honestly rather than presumptuously, and let faith and wisdom work together: keep the promise in your mouth while you keep the appointment, the budget, and the ordinary diligence the promise does not cancel. God is not offended by "if it be Your will" spoken sincerely; Jesus prayed it in Gethsemane.

**5. Keep it in your mouth, not your notebook.** There is a difference between a verse you have underlined and a verse you have said.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Write one promise beside one need**, and pray them together each morning.
- **Read Isaiah 55:8–11** and mark what the Word is compared to.
- **Read Jeremiah 1:11–12** and look up "almond" in a study note.
- **Pray one psalm aloud** as your own words this week.
