Two New Testament sentences seem to pull in opposite directions, and the whole doctrine of sanctification lives in how you hold them together.

Hebrews says the worshippers were "once purged" and "should have had no more conscience of sins" (Hebrews 10:2). John says the blood "cleanseth us from all sin" — present tense, continuous (1 John 1:7).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the cleansing was accomplished once and is applied continuously — a finished act with an ongoing effect, which is why a believer never needs to be re-saved and never stops being washed.**

## The Finished Act

### "Once Purged"

The Greek in Hebrews 10:2 is *katharizō* in the perfect — a completed action whose result stands.

That is not a process. It is a state entered — so why do we keep trying to enter it again?

**The bath is not a habit. It happened.**

### Not Repeatable

And Hebrews insists on this: "For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified" (Hebrews 10:14).

Any system that requires the sacrifice again has, by definition, not accepted that it worked.

**A finished work is not renewed. It is relied upon.**

### What It Settled

The finished act settled your *position* — how God regards you, what your record now says, what your standing is before the throne.

That is the part nobody can touch, including you.

**The cross dealt with the record. Your feelings are not the record.**

## The Continuous Application

### "Cleanseth" — Present Tense

And John's verb is present active — it keeps happening. Not "cleansed" but "cleanseth."

So there is a daily application of a once-for-all reality. The bath is finished; the feet still get washed.

**The reservoir is full. The tap still runs.**

### The Foot-Washing

And Jesus made the distinction explicit in the upper room. Peter asked for a whole bath; Jesus answered, "He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit" (John 13:10).

The bath happened when you came to Christ. The feet get dirty walking through the world — and which of the two is your actual problem today?

**You are not re-baptised into cleanness every day. You are washed where the road touched you.**

### The Word as the Water

And the instrument is named: "That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word" (Ephesians 5:26).

The daily cleansing is not a new sacrifice. It is the same finished work applied by the Word.

**Scripture is the towel.**

### Why Both Are Necessary

### Without the Once, the Continuous Becomes Earning

If the finished act were not finished, the daily washing would be a payment plan — and you would spend your life trying to keep up.

That is precisely the treadmill this month has been dismantling.

A continuous cleansing without a once-for-all bath is just religion doing laundry.

### Without the Continuous, the Once Becomes Excuse

And if the daily application did not exist, one could claim the bath while never dealing with what the road does to the feet.

That is the licence error, and John answers it two verses later: "If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves" (1 John 1:8).

The bath is real. The feet still need washing. Both sentences are true.

### The Harmony

The finished work gives the daily work its meaning, and the daily work keeps the finished work in view.

One is the foundation, the other is the maintenance — and no building is maintained that was never founded.

### Walking in the Light

### The Condition in Verse 7

And note the setting of John's promise: *if we walk in the light*.

The cleansing is not conditional on perfection. It is conditional on exposure.

Walking in the light means living where you can be seen — confessing, not concealing.

The blood cleans what the light reveals, which is why hiding stops the process rather than the verdict.

### Confession as Cooperation

And the next verse makes it practical: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive" (1 John 1:9).

*Just* is the strange word. Forgiveness on the basis of justice, because the price was paid.

Confession is not informing God. It is agreeing with Him so the washing can reach the place you were protecting.

### The Advocate

And if the failure is serious enough to feel unforgivable, John has already answered: "we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous" (1 John 2:1).

Not a prosecutor. An advocate — and the same Person whose blood is doing the cleansing.

The defence attorney is the one who paid the settlement.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, continuous cleansing is not continuous atonement. Christ is not re-crucified; His blood is not re-shed. What continues is the application, not the sacrifice.

Second, the daily washing is not a licence to accumulate dirt. Grace does not make sin cheaper; it makes honesty possible.

**The **The Blood of Jesus** is the reservoir and the **The Word of God** is the tap; the believer lives between a finished fact and a daily washing, and neither cancels the other.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Stop trying to re-bathe.** The bath happened. Deal with the feet.

**2. Confess specifically, today.** What the light shows, the blood cleans.

**3. Read 1 John 1:5–2:2 every day this week.**

**4. Let the Word wash you.** Read something longer than a verse.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Cleansing is not passivity. **The Word of God** does the washing as you stay in it, and wisdom means staying in it deliberately rather than accidentally.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read John 13:1–11** and note the distinction Jesus makes.
- **Read Hebrews 10:1–14** and mark every *once* and *for ever*.
- **Confess one thing you have been concealing.**
- **Read Ephesians 5:25–27** and mark the instrument.
- **Thank God for both cleansings** — the bath and the feet.
