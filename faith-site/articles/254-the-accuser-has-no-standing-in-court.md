Zechariah 3 is the clearest courtroom scene in the Old Testament, and its staging carries the whole point.

Joshua the high priest stands before the angel of the LORD. Satan stands "at his right hand to resist him" (Zechariah 3:1).

Read the geography slowly, because it is the argument.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **in the courtroom of Zechariah 3, the accuser is standing at the defendant's right hand — not on the bench — and a prosecutor without a bench has no power to sentence anyone.**

## The Staging

### Where Each Party Stands

Joshua stands before the LORD. Satan stands beside Joshua. The presiding figure is neither one of them.

The accuser occupies the same position we would call the prosecution's table.

**He is a party to the case, not the court.**

### What "To Resist" Means

The Hebrew is *śāṭan* — the very word that became his name: to oppose, to be an adversary.

His function here is not temptation. It is opposition in a legal setting.

**He is not the tempter in this chapter. He is the one with a file.**

### The Filthy Garments

And the file was not empty: Joshua was "clothed with filthy garments" (Zechariah 3:3).

The accusation was accurate. That is what makes the next verse so important.

## The Rebuke

### Before Joshua Says a Word

"And the LORD said unto Satan, The LORD rebuke thee, O Satan; even the LORD that hath chosen Jerusalem rebuke thee: is not this a brand plucked out of the fire?" (Zechariah 3:2).

The rebuke comes first, and it comes from the bench.

Joshua has offered no defence, no explanation, and no change of clothes. He has not spoken at all.

**The verdict was not based on the defendant's argument, and it was not delayed for one.**

### The Ground of the Rebuke

And notice the reason given: *the LORD that hath chosen Jerusalem*. Not "because he repented." Not "because the garments were not that bad."

The ground is election. God's own choice.

**The case was decided on the character of the judge, not the record of the accused.**

### The Brand From the Fire

The image that closes verse 2 is the strongest: a stick pulled from a fire is not a trophy. It is evidence of rescue, and it is still smoking.

**A rescued person is God's argument against the accuser. The brand proves the rescuer showed up.**

### The New Clothes

### Ordered, Not Earned

Then comes the command: "Take away the filthy garments from him" (Zechariah 3:4).

The change of clothing is ordered by the court. Joshua does not wash them, mend them, or ask for better ones.

Righteousness was given to a man who was still wearing the evidence against him.

### The Exchange

And the angel adds: "Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment."

Then a fair mitre on his head — the sign of the priestly office, restored.

He was not merely cleaned up. He was given a status he had forfeited.

### Why the Order Matters

Accuser rebuked, *then* garments changed.

If the clothes came first, we would conclude the rebuke was earned. The order proves grace.

God does not clean you up to win the case. He wins the case and then cleans you up.

### The Courtroom Now

### The Same Charge, A Better Answer

Revelation calls him "the accuser of our brethren... which accused them before our God day and night" (Revelation 12:10).

Same activity, same opponent, millennia later. But the answer has changed entirely.

"Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect? It is God that justifieth" (Romans 8:33).

The charge is still filed. It is simply not received.

### What the Accuser Can Still Do

Be clear about his limits. He cannot condemn, cannot remove your standing, cannot alter your inheritance.

He can speak. And he can be believed — which is the whole of his strategy.

His only power is persuasive, which is why the truth makes you free rather than merely safe.

### What He Cannot Do

He cannot approach the bench. He has no verdicts, no sentences, no authority to bind.

The binding he does is only ever the kind a liar does to someone who has not checked the facts.

A prosecutor who has been barred from the building can still send letters. You do not have to answer the post.

### Standing Firm

### Know the Court You Are In

The first defence is orientation. When accusation comes, ask which room you are in — the defendant's dock or the family dining room.

### Resist, Do Not Debate

James's instruction is crisp: "Resist the devil, and he will flee from you" (James 4:7).

*Resist* is a military word, *anthistēmi*, to stand against. Not to negotiate.

### Submit First

And the same verse begins with the order that makes resistance possible: "Submit yourselves therefore to God."

**You cannot resist a prosecutor while doubting the judge.**

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, spiritual warfare is not obsession. Some believers give the enemy more attention than the gospel. Zechariah gives him one verse.

Second, the accuser is real. Dismissing him entirely means dismissing the scriptures that name him. The right posture is unmoved attention, not denial.

**The **Spiritual Warfare** we wage is not against flesh — and much of it is simply refusing to argue with a voice that has no jurisdiction.**

## What This Means for You

**1. Locate the voice.** Ask: bench or dock?

**2. Stop building a defence.** He is not the judge, so your defence is not for him.

**3. Learn the rebuke.** "The Lord rebuke thee" is a complete answer.

**4. Read Zechariah 3 as a story about your standing.**

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** Faith stands; wisdom measures where you actually stand. Your **Legal Position** is in Christ, and **The Name of Jesus** is the authority in which a believer stands, not a phrase to shout.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Zechariah 3:1–5 five times this week.**
- **Draw the courtroom** — who stands where.
- **Write down the last accusation you believed.**
- **Answer it with Romans 8:33.**
- **Read James 4:7–8** and do both halves.
