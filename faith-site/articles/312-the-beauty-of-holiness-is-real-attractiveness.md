Psalm 96:9 is one of the most mistranslated lines in the Psalms, and the mistranslation has cost the church more than it knows.

"O worship the LORD in the beauty of holiness: fear before him, all the earth."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the Hebrew behind "the beauty of holiness" is a word for adornment and splendour — which means holiness was never the enemy of attraction, and the reason so much of it looks unattractive is that we have been imitating a rule instead of wearing a garment.**

## The Word

### Hadar

And the Hebrew is *hadrat qōdesh* — the *hadar* of holiness. And *hadar* does not mean "prettiness." It means splendour, majesty, adornment — the word used of a king's regalia and a bride's array.

It is the word for something that makes people stop.

**The psalm does not say holiness is attractive. It says holiness is adorned.** So what would it look like to wear it?

### The Alternative Reading

And the common reading treats the phrase as an instruction about manner: worship in a holy way, which usually means quietly, soberly, and in dark clothing.

The Hebrew is not a mood. It is a garment.

**God was never asking for a funeral face. He was describing a wedding dress.** And a wedding dress is not a mood.

### Where the Adornment Comes From

And Psalm 29:1–2 gives the parallel: "Give unto the LORD the glory due unto his name; worship the LORD in the beauty of holiness."

The glory being given is *His*. The adornment being worn is *His*. Not a self-manufactured solemnity.

**You do not dress yourself in holiness. You are dressed in His.**

## Why Holiness Looks Unattractive

### Because It Became a List

And the first reason is reduction. Holiness was turned into a set of prohibitions — a list of things not to do, wear, watch, or say.

A list can be obeyed and still leave a person thoroughly unattractive, because a list has no radiance.

**You can keep every rule and possess no *hadar*.** And who taught you that the two were the same?

### Because It Became a Performance

And the second is strain. When holiness is a performance, the performer is always anxious, and anxiety is not beautiful.

It produces the pinched face and the brittle tone that have made the word "holy" sound like a warning.

**Strain is the surest way to make something good look bad.**

### Because Grace Was Left Out

And the third, deeper reason is that we taught holiness without the cross. If the standard is the price of acceptance, the person carrying it is always afraid.

But the gospel says the acceptance came first, and the character grows out of it.

**A person who is already loved can afford to become lovely.**

### The Attractiveness Itself

### Zechariah's Bells

And Zechariah gives the startling picture of what holiness actually looks like in a life: "In that day shall there be upon the bells of the horses, HOLINESS UNTO THE LORD" (Zechariah 14:20).

Not on the priest's turban only. On the horse's bridle. On the kitchen pots.

**Holiness that has become ordinary is holiness that has become beautiful.**

### The Attractiveness Is Moral

And the beauty is not cosmetic. It is the attractiveness of a person who tells the truth, keeps their word, does not flatter, and can be trusted in a crisis.

Nobody finds that ugly. They may be made uncomfortable by it, which is a different response entirely.

**The world calls it conviction and resents it in public, and looks for it in private.**

### It Is Also Joyful

And Psalm 96 is not a quiet psalm. It is a shout: "O sing unto the LORD a new song" (Psalm 96:1). It has *hadar* because it has delight.

Joy is the finish on the garment.

### Wearing It

### Receive Before You Wear

And the first discipline is to stop trying to generate holiness and start receiving it. The garment is given before it is worn.

Which is why the New Testament calls it clothing: "put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ" (Romans 13:14).

You cannot tailor what you have already been given.

### Worship as the Wardrobe

And the second is that worship is where the dressing happens. Psalm 96:9 is, after all, an instruction about worship.

The person who spends time in His presence begins to look like the room they have been in.

Adoration is not a mood. It is a change of clothing.

### Let It Reach the Ordinary

And the third is Zechariah's pots. The test of *hadar* is not the sanctuary; it is the kitchen, the car, the group chat, and the way you speak to someone who cannot help you.

A holiness that only appears in public is a costume, and costumes come off.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a call to make holiness fashionable. The point is not that godliness needs a rebrand; it is that the real thing has always been more attractive than the counterfeit.

Second, this is not permission for worldliness. Attractiveness is not the measure — *hadar* comes from Him, not from the culture's approval.

**The **Righteousness is what makes the garment; The Consciousness of Righteousness is knowing you are wearing it; and The Divine Nature is what the fabric is made of.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Psalm 96 aloud, three times.

2. Write the word *hadar* on something you see daily.

3. Find one rule you keep out of fear and give it back to grace.

4. Ask someone who knows you whether your holiness is attractive or merely correct.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** A lovely character still needs to be lived with skill. **The Divine Nature** is the garment; wisdom knows how to wear it in rooms where it will cost you something.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read 1 Chronicles 16:23–34.**
- **Read Zechariah 14:16–21.**
- **Worship for ten minutes every morning.**
- **Take one prohibition off your list.**
- **Do one beautiful, hidden thing** for someone who will never repay you.
