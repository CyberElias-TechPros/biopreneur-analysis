John begins his Gospel in eternity and arrives, within fourteen verses, in a body — and the sentence he uses to make that journey is the one we are studying.

"And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth" (John 1:14).

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **the Word did not visit flesh; He became it — and the verb John uses for dwelling is the word for pitching a tent, which means the Incarnation was not a brief appearance but God moving into the neighbourhood of human weakness permanently.**

## The Verb

### Eskēnōsen

And the word is *eskēnōsen* — He tabernacled, He pitched a tent. From *skēnē*, a tent.

John is deliberately reaching back to the wilderness, where the glory of God settled over a portable tent among a travelling people.

**He did not build a palace and summon us. He pitched a tent and stayed.** So why do we keep looking for Him in the palace?

### Among Us

And the preposition is *en hēmin* — in us, among us. Not near, not over, not above.

The tent was in the middle of the camp.

**The glory chose the address of the ordinary.** Would you have looked for it there?

### We Beheld

And John adds a personal witness: *etheasametha* — we gazed, we beheld as spectators of something remarkable. The same verb family as "beheld his glory" in the same Gospel's account of the cross.

The glory is not a vague radiance. It is a person you can watch.

**What John beheld on the mountain and in the garden was the same glory.** And one of them was a crucifixion.

## The Shock of It

### Flesh

And *sarx* is not a polite word. It is the vulnerable, capable-of-hunger-and-injury word for a living body.

John could have said He became a man. He said flesh.

**The eternal Word became the kind of thing that can be wounded.**

### Not an Appearance

And the church's oldest battle was over exactly this. John later writes that many deceivers would not confess Jesus Christ *coming in the flesh* (2 John 7).

The body was not a costume.

**A ghost cannot bleed, and the plan required blood.**

### Not a Compromise

And the opposite error is to think the Word stopped being God in the process. John has already said "the Word was God" (John 1:1), and he does not take it back.

Fully God and fully flesh, in one person, at one address.

**The tent did not reduce the glory. It made it visible.**

### Why It Matters

### Because God Is Now Knowable

And John says the Word has declared the Father (John 1:18) — exegeted Him. The invisible God has an explanation now, and the explanation has a face.

**If you have seen Him, you have seen the Father.**

### Because He Is Now Touchable

And John's letter opens with hands: "which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life" (1 John 1:1).

Healing has a hand. Comfort has a shoulder. The Gospel is not a philosophy transmitted through a gap.

**Christianity begins with a body you could touch.**

### Because He Is Now Approachable

And Hebrews draws the conclusion: we do not have a high priest who cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities (Hebrews 4:15).

The Incarnation is the reason He can be sympathetic rather than merely informed.

He does not pity us from an office. He knows what a Tuesday costs.

### Living Near Him

### Take the Tent Seriously

And the first practice is to stop treating the Incarnation as a doctrine for December. The God who pitched a tent is not far off in June.

He is the God of the kitchen, not only of the sanctuary.

The whole point of pitching a tent is that it is where the people already are.

### Bring Him the Body Stuff

And the second is to stop dividing your life into the spiritual and the physical. He sanctified flesh by wearing it.

Sleep, work, sickness, and hunger are all now territory He has stood on.

Nothing human is foreign to a God who took a body.

### Be a Tent

And the third is the startling extension: Paul says the same indwelling continues in a believer's body (1 Corinthians 6:19). The tabernacling did not end at the Ascension.

You are the address now.

The Incarnation was the pattern. The indwelling is the programme.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a denial of His deity. The same verse calls Him the only begotten of the Father; the tent does not dilute the throne.

Second, this is not a claim that every ache is a message. The body is a gift and a servant, not an oracle.

**The **The Presence of God is the thing that moved in; The Finished Work is what the body came to accomplish; and The Divine Nature is what He imparts to the flesh He now inhabits.

## What This Means for You

1. Read John 1:1–18 slowly, once a day this week.

2. Note every verb of nearness in the passage.

3. Bring one ordinary bodily thing to Him today.

4. Ask Him to be as real as the furniture.

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** The nearness of Christ does not make medicine unnecessary. **The Presence of God** is a person; wisdom still uses the means He made, from sleep to treatment.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Philippians 2:1–11.**
- **Read Hebrews 2:9–18.**
- **Write out John 1:14 by hand.**
- **Say "God moved in" on a hard morning.**
- **Do one ordinary thing as an act of worship.**
