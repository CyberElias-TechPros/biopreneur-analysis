Ezekiel 22 is one of the darkest chapters in the prophets, and it ends with a sentence that should be read slowly.

"And I sought for a man among them, that should make up the hedge, and stand in the gap before me for the land, that I should not destroy it: but I found none" (Ezekiel 22:30).

God went looking. He found no one.

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **intercession is not asking God to be more merciful than He is; it is standing, at cost, in the place where mercy and judgement meet — and God goes looking for people willing to do it.**

## The Image

A gap in a hedge is what happens when walls are left unrepaired. In ancient cities, a breach in the wall was the place where the city would be entered.

So the man who stands in the gap is not doing something decorative. He is taking the most dangerous position in the city.

**The Inheritance of the Believer** — what is already secured for the family of God — is what an intercessor is asking to see extended. **Communion With the Holy Spirit** — the fellowship in which the intercessor prays — is the source of the burden. **The Life of Faith** — sustained by persistence — is what keeps a person in the breach.

Consider a man who notices his neighbour's roof is failing in the rain, and if the only thing he does is mention it over dinner, then he has observed the gap rather than standing in it.

### Ezekiel's Context

The chapter before this one is a catalogue. Prophets have "devoured souls" for money (Ezekiel 22:25). Priests have "put no difference between the holy and profane" (Ezekiel 22:26). Princes have "shed blood" (Ezekiel 22:27). The people have used oppression and vexed the poor (Ezekiel 22:29).

Every layer of leadership has failed. And then God looks for one man — not one institution, not one reform programme — who will stand in the gap.

The search comes after the diagnosis, and the diagnosis explains why the gap existed.

### The Two Other Intercessors

Scripture gives examples of people who did what Ezekiel described.

**Abraham** haggled over Sodom, six times, in one of the boldest prayers in the Bible: "Peradventure there be fifty righteous within the city" (Genesis 18:24). He interceded for a city that was not his, for people he did not know.

**Moses** stood in an even sharper situation. God said to him, "Now therefore let me alone, that my wrath may wax hot against them, and that I may consume them: and I will make of thee a great nation" (Exodus 32:10). And Moses refused the offer to be the new beginning, and went down to plead for people who were even then worshipping a calf.

Note what Abraham and Moses had in common: they were willing to be inconvenienced and identified with the people they prayed for.

### Intercession Identifies

That is the key. An intercessor does not pray from a distance; he takes his place with the people he is praying for.

Moses says, "Yet now, if thou wilt forgive their sin — and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book which thou hast written" (Exodus 32:32). Paul says something similar about his own nation: "I could wish that myself were accursed from Christ for my brethren" (Romans 9:3).

That is the posture. It is not moderate. It is not the tone of a person making a reasonable suggestion.

### What Intercession Is Not

Three boundaries are needed, because this teaching has been abused.

**It is not a technique that overrides God's will.** Intercession is agreeing with God's mercy, not pressuring Him into a decision. Moses's prayer is answered because God's own character was the argument: "Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, thy servants" (Exodus 32:13).

**It is not a burden you carry alone.** Ezekiel says "a man." But the New Testament makes intercession mutual — "Pray one for another, that ye may be healed" (James 5:16) — and never suggests one intercessor carries a whole community's weight.

**It is not a substitute for acting.** Isaiah's parallel passage includes both: "And he saw that there was no man, and wondered that there was no intercessor: therefore his arm brought salvation unto him" (Isaiah 59:16). The arm of God and the intercessor are described together, not as alternatives.

### Who Takes the Position Now

Hebrews makes the foundational claim: "Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them" (Hebrews 7:25).

And Romans 8 adds the Spirit: "the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered" (Romans 8:26).

So the deepest intercession is already happening — Christ at the right hand, and the Spirit within. Your intercession joins something rather than starting it.

That reframes the burden. You are not the only one standing in the gap; you are standing where the gap has already been filled at its deepest level.

### How to Stand

Practically, intercession has a shape.

**Name people.** Not "bless everyone." Names, one by one.

**Pray with their interest in mind.** Not your preferences about them.

**Stay with it.** The gap does not close over a week.

**Let the burden be shared.** Ask others to pray with you.

**Watch for the action it requires.** The intercessor may be the one sent. Have you asked where yours might be?

### The Honest Boundary

One word of care, because intercessors are often the ones who burn out.

A person who prays for everybody and rests for nobody is not more spiritual than others. He is out of order. Scripture never presents intercession as a burden that consumes the intercessor's own life with God.

And if you have carried someone for years and seen nothing, that is a grief, not a failure of your faith. The "I found none" in Ezekiel is God's lament, not a bill handed to you.

## What This Means for You

**1. Find your gap.** What breach are you positioned near — a family, a workplace, a street?

**2. Pray by name.** Intercession is specific or it is not intercession.

**3. Share the weight.** Invite two people into the prayer rather than carrying it alone.

**4. Be ready to be sent.** Standing in the gap often ends in an errand.

**5. Let Faith and Wisdom work together here.** Faith says the burden for others is from God and worth bearing. Wisdom says that means a sustainable rhythm — boundaries, rest, and honest limits — because the God who searches for an intercessor is not asking for a casualty.

## Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Ezekiel 22:23–31** and note the failures listed before the search.
- **Read Genesis 18:20–33**, and count how many times Abraham asks.
- **Choose three people** and pray for them by name daily this week.
- **Ask one friend** to stand with you in it.
