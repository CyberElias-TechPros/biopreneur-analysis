Luke 10:42 is one sentence, spoken to a frustrated hostess, and it has been quoted badly for centuries.

"But one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her."

Here is the central truth of this teaching: **Mary's seat at His feet was not a withdrawal from work but a position of government over it — and Jesus called it the one necessary thing, which means the presence is not the reward for finishing your tasks but the seat from which they get ordered.**

## The Scene

### Martha's Complaint

And Martha's grievance is completely reasonable on its face: "Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone?" (Luke 10:40).

She is doing the work. Somebody has to feed the guests.

**Every burned-out servant has prayed that exact prayer.** So what makes it a complaint instead of a request?

### The Word for Cumbered

And Luke's description is precise: *periespato* — she was drawn about, distracted, pulled in several directions at once. And *peri pollēn diakonian* — about much serving.

The problem is not the serving. It is the *much*, and the fragmentation.

**The issue was never the kitchen. It was the scattering.** And a scattered servant is a tired servant.

### The Rebuke

And Jesus' answer is tender and unyielding at once: "Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things" (Luke 10:41).

Two names, the way you say a name to somebody you love who is spiralling.

**He did not rebuke the work. He named the anxiety.**

## The Seat

### Sitting, Not Standing

And Mary was seated — *parakathizomenē*, sitting beside, at His feet, in the posture of a disciple.

In that culture, that was the posture of a learner, not a loiterer.

**She was not avoiding chores. She had taken a seat in the school.**

### The Good Part

And Jesus calls it *tēn agathēn merida* — the good portion. *Meris* is a share, an allotment, the word used for dividing an inheritance.

Which reframes the whole argument. Martha thought the issue was labour distribution. Jesus framed it as inheritance.

**One sister was serving dinner. The other had taken her portion.**

### Not Taken Away

And the promise is that this part is permanent: *ouk apheraithēsetai* — it shall not be removed.

The dinner would be eaten and forgotten. The seat would remain.

**A meal ends. A portion does not.**

### Why It Is a Seat of Government

### Because Order Comes From There

And the practical claim of this teaching is that decisions made from the presence are different from decisions made from the panic.

Mary would still have had to cook. She would have cooked as somebody who had listened first.

**The presence does not remove the task. It reorders the person doing it.**

### Because Identity Is Settled There

And a person who has sat at His feet knows whose they are before they know what they are producing.

That is the difference between service and self-validation.

Martha was serving to be seen. Mary was sitting to be known.

### Because the Household Is Ordered There

And there is a detail worth noticing: Jesus did not tell Martha to stop serving. He told her she was troubled about many things.

The seat does not subtract the tasks. It sorts them, so that the many become the few that actually matter today.

Most of our exhaustion is not from too much work but from work that was never ordered.

### Because Strength Is Distributed There

And the same Mary who sat here is the one who later anointed His feet before the cross (John 12:3), at a moment when the others were calculating.

The seat produces the courage for the harder rooms.

Time at His feet is what makes a person able to stand later.

### Taking the Seat

### First, Not Last

And the first practice is order. The seat goes at the beginning of the day, not at the end of the list when everything else has failed.

Whatever gets your first hour is governing your life.

A presence taken last is a presence taken as a leftover.

### Sit Without Producing

And the second is the harder discipline: sitting without an agenda, a request, or a notepad. Mary was listening, not negotiating.

Relationship requires unproductive time.

You cannot be friends with somebody you only ever petition.

### Refuse the Comparison

And the third is Martha's actual sin, which was not the cooking. It was the measuring.

"Lord, dost thou not care that my sister..." is the sound of a person comparing portions.

Comparison is what turns a gift into a grievance.

### The Boundary

Two guardrails.

First, this is not a defence of laziness. Jesus ate the meal; somebody cooked it. Martha's service was real and needed.

Second, this is not a rule about posture. The seat is a relationship, not a chair; it can be taken in a car, a queue, or a night shift.

**The **The Presence of God** is the seat; **Communion With the Holy Spirit is the conversation held there; and The Life of the Spirit is what the sitting produces in the rest of the day.

## What This Means for You

1. Read Luke 10:38–42 three times.

**2. Identify your "much serving."**

**3. Decide what gets your first twenty minutes.**

**4. Sit once this week with no agenda.**

**5. Keep faith and wisdom together.** The seat does not cancel the schedule. **The Presence of God** is the priority; wisdom still plans the day, and a plan made from the seat is a better plan.

### Putting It to Work This Week

- **Read Psalm 27:1–6.**
- **Read John 15:1–11.**
- **Move one thing earlier in your day** and put the seat there.
- **Serve without announcing it.**
- **Note what changes in your tone.**

**The central truth, said plainly:** Mary's seat at His feet was not a withdrawal from work but a position of government over it — and Jesus called it the one necessary thing, which means the presence is not the reward for finishing your tasks but the seat from which they get ordered.

> "Father, keep me seated at Your feet before I ever stand to serve. Let Your presence be the first appointment of my day and the government of everything that follows, and let me not be cumbered about many things, in Jesus' Name. Amen."
